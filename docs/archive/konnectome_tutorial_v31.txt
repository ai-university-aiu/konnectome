THE KONNECTOME TUTORIAL

Version 31.

A complete, top-to-bottom guide to konnectome, written for a beginner, a learner, a layperson, a newcomer, a novice. No prior knowledge is assumed. Every term is explained the first time it is used.


PART ONE - WHAT KONNECTOME IS, IN ONE BREATH


konnectome is a computer program that is trying to grow a mind the way nature grows one: the smallest working piece first, and each piece earning the next.

The word Proto-AGI describes it. Proto means early or first. AGI stands for Artificial General Intelligence, which means a machine that can reason broadly about many kinds of problems, the way a person can, rather than being good at only one narrow task. So a Proto-AGI is an early, honest prototype on the road toward that goal. konnectome is a prototype, not a finished mind, and it says so plainly.

The name konnectome comes from the word connectome. A connectome is the complete map of the connections between the parts of a brain - which piece talks to which, and how. konnectome builds a small, inspectable version of such a map in software.

One more promise is built into konnectome: it is a glass box, not a black box. A black box gives you an answer but hides its reasons. A glass box lets you see every step. In konnectome, every decision can be read and explained; nothing important is hidden inside numbers no one can interpret.


PART TWO - THE FAMILY OF REPOSITORIES


A repository (often shortened to repo) is a folder of files, tracked over time by a tool called Git, usually stored online at a site called GitHub. konnectome is one of four related repositories, all owned by the same organization, called ai-university-aiu. Think of them as a family.

• PrologAI is the language. It is written in Prolog, which is a programming language built around logic and rules rather than step-by-step instructions. PrologAI is a glass-box cognitive architecture, meaning a inspectable framework for building thinking systems. konnectome is written using PrologAI.

• Causalontology is the data structure. A data structure is an agreed way of arranging information so that programs can share it. Causalontology is a shared vocabulary for describing causes and effects - what makes what happen. konnectome stores its thoughts as Causalontology records.

• Mentova is the flagship application - the first full mind built on this family - and it holds the reasoning core that scores perfectly on a famous public reasoning test called the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus for Artificial General Intelligence (ARC-AGI).

• konnectome is the newest member. It uses the finished PrologAI language and the frozen Causalontology data structure to build brain-like machinery, following a book (described in Part Six).

konnectome does not change its cousins by reaching into them. If konnectome ever needs something new from PrologAI or Causalontology, it writes the request down in a change order (explained in Part Sixteen) and waits; it never edits a cousin in place.


PART THREE - THE VOCABULARY, DEFINED SIMPLY


These are the words you will meet. Each is defined in plain language.

• Slice: the unit of building. A slice is the smallest complete, tested, merged piece of work - one pull request that adds one working capability, with its tests written first, its independent review completed, and its documents updated in the same change - so that after every slice konnectome is whole, green, and honest, never half-built. This tutorial's later parts are named after the slices they tell (Part Nine tells slice 11, and so on).

• Rung: one stage of the developmental ladder konnectome climbs - one broad capability of a growing mind, such as keeping time, sensing, speaking, reasoning, feeling, or inhabiting a body. The six rungs are listed in Part Seven, and a rung is claimed only when its stage's trials actually run. Rungs are climbed; slices are how.

• Tick: one step of simulated time. konnectome does not use clock seconds; it counts in ordinal ticks, which just means tick one, tick two, tick three, and so on, like frames of a film.

• Construct: any part of the modeled brain - a region, a connection, or a chemical. Each construct has a name and a state (its current condition, such as how active it is).

• Archetype: a shape of behavior. konnectome's guiding book claims that the whole brain runs on only six basic behaviors, called archetypes, rather than a hundred special cases. They are named relay, integrator, oscillator, attractor, gate, and comparator (all explained in Part Eight).

• Synchronous update: a rule for advancing time fairly. On each tick, every construct first looks at everyone's current state and figures out its own next state; only after everyone has decided does everyone change at once. This way no construct gets to peek at a neighbor's future. Doing this in two separate passes (first compute, then commit) is the safe way, and konnectome is careful to keep the two passes apart.

• Ontology: an organized inventory of what exists and how the things relate. Causalontology is an ontology specialized for causes and effects.

• Content-addressed identity: a way of naming a record by its own contents. A hash is a mathematical fingerprint: feed it any data and it returns a fixed-length code, and any change to the data changes the code. Causalontology names each record by the hash of its contents, so two people who write down the same fact automatically get the same name for it. The specific hash used is called the Secure Hash Algorithm 256-bit (SHA-256).

• token_occurrence: the Causalontology word for a particular happening at a particular time - for example, one specific tick. konnectome records each tick as a token_occurrence.

• Causal Relation Object (CRO): the Causalontology word for a reified cause-and-effect claim - the record that says these causes produce that effect. Reified means turned into a thing you can hold, name, and examine, rather than a bare arrow.

• Conformance vector: a published test case - an input and the exact answer a correct program must give. Causalontology has one hundred thirty-seven of them (it had one hundred nineteen at version 3.0.0; version 4.0.0 added eighteen more for its three new kinds of record), and staying correct means passing all of them.


PART FOUR - HOW KONNECTOME IS GOVERNED: THE CONSTITUTION


konnectome is built under a written CONSTITUTION.md file of seventeen numbered rules called commandments. Here they are in plain language.

• One: build with Artificial General Intelligence and Artificial Super Intelligence (a mind beyond human level) in mind, guided by three roadmap documents.

• Two: Causalontology is the structure for konnectome's thoughts, and it is frozen - changed only through a gated request process, never edited directly.

• Three: PrologAI is the language, under the same gate; konnectome never edits it directly.

• Four: every wall the build hits is written first in the ledger (a running scoreboard), then routed to exactly one place.

• Five: the six-step waterfall method (Part Five) guides the work.

• Six: English Readable Code - every line of code carries one plain-English comment above it.

• Seven: six documents, one per method phase, seeded from the guiding book's appendices.

• Eight: an audit - every named brain part in the book must have a matching code piece, tracked in a coverage list.

• Nine: only the latest version of any document lives in the open; older versions move to an archive folder.

• Ten: any code change is mirrored into the six method documents, with versions bumped and old ones archived.

• Eleven: the README (the repository's front page) is kept current and in the project's crimson-to-gold colors.

• Twelve: whole words only - names are whole English words joined by underscores, never cryptic abbreviations.

• Thirteen: a safety gate - no change may break the ARC-AGI scores or the Causalontology conformance vectors (one hundred nineteen at version 3.0.0, one hundred thirty-seven at version 4.0.0).

• Fourteen: branch-and-report discipline - work on a side branch, open a pull request, never credit an artificial-intelligence tool as an author, and use no Roman numerals.

• Fifteen: this tutorial - keep a complete, beginner-friendly tutorial, versioned and updated as the code grows.

• Sixteen: the build log - every activity of the build is logged to a versioned document called BUILDING_KONNECTOME (version 2 today), written with two purposes at once: as a scientific record whose every number is exact, and as a story a general reader can enjoy.

• Seventeen: context-window management - because the assistant cannot see how full the context window is, it offers a hand-off at each milestone and the owner watches the /context gauge; when the window is high, a dated hand-off document is written and the session pauses, so a fresh session can continue from the files on disk.


PART FIVE - THE METHOD: SPARCD, ENGLISH READABLE CODE, WHOLE WORDS


konnectome follows a waterfall method, which means doing each phase in order and writing it down. The method is called SPARCD, one letter per phase:

• S, Specification: what the system must do.

• P, Pseudocode: the logic in plain, careful English before any real code.

• A, Architecture: the major parts and how they fit.

• R, Refinement: improving the design by writing tests first, then code.

• C, Completion: the finished, tested build.

• D, Demonstration: showing what it can actually do.

Six documents in the docs/ folder, one per phase, hold this method for konnectome. Each was seeded from the matching appendix of the guiding book, and each is bumped to a new version whenever code changes.

English Readable Code, abbreviated ERC, is the rule that every single line of program code has one plain-English sentence directly above it explaining that line. This lets a newcomer read the intent without knowing Prolog.

Whole-Word System means every name is made of whole English words in lower case joined by underscores (for example, tick_engine and connection_graph), never short codes. This is for clarity.


PART SIX - THE ARCHITECTURE: TEN COMPONENTS


konnectome follows a book titled Nature's Cognitive Architecture, which describes a mind as ten cooperating components. konnectome builds them one at a time.

• Component 1, the Construct Registry: the parts bin that holds every construct.

• Component 2, the Scheduler (or Tick Engine): the dumb timekeeper that advances one tick at a time and runs the two-pass synchronous update. It has no opinions; it only keeps time.

• Component 3, the Connection Graph: the connectome as data - the directed, weighted links between constructs.

• Component 4, the Neuromodulatory Bus: a broadcast channel for brain chemicals (such as dopamine) that set the global mood and the learning signal.

• Component 5, the Drive System: the homeostatic drives (needs kept near a set-point, like hunger near fullness) that create motivation and reward.

• Component 6, the Action Selector: chooses exactly one action from the competing proposals and never invents one of its own.

• Component 7, the Override Controller: lets old, vital needs (like breathing) override slower deliberation.

• Component 8, the Plasticity Engine: changes the connection weights over time - this is learning.

• Component 9, the Observer: watches and records everything at every tick, changing nothing. It is what makes konnectome a glass box.

• Component 10, the Perturbation Interface: lets an experimenter poke the system (lesion a part, shift a chemical) to run experiments.


PART SEVEN - THE BUILD LADDER


The guiding book gives a developmental ladder, inspired by how human infants grow (the stages of the psychologist Jean Piaget) and by developmental robotics (building machines that learn in the same infant-to-adult order). The book counts the rungs from zero, the way builders often count floors from the ground, and this tutorial follows the book so the numbers here match what the capstone story prints. konnectome climbs it smallest rung first:

• Rung zero: a heartbeat - a stable, repeating tick loop that keeps time.

• Rung one: senses wired in, and holding a hidden object in mind.

• Rung two: symbols and language - words grounded in experience, and pretend play.

• Rung three: reasoning - logic and cause-and-effect trials.

• Rung four: emotion and understanding other minds.

• Rung five: a body - inhabiting a robot and using its senses.

The build never leaps ahead; each rung must stand before the next is attempted.


PART EIGHT - WHAT IS BUILT SO FAR


konnectome's code lives in packs. A pack is a small, self-contained module with three files: a manifest (its name and version), the code, and a test suite. Twenty-seven packs exist so far.

• tick_engine (Component 2, the heartbeat). It runs the system for a chosen number of ticks using the two-pass synchronous update. A passive observer records each tick. Its tests prove it keeps time, records each tick once, never lets a construct see a neighbor's future, and gives the exact same result every run.

• archetype (the six rules). It holds the six behaviors every construct can have:
  relay - pass the input, scaled (a pipe);
  integrator - add up input while slowly leaking (a bucket);
  oscillator - keep a steady rhythm (a metronome);
  attractor - complete a remembered pattern from a fragment (a memory);
  gate - flip a switch only when pushed hard enough, then resist flipping back (a stiff switch);
  comparator - report the difference between expected and actual (a scale).
  Each rule has its own test, drawn from a famous experiment.

• observer (Component 9, the first thought). It records each tick as a Causalontology token_occurrence, naming it by the hash of its contents through the reused PrologAI causal_core code. This is konnectome's first durable thought, stored in the shared data structure without changing a line of it. Its tests prove the record is valid, correctly named, repeatable, and different for different ticks.

• connection_graph (Component 3, the wiring). It holds the directed, weighted links between constructs, gathers the total input arriving at each construct, and lets a signal travel across a chain one hop per tick. Its main test presents a signal at one end of a three-link chain and confirms it arrives at the far end after exactly three ticks, and not one tick sooner.

• neuromodulator_bus (Component 4, the broadcast bus). It is the one shared channel of brain-chemical levels - dopamine, norepinephrine, and the rest. Source parts write a level onto it, and every other part reads; an absent chemical reads as zero. Its tests prove a broadcast level can be read back, the newest broadcast wins, and different chemicals are held independently.

• drive_system (Component 5, the reward loop). It holds homeostatic drives - needs kept near a set-point, like temperature near thirty-seven. Each tick a drive measures how far its monitored value sits from its set-point, and the reward for the whole system is the total amount by which that distance shrank across all drives. Moving closer earns a positive reward; moving away earns a negative one; the very first tick earns none. The reward is then broadcast as dopamine on the bus. Its tests prove all of these behaviors.

• action_selector (Component 6, the referee). It receives candidate actions, each with a salience (a strength or urgency), and releases exactly one - the strongest - while suppressing the rest. Its one iron rule is that it never invents an action: what it releases is always one of the choices it was given. A pressing drive can raise the salience of the action that would relieve it, so what the system most needs tends to win. Its tests prove the strongest wins, exactly one is released, nothing is ever invented, ties break the same way every time, and a pressing drive can bias its action to win.

• plasticity_engine (Component 8, learning). It is the three-factor learning rule that makes the map alive. For each connection, the weight change is the product of three things: how active the sending end was, how active the receiving end was, and how much dopamine (reward) was present. A connection grows stronger only when both ends fire together AND the moment was rewarded. This closes the loop of the mind: the system wants, acts, is rewarded, and learns. Its tests prove that coincident activity plus dopamine strengthens a connection, that without dopamine nothing is learned, that a silent end learns nothing, and that a computational connection does not change.

• override_controller (Component 7, the safety). It enforces which drives may seize control. Old, vital drives - breathing above all - are ranked most urgent, and when such a drive is in distress it overrides slower deliberation and forces its own action. Its iron rule is that the breathing drive can never be suppressed: the system cannot deliberate itself to death. Its tests prove a calm system keeps deliberating, a vital drive in distress seizes control, the most vital drive wins when several compete, and breathing cannot be overridden.

• perturbation_interface (Component 10, the experimenter's hand). It lets a researcher poke the glass box: lesion a part (silence it), cut a connection (remove it), or shift a chemical (for example, deplete dopamine to model Parkinson's disease). Its tests prove each poke works, including a trial where depleting dopamine halts learning.

• cognitive_cycle (the whole mind in one loop). It wires all ten components together into a single tick, in the right order: the drives compute a reward and the bus broadcasts it as dopamine; every construct updates in the two-pass way; the action selector releases one action, which the override controller may seize; the plasticity engine learns from what just happened; and the observer records the tick as a thought. Its tests prove the whole loop runs - a reward becomes dopamine and strengthens the active connection, a vital drive overrides the normal action, each tick records its own thought, and the same start always gives the same result. It is a closed sensorimotor loop: the drives themselves propose the actions, a construct's gain is set by the bus, and the body responds to what the mind does - so acting to reduce a need actually moves the body toward its set-point, and that improvement becomes the next tick's reward.

• thought_combination (combining thoughts). This is how konnectome forms new thoughts by combining old ones. An atomic thought is a Causalontology occurrent (an event or a process, a kind of verb). To combine thoughts - to say these inputs produce that output - it mints a Causal Relation Object: a reified, content-addressed, unchangeable cause-and-effect record whose causes are the input thoughts and whose effect is the new one. Because a combination points at its thoughts rather than swallowing them, one combination's effect can be reused as another's cause - that is a chain of thought, and a branching, converging set of them is a tree of thought. Its tests prove an atomic thought is a content-addressed occurrent, a combination names itself by its own content and is reproducible, only real thoughts may be combined, an empty combination is rejected, marking a combination as sufficient gives it its own identity, and effect-to-cause links form a chain of thought. It reuses PrologAI's causal_core and never touches the frozen data structure.

• other_minds (modelling other minds). This is how konnectome represents another agent's mind, including a belief that is false and a belief nested inside a belief. Theory of mind means holding a picture of what someone else believes, wants, or intends, even when it differs from what you yourself know. A false belief is a belief that does not match the world: in the classic test, a girl named Sally hides a marble in a basket and leaves; someone moves it to a box; and a mind with theory of mind predicts that Sally, on her return, will look in the basket, because that is where she still believes it is. konnectome reuses PrologAI's ready-made theory_of_mind machinery and, inside its own running memory, holds Sally's false belief, predicts she looks in the basket, and even nests the model - konnectome holds a model of Sally holding a belief. At slice 11 this pack (version 0.1.0, eight tests) hit a wall: the shared Causalontology data structure had no kind of record for a belief, so the model could not be written out as a shareable record. At slice 12 the wall is CLOSED: Causalontology 4.0.0 added a belief-shaped kind of record (called an attitude), and this pack (now version 0.2.0, ten tests) mints the belief - true, false, and nested - as a real, shareable record, while still keeping the honest fact that a plain assertion names only its signer (see Part Nine for the wall, Part Ten for the closure).

• prediction_loop (expecting, and being surprised). This is how konnectome expects something and then measures how wrong it was. A prediction is a guess about what will happen; a prediction error is the signed, graded gap between what was expected and what actually happened - the machine form of surprise. konnectome holds an expectation across many ticks, records what actually happened as a Causalontology token_occurrence, and each tick works out the signed error (for the hidden-object test, a signed minus-one when the screen lifts and the object is gone). At slice 11 this pack (version 0.1.0, ten tests) hit a wall: the data structure had no kind of record for an expected happening as distinct from a real one, so a forecast and a report of the same thing got the exact same fingerprint, and a prediction could not be told apart from a claim of fact. At slice 12 the wall is CLOSED: Causalontology 4.0.0 added a kind of record for a forecast (predicted_occurrence) and a kind for the surprise itself (prediction_error), and this pack (now version 0.2.0, sixteen tests) mints the forecast before arrival, the real outcome on arrival, and the signed surprise that pairs them (see Part Nine for the wall, Part Ten for the closure).

• self_provenance (grading and disowning the mind's own thoughts). This is how konnectome takes responsibility for a thought instead of merely having it. Provenance means the record of where a claim comes from and how much to trust it. With this pack, konnectome makes an assertion about one of its own records - a belief it attributed, a forecast it made - and on that assertion it writes an evidence grade (how the claim was come by: by acting, by watching, by simulating, by reasoning, by a human hint, or by import, ranked strongest to weakest in that order) and a confidence (a number from zero to one). When a later tick proves a thought wrong - the screen lifts and the expected object is gone - konnectome retracts its own assertion, the honest exit; and when a forecast is confirmed it supersedes the old grade with a stronger one. Its seventeen tests prove all of this, including that the mind that HOLDS a belief and the mind that VOUCHES for it are kept distinct, and that a graded thought is a shareable record even before it is cryptographically signed. One honest limit is written down: a real signature needs a private key, which is a secret and may never live in the code, so konnectome carries its public identity and leaves the actual signing to whoever runs it. It reuses PrologAI's causal_core and never touches the frozen data structure (see Part Eleven for the whole story).

• capstone_demonstration (the mind, run and told whole). This is the one pack that exists so a person can WATCH everything above happen. It boots the whole mind, runs it for a chosen number of ticks, and prints a story in plain English in which every line is carried by a real record: the heartbeat and its rewards, the learning, the regulated body, the forecast and the surprise, a chain of combined thoughts, the mind's first grounded words and its pretend game under quarantine, Sally's false belief and the empathy trial in which the mind feels another's trouble and helps, and the mind grading and - when the world disagrees - taking back its own claims. As of slice 18 the story tells Rung Two and the Rung Four deepening in its own telling, so its printed honest-limits list has shrunk by a rung. Its thirty-six tests prove the story is deterministic (told twice, it is identical), that it cannot flatter the mind (the tests re-run the same world outside the story and check the dynamics directly), that the milestones appear in ladder order, and that the story names honestly what it does not demonstrate. It comes with the repository's first runnable command, described in Part Fifteen (see Part Twelve for the whole story).

• symbol_exchange (first words, grounded in experience). This is konnectome's first step into language. A word here is never a token looked up in a table: it may only be grounded in - paired with - an inner condition the mind has actually lived through a real run. The mind lives from too-warm to settled, and so earns two words: warm (grounded in the deficit it felt) and settled (grounded in the satisfaction it reached). It can then use each word in both directions - produce the word for its current condition, and recognize the word when it hears it, the hearing passing through a reused word bank so it genuinely changes the mind's state. Best of all, its replies are coloured by its drives: asked the same thing, a too-warm mind answers warm, settled (what I am, what I seek) and a settled mind answers just settled. Its nineteen tests prove all of this, including the refusals that keep it honest: a word cannot be grounded in a condition the mind never lived (see Part Thirteen for the whole story).

• pretend_play (pretending, without confusing it for the real). This is how konnectome imagines a situation that is not happening and reasons inside it safely. It reuses a ready-made quarantine: facts live in named realities (observed, desired, expected, imagined, recalled), a pretend rollout is sealed automatically into the imagined reality, and nothing pretended ever touches the observed record. Moving a fact between realities takes a deliberate act called promotion - and konnectome adds one rule of its own: pretend may NEVER be promoted into the observed record, because only the world's own reveal writes what was observed. A pretend thought can still be written down and owned: it is minted as an ordinary combined thought (a generic that claims no happening) and the mind's stance on it is graded simulation, honestly below observation. Its twelve tests prove the quarantine, the refusal, and the record (see Part Thirteen for the whole story).

• empathy (feeling with another, and social pain). This is how another's trouble becomes this mind's reason to help. It rests on a striking finding of social neuroscience: in the brain, social rejection activates the same circuitry as physical pain - a broken heart and a broken arm share machinery. konnectome reproduces that literally: it has a social-connection drive that defends a variable called belonging exactly the way the temperature drive defends thirty-seven, and a rejection is registered by the very same error-and-reward machinery as a too-warm body - the tests compute both pains through the same code and show the numbers equal. Empathy is then resonance: when konnectome models another agent (Ana) as being in distress, its OWN belonging falls, its own error rises, and its own drives propose the helping action - which the selector releases only if it genuinely outweighs the mind's other needs, and which is never invented. Helping restores connection, for both. Breathing still overrides everything, even kindness. And the mind owns its social read: Ana's modelled distress is minted as a shareable record (Ana FEARS her connection severed), the mind stands behind the attribution at observation grade, and - if Ana turns out to be fine - it takes the attribution back, on the record. Its seventeen tests prove all of this (see Part Fourteen for the whole story).

• situation_appraisal (judging a situation good or bad for the mind's own goals). This is the appraisal pilot, the first of Rung Four's last two trials. It does not invent a new feeling; it reads one the mind already has. A situation is judged by what it does to a drive's set-point: if the situation violates a defended set-point, the mind appraises it as a threat and FEARS it; if the set-point is met, or the situation is moving the mind toward it, the mind appraises it as good and DESIRES it. The good-or-bad reading is exactly the drives' own error, read through a valence. And the mind owns the appraisal on the record - as an attitude of the standard's own vocabulary, graded at observation, and taken back honestly if a later look at the world corrects it. Its fourteen tests pin the reading in both directions, the honest retraction, and (from slice 20) that two float situations equidistant across the set-point read as a neutral no-change rather than a phantom good or bad.

• arousal_regulation (bringing an aroused state back to rest). This is the regulation pilot, the second of the two. Arousal is carried the way the guiding book says it is - as norepinephrine, one of the brain chemicals on the shared neuromodulatory bus - and regulation is simply the return of that level toward its calm resting baseline over time. Each tick closes half the remaining distance to baseline, and once the level is within a hair of rest it settles exactly at baseline and stops - it never overshoots into the opposite extreme, the same discipline the drive system uses to settle a body at its set-point without going past it. Its fourteen tests fix the halving, the monotone return, the exact settle, the no-overshoot guarantee, and (from slice 20) that a non-finite arousal is refused so the return always terminates. With these two, Rung Four is finished.

• concrete_operations (the logic trials of the reasoning rung). This is one half of Rung Three's opening. It reasons about concrete, grounded situations, and its rule is that you must always be able to WATCH it reason. Ask it whether a block resting on a cup resting on a table stands above the table and it does not merely answer yes - it hands back the whole derivation as a list of named steps: resting is above, resting is above, then a transitive chain joining the two. It also sorts a small collection of objects into groups by one property and then re-sorts the same objects by another (round things and square things become red things and green things on request); it puts objects in order along a dimension such as size; and it judges whether a transformation changed an amount or only changed how the amount LOOKS - water poured into a taller, thinner glass is the same water, while adding fifty really is fifty more. Its twenty-six tests pin all of that, and pin what it refuses: a support cycle, a malformed fact, an object with no value or an unbound value on the sorting property, a negative addition, a removal larger than what is there.

• cause_and_effect (the causal trials of the reasoning rung). This is the other half. It answers the question a correlation can never answer: is this a real cause, or did these two things merely happen together? And it answers it BY THE RECORD - the causal records the mind minted back at slice 10. If a record (or an unbroken chain of them) links the two events, the answer is "genuine cause", and the reason given is the chain of record identifiers themselves, so you can check the mind's evidence. The rooster crows and the sun rises every morning, but no record links them, so the honest answer is "co-occurrence only". If a record points the other way, the mind says so rather than quietly flipping it. And a forged record - one shaped like a real one but never actually minted - is refused, because the mind re-derives every record's fingerprint from its own contents before trusting it. The pack also plans: given a start, a goal, and the actions it knows, it finds the SHORTEST sequence that gets there, justifies each step with the states it leaves and reaches, and commits to the plan by owning it as an intention. Its twenty-five tests pin all of that. Both new packs grade what they conclude as DERIVATION rather than observation - the mind reasoned these things out, it did not see them, and the record says so.

• category_formation (the categories the mind forms for itself). This finished the first of Rung Three's two deferred halves. Show the mind a stream of experiences - a red round fruit, another red round fruit, a blue round toy, a blue square toy - and it coins its OWN categories from what the experiences share: nobody tells it "fruit" and "toy"; the groups fall out of the overlap. It then sorts objects into those self-made categories, honestly reports an object that fits none of them as outside every category, and re-sorts the whole collection by a different property on request by handing the job to the sorter it already had. Even the way it OWNS this conclusion is honest: the record can only be minted by actually running a learning episode, so a hand-written list of categories can never be passed off as something the mind learned. Its twenty-six tests pin all of that.

• quantity_model (the represented model of quantity). This finished the second deferred half, and it is the pack that stops the mind being fooled by looks. An amount is represented as individuated units - unit one, unit two, unit three, unit four, unit five - plus a description of how it currently looks. Pour those five units from a short wide glass into a tall thin one and the verdict is "conserved at five", not because "pour" is on a safe list, but because the mind re-counts its units on both sides and finds the same five. The proof that this is real understanding and not a lookup: a sneaky transformation that CALLS itself a pour but spills a unit is judged "changed, five to four" - the name said appearance-only, the world said otherwise, and the model sided with the world. Its twenty-nine tests pin all of that, and the conservation verdict too can only be owned on the record by actually running the trial.

• simulated_body (the honestly-named stand-in machine). The guiding book's embodied rung demands a real robot, and this pack does not pretend to be one: it is a tiny simulated machine - a camera, a battery that drains, and three actuators (steer around, recharge, hold still) - built so the seam a real robot would plug into can be built and tested today. Its sixteen tests pin the physics.

PART NINE - SLICE 11: OTHER MINDS, PREDICTION, AND THE FIRST TWO WALLS


Slice 11 is a turning point in the build, because it is the first slice that hit a wall - and hitting a wall, honestly recorded, is exactly what this build method is for. Here is the whole story in plain language.

First, three ideas.

• Theory of mind. This is the ability to hold a picture of what is going on inside someone else's head - what they believe, want, or intend - even when it differs from what you yourself know. A child develops it in the preschool years, and it is widely thought to be a building block of real social intelligence.

• A false belief. This is a belief that does not match reality. The classic test is the Sally-and-the-marble story: Sally puts a marble in a basket and leaves the room; while she is gone, someone moves the marble to a box; when Sally comes back, where will she look? A mind with theory of mind answers the basket, because that is where Sally still believes the marble is, even though we know it is really in the box. Predicting the basket - the wrong-but-honest place - is passing the false-belief test.

• A prediction error. A prediction is a guess about what is about to happen. A prediction error is the gap between that guess and what actually happened, with a direction and a size - in plain words, a measured surprise. If you expect an object to still be behind a screen and the screen lifts to show nothing, your surprise is a prediction error, and konnectome computes it as a signed number.

Second, the two new packs.

• other_minds gives konnectome theory of mind, by reusing PrologAI's ready-made theory_of_mind machinery. Inside its own running memory, konnectome holds Sally's false belief, predicts she will search the basket, and even nests the model - konnectome holds a model of Sally holding a belief.

• prediction_loop gives konnectome the expect-and-compare loop. It remembers what it expected, records what actually happened, and each tick works out the signed prediction error between them.

Both packs run, and all of their tests pass. So what is the problem?

Third, what a wall is, and why these two are real.

• A wall, in this build method, is a moment when konnectome needs something it cannot express - not because konnectome's own code is weak, but because the shared data structure it must write its thoughts into (Causalontology, frozen at version 3.0.0) has no shape for the thing. konnectome is not allowed to quietly work around a wall; it writes the wall down in the ledger (Part Sixteen) and routes it to the right place.

• Wall-1 (theory of mind). konnectome can hold Sally's false belief privately, but it cannot write that belief out as a shareable Causalontology record, because Causalontology has eighteen kinds of record and not one of them is for a belief. The closest kind records only who signed a claim, not whose mind holds it; it cannot hold something false without treating it as a fact; and it cannot nest one belief inside another. A model of someone else's mind is of little use if it can never leave your own mind to be shared - so this is a real wall.

• Wall-2 (prediction). konnectome can compute a prediction and its error privately, but Causalontology has no kind for an expected happening as separate from a real one. So a forecast (what I expect at tick seven) and a report (what happened at tick seven), if they describe the same thing, get the exact same fingerprint - the prediction is indistinguishable from a claim of fact. A predictive loop that cannot be inspected or shared as a prediction is only a private number - so this too is a real wall.

Both walls passed the build's key test - the thing genuinely needs to be SHARED between minds and repositories, not merely computed inside one - so both were routed to the Causalontology change order.

Fourth, what happens next.

The change order is the held wish-list of additions to the frozen data structure. Slice 11's two walls release two of its sections: Section B (a kind for an agent's attitude - belief, desire, intention - that can be false and can nest) and Section A (kinds for a predicted happening and a prediction error). With the owner's written go-ahead, given on the twenty-second of July twenty twenty-six, those two sections ship together as the next major version of the data structure, Causalontology 4.0.0. Once that lands, konnectome will be able to SHARE what it can already privately hold - a belief, and a prediction - and the two walls will close.


PART TEN - SLICE 12: THE CLOSURE, AND HOW A WALL GETS CLOSED


Slice 11 ended on a cliffhanger: two walls, honestly recorded, waiting for the shared data structure to grow. Slice 12 is where they close. This is the moment the whole build method was designed to reach, so it is worth watching closely.

First, what Causalontology 4.0.0 added, and why.

A wall in this build is never patched by a quiet workaround. It is written down, routed to exactly one place, and closed only when that place delivers. Wall-1 and Wall-2 were routed to the Causalontology change order - the held wish-list of additions to the shared data structure. With the owner's written go-ahead, that wish-list shipped as a new version of the data structure, Causalontology 4.0.0. It grew the list of kinds of record from eighteen to twenty-one by adding exactly the three that were missing:

• attitude - a record for what a mind believes, wants, or intends. Crucially, it names the holder (whose mind this is), so a belief can be told apart from a fact, can be wrong without poisoning the shared record of what is really true, and can even point at another attitude, so one belief can sit inside another.

• predicted_occurrence - a record for a forecast, a thing expected to happen, kept separate from a record of a thing that actually did happen.

• prediction_error - a record for the surprise itself: what was expected, what was observed, and the signed gap between them.

Because these three were added and nothing old was changed, all one hundred nineteen of the earlier test cases still pass exactly as before, and eighteen new ones were added, for one hundred thirty-seven in total. Adding without breaking is the iron rule of every change order.

Second, what konnectome can now record and share. Before, konnectome could privately think these thoughts but never write them down for another mind to read. Now it can:

• a belief that can be false and nested. konnectome mints Sally's true belief, her FALSE belief (which sits quietly beside the real state of the world without pretending to be it), and the nested belief (konnectome's model of Sally's belief) - each as a real, shareable, content-addressed record.

• a forecast distinct from a report. The expected object-behind-the-screen is now minted as a predicted_occurrence before the screen lifts, and the real outcome as an ordinary happening - two different records, no longer sharing one fingerprint.

• the surprise as a first-class record. The signed gap between the two is minted as a prediction_error record an outside auditor can read and grade, instead of a number that vanished at the end of the tick.

Third - and this is the part the build is proudest of - the complete life of a wall, shown from end to end:

• HIT. Building slice 11 for real, konnectome needed to share a belief and a prediction and could not, because the eighteen kinds had no shape for either. A wall is only ever discovered by building, never guessed at from an armchair.

• RECORDED. Each wall was written into the ledger, the honest scoreboard, as a numbered finding with the evidence for it.

• ROUTED. Each was sent to exactly one destination. A belief and a prediction must be SHARED between minds, so both went to the Causalontology change order - not to the language, and not into konnectome's own private code.

• RELEASED. With the owner's written authorization, the change order shipped as Causalontology 4.0.0.

• EXECUTED and CLOSED. konnectome built the export half - the two packs now mint the new kinds - and the two walls closed.

There was even a third wall along the way, and it shows the method handling a surprise of its own. When konnectome went to mint the new kinds, it found that the PrologAI library it borrows had not yet learned the three new kinds either - it still knew only the old eighteen. That is a language-library gap, not a data-structure gap, so it was recorded as Wall-3 and routed to the PrologAI change order instead. It was delivered the same day, as an addition called causal_core 1.1.0, with every safety gate green. So three walls were hit and all three were closed, each routed to the one right place, and not one cousin was ever edited by reaching into it.

That is the whole thesis of the build in a single slice: find what is missing by building, write it down, route it to exactly one place, and let that place deliver it through its own gate - so the growing mind rests on a foundation that is shared and honest, never on a private workaround.


PART ELEVEN - SLICE 13: THE MIND GRADES AND DISOWNS ITS OWN THOUGHTS


Slice 12 gave konnectome the ability to write its beliefs and its forecasts down as shareable records. But writing a thought down is not the same as standing behind it. Slice 13 adds the missing half: konnectome now takes RESPONSIBILITY for its own thoughts - it says how it came to each one, how sure it is, and, when a later tick proves it wrong, it takes the thought back, on the record.

First, the idea of provenance. Provenance is the history and standing of a claim: who says it, on what grounds, and how much to trust it. In everyday life you weigh a fact you saw with your own eyes more than a rumor you were told; provenance is that weighing, written down. Causalontology, the shared data structure, has always had a provenance layer for exactly this - an assertion (a claim about a record) and a retraction (a take-back) - but until now konnectome had never used it on its own thoughts.

Second, what the new pack does. The self_provenance pack lets konnectome do five things:

• Assert. It makes a claim about one of its own records - for instance, that Sally really does hold the belief konnectome attributed to her, or that the object really will be behind the screen at tick six.

• Grade the evidence. Every assertion carries an evidence grade from a fixed ladder, strongest to weakest: acting on the world (intervention), watching it (observation), running a model (simulation), reasoning it out (derivation), a human's hint (human_hint), and an import from elsewhere (imported). A forecast konnectome makes from its own model is honestly graded simulation - below a real observation - because that is what it is.

• State a confidence. A number from zero to one, saying how sure the mind is.

• Retract. When a later tick disproves a thought - the screen lifts and the expected object is gone - konnectome withdraws its own earlier assertion, giving a plain reason. This is the honest exit: a mind that can be wrong on the record and correct itself.

• Supersede. When a forecast is instead confirmed, konnectome retracts the tentative simulation-graded claim and restates it, now graded observation - the stronger evidence - about the very same thing.

Third, why this slice hit no wall. Slices 11 and 12 hit walls because the shared data structure lacked a shape for a belief and for a prediction. This slice needed no new shape: the provenance layer was already there, waiting, from earlier versions of Causalontology. So slice 13 simply used it, and closed a loose end slice 12 had honestly left open - that konnectome minted belief and prediction records but had not yet graded or retracted any of them. No wall was hit; a promise was kept.

Fourth, the one honest limit, written down. A real cryptographic signature - the thing that lets a stranger VERIFY, not merely read, konnectome's claim - needs a private key. A private key is a secret, and konnectome's own rules forbid any secret from living in its code. So konnectome carries its public identity and mints records that are complete, named by their contents, and shareable, but leaves the actual act of signing to whoever runs the mind and holds its key. A strict reader would set the unsigned records aside until that signature arrives. The build claims exactly what it built - a mind that grades and disowns its own thoughts - and honestly does not claim the signing it deferred.


PART TWELVE - SLICE 14: THE CAPSTONE, WHERE THE MIND RUNS AND TELLS ITS OWN STORY


Everything before this slice could be checked, but only by reading test files. Slice 14 adds the thing a newcomer actually wants: one command that boots the mind, lets it live for a while, and prints what happened - in plain English, with every claim carried by a real record.

First, what the story shows, in the ladder's own order.

• The heartbeat and the body. The mind boots with its body too warm - temperature forty against a defended set-point of thirty-seven - and one drive watching that number. Tick by tick, the story prints the reward the drives computed, the dopamine broadcast that carries it, the one action released, and the record the observer minted. By the end, the body sits at thirty-seven and the learnable connection has grown stronger: wanting, acting, and learning, visible in numbers.

• The forecast and the surprise. An object is hidden behind a screen; the mind holds a forecast that it still exists. The story is told twice: once kindly (the screen lifts, the object is there, the signed error is zero) and once cruelly (the screen lifts on nothing, the signed error is minus one - the machine form of surprise).

• The chain of thought. Rain causes wet ground; wet ground causes a slippery path. Two combined thoughts, each a content-addressed record, and the story shows that the first one's effect really is the second one's cause - a chain a reader can check from the records alone.

• The false belief. The marble is really in the box, but Sally believes it is in the basket, and the mind holds both records - the world's and Sally's - without letting the false one poison the true one. Asked where Sally will search, it answers from her belief: the basket.

Second, why you can trust the story. The story is deterministic: told twice, it is identical down to every fingerprint, because every record is named by its own contents. And the story cannot flatter the mind: the pack's tests re-run the very same world outside the narration and check directly that the body really settled and the weight really grew - and the narration itself refuses to print a claim the run did not achieve. The story even ends by saying what it does NOT show: the rungs not yet climbed, and the fact that its records are shareable but not yet cryptographically signed.

It needed no new shape from the data structure and no new power from the language - so, like slice 13, it hit no wall and recorded no new limit. It simply made the whole build watchable.


PART THIRTEEN - SLICE 15: FIRST WORDS AND PRETEND PLAY - RUNG TWO OPENS


Slice 14 let you watch the mind live. Slice 15 begins the rung many readers ask about first: language. It opens Rung Two of the ladder - the rung of symbols, words, and pretend - and it opens it the honest way, at the bottom.

First, what it means to ground a word. A chatbot connects words to other words. konnectome connects a word to something it has lived. Before it may use the word warm, the mind must actually have BEEN too warm - lived through ticks in which its temperature drive was in deficit - and before it may use settled, it must actually have reached its set-point. The grounding run is a real run of the whole mind, the same too-warm-to-settled world the capstone tells; and if you try to ground a word in a condition the mind never lived, it refuses, with a named error. Experience first, words second.

Second, the smoke test: one symbol, both directions. Given its grounded words, the mind can produce the right word for its current condition (speaking), and, hearing a word, can say which condition it names (understanding) - and the hearing is real: the word passes into a reused word bank and leaves a trace, so listening genuinely changes the mind's state.

Third, the pilot's key property: the mood colours the words. Ask the mind the same thing twice - once while its body is too warm, once while it is settled. The too-warm mind answers warm, settled: a childlike, two-word sentence meaning this is what I am, and this is what I seek. The settled mind answers simply settled. Same question, different inner weather, different words. A program that predicts likely next words cannot do this, because it has no inner weather; a mind with drives can, and that difference is the whole point of the rung. The simplicity of the speech is deliberate: a three-year-old's sentences are short, and an honest three-year-old is worth more than a fluent mask.

Fourth, pretend play. The other half of the rung is imagination: representing a situation that is not happening, and reasoning inside it without ever confusing it for the real record. konnectome reuses a ready-made quarantine of five named realities - observed, desired, expected, imagined, recalled. When the mind pretends (say: the sun beats down and its settled body grows too warm again - a path it never actually lived), everything the pretence visits is sealed automatically into the imagined reality. The observed record - what really happened - is never touched. Moving a fact between realities takes a deliberate act, and konnectome forbids the one move that could ever lie: pretend may never be promoted into the observed record.

Fifth, the watch that was kept. The owner asked this slice to watch honestly for the finding that would unlock a held addition to the shared data structure (a world-level reality tag, Section C of the change order). The watch's result: no such finding. The reused quarantine and the existing per-claim machinery were enough, and no need ever arose to SHARE a whole imagined world with another mind. So nothing was built from the armchair, and the section stays held - a non-finding, recorded as carefully as a finding would have been, in the ledger.


PART FOURTEEN - SLICE 16: SOCIAL PAIN, AND FEELING WITH ANOTHER - RUNG FOUR DEEPENS


Slice 16 climbs to the top of the emotional and social rung with the two trials the guiding book names: the social-pain test and the empathy trial.

First, the social-pain test, which is a real experiment before it is a test. Social neuroscientists found that being rejected activates the same brain circuitry that registers physical pain - the evidence that the need for connection is a defended set-point, not a luxury. konnectome re-runs the experiment in miniature. It gains a social-connection drive that watches a body variable called belonging and defends a set-point, exactly as the temperature drive watches temperature. Deliver a rejection - sever belonging - and the SAME code that registers a too-warm body registers the loss: the same error computation, the same step, a reward of minus one, dopamine of minus one on the shared broadcast. The tests compute a physical thwarting and a social one of equal size through the identical machinery and assert the two numbers equal. No special emotion pathway was built, because the book says feeling is what the drive machinery already is.

Second, the empathy trial. Empathy here is not a sentiment bolted on; it is resonance through the mind's own body. When konnectome models another agent - call her Ana - as being in distress, that model lowers konnectome's OWN belonging. Its own error rises; it literally feels worse because she does. And that changed inner state changes what it does: its drives propose the helping action alongside everything else it needs, and the action selector releases whichever need is greatest. The load-bearing test gives the mind a mildly pressing physical need and asks twice: with Ana fine, the mind tends its own temperature; with Ana in full distress, the resonated social pain outweighs the physical need and the mind helps - and helping restores connection for both. Three honesty rules hold throughout: the helping action is proposed by the drives and never invented by the selector; breathing still overrides everything, even kindness; and the whole trial is deterministic.

Third, owning the social read. Modelling someone as distressed is a claim about them, and claims must be owned. The modelled distress is minted as a shareable record using the data structure's own vocabulary of attitudes: Ana FEARS her connection severed. konnectome stands behind that attribution with an assertion graded observation - it watched the rejection happen - and if Ana turns out to be fine, it retracts the attribution, on the record, under its own name. The mind can now be wrong about another's heart and correct itself the same honest way it corrects a wrong forecast about a hidden object.

What was once said to remain on this rung is now built. As of slice 19, Rung Four is FINISHED: the appraisal trial (judging a situation as good or bad for one's own goals) and the regulation trial (bringing an aroused state back toward baseline) join the smoke test, the theory-of-mind trial, and the empathy trial. Both were built the honest konnectome way - by reusing what the mind already is, not by bolting on an emotion engine. Appraisal (the situation_appraisal pack) reads a situation's goodness straight from the drives' own set-point error: a violated set-point is a threat the mind FEARS, a met or improving one is GOOD and the mind DESIRES it, and the verdict is owned on the record as an attitude, graded, and taken back if the world later corrects it. Regulation (the arousal_regulation pack) carries an aroused state as norepinephrine on the shared neuromodulatory bus and brings it back down toward its resting baseline over ticks - halving the distance each tick and settling exactly at rest, never overshooting, the same way the drive system settles a body at its set-point. The capstone story, since slice 18, already narrates this rung's deepening and Rung Two's first words; what remained for the mind after that were the rungs it had not opened at all - Rung Three's concrete reasoning, opened in the next Part, and Rung Five's embodiment.


PART FIFTEEN - SLICE 21: THE REASONING RUNG OPENS, AND WHAT IT HONESTLY DOES NOT YET DO


Slice 21 opens Rung Three, the reasoning rung - the stage the psychologist Jean Piaget called concrete operational, roughly ages seven to eleven, when a child begins to reason with logic and rules about real, concrete things in front of them. The guiding book asks for one smoke test and five trials, and it attaches one condition to all of them: every inference must be a named, watchable operation, never an opaque leap. That condition is the whole point. A system that produces the right answer by means nobody can inspect has not demonstrated reasoning; it has demonstrated a correct guess.

THE SMOKE TEST, PASSED IN FULL. A block rests on a cup; the cup rests on a table; therefore the block is above the table. The mind derives it - and hands back the derivation itself, as data: first, resting on something means being above it (a step named resting_is_above); again for the cup and the table; then a step named transitive_chain joins the two into the conclusion. A test reads those steps and checks each one is named and carries the facts it consumed. So the claim "you can watch it reason" is enforced by a test, not merely promised in a comment.

THE THREE TRIALS THAT ARE FULLY REALIZED. Seriation: put objects in order along a dimension, so a pea, an apple, and a brick sort themselves smallest to largest. Causal reasoning: tell a genuine cause from a mere coincidence - and do it by the RECORD. If a causal record links the sun shining to the stone warming, the answer is "genuine cause", and the mind hands over the record's own identifier as its reason. If the rooster's crow and the sunrise have no record between them, the honest answer is "co-occurrence only", however many mornings they have shared. This is the thing pattern-matching systems famously get wrong, and konnectome gets it right for a structural reason: its data structure reifies causation, so a cause is a thing it can point at. Planning: given where it is, where it wants to be, and the actions it knows, the mind finds the shortest sequence that gets there and justifies every step.

THE TWO TRIALS THAT ARE OPENED BUT NOT FINISHED - AND THIS IS SAID PLAINLY. Classification asks for two things: sorting a collection by a property and re-sorting it by another on request, AND that the categories be ones the mind formed from its own experience. The first half is built; the second is not. Conservation asks whether the mind models the world rather than its surface - whether it knows that water poured into a taller, thinner glass is the same water. What is built is the SHAPE of that judgement: transformations on a known list change only appearance and conserve the amount, while adding or removing really changes it. What is not built is a genuine represented model of quantity that would make the verdict follow from understanding rather than from a lookup. Both gaps were written into the code's own header, into the ledger, and here. THIS IS THE METHOD WORKING. It would have been easy to write "Rung Three: done". The rule of this build is that a claim is sized to what was actually demonstrated, and a partial rung is recorded as partial - and the next part tells how those two halves were then actually built.

A NOTE ON THIS SLICE'S REVIEW, TOLD STRAIGHT. The independent refute-the-work reviewer was stopped mid-run by the owner, so the builder re-ran the review's planned reproductions itself - the byte-identical double telling (now pinned by a permanent test), the isolation of the category episode's store reset, the arithmetic behind the eight-tick landing, and a line-by-line comment walk of the new chapters. That is a weaker guarantee than an independent reviewer, and the ledger records it as exactly that.


PART EIGHTEEN - SLICE 24: THE BODY SEAM - EMBODIMENT OPENED AS GROUNDWORK, AND SAID SO


The last rung of the ladder is the embodied one, and the book is blunt about it: the loop must close "through a real machine". There is no robot in this repository, and slice 24 refuses to pretend otherwise. What it builds instead is the SEAM - the three connections a real robot would one day plug into - and a small, honestly-named stand-in machine to test the seam against today.

THE STAND-IN. A pack called simulated_body - the word simulated in its own name - carries a camera, a draining battery, and three actuators. It is a world, not a mind: every operation is a pure, deterministic step from one body state to the next.

THE THREE CONNECTIONS. A pack called body_interface builds exactly what the book names. The sensors become the senses: the camera's readings surface as the mind's percepts, unchanged. The body state becomes the homeostatic state: the battery level is handed to the drive system as-is, and an energy drive defends a full charge exactly the way the temperature drive defends thirty-seven degrees - so a low battery is FELT AS HUNGER, the book's own sentence, tested literally. And the released actions drive the actuators: when the mind's selector releases the recharge, the machine recharges.

THE LOOP, CLOSED IN SIMULATION. Show the camera an obstacle and run one pass: the mind senses it, proposes steering (a reflex), releases it, and the body moves - and the camera reads clear again, because acting changed what the machine sees. Drain the battery tick by tick and the mind recharges of its own accord, exactly when the hunger presses and never before, on a rhythm so deterministic the tests pin it as an exact trace.

WHAT THE REVIEW ADDED. The reviewer was interrupted again, but its two leads were both real: a missing comment line, fixed; and a sharp question - could a hand-built record be stanced as OBSERVED? It could. Now it cannot: the stance re-derives the record's fingerprint from its own contents and refuses a forgery by name, because observation is the strongest grade the mind grants itself and a pass that never ran must never earn it. The builder finished the remaining checks itself and the ledger records the whole disposition as the mixed review it was.

THE BOUNDARY, KEPT. The one-command story was not touched: it still says, in its honest-limits list, that embodiment is not demonstrated, and it still gives the book's reason - a real machine. The groundwork changed what the repository can do; it changed nothing about what the story claims. That line between groundwork and rung is the whole point of this slice.


PART NINETEEN - SLICE 25: THE STORY LEARNS THE BODY SEAM - TOLD, AND STILL NOT CLAIMED


Slice 24 built the seam and deliberately left the story silent about it, so the line between groundwork and rung stayed visible. Slice 25 crosses that gap the only honest way there is: it teaches the story to TELL the seam while refusing, in the chapter's own words, to claim the rung.

THE NEW CHAPTER. Between the finished Rung Four and the provenance layer, the one-command story now prints a chapter called THE BODY SEAM - RUNG FIVE'S GROUNDWORK, TOLD AND NOT CLAIMED. Its first sentence is the refusal: there is no real machine here, and this chapter does not pretend one. Then it tells, over live runs a reader can watch: the obstacle the camera sees surfacing as the mind's percept, unchanged; one closed pass of the loop - sense, propose, select, command, enact - releasing the steering reflex and moving the body, after which the camera reads clear, because acting changed what the machine sees; the hunger identity - a battery at half charge and a body half a degree from thirty-seven reading EQUAL through the very same error predicate, which is the book's low-battery-felt-as-hunger sentence made a number; the survival rhythm - the battery draining a quarter charge per tick, the mind holding still at a mild hunger and recharging OF ITS OWN ACCORD at the pressing one, again and again, with nothing outside the mind commanding the act; and the one deliberate tie, where a starving battery outranks a seen obstacle because the drives come before the reflexes.

GUARD-THEN-TELL, AS ALWAYS. Every sentence in the chapter sits beneath a strict live check in the chapter's own code. If any of the reused engines ever answers differently - a different release, a different rhythm, a different number - the story refuses to print rather than lie. And the chapter's record is owned the strong way: the loop's outcome is minted by running the pass right inside the record predicate, and the printed stance is granted only after the record proves, by re-deriving its own content address, that it was genuinely minted.

THE BOUNDARY, KEPT AGAIN. The story's honest-limits list still names embodiment, word for word, with the book's real-machine reason - and it now adds the boundary aloud: the seam chapter is GROUNDWORK, and groundwork is not the rung. The story does not promote it. Reaching, navigation, and true survival wait for a machine that is real.


PART TWENTY - SLICE 26: THE GATE THAT LEARNED TO SEE ITS OWN BLIND SPOT


konnectome protects itself with four standing gates - small programs that scan the whole repository before any change is merged and refuse the merge if a rule is broken. One of them enforces the naming rule: every predicate (a predicate is Prolog's word for a named piece of logic) must begin with its pack's full whole-word name.

Back at slice 22, a review discovered something embarrassing and recorded it honestly: the naming gate had a blind spot. It hunted for KNOWN abbreviations, so a helper predicate with an unfamiliar unprefixed name walked straight past it. Since then, the ledger has carried a standing instruction: until the gate learns to see this, every reviewer must hunt for such helpers by hand.

Slice 26 retires that instruction the only proper way - by teaching the gate. The gate now applies the rule exactly as the constitution states it: EVERY name defined in a pack's module must begin with the pack's own name (or the one sanctioned shared namespace). Not just the common ones, not just the known abbreviations - every one.

And the gate is proven able to fail before it is trusted to pass. A small FIXTURE - a deliberately broken example pack, clearly labelled, stored outside the real pack directory so it is never actually loaded - carries exactly the old blind-spot shape. A new self-test command runs the gate against that fixture and passes only if the gate catches the deliberate violation by name. If anyone ever weakens the gate, the self-test fails and says so: the hole is open again.


PART TWENTY-ONE - SLICE 27: THE DELAY LINES - A SIGNAL LEARNS TO TAKE ITS TIME


In a real brain, a signal does not jump instantly from one region to another. It travels along a nerve fibre, and the trip takes time - more time for a longer fibre, less for one wrapped in myelin (myelin is the fatty insulation that speeds a nerve signal along). The guiding book builds this into its map: every connection between two parts of the mind carries a DELAY, the number of ticks (a tick is one beat of the mind's clock) the signal spends in transit.

From the very beginning, konnectome's connection graph - the map of who talks to whom - wrote that delay down in every connection. But until now the number was only recorded, never obeyed: every signal crossed every connection in exactly one tick, and the ledger honestly listed multi-tick delays as pending. Slice 27 makes the number true.

THE DELAY LINE. The idea is a small queue, like a short length of pipe. When a signal is sent down a connection with a delay of, say, three ticks, it enters the back of that connection's pipe. Each tick it moves one step forward. On the third tick it comes out the front and is delivered - scaled by the connection's weight - and not one tick sooner. The book states the test plainly, and konnectome now runs it as a real test: a signal crossing a two-tick hop and then a three-tick hop arrives at exactly tick five, with the far end provably silent at ticks one through four.

NOTHING OLD BROKE. With every delay set to one tick, the new delayed machinery reproduces the old one-tick behaviour exactly - state for state, tick for tick - and a test pins that identity forever. And a value already in the pipe is safe: even if the sender changes its mind afterward, what was sent still arrives, because the pipe remembers what entered it, not what the sender feels now.

REFUSALS, SAID ALOUD. This slice's independent review hunted for silent failures - places where a mistake would vanish without a word - and found real ones: a construct of an unknown kind could quietly drop out of the state, and a connection from a name the state had never heard of could quietly destroy its pipe and everything in transit. All were fixed at the root and pinned with permanent tests: the graph now refuses such mistakes by name, throwing a clear error instead of absorbing the lie in silence. A delay of zero or less is refused too, because a signal never arrives before it is sent.

PART TWENTY-TWO - SLICE 28: THE ELIGIBILITY TRACE - A REWARD ONE TICK LATE STILL FINDS ITS CONNECTION


Back in slice 8, konnectome learned the brain's learning rule: a connection between two parts of the mind strengthens when its two ends are active TOGETHER and, at that same moment, dopamine (the brain's this-mattered chemical) says the moment counted. That rule has a hidden weakness: the reward almost never arrives at the same instant as the action. You press the lever, and the food comes a moment later. If learning demanded perfect simultaneity, the reward would arrive to find the coincidence already gone, and nothing would ever be learned.

The guiding book's answer, in its Chapter 10 and its Section A2.6, is the ELIGIBILITY TRACE. Slice 28 builds it.

THE TRACE. Each connection keeps a small fading number - a memory of its own recent coincidence. Every tick, two things happen to it: it fades (the old number is multiplied by a fading factor slightly less than one, such as nine tenths), and the tick's fresh coincidence (the product of the two ends' activities) is added on. So a connection whose ends just fired together holds a strong trace, and the trace melts away tick by tick if nothing follows.

THE BRIDGE. When the reward finally arrives, dopamine does not look for connections that are active NOW - it looks for connections whose TRACE is still warm, and strengthens each one in proportion to that trace. The book's own acceptance test is now a real, running test in konnectome: two ends fire together at tick one; at tick two everything is silent and the trace has faded to nine tenths; the reward arrives only then - and the weight still grows, by exactly the faded amount. The companion test shows the other half of the bargain: withhold the reward, and the trace just fades, nine tenths, then eighty-one hundredths, and the weight never moves. A trace is a memory, not a slow-release reward.

REFUSALS, SAID ALOUD. This slice's independent review hunted the silent-failure family again and found real ones: a connection whose kind was left as an unfilled blank could be silently treated as learnable; a thing that was not a connection at all could be silently skipped, or make the machinery quietly fail with no explanation. All were fixed at one root - a single shared checkpoint that inspects the whole map before either new step touches a number - and pinned with permanent tests. The learning rule now refuses, by name, a fading factor that would never fade, a malformed connection, an unknown or unfilled kind, a duplicated connection or trace, a trace whose connection has vanished, and a connection whose trace has vanished. A wrong answer said aloud beats a plausible one said silently.

PART TWENTY-THREE - SLICE 29: HOMEOSTATIC SCALING - THE BRAKE THAT KEEPS LEARNING FROM EXPLODING


Slices 8 and 28 gave konnectome a learning rule with a hidden danger. Strengthening is a loop that feeds itself: a strong connection makes its target more active, more activity makes more coincidence, more coincidence makes the connection stronger still. Left alone, that loop runs away - in a real brain the result is a seizure; in a model it is weights climbing forever.

The guiding book's answer, from its Chapter 10, is HOMEOSTATIC SCALING (homeostasis is the defending of a set-point, the way your body defends thirty-seven degrees). Each region of the mind watches its own average activity. Too loud, and ALL of its incoming connections are gently scaled down. Too quiet, and they are gently scaled up. Learning stays fast and selective; the brake is slow and even-handed; together they are stable.

Slice 29 builds it in three small pieces. First, a running average per region - a number that drifts toward whatever the region's activity has recently been (old average, mostly kept; new activity, a little mixed in). Second, a scaling step: every connection INTO a region is multiplied by one small shared factor - just above one if the region is too quiet, just below one if it is too loud, exactly one if it sits at its target. Third, a floor at zero: a connection can be silenced, but scaling can never flip it to negative.

THE PROOF IS A WORST CASE, RUN ON PURPOSE. For fifty ticks, a connection's two ends fire at maximum while the reward chemical is at maximum - the learning rule alone would drive the weight past five and keep climbing. With the brake applied every tick, the weight rises and then simply stops, at a fixed point just below two, and stays there. The explosion the book warns about is the thing the test proves cannot happen.

REFUSALS, SAID ALOUD. This slice's independent review found the season's subtlest silent failure: one malformed entry in the average store made one step quietly fail while ANOTHER step quietly accepted the very same store - one store, two verdicts, neither of them spoken. The fix went to the single root: one shared judgement of what a well-formed store is - right shape, no unfinished ends, no duplicate names - used by every step and every constructor alike, including the slice-28 trace store, so no two parts of the learning machinery can ever again disagree in silence. Seven permanent tests pin it.

PART TWENTY-FOUR - SLICE 30: LOCAL CHEMISTRY - ONE MOLECULE, MANY CONCENTRATIONS


konnectome has long had a neuromodulatory bus - the one deliberate global value in the whole architecture, modelling brain chemicals like dopamine that are broadcast widely rather than wired point to point. But the guiding book asks for more than one number per chemical: a real molecule diffuses from its small source nucleus and reaches DIFFERENT concentrations in different territories of the brain. The book's requirement says it plainly: a broadcast field with a level per target territory.

Slice 30 builds that, with one elegant simplification: every construct IS its own territory. Beside the global level, the bus can now hold local levels - dopamine at four tenths everywhere, but nine tenths in one particular region. A region with its own local level reads that; a region without one simply reads the global broadcast, the diffuse field; and if there is no chemistry at all, it reads silence. Nothing anywhere had to be rewired: a bus with no local levels behaves exactly as the old bus did, and a test pins that identity.

The same slice closes an honest debt from slice 27. When the delay lines were built, the step that honours multi-tick travel times refused, by name, the relay whose gain the bus sets - time and chemistry had never met. Now they compose: a new step carries the delay lines and the bus together, and the gain is read AT THE MOMENT OF DELIVERY, never at the moment of sending. A signal that spends three ticks in transit is amplified by the chemistry of the tick it arrives on - just as a real synapse responds to the chemicals bathing it now, not to the ones present when the signal left the other end.

REFUSALS, SAID ALOUD. The independent review found no real defect this time - the timing, the identity, and the compatibility all survived attack - but it found two small doors left ajar: a compound term shaped like a territory entry could pose as a chemical's name and silently overwrite a local level, and an unfilled name could be silently guessed instead of questioned. One guard closed both doors: every chemical and territory name must be a plain word, and anything else is refused by name at every entrance to the bus. Five permanent tests pin the guard.

PART TWENTY-FIVE - SLICE 31: THE GEOGRAPHY OF REWARD - EACH PLACE LEARNS BY ITS OWN CHEMISTRY


Slice 30 gave konnectome local chemistry: one molecule, different concentrations in different territories. But one construct still ignored it - the plasticity engine, the learning rule itself, still read dopamine as a single global number. A territory could have its relays amplified by its own local chemistry, yet its LEARNING was governed by the world average. Slice 30's own log recorded that honestly as the natural next step, and slice 31 takes it.

The change is one idea: when a connection learns, it now asks about the chemistry of the place the signal ARRIVES. The three-factor rule keeps its shape - the sending end's activity, times the receiving end's activity, times the dopamine level, times a small learning rate - but that dopamine level is now read in the receiving construct's own territory. A territory with its own local concentration learns by that; a territory without one learns by the global broadcast, exactly as before; and a territory whose local level is explicitly zero does not learn at all, however loud the global reward. The local level wins - it does not blend.

The same law governs the delayed kind of learning too. The eligibility trace of slice 28 - the fading memory that lets a reward one tick late still find the connection that earned it - is now spent at the receiving territory's concentration at the moment of spending. One global reward can therefore teach different places different amounts: full strength in one region, half in another, nothing in a silenced one. Reinforcement now has a geography.

Because slice 30 decided that every construct simply IS its own territory, nothing had to be rewired: every caller passes the same bus it always passed, and on a bus with no local levels every weight comes out exactly as the old global rule produced - every earlier test still passes untouched, which is the proof.

REFUSALS, SAID ALOUD. The independent review found three real defects, all with one root, each reproduced before it was fixed: the LIVE learning step - the one the cognitive cycle calls every tick - had never been given the loud refusal perimeter its three siblings (the trace step, the reward step, the scaling step) always had. A nonsense term made it silently fail; an unfilled kind was silently treated as a connection that does not learn; a duplicated connection silently learned the same lesson twice. One fix at the root gave the live step the same shared judgement the others use, and three permanent tests pin it. Ten tests grew from the slice in all: seven for the geography of reward, three for the perimeter.

PART TWENTY-SIX - SLICE 32: THE LIVING LOOP CARRIES ITS WHOLE LEARNING BODY


By slice 31, konnectome's learning had three organs beyond the basic weight change: the eligibility trace of slice 22's story (built in slice 28), a fading memory that lets a late reward still find the connection that earned it; the running average of slice 29, a slow per-region record of how active each part has recently been; and the homeostatic brake of the same slice, which uses that average to keep learning from exploding. But the cognitive cycle - the loop that actually runs the mind, tick after tick - only ever called the basic learning step. The trace and the average existed only where a test harness remembered to carry them by hand. The ledger recorded that honestly as the next smallest step, and slice 32 takes it.

The change is again one idea: the world the loop carries now includes the learning body's whole state. The world is a single bundle of named values (the tick count, the body, the drives, the chemical bus, the connections, and so on), and it grows six new entries: the trace store, the average store, and the four small numbers that pace them - how fast a trace fades, how fast an average moves, what activity level each region should defend, and how strongly the brake corrects toward it. Every tick now beats the whole body in order: the fast learning step first, then the traces fade and absorb the tick's fresh coincidences, then the averages take one smoothing step toward the tick's activity, and finally the brake nudges each region's incoming connection strengths toward its target.

One honest subtlety: in the worlds konnectome actually boots for its demonstrations, the brake's correction rate is set to zero - armed but at rest, exactly like the emergency-override drive that sits in the same worlds armed but calm. A test proves the rest is real: with the rate at zero, even an absurd activity target changes nothing, so every story the old worlds told is still told word for word. A twin test proves the brake is alive: turn the rate up, and a quiet region's incoming weight is pulled above what learning alone gave it. And the reward step - the spending of the traces - deliberately stays outside the loop, because deciding that a reward has arrived belongs to whoever runs the mind, not to every tick automatically.

REFUSALS, SAID ALOUD. A world missing any of the six new entries is refused by the missing entry's name, never half-run. And the independent review - the eighth straight completion of the method - found one real defect, reproduced before it was fixed: the loop's own front door claimed in its comment to refuse a negative tick count, but the code just silently failed instead, and a nonsense count died with a confusing arithmetic error. One fix at the root gave the tick count a proper loud check - time moves forward in whole ticks - and two permanent tests pin it. Seven tests grew from the slice in all: five for the living loop, two for the perimeter.


PART TWENTY-SEVEN - HOW TO RUN IT YOURSELF


konnectome uses a program called SWI-Prolog to run its code and tests. From the konnectome folder, these commands check the build. Each prints a clear pass-or-fail result.

• Run the whole mind and read its glass-box story (the capstone of Part Twelve; the optional number is how many ticks to live):
  bin/run_capstone_demonstration.sh
  bin/run_capstone_demonstration.sh 20

• Run one pack's tests (for example, the tick engine):
  bin/run_pack_tests.sh tick_engine

• Check that every name follows the whole-word rule:
  bin/check_pack_naming.sh

• Check that no lower layer depends on a higher one:
  bin/check_layers.sh

• Check the layer-to-stratum binding:
  bin/check_layer_binding.sh

• Check the reality discipline - that no pack but pretend_play so much as names the reused imagination machinery, by predicate, module qualifier, or import, so pretend only ever crosses toward reality through the guarded doorway (see Part Thirteen):
  bin/check_reality_discipline.sh

A layer is a numbered level of the code; the rule is simply that a lower level may not depend on a higher one, so the foundation never leans on the roof.


PART TWENTY-EIGHT - THE LEDGER AND THE CHANGE ORDERS


As konnectome is built, it sometimes meets a wall - something the language or the data structure cannot yet express. Every wall is written down first in the ledger, a file named konnectome_ledger_v1.txt that is the honest scoreboard of the whole build. From the ledger, a wall is routed to exactly one of three places:

• If the missing thing must be shared between programs as a signed, named record, it is a data-structure gap, and it goes to the Causalontology change order (a held file that gathers such requests).

• If it is a missing feature of the language, it goes to the PrologAI change order.

• If konnectome can solve it in its own code, it becomes a new pack or module.

A wall that turns out to be unnecessary is recorded just as honestly as one that needed building. As of slice 12, konnectome had hit three walls and CLOSED all three. Wall-1 (theory of mind) and Wall-2 (prediction) were routed to the Causalontology change order and closed when it shipped as Causalontology 4.0.0 (the belief and the prediction kinds); Wall-3 (the closure's own wall - the borrowed PrologAI library had not yet learned the new kinds) was routed to the PrologAI change order and closed the same day by an addition called causal_core 1.1.0. Slice 13 then added the provenance pack and hit no wall at all: it closed an honest loose end from slice 12 (that konnectome minted belief and prediction records but had not yet graded or retracted any of them) and recorded one new honest limit - that a real signature needs a private key, which may never live in the code. Slice 14 then added the capstone demonstration and likewise hit no wall: it only gathered what was already built into one runnable, watchable story. Slice 15 opened Rung Two (first words and pretend play) and hit no wall either - and it kept an explicit watch for the finding that would release the held imagination section of the change order, recording honestly that no such finding arose: the reused quarantine and the per-claim machinery sufficed, so the section stays held. Slice 16 deepened Rung Four (social pain and the empathy trial) and hit no wall as well: the existing drive machinery WAS the pain pathway, and the existing attitude vocabulary already carried the modelled distress. Slice 17 turned the pretend-into-observed refusal from a discipline into a checkable fourth gate, proven able to fail before it was trusted to pass, and hit no wall. Slice 18 grew the capstone story to tell Rung Two and the Rung Four deepening in its own telling, and hit no wall - it only gathered chapters the earlier slices had already built. Slice 19 finished Rung Four with the appraisal and regulation pilots and hit no wall either: appraisal is the drive system's own error read as good or bad, and regulation is the neuromodulatory bus's own arousal level brought home to baseline - both reuse, no new machinery. Slice 20 then paid the pilots' review debt: an independent review found three latent defects (a rounding-boundary misread in appraisal and two in regulation), and each was fixed at its root and pinned with a permanent test, so the feeling rung is not just finished but proven. konnectome has also recorded four small observations for the owner (the newest: that the shared attitude vocabulary carries fear but not satisfaction), and it has still changed neither cousin by reaching into it - every change went through a gated change order.
