LAYER 8, THE MICROCIRCUIT LAYER: MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS - THE OUTLINE

The per-type mode registers, extracted from LAYER_08_MODES_MANUSCRIPT (which holds the full entries: registers, per-mode transfer functions, transition tables, and fault regimes).

Each entry below: the type, its source key, and its named mode register.

Date of compilation: August 9, 2026.


ENTRY 1. CONVERGENCE (Fan-In Integration Motif) (The Evidence-Pooling Junction) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 1)

◦ Averaging Mode - The Smoother - pool many weak asynchronous inputs and pass their mean, suppressing noise.

◦ Integrating Mode - The Evidence Ramp - accumulate input over the membrane window, raising rate with the running total toward a decision.

◦ Coincidence Mode - The Simultaneity Gate - fire supralinearly only when a few strong inputs arrive within a couple of milliseconds.

ENTRY 2. DIVERGENCE (Fan-Out Broadcast Motif) (The One-to-Many Megaphone) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 2)

◦ Tonic Mode - The Background Hum - hold a steady ambient transmitter level that biases the gain of every target.

◦ Phasic Mode - The Global Alarm - deliver a transient synchronous pulse copied to all subscribers at once.

ENTRY 3. THE FEEDFORWARD CHAIN (Serial Relay Motif) (The Signal Bucket-Brigade) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 3)

◦ Relaying Mode - The Faithful Carry - reproduce the input at the far end after a fixed delay with minimal reshaping.

◦ Non-Relaying Mode - The Closed Gate - the thalamic variant switched into burst firing by hyperpolarisation, ceasing faithful transmission and signalling novelty instead.

ENTRY 4. THE RECURRENT LOOP (Reciprocal Pair) (The Two-Cell Memory Latch) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 4)

◦ Transient-Amplifier Mode - The Fading Echo - loop gain below one, so a nudge is boosted then decays.

◦ Integrator Mode - The Perfect Tally - loop gain at one, so input is summed without decay.

◦ Bistable Mode - The Held Bit - loop gain above one with saturation, holding a discrete on-state after the trigger ends.

◦ Ringing Mode - The Reverberator - high gain with fast synapses and delay, so the pair oscillates rather than latches.

ENTRY 5. THE FEEDFORWARD LOOP (Triad Motif) (The Two-Path Change Detector) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 5)

◦ Incoherent Mode - The Onset Flag - the indirect path opposes the direct one, producing a pulse at input onset and offset.

◦ Coherent Mode - The Patience Filter - the indirect path agrees, passing only inputs that outlast the indirect-path delay.

ENTRY 6. RECURRENT (MUTUAL) EXCITATION WITHIN A POPULATION (Self-Amplifying Pool) (The Bonfire Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 6)

◦ Stable-Amplifier Mode - The Damped Bonfire - subcritical gain, boosting input transiently then settling.

◦ Attractor Mode - The Held Pattern - balanced supercritical gain with inhibition, holding one of several self-sustaining patterns.

◦ Runaway Mode (admitted only as fault) - The Wildfire - excitation exceeding inhibition's control, hypersynchronous discharge.

ENTRY 7. FEEDFORWARD INHIBITION (Input-Driven Delayed-Brake Motif) (The Coincidence-Window Cutter) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 7)

◦ Idle Mode - The Waiting Brake - interneuron near threshold, little passed.

◦ Tight-Gate Mode - The Narrow Window - under strong synchronous drive, a sharp delayed veto that admits only near-coincident excitation.

◦ Widened-Gate Mode - The Loosened Window - after repetitive drive depresses excitation onto the parvalbumin cell, the aperture transiently opens.

ENTRY 8. FEEDBACK (RECURRENT) INHIBITION (Self-Braking Interneuron Loop) (The Activity Thermostat) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 8)

◦ Stabiliser Mode - The Gentle Governor - at low drive, holding activity near a set point.

◦ Oscillator Mode - The Sawtooth Pacer - at moderate drive with short delay, pacing a gamma rhythm (the pyramidal-interneuron gamma motif in embryo).

◦ Ceiling Mode - The Hard Cap - at high drive, saturating into a firm ceiling on rate.

ENTRY 9. LATERAL INHIBITION (Neighbour-Suppressing Interneuron Motif) (The Contrast-Carving Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 9)

◦ Contrast-Enhancement Mode - The Edge Sharpener - weak lateral inhibition sculpting a surround around each active unit.

◦ Selection Mode - The Neighbour Silencer - strong recurrent lateral inhibition tipping into winner-take-all suppression (the competitive motifs are lateral inhibition turned up).

ENTRY 10. RECURRENT INHIBITION OF THE RENSHAW TYPE (Motor-Collateral Feedback Interneuron) (The Final-Path Governor) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 10)

◦ High-Gain Mode - The Steady-Hold Brake - raised Renshaw excitability for fine steady holds, damping motoneuron rate.

◦ Low-Gain Mode - The Released Governor - reduced recurrent inhibition for strong synchronous efforts.

◦ Phase-Modulated Mode - The Gait Governor - gain scaled cycle by cycle during locomotion.

ENTRY 11. RECIPROCAL INHIBITION (Mutually-Silencing Antagonist Pair) (The Push-Pull Pair) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 11)

◦ Static-Relaxation Mode - The Antagonist Release - the resting regime, one side active relaxes its opponent.

◦ Oscillator Mode - The Take-Turns Engine - with fatigue added, a clean alternation (the half-centre oscillator).

◦ Flip-Flop Mode - The Locked Latch - with mutual gain raised, a bistable switch.

◦ Locked Mode - The Stiffened Joint - both sides driven together during co-contraction, the see-saw pinned.

ENTRY 12. PRESYNAPTIC INHIBITION (Axo-Axonic Terminal Gating Motif) (The Input Tap) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 12)

◦ Tonic-Set Mode - The Standing Tap - a background level of presynaptic inhibition setting resting afferent gain.

◦ Movement-Gated Mode - The Closed Tap - sharply increased gating of chosen afferents during voluntary movement.

ENTRY 13. THE PERISOMATIC-DENDRITIC DIVISION OF LABOUR (Two-Site Interneuron Targeting Motif) (The Two-Handed Governor) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 13)

◦ Perisomatic-Dominant Mode - The Output Timer - parvalbumin (abbreviated PV) inhibition at the soma enforces precise, sparse output timing.

◦ Dendritic-Clamp Mode - The Input Guard - somatostatin (abbreviated SST) inhibition builds on the dendrites to clamp incoming excitation.

◦ Dendrite-Open Mode - The Learning Window - the dendritic brake lifted (by vasoactive-intestinal-peptide cells), opening dendrites for plasticity.

ENTRY 14. DISINHIBITION (The Inhibition-of-Inhibition Motif) (The Permission Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 14)

◦ Guarded Mode - The Closed Permit - the somatostatin guard on, pyramidal dendrites clamped.

◦ Released Mode - The Open Permit - vasoactive-intestinal-peptide firing lifts the guard, dendrites open during arousal and attention.

◦ Behaviour-Locked Mode - The Task Permit - a transient flip during a specific bout such as running or whisking.

ENTRY 15. VOLUME (NEUROGLIAFORM) INHIBITION (The Unaddressed GABA-Cloud Motif) (The Fog Brake) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 15)

◦ Tonic-Damp Mode - The Thin Haze - weak background dampening at low activity, setting regional excitability.

◦ Blanket Mode - The Deep Fog - a burst lays a long-lasting inhibitory blanket that can silence a patch and reset local oscillatory phase.

ENTRY 16. WINNER-TAKE-ALL (Mutual-Inhibition Competitive Selection Motif) (The One-Survivor Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 16)

◦ Deliberation Mode - The Open Race - subcritical gain, graded and reversible competition among candidates.

◦ Commitment Mode - The One Survivor - attractor regime, a categorical choice with hysteresis, winner sustained and losers suppressed.

◦ Rivalry Mode - The Flip-Flopper - near-tie oscillation in which candidates alternate.

ENTRY 17. SOFT WINNER-TAKE-ALL AND SPARSE CODING (k-Winners Competitive Thinning Motif) (The Few-Survivors Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 17)

◦ Encoding Mode - The Loosened Committee - novelty and acetylcholine loosen inhibition, admitting more winners (larger k).

◦ Familiar Mode - The Tight Committee - familiarity tightens inhibition, fewer winners.

◦ Neurogenesis Mode - The Rookie Round - young, hyperexcitable granule cells compete on easier terms for a few weeks, refreshing the code.

ENTRY 18. DIVISIVE NORMALIZATION (Pooled-Activity Divisive Scaling Motif) (The Fair-Share Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 18)

◦ Fast-Shunt Mode - The Instant Divider - shunting inhibition dividing each response by pooled activity within 5 to 20 milliseconds.

◦ Depression Mode - The Sluggish Divider - short-term depression extending the division over 50 to 300 milliseconds.

◦ Adaptation Mode - The Slow Rescaler - slow adaptation carrying the same arithmetic over seconds, matching the pool to recent statistics.

ENTRY 19. GAIN MODULATION (Multiplicative Slope-Control Motif) (The Multiplier) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 19)

◦ Low-Gain Mode - The Drowsy Knob - too little gain, signals sinking into noise.

◦ Optimal-Gain Mode - The Sweet Spot - an intermediate optimum sharpening signal-to-noise.

◦ High-Gain Mode - The Over-Driven Knob - too much gain, amplifying noise and locking circuits into over-responsive states.

ENTRY 20. THE PUSH-PULL (OPPONENT) MOTIF (Sign-Splitting Dual-Channel Motif) (The Two-Handed Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 20)

◦ Opponent-Difference Mode - The Two-Handed Subtractor - one channel pushes while its opposite pulls, encoding the signed difference about a pivot.

◦ Pivot-Reset Mode - The Recentred Balance - the crossing point re-regulated to the current mean (adaptation level, joint equilibrium, glycaemic set point).

ENTRY 21. COINCIDENCE DETECTION WITH DELAY LINES (The Jeffress Interaural-Time Comparator) (The Microsecond Stopwatch) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 21)

◦ Coincidence-Gate Mode - The Microsecond Stopwatch - a fast leaky coincidence gate with a fixed narrow window, firing on sub-millisecond arrival differences.

◦ Retuned-Window Mode - The Adjusted Gate - the same gate with a slower gain and best-delay adjustment set by glycinergic inhibition.

ENTRY 22. THE GAMMA OSCILLATOR, PYRAMIDAL-INTERNEURON TYPE (Pyramidal-Interneuron Network Gamma, PING) (The Cortical Metronome) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 22)

◦ Quiet Mode - The Idle Clock - below a drive threshold, quiet or irregular firing.

◦ PING Mode - The Cortical Metronome - above threshold, locked rhythmic gamma paced by excitation.

◦ interneuron-only gamma (ING)-Slip Mode - The Faster Ring - with weak excitation and strong mutual inhibition, a faster interneuron-paced beat (toward Chapter 23).

ENTRY 23. THE GAMMA OSCILLATOR, INTERNEURON-ONLY TYPE (Interneuron Network Gamma, ING) (The Self-Ringing Brake) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 23)

◦ Coherent-interneuron-only gamma (ING) Mode - The Self-Ringing Brake - with adequate tonic drive and strong gap-junction coupling, a coherent fast beat at the high end of or above the gamma band.

◦ PING-Transition Mode - The Handover - as excitatory drive strengthens, a smooth handover to pyramidal-paced gamma at somewhat lower frequency.

◦ Asynchronous Mode - The Broken Beat - with too little drive or weak coupling, asynchronous or clustered firing rather than a clean beat.

ENTRY 24. THETA-GAMMA NESTING (Cross-Frequency Phase-Amplitude Coupling) (The Phase Coder) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 24)

◦ Theta-Nested Mode - The Phase Coder - during active exploration, rapid-eye-movement sleep, and attention, each theta cycle carves into gamma slots carrying an ordered list.

◦ Non-Theta Replay Mode - The Ripple Player - in the non-theta state the coupling dissolves and the hippocampus produces sharp-wave ripples for memory replay.

◦ CA3-Listening versus Entorhinal-Listening Mode - The Channel Selector - slow gamma nested when CA1 listens to CA3 (memory), fast gamma when listening to entorhinal cortex (the present).

ENTRY 25. THE HALF-CENTRE OSCILLATOR (Reciprocal-Inhibition Alternator with Fatigue) (The Take-Turns Engine) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 25)

◦ Quiescent Mode - The Stalled Engine - below drive threshold the pair sits silent or in tonic co-activation, producing no rhythm.

◦ Release (Adaptation) Mode - The Fatigue Handover - the active pool tires until it can no longer hold down its partner, which then surges up.

◦ Escape (Rebound) Mode - The Spring-Loaded Handover - the suppressed pool escapes inhibition through its own post-inhibitory rebound and seizes the floor before its partner has fully tired.

ENTRY 26. THE CENTRAL PATTERN GENERATOR (Coupled Rhythm-and-Pattern Network) (The Autopilot for Muscles) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 26)

◦ Off Mode - The Idle Autopilot - below drive threshold the network is silent and only isolated reflexes remain.

◦ Locomotor Gait Modes - Walk, Trot, Gallop and Hop - distinct interlimb coordination patterns selected by drive level and by reconfiguration of the commissural coupling.

◦ Eupnoea Mode - The Quiet Breath - the normal respiratory rhythm generated by the pre-Botzinger complex.

◦ Sigh Mode - The Big Breath - a slow, large-amplitude biphasic augmented breath interleaved with eupnoea from a partly distinct subpopulation.

◦ Gasp Mode - The Last-Resort Breath - a fast, high-amplitude rhythm falling back onto the most intrinsic pacemaker mechanism under low oxygen.

ENTRY 27. THE PACEMAKER NETWORK (Coupled Cellular Oscillators Pulled to Synchrony) (The Chorus of Clocks) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 27)

◦ Synchronised Mode - The Chorus - coupling above the critical strength pulls every cellular clock into one coherent phase and a sharpened shared period.

◦ Desynchronised Mode - The Cacophony - coupling too weak, so cells free-run at their own natural frequencies with no tissue rhythm.

◦ Re-Entraining Mode - The Resetting Chorus - the locked population slides its phase to track a shifted external cue (the slow recovery from jet lag).

ENTRY 28. THE SYNFIRE CHAIN (Feedforward Wave Sequence Generator) (The Bucket-Brigade of Spikes) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 28)

◦ Propagation Mode - The Marching Volley - a stable synchronous packet travels pool to pool at fixed inter-pool delays, the healthy operating mode.

◦ Idle Mode - The Waiting Brigade - no trigger has arrived, so the chain is silent.

◦ Learning Mode - The Self-Carving Chain - spike-timing-dependent plasticity strengthens forward links so the chain organises out of unstructured recurrence.

ENTRY 29. THE AUTOASSOCIATIVE ATTRACTOR NETWORK (Recurrent Pattern-Completing Excitatory Pool) (The Cue-To-Memory Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 29)

◦ Storage Mode - The Write Head - dentate detonator input dominates and plasticity is enabled to lay down a new pattern.

◦ Recall Mode - The Pattern-Completer - perforant-path cues dominate and the recurrent web settles onto the nearest stored pattern.

◦ Replay Mode - The Offline Rehearsal - the network free-runs stored sequences during sharp-wave ripples in rest and sleep.

ENTRY 30. THE POINT ATTRACTOR OF PERSISTENT ACTIVITY (Recurrently Sustained Delay-Period Ensemble) (The Working Memory Cell Assembly) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 30)

◦ Baseline Mode - The Empty Slot - the pool rests near its low fixed point holding nothing.

◦ Memory Mode - The Loaded Slot - a cue has flipped the assembly to its elevated stable state, where it holds after the input ends.

◦ Reset Mode - The Clear Signal - the transient clearing regime in which a global inhibitory volley or reward-signalled response command flips the assembly back to baseline.

ENTRY 31. THE LINE ATTRACTOR (Velocity-To-Position Recurrent Integrator) (The Neural Integrator) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 31)

◦ Tuned Mode - The Perfect Ledger - feedback tuned just below unity so every level of activity is near-stable and the running sum is held for tens of seconds.

◦ Leaky Mode - The Draining Ledger - feedback too weak, so the stored value decays back toward centre.

◦ Unstable Mode - The Runaway Ledger - gain above unity, so activity grows and the value ratchets outward.

ENTRY 32. THE RING ATTRACTOR (Circular Continuous Attractor With Local Excitation And Long-Range Inhibition) (The Inner Compass Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 32)

◦ Cue-Anchored Mode - The Landmark Compass - the bump is pinned to visual landmarks and reports true heading.

◦ Path-Integration Mode - The Dead-Reckoning Compass - in darkness the bump is driven by angular velocity alone and slowly drifts.

◦ Teleport Mode - The Jumping Compass - conflicting cues make the bump jump discretely to a new bearing rather than fade between.

◦ Sleep Mode - The Wandering Compass - the bump roams the ring under internal dynamics, decoupled from the world.

ENTRY 33. THE EXPANSION RECODER (Sparse High-Dimensional Recoding Motif) (The Overlap-Breaker) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 33)

◦ Sparse Separation Mode - The Overlap-Breaker - the fierce inhibitory brake keeps only a few percent of cells active, decorrelating overlapping inputs.

◦ Adjustable-Sparseness Mode - The Tunable Sieve - neuromodulators transiently sharpen or relax the threshold to tune separation to demand.

ENTRY 34. THE COMPARATOR (Predicted-Versus-Actual Difference Detector) (The Surprise Meter) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 34)

◦ Match Mode - The Quiet Bell - prediction cancels reality and the cell reports familiarity.

◦ Mismatch Mode - The Ringing Bell - reality escapes cancellation and the cell reports novelty, often with dendritic plateaus and bursts.

ENTRY 35. THE SUPERVISED ERROR-CORRECTION MODULE (Teacher-Instructed Weight-Tuning Motif) (The Perceptron Cell) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 35)

◦ Learning Mode - The Corrected Pupil - paired parallel-fibre and climbing-fibre activity depresses the co-active weights, driving output toward lower error.

◦ Rebalancing Mode - The Forgetting Pupil - unpaired parallel-fibre activity potentiates weights, keeping them from saturating.

◦ Forward Mode - The Calibrated Filter - between teaching events the cell simply reports its weighted sum as a modulation of its high tonic simple-spike rate.

ENTRY 36. THE MUTUAL-INHIBITION FLIP-FLOP (Reciprocally Inhibiting Bistable Switch Motif) (The Either-Or Latch) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 36)

◦ Mode A - The First Latch - one pool dominant and self-holding (for example awake).

◦ Mode B - The Second Latch - the other pool dominant and self-holding (for example asleep).

ENTRY 37. THE CANONICAL CORTICAL COLUMN (Six-Layered Thalamocortical Master Template) (The Universal Cortical Tile) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 37)

◦ Balanced Waking Mode - The Idling Tile - large excitatory and inhibitory currents cancel to within a few percent, leaving irregular sparse firing at 0.1 to a few hertz.

◦ Desynchronised Processing Mode - The Attending Tile - during active sensory processing the column runs in a low-amplitude high-frequency regime, gamma-locked when vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) disinhibition opens it.

◦ Up-Down Slow-Oscillation Mode - The Sleeping Tile - in deep sleep the whole column breathes below 1 hertz, alternating silent down states with active up states.

ENTRY 38. THE PREDICTIVE CODING MOTIF (Hierarchical Error-Minimising Column Read) (The Expectation-Correcting Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 38)

◦ Balanced Inference Mode - The Fair Auditor - priors and evidence weighted by their true reliabilities, the healthy default.

◦ Prior-Dominated Mode - The Believing Auditor - precision turned up on priors, so the system perceives what it expects.

◦ Evidence-Dominated Mode - The Sceptical Auditor - precision turned up on evidence, so nothing ever becomes familiar.

◦ Evidence-Gated Mode - The Dreaming Auditor - prediction running with the sensory stream gated off, as in rapid-eye-movement dreaming.

ENTRY 39. THE THALAMIC RELAY TRIAD WITH RETICULAR GATE (Relay-Interneuron-Reticular Composite) (The Searchlight Gate) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 39)

◦ Tonic Relay Mode - The Open Gate - relays depolarised, transmitting driver input faithfully with attention-steered gain (waking).

◦ Burst Mode - The Alert Flare - a hyperpolarised relay answers a delayed input with a high-frequency burst, low fidelity but high detectability, flagging novelty.

◦ Spindle-Gated Mode - The Waxing Lock - reticular-relay volleys at 11 to 16 hertz progressively close the gate at sleep onset.

◦ Delta-Locked Mode - The Deep Lockdown - relays hyperpolarise further and 1-to-4-hertz delta plus the slow oscillation drop transfer toward zero.

ENTRY 40. THE BASAL GANGLIA SELECTION MOTIF (Disinhibitory Channel-Release Composite) (The Permission Machine) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 40)

◦ Deliberative Mode - The Careful Chooser - slow, dopamine-sensitive, goal-directed selection through the associative loop.

◦ Habitual Mode - The Autopilot Chooser - overtrained corticostriatal weights in the sensorimotor loop select without deliberation.

◦ Hypokinetic Mode - The Reluctant Chooser - low dopamine raises selection thresholds so nothing is released easily.

◦ Hyperkinetic Mode - The Trigger-Happy Chooser - excess dopamine lets weak candidates win.

◦ Replay Mode - The Sleeping Rehearser - during sleep the loops disengage from behaviour but replay to consolidate the day's selections into skill.

ENTRY 41. THE HIPPOCAMPAL TRISYNAPTIC TEMPLATE (Series-Composed Memory Loop) (The Memory Assembly Line) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 41)

◦ Online Theta Mode - The Running Line - encoding and retrieval interleaved on the theta rhythm during active behaviour.

◦ Offline Ripple Mode - The Night Shift - compressed replay of stored sequences during sharp-wave ripples in rest and slow-wave sleep.

◦ Within-Theta Sub-Modes - Read Phase and Write Phase - the oriens-lacunosum-moleculare gate tilts CA1 between reality-dominated and memory-dominated on alternate theta phases.

ENTRY 42. THE CEREBELLAR CORTICAL TEMPLATE (Uniform Adaptive-Filter Module) (The Calibration Crystal) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 42)

◦ Calibrated Mode - The Silent-Teacher Crystal - climbing fibres near silent and Purkinje output faithfully cancels predicted consequences.

◦ Adapting Mode - The Re-Tuning Crystal - the world has changed (new spectacles, a growing limb, a damaged muscle), olivary firing climbs and the weights walk.

ENTRY 43. THE MONOSYNAPTIC STRETCH REFLEX ARC (Ia-to-Motoneuron Direct Reflex) (The One-Synapse Snap) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 43)

◦ Length-Servo Mode - The One-Synapse Snap - the arc's single mode, resisting muscle stretch with a proportional, velocity-sensitive contraction.

ENTRY 44. THE POLYSYNAPTIC WITHDRAWAL ARC WITH CROSSED EXTENSION (Nociceptive Flexion-Extension Reflex) (The Pull-Back-and-Brace Program) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 44)

◦ Withdraw-and-Brace Mode - The Pull-Back Program - the intended mode, flexing the hurt limb away and extending the other to take the falling weight.

◦ Recruitment Sub-Mode - The Spreading Response - as stimulus strength rises the same program recruits more muscles, more segments and finally the crossed limb.

ENTRY 45. THE GOLGI TENDON (FORCE-LIMITING) ARC (Ib Autogenic Inhibition Reflex) (The Tension Governor) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 45)

◦ Autogenic-Inhibition Mode - The Tension Governor - during quiet standing and static holds the arc inhibits its own muscle in proportion to force.

◦ Reversed-Excitation Mode - The Load-Bearer - during the stance phase of locomotion the same Ib input reverses to reinforce extension and hold the leg stiff against the ground.

ENTRY 46. THE VESTIBULO-OCULAR THREE-NEURON ARC (Semicircular-Canal-to-Eye-Muscle Reflex) (The World-Steadying Loop) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 46)

◦ Stabilise Mode - The World-Steadying Loop - the default mode, moving the eyes exactly opposite the head to hold the image still.

◦ Cancellation Mode - The Following Eye - when eyes and head move together to track a target, the reflex is deliberately suppressed.

ENTRY 47. THE BAROREFLEX ARC (Arterial-Pressure Negative-Feedback Reflex) (The Blood-Pressure Thermostat) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 47)

◦ Pressure-Regulating Mode - The Blood-Pressure Thermostat - the single continuous operating mode, holding arterial pressure to a defended set point.

◦ Fast (Vagal) Sub-Arm - The Quick Parry - the parasympathetic heart-rate response.

◦ Slow (Sympathetic) Sub-Arm - The Steady Tone - the vascular and slower cardiac response.

ENTRY 48. THE VAGO-VAGAL LOOP (Gut-to-Brainstem-to-Gut Vagal Reflex) (The Digestion Autopilot) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 48)

◦ Accommodative Relaxation Mode - the Receive Mode - senses arrival or anticipation of food and actively relaxes the stomach so volume can rise without pressure rising.

◦ Propulsive-Secretory Mode - the Process Mode - selects the excitatory (cholinergic, using acetylcholine) enteric limb to contract and to drive acid and enzyme secretion.

◦ Restraint Mode - the Hold-Back Mode - the enterogastric setting that slows gastric emptying when the small intestine is already loaded.

ENTRY 49. THE SYMPATHETIC GANGLION DIVERGENCE MOTIF (Preganglionic-to-Postganglionic Fan-Out) (The Alarm Distribution Board) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 49)

◦ Faithful-Relay Mode - the Copy Mode - at low input, each secure nicotinic (fast acetylcholine) synapse copies spikes one-for-one with minimal build-up.

◦ Sustained-Amplifier Mode - the Surge Mode - at high input, slow muscarinic and peptidergic potentials accumulate, raising postganglionic excitability and stretching output in time.

ENTRY 50. THE ENTERIC PERISTALTIC CIRCUIT (THE LAW OF THE INTESTINE) (Polarised Ascending-Excitatory, Descending-Inhibitory Reflex) (The Squeeze-Behind, Relax-Ahead Wave) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 50)

◦ Propulsive Peristalsis Mode - the Push-Through Wave - the classic polarised reflex, contract-behind and relax-ahead, propelling contents aborally.

◦ Segmentation Mode - the Mixing Churn - non-propagating contractions that mix rather than move, dominant during active digestion.

◦ Migrating Motor Complex Mode - the Housekeeper Sweep - the fasting wave that periodically sweeps residue along the empty gut.

ENTRY 51. CENTRE-SURROUND ANTAGONISM (Concentric Opponent Receptive Field Motif) (The Edge-Carving Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 51)

◦ Photopic Acuity Mode - the Daylight Sharpener - dopamine uncouples horizontal cells, narrowing the surround and favouring fine spatial detail.

◦ Scotopic Sensitivity Mode - the Twilight Pooler - low dopamine lets the horizontal-cell network couple widely, broadening the surround and pooling light for detection.

ENTRY 52. THE ON-OFF PUSH-PULL SPLITTER (Sign-Inverting and Sign-Conserving Bipolar Divergence) (The Bright-and-Dark Fork) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 52)

◦ Rectified-Split Mode - the Bright-and-Dark Fork - the single standing mode, in which one cone signal is read by an ON (sign-inverting, metabotropic) channel and an OFF (sign-conserving, ionotropic) channel that carry increments and decrements on separate lines.

ENTRY 53. THE ROD CONVERGENCE (SCOTOPIC POOLING) PATHWAY (Piggyback Rod Signal Injection Motif) (The Photon-Pooling Night Line) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 53)

◦ Scotopic Pooling Mode - the Photon Counter - deep dark, maximal spatial and temporal summation, connexin-36 coupling set for sensitivity.

◦ Mesopic Blend Mode - the Twilight Handover - rod and cone signals mix and dopamine begins to uncouple the network.

◦ Photopic Handoff Mode - the Daylight Standby - the pathway saturates and vision passes entirely to the cone system.

ENTRY 54. THE DIRECTION-SELECTIVITY CIRCUIT (Asymmetric Starburst Inhibition Motif) (The Motion-Reading Wiring) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 54)

◦ Directional-Discrimination Mode - the Motion Reader - the working regime, in which preferred-direction motion escapes inhibition and null-direction motion is vetoed by well-timed starburst inhibition.

◦ Untuned-Fallback Mode - the Timing-Lost Regime - at very low or very high image speeds the timing relationship breaks down and directional selectivity weakens toward a non-directional response.

ENTRY 55. THE OLFACTORY GLOMERULAR MODULE (Convergent Receptor-Identity Sorting Motif) (The One-Smell Pixel) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 55)

◦ High-Sensitivity Pooling Mode - the Faint-Whiff Gatherer - at low odorant concentration, sensitivity and convergent pooling dominate to detect weak signals.

◦ Gain-Controlled Normalisation Mode - the Anti-Saturation Clamp - at high concentration, dopaminergic and GABAergic inhibition suppress input to prevent saturation and hold identity constant.

ENTRY 56. THE DENDRODENDRITIC RECIPROCAL INHIBITION MOTIF (Two-Way Dendritic Synapse Sharpening Motif) (The Axonless Handshake) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 56)

◦ Fast Gamma Mode - the Rhythm Clock - spike-driven, prompt reciprocal inhibition that organises principal cells into gamma-band time slots.

◦ Slow Graded Mode - the Lingering Damper - subthreshold, N-methyl-D-aspartate-and-calcium-driven inhibition that produces lasting, graded suppression.

ENTRY 57. THE INTERAURAL LEVEL SUBTRACTION MOTIF (Excitation-Minus-Inhibition Loudness-Comparison Motif) (The Louder-Ear Subtractor) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 57)

◦ Graded-Subtraction Mode - the Balance Point - the informative regime around the midline, where small changes in interaural level produce large changes in firing.

◦ Saturated Mode - the Rail - when sound is far to one side, the cell sits at maximum or minimum firing and no longer discriminates.

ENTRY 58. THE EFFERENT GAIN-CONTROL LOOP OF THE COCHLEA (Olivocochlear Amplifier-Damping Motif) (The Turn-Down-the-Ear Loop) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 58)

◦ High-Gain Quiet Mode - the Wide-Open Ear - in quiet, the olivocochlear efferents rest and the cochlear amplifier runs at maximum sensitivity.

◦ Damped Unmasking Mode - the Turn-Down Setting - in loud or noisy conditions, medial olivocochlear drive onto outer hair cells lowers amplifier gain, protecting the ear and unmasking transients in continuous noise.

ENTRY 59. THE HYPOPHYSIOTROPIC CASCADE AMPLIFIER (Portal-Coupled Three-Stage Endocrine Fan-Out) (The Chemical Amplifier) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 59)

◦ Basal Mode - the Idle Amplifier - low-frequency releasing-hormone pulses and resting gain.

◦ Stimulated Mode - the High-Gain Surge - stress, cold, or reproductive signals raise pulse frequency and stage gains for a large output.

◦ Suppressed Mode - the Braked Amplifier - the feedback loop clamps output below basal.

ENTRY 60. THE CLOSED-LOOP NEGATIVE FEEDBACK MOTIF (Hormone-Axis Feedback Thermostat) (The Set-Point Keeper) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 60)

◦ Homeostatic Mode - the Set-Point Keeper - holds the controlled hormone at a fixed target against disturbances.

◦ Allostatic Mode - the Moved Target - the set point is deliberately shifted (for example the raised cortisol set point of chronic stress).

◦ Broken-Loop Mode - the Runaway - feedback is lost and the controlled hormone runs to extremes (a fault regime, admitted only because it names the failure).

ENTRY 61. THE KNDY PULSE GENERATOR (Arcuate Kisspeptin-Neurokinin-Dynorphin Relaxation Oscillator) (The Fertility Clock) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 61)

◦ Pulsatile Mode - the Fertility Tick - neurokinin B recruits and synchronises the arcuate population into volleys and dynorphin shuts each volley off, setting the gonadotropin-releasing-hormone pulse.

◦ Suppressed Mode - the Silenced Clock - prepubertal, lactational, or energy-deficit states slow or stop the tick.

◦ Surge Mode - the Ovulatory Burst - a high-output, positive-feedback burst driven with the partner anteroventral-periventricular kisspeptin population.

ENTRY 62. THE MAGNOCELLULAR BURST SYNCHRONIZER (Oxytocin Milk-Ejection Burst Network) (The Milk-Ejection Circuit) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 62)

◦ Asynchronous Background Mode - the Idle Chorus - oxytocin neurons fire independently at one to three spikes per second with no coordinated output.

◦ Synchronised Bursting Mode - the Milk-Ejection Pulse - the population locks into a shared explosive burst every few minutes, each burst ejecting a pulse of oxytocin.

◦ Facilitated Mode - the Primed Chorus - a sub-state in which small amounts of oxytocin in the hypothalamus promote bursting.

ENTRY 63. THE COUPLED CIRCADIAN OSCILLATOR NETWORK (Suprachiasmatic Peptide-Coupled Cellular-Clock Ensemble) (The Master Clock) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 63)

◦ Entrained Mode - the Locked Clock - phase-locked to the external light-dark cycle at exactly 24 hours.

◦ Free-Running Mode - the Drifting Clock - in constant conditions, oscillating at its intrinsic period (a little over 24 hours in humans).

◦ Desynchronised Mode - the Split Clock - core and shell, or clock and periphery, transiently out of phase, as in jet lag or shift work.

ENTRY 64. THE ISLET SYNCYTIUM WITH HUB CELLS (Gap-Junction-Coupled Beta-Cell Sheet) (The Insulin Wave Chorus) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 64)

◦ Quiescent Uncoupled Mode - the Silent Sheet - below the glucose threshold, cells are silent and effectively uncoupled in output.

◦ Synchronised Wave Mode - the Insulin Chorus - above threshold, connexin-36 coupling forces coherent bursting and islet-wide calcium waves.

◦ Continuous-Spiking Mode - the Flat-Out Setting - at very high glucose, sustained firing.

ENTRY 65. THE ISLET PARACRINE TRIAD (Mutual Local-Secretion Regulation of Alpha, Beta and Delta Cells) (The Three-Cell Sugar Cabinet) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 65)

◦ Insulin-Dominant Mode - the Store Setting - at high glucose the beta node dominates and restrains the alpha node.

◦ Glucagon-Dominant Mode - the Mobilise Setting - at low glucose the alpha node dominates and drives counter-regulation.

◦ Somatostatin-Braked Mode - the Shared Damper - the delta node scales its braking to whichever opponent is loud, holding the ratio within a safe band.

ENTRY 66. THE FOLLICULOSTELLATE RELAY NETWORK (Gap-Junction-Coupled Non-Endocrine Web of the Anterior Pituitary) (The Gland's Star Wiring) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 66)

◦ Coupled Conductor Mode - the Star Grid Live - well-coupled, the web carries a stimulus at one point as a travelling calcium wave to distant endocrine clusters, synchronising secretion.

◦ Uncoupled Fragmented Mode - the Broken Grid - inflammatory signals close the connexin channels and the web fragments into isolated domains.

ENTRY 67. THE ULTRADIAN FEEDBACK OSCILLATOR OF THE STRESS AXIS (Delay-Coupled Pituitary-Adrenal Relaxation Oscillator) (The Hourly Cortisol Clock) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 67)

◦ Sub-Threshold Steady Mode - the Flat Line - below a threshold central drive the loop settles to a constant glucocorticoid level with no oscillation.

◦ Self-Pulsing Ultradian Mode - the Hourly Clock - above threshold, delayed negative feedback between pituitary and adrenal produces roughly hourly pulses with no upstream pacemaker.

◦ Stress-Perturbed Mode - the Override Ripple - acute stress shifts pulse phase and amplitude, with the response depending on the phase at which the stressor arrives.

ENTRY 68. THE ASTROCYTE SYNCYTIUM (Gap-Junction-Coupled Glial Network) (The Glial Power-and-Drainage Grid) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 68)

◦ Resting Idle Mode - the Baseline Grid - holding ionic and metabolic set points at rest.

◦ Buffering Mode - the Active Drain - activity-driven, absorbing and redistributing potassium and clearing glutamate with gap junctions fully engaged.

◦ Propagating-Wave Mode - the Signalling Ripple - strong stimulation triggers regenerative intercellular calcium waves across the network.

◦ Uncoupled Mode - the Collapsed Grid - inflammation, acidosis, and injury close connexin channels, coupling collapses, buffering fails, and tissue becomes hyperexcitable (a fault regime, admitted because it names the failure).

ENTRY 69. THE TRIPARTITE SYNAPTIC ISLAND (Astrocyte-Enwrapped Synaptic Territory) (The Slow Listener) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 69)

◦ Silent Mode - the Quiet Listener - sub-threshold synaptic activity produces only scattered local calcium blips with no territorial output.

◦ Integrating Mode - the Summing Listener - sustained activity summates calcium toward threshold.

◦ Surge Mode - the Flood Release - threshold crossed, a global calcium event sweeps the island and gliotransmitter (glutamate, D-serine, adenosine triphosphate) floods the territory, shifting plasticity thresholds en masse.

◦ Coupled Mode - the Spreading Listener - calcium waves pass through gap junctions to neighbouring islands, widening the affected area.

ENTRY 70. THE MICROGLIAL PRUNING LOOP (Complement-Tagged Synapse Elimination Circuit) (The Weakest-Link Eater) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 70)

◦ Surveillant Mode - the Patrolling Watch - resting microglia sample continuously without eating.

◦ Pruning Mode - the Weakest-Link Eater - during developmental windows, microglia engulf complement-tagged weak synapses.

◦ Reactive Mode - the Inflammatory Phagocyte - injury or disease shifts microglia to inflammatory phagocytosis that can prune pathologically (a fault regime).

◦ Aberrant Mode - the Mis-Set Pruner - over- or under-active complement tagging removes too many or too few synapses (a fault regime).

ENTRY 71. THE NEUROVASCULAR COUPLING UNIT (Activity-to-Blood-Flow Signalling Ensemble) (The Demand-Driven Fuel Valve) (keyed to microcircuits catalogue Chapter 71)

◦ Resting Tone Mode - the Idle Valve - tonic pericyte and smooth-muscle tone set a baseline flow.

◦ Functional-Hyperaemia Mode - the Demand Open - local activity dilates vessels and raises flow to feed active neurons.

◦ Autoregulatory Mode - the Pressure Buffer - the same actors hold flow constant against changes in arterial blood pressure.

◦ Dysfunctional Mode - the Sluggish Valve - hypertension, small-vessel disease, and Alzheimer disease blunt or delay the response (a fault regime).
