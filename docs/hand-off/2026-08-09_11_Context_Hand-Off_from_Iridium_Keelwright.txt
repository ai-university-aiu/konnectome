PART SEVEN - SIGN-OFF

Two slices, and both of them were really about the same thing: writing down what a system is allowed to do, in a place where it cannot be quietly forgotten.

Slice 41 had a question the corpus genuinely refuses to answer - when something above a part tells it to change regime, is that an order or a nudge? - and the temptation with a question like that is to pick the reading that suits the code and let the corpus seem to have said it. konnectome picked command, wrote down three reasons that are its own and not the corpus's, wrote down the conflict rule that follows, and then wrote down the list of things the decision does NOT settle, so that the next session inherits exactly what was chosen and not a inch more.

Slice 42 had a rule konnectome had been keeping faithfully for three slices out of memory, and turned it into a refusal that fires. A mode has a rule; a fault has none and never will; and a system that admits a fault as a mode acquires a designed behaviour for being broken and runs it, calmly and forever, reporting nothing. A machine with an official way to be broken never reports being broken.

Between them the owner delivered a seventh phase and asked for the keel to be laid, and the honest thing about konnectome_0_definition is written into its own last paragraph: it arrived forty-one slices late, so it is not a prediction but a reading-back - the concept recovered from the record of what this build actually did every time it had to choose. That is a weaker document than one written first. It is also, now, the only place where what konnectome refuses to be is written down in one list, and three of those refusals had each been argued at least once before anyone thought to write them.

And the most useful thing found this session was found twice, by accident, from two directions that have nothing to do with each other. konnectome's tick still has no seconds in it. A watchdog needs a window and there is no unit to measure one in. It costs one recorded decision and one test, and it has now blocked work in three families.

Write down what the thing is, and what it may never be. The code will keep both promises longer than anyone will keep remembering them.
