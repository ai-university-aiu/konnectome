LAYER 7, THE SYNAPTIC AND JUNCTIONAL LAYER: THE TYPES

A Complete, Comprehensive, and Thorough List and Outline of the Synaptic and Junctional Types of the Human Nervous and Endocrine System

Companion outline to the Layer 6 catalogues (302 neuron types; 85 non-neuronal cell types), the Layer 8 catalogue (71 microcircuit types), and the Layer 9 manuscript, in the Certified_Neuroscientist corpus of D. R. Dison, Founder, Artificial Intelligence University (AIU).

Date of compilation: August 9, 2026.


HOW TO READ THIS OUTLINE

Layer 7 is the framework's one relation-layer: its unit is not a cell and not a circuit but the junction between cells - the synapse, built from parts owned by two different cells, whose defining state variable, the weight, belongs to the relationship and to neither partner.

This outline catalogues the junction types themselves: sixty-four of them, in eight parts.

A note on what counts as a type here: a synaptic type is a recurring junctional design - a characteristic combination of messenger, machinery, geometry, and partners - just as a Layer 8 motif is a recurring wiring diagram.

One physical synapse in the tissue is typically several of the entries below at once: a single cortical excitatory contact is a glutamatergic synapse (Part One) on a dendritic spine (Part Two) wrapped by an astrocyte into a tripartite ensemble (Part Seven).

The parts of this outline are therefore AXES OF CLASSIFICATION as much as bins, and each entry is the pure type along its axis.

Every entry carries one of two tags.

• (COG) means the junction type participates in COGNITION: its transmission or its plasticity contributes to perception as interpreted, attention, memory, decision, or learning.

• (NON-COG) means the junction type serves a substrate or other role: motor execution, autonomic and endocrine regulation, early sensory relay, developmental scaffolding, or housekeeping.

As throughout the corpus, the tag records the principal role, with the text noting where the role changes with the address.


PART ONE. THE CHEMICAL SYNAPSE, BY MESSENGER SYSTEM

The classical synapse in its chemical varieties: vesicle, cleft, receptor - sorted by what is in the vesicle.

1. The Glutamatergic Fast Excitatory Synapse (AMPA and NMDA) (COG).

The brain's standard excitatory contact - alpha-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptors for speed, N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptors as the coincidence-detecting, calcium-admitting teachers - the majority synapse of the central nervous system and the principal site of long-term potentiation.

2. The Kainate-Receptor Synapse (COG).

The third ionotropic glutamate system, slower and more modulatory, prominent at hippocampal mossy fibre contacts, tuning excitability on both sides of the cleft.

3. The Metabotropic Glutamate Synapse (COG).

Glutamate read not as a current but as a message to G-protein cascades - perisynaptic receptors that set gain, gate plasticity, and drive the endocannabinoid reply of entry 15.

4. The GABA-A Fast Inhibitory Synapse (COG).

The standard brake: gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) opening chloride channels within a millisecond - the synapse of every interneuron motif in the Layer 8 catalogue, and the target of benzodiazepines and most anaesthetics.

5. The GABA-B Slow Metabotropic Synapse (COG).

The same messenger read slowly through G-protein-coupled receptors onto potassium channels and calcium channels - inhibition as a state change, hundreds of milliseconds long, and the receptor of the neurogliaform blanket.

6. The Glycinergic Synapse (NON-COG).

The spinal cord and brainstem's own fast brake - the synapse of the Renshaw cell and the Ia reciprocal circuit, and the synapse poisoned by strychnine and tetanus toxin.

7. The Nicotinic Cholinergic Synapse (NON-COG).

Acetylcholine opening a fast cation channel: the synapse of every autonomic ganglion and of the neuromuscular junction - the periphery's standard fast relay, with a smaller central deployment modulating attention circuits.

8. The Muscarinic Cholinergic Junction (COG).

Acetylcholine read slowly through G-protein-coupled receptors - the cortex's and hippocampus's mode-setting input from the basal forebrain, the switch that turns attention and encoding states on, and the parasympathetic effector signal in the periphery.

9. The Dopaminergic Synapse and Varicosity (COG).

Dopamine released from en passant varicosities, acting on D1- and D2-class receptors over micrometres and hundreds of milliseconds - the teaching signal of the basal ganglia selection motif and the reward system's broadcast wire.

10. The Noradrenergic Varicosity (COG).

Noradrenaline sprayed cortex-wide from locus coeruleus varicosities - the gain and arousal dial of the framework's volume transmission channel, more broadcast than synapse yet anchored at recognisable junctional sites.

11. The Serotonergic Synapse and Varicosity (COG).

Serotonin acting through more than a dozen receptor classes - the most receptor-diverse messenger system in the brain, setting mood-coloured operating points across the forebrain.

12. The Histaminergic Varicosity (NON-COG).

The wake-promoting broadcast from the tuberomammillary nucleus - the arousal substrate whose blockade is why antihistamines sedate.

13. The Peptidergic (Dense-Core Vesicle) Synapse (NON-COG).

Neuropeptides - substance P, enkephalin, oxytocin, neuropeptide Y, and dozens more - released from dense-core vesicles, requiring burst firing, diffusing far, acting long: the slow annotation channel that rides alongside every fast transmitter.

14. The Purinergic Synapse (NON-COG).

Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) as a transmitter in its own right, with its breakdown product adenosine as the brake - the synapse family of autonomic effectors, of glial signalling, and of sleep pressure.

15. The Endocannabinoid Retrograde Signal (COG).

The backwards synapse: the postsynaptic cell manufactures lipid messengers on demand that travel back across the cleft to presynaptic cannabinoid receptors, turning the input down - the volume control by which a neuron edits its own inputs.

16. The Nitric Oxide Retrograde Gaseous Signal (COG).

A gas as messenger: made postsynaptically at active NMDA receptor synapses, diffusing backwards and sideways through membranes, recruiting neighbours into plasticity - the framework's most local broadcast.


PART TWO. THE SYNAPSE BY GEOMETRY AND TARGET

Where on the receiving cell the contact lands, and what shape it takes - because placement is computation.

17. The Axospinous Synapse (The Spine Synapse) (COG).

The excitatory contact onto a dendritic spine - the mushroom, thin, and stubby spine heads that compartmentalise calcium and make each synapse a private computational unit; the majority synapse of the cortex and the physical majority of human memory.

18. The Perforated Synapse (COG).

The large, discontinuous-density variant of the spine synapse - the enlarged, often recently potentiated contact, a morphological signature of stored change.

19. The Axodendritic Shaft Synapse (COG).

The contact onto the dendritic shaft itself - the standard placement of somatostatin-family dendritic inhibition and of excitation onto aspiny interneurons.

20. The Axosomatic Synapse (COG).

The contact onto the cell body - the basket cell's placement of choice, where inhibition arrives with maximum authority over spike output.

21. The Axo-Axonic Initial Segment Synapse (The Chandelier Cartridge) (COG).

The contact onto the axon initial segment itself - the chandelier cell's candlestick rows at the one point where the spike is born; the last-word veto.

22. The Presynaptic Terminal-on-Terminal Synapse (NON-COG).

An axon terminal synapsing onto another axon terminal, taxing its release before it happens - the hardware of presynaptic inhibition, deployed massively on sensory afferents in the spinal cord.

23. The Dendrodendritic Reciprocal Synapse (NON-COG).

Dendrite to dendrite, with the return contact micrometres away in the same junction - the olfactory bulb's mitral-granule design, transmission with no axon involved at either end.

24. The En Passant Varicosity (NON-COG).

Not a terminal but a bead on a passing wire: release sites strung along the axon like streetlights - the design of the autonomic ground plexus and of every monoamine broadcast axon.

25. The Serial and Triadic Synapse (COG).

Synapses arranged in chains within one glomerular capsule - terminal onto dendrite onto dendrite, as in the thalamic triad - the geometry that lets one junction gate another within a micrometre.


PART THREE. THE GIANT AND NAMED SYNAPSES

The celebrity junctions: designs so specialised and so large that anatomy gave them personal names.

26. The Neuromuscular Junction (The Motor Endplate) (NON-COG).

The synapse between motor axon and muscle fibre: hundreds of release sites, a folded receptor-packed postsynaptic gutter, and a safety factor above two - the detonator synapse that must never fail, and the target of myasthenia gravis, curare, and botulinum toxin.

27. The Calyx of Held (NON-COG).

The auditory brainstem's hand-shaped giant terminal grasping its target cell whole - the fastest, most reliable synapse in the mammalian brain, built for sound-localisation timing at hundreds of hertz.

28. The Endbulb of Held (NON-COG).

The auditory nerve's giant terminal onto bushy cells of the cochlear nucleus - the first central synapse of hearing, preserving microsecond spike timing from the ear inward.

29. The Hippocampal Mossy Fibre Bouton (COG).

The dentate granule cell's giant multi-release-site terminal gripping CA3 thorny excrescences - a conditional detonator whose explosive facilitation lets a sparse code speak with authority; the teacher synapse of the autoassociative memory motif.

30. The Climbing Fibre-Purkinje Synapse (COG).

One inferior olive axon wrapping one Purkinje cell in hundreds of release sites - the all-or-none teaching synapse of the cerebellum, firing the complex spike that instructs motor learning.

31. The Parallel Fibre-Purkinje Synapse (COG).

The student synapse to entry 30's teacher: one of about 150,000 weak contacts per Purkinje cell, individually negligible, collectively the adjustable weight matrix of the cerebellar perceptron.

32. The Photoreceptor Ribbon Synapse (NON-COG).

The retina's conveyor-belt synapse: a proteinaceous ribbon tethering thousands of vesicles for continuous graded release, transmitting darkness as a steady stream and light as its modulation.

33. The Hair Cell Ribbon Synapse (NON-COG).

The cochlea's and vestibule's ribbon design, running on otoferlin rather than the standard calcium sensors - graded, indefatigable release precise to microseconds; its congenital failure is a leading form of inherited deafness.

34. The Vestibular Calyx Synapse (NON-COG).

The balance system's chalice: an afferent ending that swallows the type I hair cell whole, mixing conventional quantal transmission with direct ephaptic and potassium-mediated coupling inside the enclosed cleft.

35. The Cerebellar Glomerulus (The Mossy Fibre Rosette) (NON-COG).

A giant mossy fibre terminal enclosed with dozens of granule cell dendrites and Golgi cell contacts in one glial-wrapped capsule - a multi-party junction serving the expansion recoder motif.

36. The Thalamic Triad Glomerulus (COG).

The encapsulated three-party junction of the thalamic relay - retinal terminal, interneuron dendrite, relay dendrite - where the attention gate of the Layer 8 catalogue is physically built.

37. The C-Bouton (NON-COG).

The large cholinergic terminal stamped onto motoneuron cell bodies, dialling motor output gain up for effortful movement - the body's built-in turbo switch.


PART FOUR. THE ELECTRICAL AND MIXED JUNCTIONS

Transmission without a messenger: cells joined by pore or by field.

38. The Neuronal Gap Junction (The Electrical Synapse) (COG).

Two half-channels of connexin 36 docked across a two-to-four-nanometre cleft, joining interneuron networks into synchronous timing grids - bidirectional, delay-free, and quietly plastic; the substrate of interneuron-only gamma.

39. The Mixed Chemical-Electrical Synapse (NON-COG).

Gap junction and chemical machinery side by side in one contact - common in fish and frog escape circuits, rare and still contested in the adult human brain; catalogued honestly as a minor human type.

40. The Glial Gap Junction Plaque (NON-COG).

The astrocyte-astrocyte and oligodendrocyte-astrocyte junctions of connexin 43 and connexin 30 - thousands of channels per plaque, welding the glial syncytium of the Layer 8 catalogue into one tissue-scale buffer network.

41. The Pannexin Hemichannel (NON-COG).

The unpaired half-channel: a pore from cytoplasm to extracellular space releasing ATP and signalling molecules without any partner cell - a junction with the world rather than with a neighbour.

42. The Ephaptic Coupling Site (NON-COG).

The junction that is not a junction: cells close enough that one cell's electrical field directly sways its neighbour's membrane - deployed deliberately at the basket cell pinceau onto the Purkinje axon and among olfactory nerve bundles.


PART FIVE. THE PLASTICITY-DEFINED AND LIFE-CYCLE TYPES

Synapses classified by where they stand in the life of a connection.

43. The Silent (NMDA-Only) Synapse (COG).

A structurally complete glutamatergic synapse with NMDA receptors but no AMPA receptors - inaudible at resting voltage, awakened by the insertion of AMPA receptors during potentiation; the brain's stock of pre-built, unwritten connections, abundant in development and in adult-born neurons.

44. The Nascent Filopodial Contact (NON-COG).

The trial synapse: a dendritic filopodium touching a passing axon, forming a provisional contact that will either mature into a spine synapse or withdraw - the physical form of wiring under evaluation.

45. The Tagged, Retracting Synapse (NON-COG).

The synapse marked for removal - complement proteins flagging weak contacts for microglial pruning - the disassembly-in-progress type, and the down escalator of the pruning loop motif.


PART SIX. THE NEUROEFFECTOR AND NEUROSECRETORY JUNCTIONS

Where the nervous system's wiring hands its message to muscle, gland, epithelium, or the bloodstream itself.

46. The Autonomic Neuroeffector Junction (NON-COG).

The loose junction of the visceral motor system: transmitter released from passing varicosities into wide clefts onto gap-junction-coupled smooth muscle sheets - no endplate, no fixed postsynaptic density, transmission by neighbourhood rather than by address.

47. The Splanchnic-Chromaffin Synapse (NON-COG).

The cholinergic synapse from sympathetic preganglionic axon onto the adrenal chromaffin cell - the one synapse whose postsynaptic reply is a hormone surge into the blood; the wire-to-hormone adapter of the framework's one-architecture claim.

48. The Neurohaemal Junction (NON-COG).

The terminal that synapses onto the bloodstream: neurosecretory endings abutting fenestrated capillaries at the median eminence and posterior pituitary, releasing hormone into the portal or systemic circulation - the interface where Layer 7 hands its cargo directly to Layer 11, and the anatomical port of the framework's circumventricular channel.

49. The Neuropod-Vagal Epithelial Synapse (NON-COG).

The gut sensor cell's genuine glutamatergic synapse onto a vagal afferent - the millisecond food-to-brainstem line, the transducer junction of the framework's vagal layer-skipping channel.

50. The Efferent Synapse onto the Hair Cell (NON-COG).

The brain's synapse onto its own sensor: olivocochlear terminals inhibiting outer hair cells through a rare nicotinic-receptor-to-potassium-channel coupling - the volume knob installed inside the microphone.

51. The Merkel Cell-Neurite Complex (NON-COG).

The skin's touch cell joined to its afferent by a genuine serotonergic synapse - the epithelial synapse that gives fine touch its sustained fidelity.

52. The Neuro-Immune Junction (NON-COG).

The emerging class of directed contacts between nerve terminals and immune cells - sympathetic terminals on lymphoid tissue, sensory fibres conversing with mast cells - junctions in the strict sense whose transmission is measured in inflammation rather than millivolts.


PART SEVEN. THE GLIAL AND NON-NEURONAL SYNAPSES

Junctions in which at least one partner is not a neuron - the Layer 7 face of the other-cells catalogue.

53. The Tripartite Synapse Ensheathment (COG).

The astrocyte process wrapped around the cleft as a genuine third party - clearing transmitter, sensing traffic through its own receptors, and answering with gliotransmitters that gate plasticity; the junctional form of the tripartite island motif.

54. The Neuron-OPC Synapse (COG).

The only known synapse onto a non-neuronal cell built with full synaptic machinery: axon terminals releasing glutamate and GABA onto oligodendrocyte precursor cells - the circuit's direct order line to its own insulation factory.

55. The Axo-Myelinic Synapse (COG).

The axon signalling to its own myelin sheath: glutamate released beneath the wrap, read by the oligodendrocyte, tuning metabolic support and myelin remodelling - the junction that makes adaptive myelination addressable.

56. The Perisynaptic Schwann Cap (NON-COG).

The terminal Schwann cell's cap over the neuromuscular junction - the peripheral tripartite arrangement, listening to transmission and directing repair.

57. The Microglia-Synapse Contact (COG).

The transient, minutes-long touch of a microglial process onto a synapse - the inspection contact of the pruning loop, through which the immune system reads and edits the connectome.


PART EIGHT. THE ENDOCRINE JUNCTIONAL EQUIVALENTS

The framework holds that the endocrine system is the same architecture on a slower wire; Layer 7 is where its junctions live.

58. The Beta Cell Gap Junction Network (NON-COG).

Connexin 36 - the neuronal connexin - joining the islet's beta cells into one electrical sheet, the junctional fabric of the islet syncytium motif; its loss fragments insulin pulses in diabetes.

59. The Pituitary Gap Junction Web (NON-COG).

The connexin 43 junctions joining folliculostellate cells to each other and to endocrine cells - the gland-spanning calcium relay fabric of the anterior pituitary.

60. The Granulosa-Oocyte Gap Junction (NON-COG).

Connexin 37 channels joining the egg to its nurse cells - the junction through which the follicle feeds, silences, and finally releases the oocyte; the most consequential gap junction in reproduction.

61. The Paracrine Junction (NON-COG).

The local diffusion contact: a secreting cell and its neighbour within micrometres, message carried by interstitial fluid - the islet's alpha-beta-delta conversation and the tissue-level default of endocrine signalling.

62. The Autocrine Loop (NON-COG).

The cell's junction with itself: secreted messenger returning to receptors on the secreting cell - the self-monitoring wire of endocrine and immune cells alike.

63. The Juxtacrine Contact Junction (NON-COG).

Signalling by touching: membrane-bound ligand meeting membrane-bound receptor - the ephrin and Notch conversations that wire development and hold tissues in register.

64. The Hormone-Receptor Virtual Synapse (NON-COG).

The endocrine synapse at a distance: gland and target cell joined not by a cleft but by the circulation, the "presynaptic" terminal being a secretory cell and the "cleft" being the bloodstream - kilometres of vasculature collapsed into one logical junction; the entry that makes the endocrine half of Layer 7 explicit rather than honorary.


THE COGNITIVE SCOREBOARD

Of the sixty-four junction types above, twenty-seven carry the (COG) tag.

• The chemical messenger systems split by speed and address: the fast addressed transmitters and their plasticity partners (entries 1 through 5, 8 through 11, 15, 16) are cognition's fabric; the peripheral relays and slow broadcasts (6, 7, 12, 13, 14) are substrate.

• Geometry is overwhelmingly (COG), because placement is computation: spine, perforated, shaft, somatic, initial-segment, and triadic placements (17 through 21, 25) are the inhibitory division of labour made physical.

• The giants split by assignment: the memory-system and cerebellar-learning giants (29, 30, 31) and the thalamic triad (36) think; the auditory, retinal, vestibular, and motor giants relay and execute.

• The electrical junctions contribute one thinker - the interneuron gap junction (38), clock fabric of gamma.

• The plasticity types contribute the silent synapse (43), memory's blank tape.

• The glial synapses seat four members at the table (53, 54, 55, 57) - the strongest confirmation yet of the Layer 6 volume's finding that a small elite of non-neuronal cells thinks along.

• The neuroeffector and endocrine junctions (Parts Six and Eight) are (NON-COG) without exception: the places where the nervous system stops computing and starts doing.

The layer's closing lesson mirrors its definition: the weight belongs to the relationship.

Every (COG) entry above is a junction whose strength is adjustable and whose adjustment IS learning; every (NON-COG) entry is a junction built for fidelity instead - and the difference between a synapse that learns and a synapse that must never change is the difference between a mind and a machine that keeps it alive.
