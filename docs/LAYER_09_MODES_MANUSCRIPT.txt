INTRODUCTION: MODES OF A PLACE

The vocabulary is the corpus standard: STATE continuous, MODE discrete; hybrid automaton; admission rule; fault watchdogs.

Applied to regions, the vocabulary reaches its richest level, because a region inherits modes from every layer below it and receives its marching orders from the layers above.

When the hippocampus is in its theta encoding mode, that one regional fact contains: pyramidal cells in their spiking modes (Layer 6), synapses in their plasticity-permissive modes (Layer 7), and the theta-gamma nesting motif engaged (Layer 8) - all coordinated, and all thrown by an arousal broadcast the hippocampus did not originate.

A region's mode is therefore the framework's clearest demonstration of downward causation in ordinary operation: brain states and hormonal axes assign regional modes wholesale, and a global state - waking, slow-wave sleep, rapid-eye-movement sleep, stress, the circadian night - is best read as one coordinated assignment of modes across every construct in this volume.

The universal menu for regions: the three great vigilance modes (awake desynchronised, slow-wave up-down alternation, rapid-eye-movement); engaged versus idling (the task-positive and default configurations); the hippocampal pair (theta encoding versus sharp-wave-ripple replay); the thalamic triple (relay, burst-alert, spindle-gated); tonic versus phasic in the neuromodulatory nuclei; basal versus surge, day versus night, and pulse versus continuous in the endocrine constructs; rest-and-digest versus fight-or-flight in the autonomic constructs; and active versus suppressed in the large-scale networks, whose anticorrelations are modes of coupling rather than of activity.

Each entry is PASTE-READY, formatted as a MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS section for its source construct and keyed by that manuscript's section number, so a future merge is a mechanical splice.

The seventy-seven entries follow in section order: the thirty-three cortical constructs, the fifteen subcortical telencephalic constructs, the five diencephalic constructs, the four brainstem constructs, the cerebellum, the spinal cord, the prevertebral ganglion cluster, the enteric nervous system, the endocrine glands, the ten large-scale networks, and the five loops and pathways.


ENTRY 2.1.1. PRIMARY VISUAL CORTEX (V1, STRIATE CORTEX, BRODMANN AREA 17) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.1)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

V1 is a thalamic relay whose gain and rhythm are re-set by brain state from above, so its mode-machine is a set of processing regimes it is switched between rather than states it chooses for itself.

Its dominant modes are the universal cortical trio (waking desynchronised processing, slow-wave up-down alternation, and rapid-eye-movement or REM activation), overlaid by a within-wake gain regime driven by attention and locomotion.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Waking Desynchronised Mode - The Open Aperture - low-amplitude fast local field potential in the beta-gamma range, faithful relay of retinal contours to the cortex.

◦ Attentive High-Gain Sub-mode - The Widened Pupil - a within-wake amplification in which attention or locomotion raises the gain on the driven channels.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - the non-REM (rapid-eye-movement) sleep regime of synchronised up and down states below one hertz.

◦ REM Activation Mode - The Ghost Screen - a wake-like desynchronised regime running on internally generated rather than retinal drive.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Open Aperture the region tracks lateral-geniculate input to lamina 4C with a gain near unity, expressing orientation and disparity tuning, local field potential dominated by 30 to 80 hertz gamma (high confidence).

◦ In the Widened Pupil cholinergic and noradrenergic drive raise response gain by roughly 20 to 50 percent on attended channels, reduce trial-to-trial variability, and sharpen gamma coherence (high confidence for the direction, modest for the exact magnitude, which is largely from rodent locomotion studies).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex firing is chopped into up states (active) and down states (silent) recurring below one hertz; feedforward transmission of real retinal signal is effectively suspended (high confidence).

◦ In the Ghost Screen the local field potential resembles waking, superficial layers of primary sensory cortex can still show patches of slow-wave activity, and drive is largely top-down (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Ascending arousal (acetylcholine from nucleus basalis of Meynert, norepinephrine from locus coeruleus) - from Tidal Cortex or Ghost Screen to Open Aperture - seconds.

◦ Attention or locomotion onset (cholinergic basal-forebrain volley, vasoactive-intestinal-peptide disinhibition) - from Open Aperture to Widened Pupil - under one second.

◦ Sleep-wake flip-flop handing over to non-REM, thalamic reticular and cortical slow oscillation - from Open Aperture to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

◦ REM-on pontine cholinergic drive - from Tidal Cortex to Ghost Screen - seconds to minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: occipital spike-wave discharge or photic-driven hypersynchrony departs from every admitted mode.

◦ Warning condition: sustained high-amplitude synchronised activity during behavioural waking (a mode collision) is the danger sign.

◦ Biological watchdog: thalamic reticular and cortical inhibitory (parvalbumin) interneurons cap runaway synchrony; these fault regimes belong to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS, not the register.


ENTRY 2.1.2. SECONDARY AND HIGHER VISUAL AREAS (V2, V3, V4, V5/MT) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.2)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

These are relay-with-attractor areas that assemble edges into form, colour, and motion, and they inherit the same brain-state trio as V1 while adding a stronger attentional-selection mode because they sit deeper in the processing hierarchy.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Waking Feedforward Mode - The Assembly Line - desynchronised processing passing form and motion up the ventral and dorsal streams.

◦ Attentive Selection Mode - The Spotlight Stage - feature-based and spatial attention biases the competition among representations.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - non-rapid-eye-movement (non-REM) synchronised alternation.

◦ REM Activation Mode - The Ghost Screen - internally driven wake-like activity; note that secondary and associative visual areas tend to desynchronise more fully in REM than primary areas do.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Assembly Line each stage extracts a more abstract feature (V2 illusory contours, V4 colour constancy, V5/MT direction of motion), gain near unity, gamma-band local computation (high confidence).

◦ In the Spotlight Stage attention raises gain on the attended feature or location by tens of percent and enhances gamma synchrony with frontal and parietal partners (high confidence for direction).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex feedforward transmission is suspended into up-down cycling below one hertz (high confidence).

◦ In the Ghost Screen these higher areas are largely desynchronised and driven by feedback, supporting visual imagery of dreams (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Ascending arousal (acetylcholine, norepinephrine) - Tidal Cortex or Ghost Screen to Assembly Line - seconds.

◦ Attentional set from frontal eye fields and posterior parietal cortex, pulvinar timing - Assembly Line to Spotlight Stage - under one second.

◦ Non-REM sleep onset - Assembly Line to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

◦ REM-on cholinergic drive - Tidal Cortex to Ghost Screen - seconds to minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: pathological hypersynchrony or migraine spreading depression crossing these areas leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: loss of feature-selective gain with preserved gross activation warns of degraded processing.

◦ Biological watchdog: inhibitory interneuron networks and pulvinar timing keep synchrony bounded; pathological regimes are held in FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.3. PRIMARY AUDITORY CORTEX (A1, HESCHL'S GYRUS, BRODMANN AREAS 41 AND 42) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.3)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

A1 is a tonotopic relay whose modes are the universal brain-state trio plus an attentive listening regime, and it is notable that primary auditory cortex, like somatomotor cortex, can retain patches of slow-wave activity even during rapid-eye-movement (REM) sleep.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Waking Listening Mode - The Open Ear - desynchronised tonotopic relay of medial-geniculate input.

◦ Attentive Tracking Sub-mode - The Cocked Ear - attention raises gain on an attended stream or frequency band.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - non-REM synchronised alternation.

◦ REM Activation Mode - The Half-Waking Ear - wake-like desynchronisation that in auditory cortex is often incomplete, with residual superficial slow waves.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Open Ear the region tracks the ventral medial-geniculate nucleus into lamina 4 with faithful frequency tuning, gamma-band local processing (high confidence).

◦ In the Cocked Ear cholinergic gain and top-down bias sharpen frequency tuning and phase-lock to an attended talker, raising effective signal-to-noise (high confidence for direction).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex firing follows up-down cycling below one hertz and stimulus transmission is gated down (high confidence).

◦ In the Half-Waking Ear the cortex is partly desynchronised yet can still register salient sound, consistent with waking to one's own name (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Ascending arousal (acetylcholine from nucleus basalis, norepinephrine from locus coeruleus) - Tidal Cortex to Open Ear - seconds.

◦ Selective-attention set from frontal and parietal cortex - Open Ear to Cocked Ear - under one second.

◦ Non-REM sleep onset - Open Ear to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

◦ REM-on pontine drive - Tidal Cortex to Half-Waking Ear - seconds to minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: temporal-lobe epileptiform discharge or auditory hallucination-linked hyperactivity leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: tinnitus-associated persistent hyperexcitability signals a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: corticothalamic feedback to the medial geniculate and local inhibition damp runaway activity; faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.4. PRIMARY SOMATOSENSORY CORTEX (S1, POSTCENTRAL GYRUS, BRODMANN AREAS 3, 1, AND 2) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.4)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

S1 is a somatotopic relay whose modes are the brain-state trio plus an active-touch regime, and like other primary sensorimotor cortex it can carry slow-wave patches into rapid-eye-movement (REM).

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Waking Touch Mode - The Open Skin - desynchronised relay of ventral-posterior thalamic input mapping the body surface.

◦ Active Palpation Sub-mode - The Reading Fingertip - attention and self-generated movement re-weight the map, with efference-copy attenuation of self-touch.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - non-REM synchronised alternation.

◦ REM Activation Mode - The Phantom Touch - wake-like desynchronisation on internally generated drive.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Open Skin the region tracks ventral posterolateral and posteromedial thalamic input to lamina 4 with faithful somatotopy (high confidence).

◦ In the Reading Fingertip attention raises gain on the palpated body part and efference copy attenuates predicted self-touch, which is why one cannot tickle oneself (high confidence).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex firing follows up-down cycling below one hertz, sensory transmission gated down (high confidence).

◦ In the Phantom Touch drive is largely top-down and can enter dream body sensation (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Ascending arousal (acetylcholine, norepinephrine) - Tidal Cortex to Open Skin - seconds.

◦ Movement or attention onset (efference copy from motor cortex, cholinergic drive) - Open Skin to Reading Fingertip - under one second.

◦ Non-REM sleep onset - Open Skin to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

◦ REM-on drive - Tidal Cortex to Phantom Touch - seconds to minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: focal sensory seizure (a spreading somatosensory march) or deafferentation phantom pain leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: maladaptive map reorganisation after amputation warns of a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: descending corticosensory gating at the dorsal column nuclei and local inhibition damp aberrant activity; faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.5. PRIMARY MOTOR CORTEX (M1, PRECENTRAL GYRUS, BRODMANN AREA 4) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.5)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

M1 is a relay-and-integrator that issues the final cortical command, and its modes distinguish holding a posture from executing a movement, on top of the sleep-wake trio, with motor cortex being a region that frequently retains slow-wave activity in rapid-eye-movement (REM).

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Movement Execution Mode - The Firing Line - active issuing of descending commands during voluntary movement, gamma and broadband desynchronisation.

◦ Postural Hold Mode - The Set Brace - beta-dominated maintenance of a steady posture, the status-quo rhythm.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - non-REM synchronised alternation.

◦ REM Atonia Mode - The Dreaming Hand - cortical activation with descending output blocked by brainstem-imposed muscle atonia.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Firing Line M1 integrates plan from premotor, basal ganglia, and cerebellum and emits corticospinal commands; beta drops and broadband and gamma activity rise around movement onset (high confidence).

◦ In the Set Brace beta-band activity (13 to 30 hertz) dominates and signals maintain-current-state, holding posture with low change (high confidence).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex output is suspended into up-down cycling below one hertz (high confidence).

◦ In the Dreaming Hand cortical motor activity can occur but is prevented from reaching muscle by glycinergic and gamma-aminobutyric-acid brainstem atonia circuits (high confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Movement command with dopaminergic vigour signal - Set Brace to Firing Line - tens to hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Movement end, return to hold - Firing Line to Set Brace - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Non-REM sleep onset - Set Brace to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

◦ REM-on with atonia (sublaterodorsal nucleus) - Tidal Cortex to Dreaming Hand - seconds to minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: excessive basal-ganglia beta (Parkinsonian rigidity) or motor seizure leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: pathological beta excess or loss of REM atonia (REM behaviour disorder, acting out dreams) warns of fault.

◦ Biological watchdog: the subthalamic hyperdirect stop pathway and brainstem atonia circuits are the guards; these fault regimes route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.6. PREMOTOR CORTEX (PMC) AND SUPPLEMENTARY MOTOR AREA (SMA), BRODMANN AREA 6 (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.6)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The premotor cortex and supplementary motor area are the planning offices of movement, so their modes separate externally cued planning from internally generated planning, over the usual sleep-wake trio.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Externally Cued Planning Mode - The Cued Draftsman - premotor preparation of movements guided by external events, including mirror activation on observing action.

◦ Internally Generated Sequencing Mode - The Inner Metronome - supplementary-motor-area assembly of self-initiated movement sequences and bimanual coordination.

◦ Motor Preparation Hold Mode - The Loaded Spring - a readiness state with rising preparatory activity before movement onset.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - non-rapid-eye-movement (non-REM) synchronised alternation, with REM sharing the same suspended-output character.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Cued Draftsman premotor cortex converts parietal spatial and object information into reach and grasp plans handed to M1 (high confidence).

◦ In the Inner Metronome the supplementary motor area sequences internally generated actions and supplies the sense of volition (high confidence).

◦ In the Loaded Spring preparatory activity builds (the readiness potential) with beta suppression before movement (high confidence).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex planning output is gated down into up-down cycling (high confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ External sensory cue via posterior parietal cortex - Tidal Cortex or hold to Cued Draftsman - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Internal goal from prefrontal cortex and basal-ganglia action selection - to Inner Metronome - hundreds of milliseconds to seconds.

◦ Approaching execution (dopaminergic go signal) - planning to Loaded Spring to M1 handoff - tens to hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Non-REM sleep onset (thalamocortical slow oscillation) - waking planning to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: alien-hand release of the supplementary motor area or supplementary-motor seizure leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: involuntary purposeful actions the patient disowns warn of a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: prefrontal veto and basal-ganglia gating hold plans until release; faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.7. DORSOLATERAL PREFRONTAL CORTEX (DLPFC, BRODMANN AREAS 9 AND 46) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.7)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The dorsolateral prefrontal cortex is a point-attractor working-memory machine, so its defining mode is the delay-period engaged state that holds a value across a gap, and it toggles against the task-negative default state as the core of the task-positive network.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Delay-Period Engaged Mode - The Held Thought - recurrent self-excitation sustains a representation across seconds absent the stimulus.

◦ Rule-Switching Mode - The Turned Rule - reconfiguration to a new task set (cognitive flexibility).

◦ Task-Negative Idle Mode - The Released Hold - disengagement toward rest as the default mode network rises.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - non-rapid-eye-movement (non-REM) synchronised alternation, with attractor states not maintained.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Held Thought persistent delay-period firing maintains the memorandum; dopamine tunes signal-to-noise on an inverted-U, so too little or too much degrades maintenance (high confidence).

◦ In the Turned Rule the network breaks the current attractor and settles into a new one, supported by cingulate conflict input (high confidence for the function).

◦ In the Released Hold task-positive activity falls and anticorrelated default-mode activity rises (high confidence for the anticorrelation, which is a robust resting-state finding).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex recurrent maintenance collapses into up-down cycling below one hertz (high confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Task onset with a to-be-held item, gated by mesocortical dopamine - Released Hold to Held Thought - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Rule change or error, anterior cingulate conflict signal - Held Thought to Turned Rule - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Salience-network (anterior insula) switch releasing the executive - Held Thought to Released Hold - seconds.

◦ Non-REM sleep onset - waking modes to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: perseveration (unable to release the attractor) or complete distractibility (unable to hold it) leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: dysexecutive perseveration or maintenance failure with intact tested intelligence warns of a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: dopaminergic tone kept within the inverted-U window and anterior-cingulate monitoring guard performance; faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.8. VENTROMEDIAL PREFRONTAL CORTEX (VMPFC) AND ORBITOFRONTAL CORTEX (OFC), BRODMANN AREAS 10, 11, 12, 25, 32 (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.8)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This is a comparator-and-integrator that assigns value, so its modes distinguish steady valuation from reversal updating when contingencies change, and it participates in the default mode network at rest.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Valuation Mode - The Price Tag - integration of multisensory and emotional input into a value estimate for each option.

◦ Reversal-Update Mode - The Reprice - rapid rewriting of value when a contingency reverses.

◦ Regulatory Mode - The Cooling Hand - top-down damping of the amygdala and autonomic outflow, the extinction pathway.

◦ Task-Negative Self-Reference Mode - The Inner Ledger - default-mode participation in self-referential and prospective valuation at rest.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Price Tag the orbitofrontal cortex, uniquely receiving all five senses plus taste and smell, computes the worth of a whole multisensory experience, dopamine carrying reward prediction error (high confidence).

◦ In the Reprice value is updated within a few trials when reward contingencies change, dopamine-gated (high confidence).

◦ In the Cooling Hand descending projections to the amygdala and periaqueductal grey implement regulation and fear extinction, the pathway psychotherapy is thought to strengthen (moderate to high confidence).

◦ In the Inner Ledger the region contributes to resting self-referential thought within the default mode network (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Option presentation with sensory and emotional input - Inner Ledger to Price Tag - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Reward contingency reversal (dopaminergic prediction error) - Price Tag to Reprice - a few trials.

◦ Threat or negative affect needing regulation (amygdala input) - to Cooling Hand - seconds.

◦ Task offset and rest, salience-network release - task modes to Inner Ledger - seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: the Phineas Gage pattern, preserved intelligence with collapsed judgement and social conduct, leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: failure of reversal learning or of emotion regulation warns of a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: reciprocal amygdala and dopaminergic prediction-error signals keep valuation calibrated; faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.9. ANTERIOR CINGULATE CORTEX (ACC, BRODMANN AREAS 24, 32, 33) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.9)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The anterior cingulate cortex is a comparator that notices when something is going wrong, so its modes are monitoring, alarm, and effort mobilisation, and with the anterior insula it anchors the salience network.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Conflict-Monitoring Mode - The Watchtower - ongoing surveillance for response conflict and error.

◦ Error-and-Pain Alarm Mode - The Struck Bell - phasic firing on error, physical pain, and social rejection.

◦ Effort-Allocation Mode - The Taskmaster - recruitment of executive control and adjustment of effort after conflict.

◦ Task-Negative Idle Mode - The Quiet Post - disengaged rest with reduced monitoring load.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Watchtower the region tracks the degree of response conflict and feeds a graded control-demand signal to the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (high confidence).

◦ In the Struck Bell it fires on errors and on the affective (unpleasant) dimension of pain, the same circuitry activated by social rejection, so a broken heart and a broken arm share tissue (high confidence).

◦ In the Taskmaster it allocates effort and biases premotor and supplementary-motor responses, norepinephrine raising urgency (moderate to high confidence).

◦ In the Quiet Post monitoring output falls at rest (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Rising response conflict - Watchtower to Taskmaster - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Committed error or pain or rejection (locus-coeruleus and dopaminergic input) - to Struck Bell - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Salience detection by anterior insula - idle to monitoring or alarm - seconds.

◦ Task offset - active modes to Quiet Post - seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: pathological error-related hyperactivity (as in obsessive-compulsive checking) or cingulate seizure leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: chronically elevated conflict or pain-affect signalling warns of a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: dopaminergic and noradrenergic tone calibrate the alarm threshold; faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.10. POSTERIOR CINGULATE CORTEX (PCC) AND PRECUNEUS, BRODMANN AREAS 23, 31, 7m (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.10)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The posterior cingulate cortex and precuneus form a metabolically expensive self-model hub that is busiest at rest, so its defining contrast is the task-negative internal mode against task-positive deactivation.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Task-Negative Self-Model Mode - The Inner Theatre - resting self-referential processing, autobiographical recall, and mental time travel.

◦ Task-Positive Deactivation Mode - The Dimmed House - suppression on the onset of demanding external tasks.

◦ Broad-Monitoring Mode - The Standing Watch - a proposed intermediate state balancing internal and external focus.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - non-rapid-eye-movement (non-REM) synchronised alternation.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Inner Theatre the region sustains autobiographical and self-schema patterns at among the highest metabolic rates in the brain, coupled to the medial prefrontal cortex (high confidence).

◦ In the Dimmed House activity drops as goal-directed networks engage, the canonical default-mode deactivation (high confidence).

◦ In the Standing Watch the region is proposed to modulate the balance between internal focus and environmental monitoring (moderate confidence, an interpretive model).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex hub activity follows up-down cycling below one hertz (high confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Onset of a demanding external task, salience-network switch - Inner Theatre to Dimmed House - hundreds of milliseconds to seconds.

◦ Task offset and rest - Dimmed House to Inner Theatre - seconds.

◦ Shifting internal-external balance - to Standing Watch - seconds.

◦ Non-REM sleep onset - waking modes to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: failure to deactivate during tasks, or early amyloid deposition and hypometabolism, leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: this is one of the earliest sites of Alzheimer hypometabolism, so persistent hypometabolism is the warning.

◦ Biological watchdog: salience-network switching enforces the internal-external toggle; degenerative fault regimes route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.11. THE DEFAULT MODE NETWORK CORE TRIAD (PUC) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.11)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The Default Mode Network Core Triad (medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate and precuneus, and angular gyrus) is an attractor-hub network, so its modes are the network-level toggle between task-negative self-reference and task-positive suppression, switched by the salience network.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Task-Negative Core Mode - The Resting Chorus - the three nodes act together at highest within-network connectivity for self-referential cognition.

◦ Task-Positive Suppression Mode - The Silenced Chorus - the triad is first to deactivate at goal-directed task onset.

◦ Constructive Simulation Mode - The Inner Rehearsal - directed use of the network for autobiographical recall, prospection, and theory of mind.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - non-rapid-eye-movement (non-REM) synchronised alternation across the hubs.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Resting Chorus the medial prefrontal, posterior cingulate, and angular nodes show the highest mutual functional connectivity of any default-mode nodes (high confidence).

◦ In the Silenced Chorus the triad deactivates within a second or so of demanding-task onset (high confidence).

◦ In the Inner Rehearsal the same network is actively engaged for mind-wandering, envisioning the future, and modelling others' minds, so it is task-negative only relative to external tasks (high confidence).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex hub coupling persists but is chopped into slow up-down cycling in non-REM sleep (moderate to high confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ External demanding-task onset, right anterior insula salience switch - Resting Chorus to Silenced Chorus - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Task offset - Silenced Chorus to Resting Chorus - seconds.

◦ Internally directed recall or prospection - Resting Chorus to Inner Rehearsal - seconds.

◦ Non-REM sleep onset - waking modes to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: failure of the triad to suppress during tasks, or the preferential Alzheimer attack on medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate nodes, leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: loss of anticorrelation with task-positive networks, or degeneration eroding the autobiographical self, warns of fault.

◦ Biological watchdog: the anterior-insula salience switch enforces the toggle; degenerative and connectivity faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.12. INSULAR CORTEX (THE INSULA) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.12)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The insula is an integrator-and-comparator of bodily state and, in its anterior division, the switch of the triple network, so its modes span quiet interoceptive monitoring, salience detection, and active network switching.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Interoceptive Monitoring Mode - The Body Gauge - steady reading of the physiological condition of every tissue.

◦ Salience-Detection Mode - The Alarm Bell - bottom-up flagging of a significant bodily or environmental event.

◦ Network-Switching Mode - The Points Lever - anterior-insula switching that suppresses the default mode network and engages the central executive network.

◦ Task-Negative Rest Mode - The Idle Gauge - low-salience resting monitoring.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Body Gauge the insula integrates lamina-I spinothalamic and vagal afferents into a felt map of the body, the anterior insula also serving as gustatory cortex (high confidence).

◦ In the Alarm Bell it compares felt state to expectation and flags salient interoceptive prediction errors (moderate to high confidence).

◦ In the Points Lever the right anterior insula causally drives the switch from default-mode to executive engagement, coupled tightly with the anterior cingulate; optogenetic anterior-insula stimulation suppresses the default mode network (high confidence for the switching role).

◦ In the Idle Gauge monitoring continues at low salience during rest (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Rising interoceptive or external salience (bodily change, unexpected event) - Body Gauge to Alarm Bell - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Confirmed salient event - Alarm Bell to Points Lever, driving executive engagement - hundreds of milliseconds to seconds.

◦ Salience resolved - switching to Idle Gauge - seconds.

◦ Autonomic mobilisation via serotonin and norepinephrine tone - across modes as arousal shifts - seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: insular dysfunction with aberrant between-network connectivity (as in depression and schizophrenia) or panic-linked interoceptive misreading leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: chronic misattribution of bodily salience warns of a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: reciprocal anterior-cingulate coupling and serotonergic and noradrenergic tone calibrate the switch; faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.13. BROCA'S AREA (LEFT INFERIOR FRONTAL GYRUS, BRODMANN AREAS 44 AND 45) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.13)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

Broca's area is an integrator-and-gate that assembles speech before it is spoken, so its modes separate active articulatory sequencing from covert inner speech and syntactic processing, over the sleep-wake trio.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Speech-Production Mode - The Assembler - active sequencing of phonology into an articulatory program handed to motor cortex.

◦ Inner-Speech Mode - The Silent Rehearsal - covert generation of speech without articulation.

◦ Syntactic-Parsing Mode - The Grammar Engine - structural processing of sentence form during comprehension and production.

◦ Task-Negative Idle Mode - The Rested Tongue - disengaged rest.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Assembler the region converts phonological content from Wernicke's area into a motor plan for the larynx, tongue, and lips via motor cortex (high confidence).

◦ In the Silent Rehearsal the same sequencing runs without motor output, supporting verbal working-memory rehearsal (moderate to high confidence).

◦ In the Grammar Engine the region contributes to syntactic structure building, dopamine modulating sequencing (moderate confidence).

◦ In the Rested Tongue language circuitry is quiescent (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Intention to speak from dorsolateral prefrontal cortex - Idle to Assembler - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Suppressing overt output - Assembler to Silent Rehearsal - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Complex sentence structure - to Grammar Engine - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Task offset - active modes to Rested Tongue - seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: Broca's aphasia (effortful agrammatic speech with preserved comprehension) leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: effortful halting output with painful awareness of the deficit warns of a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: reciprocal arcuate-fasciculus feedback from Wernicke's area monitors output; lesion faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.14. WERNICKE'S AREA (LEFT POSTERIOR SUPERIOR TEMPORAL GYRUS, BRODMANN AREA 22) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.14)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

Wernicke's area is a lexical-semantic attractor that maps sound onto meaning, so its modes separate comprehension by pattern completion from lexical retrieval for production, over the sleep-wake trio.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Comprehension Mode - The Meaning Maker - mapping incoming sound patterns onto stored word meanings by pattern completion.

◦ Lexical-Retrieval Mode - The Word Finder - supplying phonological word forms to Broca's area for production.

◦ Reading-Input Mode - The Script Reader - accepting written-language input relayed through the angular gyrus.

◦ Task-Negative Idle Mode - The Quiet Lexicon - disengaged rest.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Meaning Maker the region completes word-meaning representations from acoustic input, cholinergic tone gating access (high confidence for the function).

◦ In the Word Finder it supplies phonological content over the arcuate fasciculus to Broca's area, its selective disconnection producing conduction aphasia (high confidence).

◦ In the Script Reader visual word form arrives via the angular gyrus and is mapped to meaning (moderate to high confidence).

◦ In the Quiet Lexicon lexical circuitry is quiescent (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Incoming speech from primary auditory cortex - Idle to Meaning Maker - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Intention to name or speak - to Word Finder - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Reading onset (angular gyrus input) - to Script Reader - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Task offset - active modes to Quiet Lexicon - seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: Wernicke's aphasia (fluent empty speech with severe comprehension loss and no awareness of deficit) leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: fluent but meaningless output combined with unawareness warns of a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: the semantic network and reciprocal Broca coupling normally cross-check output; lesion faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.15. THE MEDIAL TEMPORAL INTERFACE CORTICES: ENTORHINAL, PERIRHINAL, AND PARAHIPPOCAMPAL (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.15)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

These are the funnel through which cortex reaches the hippocampus, so as a composite their modes track the hippocampal encoding-versus-consolidation cycle plus theta-paced gating, with the entorhinal grid map as an attractor.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Encoding-Gateway Mode - The Open Funnel - theta-paced feedforward flow of what and where information into the hippocampus during active exploration.

◦ Retrieval-Return Mode - The Reverse Funnel - entorhinal return projections mediating memory recall to neocortex.

◦ Consolidation-Replay Mode - The Night Relay - sharp-wave-ripple-coupled dialogue with cortex during rest and slow-wave sleep.

◦ Grid-Map Attractor Mode - The Standing Lattice - the medial entorhinal grid-cell metric holding a stable spatial code.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Open Funnel perirhinal object (what) and parahippocampal spatial (where) streams converge on entorhinal cortex and enter the hippocampus by the perforant path, theta-band paced (high confidence).

◦ In the Reverse Funnel entorhinal return projections carry recalled content back to neocortex (high confidence).

◦ In the Night Relay the interface participates in ripple-coupled replay coordinating hippocampal-cortical consolidation during slow-wave sleep (high confidence).

◦ In the Standing Lattice grid cells fire in a hexagonal lattice providing the spatial metric, a continuous-attractor code (high confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Active exploration and hippocampal theta (paced from the medial septum) - to Open Funnel - seconds.

◦ Retrieval cue from cortex - to Reverse Funnel - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Rest or slow-wave sleep, ripple onset - Open Funnel to Night Relay - seconds to minutes.

◦ Movement through space - engagement of the Standing Lattice grid metric - continuous.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: medial-temporal epileptiform discharge (this is a common seizure-onset zone) or early entorhinal tau pathology leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: entorhinal degeneration (an earliest Alzheimer site) warns of a fault regime.

◦ Biological watchdog: theta pacing and local inhibition keep the gateway rhythmically bounded; faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.16. RETROSPLENIAL CORTEX (BRODMANN AREAS 29 AND 30) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.16)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The retrosplenial cortex is a comparator that translates between the map in your head and the view from where you stand, so its modes track active navigation against default-mode-linked internal mapping.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Reference-Frame Translation Mode - The Compass Rose - active conversion between egocentric (self-relative) and allocentric (map-relative) spatial frames during navigation.

◦ Heading-Integration Mode - The Steady Needle - maintenance of head-direction and place coding during movement.

◦ Task-Negative Internal-Mapping Mode - The Armchair Map - default-mode-linked scene construction and imagined navigation at rest.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - The Tidal Cortex - non-rapid-eye-movement (non-REM) synchronised alternation.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the Compass Rose the region translates between egocentric and allocentric coordinates using anterior-thalamic, subicular, and parahippocampal input (high confidence for the function).

◦ In the Steady Needle it integrates heading and place signals during ongoing movement, acetylcholine supporting map learning (moderate to high confidence).

◦ In the Armchair Map it contributes to imagined scenes and mental navigation within the default mode and spatial-navigation systems at rest (moderate confidence).

◦ In the Tidal Cortex activity follows up-down cycling below one hertz (moderate to high confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Active navigation onset (movement, visual scene input via parahippocampal cortex) - Armchair Map to Compass Rose - hundreds of milliseconds to seconds.

◦ Sustained movement - engagement of the Steady Needle - continuous.

◦ Rest and imagined navigation, default-mode engagement - active modes to Armchair Map - seconds.

◦ Non-REM sleep onset - waking modes to Tidal Cortex - tens of seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: topographical disorientation (landmarks recognised but unusable for navigation) leaves the admitted set.

◦ Warning condition: this is an early Alzheimer hypometabolism site, so failure to translate frames despite intact landmark recognition warns of fault.

◦ Biological watchdog: reciprocal anterior-thalamic and hippocampal coupling cross-check the map; degenerative faults route to FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.


ENTRY 2.1.17. POSTERIOR PARIETAL CORTEX (BRODMANN AREAS 5, 7, 39, 40) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.17)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The posterior parietal cortex is an integrator of multisensory space, and its modes are set jointly by the sleep-wake machinery below and by the tug-of-war between the dorsal attention network and the default mode network above, in which its subdivisions sit on opposite sides.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Waking Desynchronised Processing Mode - the Open Atlas - the tonic awake regime in which the spatial map is live and continuously updated.

◦ Engaged Spatial-Attention Mode - the Surveyor's Beam - task-positive operation with the dorsal attention network, aiming attention and guiding reach and gaze.

◦ Idling Default Mode - the Daydream Annex - the angular gyrus running with the default mode network during rest and internal thought, while dorsal spatial machinery stands down.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - the Night Ledger - the deep-sleep alternation of whole-population Up and Down states at below 1 hertz, serving consolidation.

◦ Rapid-Eye-Movement (REM) Mode - the Unmoored Map - a desynchronised, internally driven regime in which spatial imagery runs without sensory anchoring.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Open Atlas: fuses somatosensory, dorsal visual, and vestibular input into a common egocentric frame at moderate gain, with alpha rhythm (roughly 8 to 13 hertz) marking regions being held quiet; high confidence.

◦ Surveyor's Beam: gain rises under acetylcholine and norepinephrine, alpha power lateralises so that it climbs over the map of the ignored hemifield and falls over the attended one, and gamma (roughly 30 to 100 hertz) rises locally where computation is live; high confidence, the alpha lateralisation is a robust electroencephalography finding.

◦ Daydream Annex: the angular gyrus (Brodmann area 39) shifts to semantic and self-referential traffic with the posterior cingulate and medial prefrontal cortex, while the superior parietal machinery idles; high confidence for the network membership, moderate for the sharpness of the switch.

◦ Night Ledger: input is largely gated off, and the region alternates between synchronous depolarised Up states and silent Down states under the slow oscillation, replaying with the hippocampus by way of the parahippocampal route; high confidence.

◦ Unmoored Map: acetylcholine returns without norepinephrine, and the map runs on internal input, which is the plausible substrate of spatially vivid dreaming; moderate confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Task demand or a salient target, broadcast as top-down bias from the frontal eye fields and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex plus a locus coeruleus norepinephrine phasic burst - Open Atlas or Daydream Annex to Surveyor's Beam - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Task completion and rest - Surveyor's Beam to Daydream Annex - seconds, as the anticorrelated networks trade dominance.

◦ Sleep onset, driven by the ventrolateral preoptic flip-flop withdrawing the ascending arousal broadcasts - waking modes to Night Ledger - minutes.

◦ REM onset, driven by the pontine REM switch restoring cholinergic tone - Night Ledger to Unmoored Map - roughly 90-minute cycling.

◦ Waking, driven by the ascending arousal system - sleep modes to Open Atlas - seconds to minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: alpha that fails to lateralise with attention marks a beam that will not aim; right-hemisphere loss of the machinery altogether yields hemispatial neglect, a fault, not a mode.

◦ Watchdog: the ventral attention network's circuit-breaker interrupt, arriving by way of the temporoparietal junction, is the safeguard against a beam locked too tightly on one target.


ENTRY 2.1.18. TEMPORAL ASSOCIATION CORTEX AND THE INFEROTEMPORAL CORTEX (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.18)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This is attractor cortex, a landscape of stored object and semantic patterns, and its modes are the different ways of visiting that landscape: driven by the eyes, driven by memory search, or replayed in sleep.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Stimulus-Driven Recognition Mode - the Namer of Things - feedforward invariant object recognition during active vision.

◦ Top-Down Retrieval Mode - the Library Walk - semantic and imagery retrieval in which prefrontal and anterior-temporal signals reactivate representations without a matching stimulus.

◦ Idling Default Mode - the Closed Stacks - resting operation with low visual drive, portions of the semantic system running with default mode traffic.

◦ Slow-Wave Up-Down Mode - the Night Rebinding - deep-sleep Up-Down alternation during which perirhinal-hippocampal dialogue consolidates object memory.

◦ rapid-eye-movement (REM) Mode - the Gallery of Sleep - internally generated activation of object and face representations, the plausible source of dream imagery.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Namer of Things: ventral-stream input is pattern-completed into the nearest stored object attractor within roughly 100 to 200 milliseconds, invariant to size, angle, and lighting, with local gamma marking the settled percept; high confidence.

◦ Library Walk: input from the inferior frontal cortex and the anterior temporal semantic hub selects and sustains representations at raised gain, controlled retrieval showing coupling between frontal control regions and temporal cortex in magnetoencephalography; high confidence for the phenomenon, moderate for parameters.

◦ Closed Stacks: spontaneous low-rate drift across the attractor landscape, feeding mind-wandering content; moderate confidence.

◦ Night Rebinding: below-1-hertz Up states carry replayed patterns to and from the medial temporal interface; high confidence for the dialogue, moderate for content specificity.

◦ Gallery of Sleep: cholinergic tone high, external gating shut, attractors visited under internal drive; moderate confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Stimulus onset through the ventral stream - any waking mode to the Namer of Things - roughly 100 milliseconds.

◦ A retrieval demand from the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, with cholinergic attentional gain from the basal forebrain - Namer of Things or Closed Stacks to the Library Walk - hundreds of milliseconds to seconds.

◦ Sleep onset by the hypothalamic flip-flop - waking modes to the Night Rebinding - minutes.

◦ The pontine REM switch - Night Rebinding to the Gallery of Sleep - 90-minute cycling.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: an attractor landscape that erodes gives semantic dementia, and focal fusiform loss gives prosopagnosia; both are structural faults outside the register.

◦ Watchdog: none notable beyond the general cortical inhibitory balance; runaway pattern completion is normally checked by local inhibition.


ENTRY 2.1.19. FRONTAL EYE FIELDS (FEF, BRODMANN AREA 8) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.19)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The frontal eye field is a gate-and-selector running a spatial priority map, and its operating regimes are the distinct jobs that map can serve: holding the eyes still, aiming attention without moving them, and releasing a saccade, which is a rapid ballistic eye movement.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Fixation-Hold Mode - the Steady Gaze - fixation neurons dominant, saccades suppressed, the map quiet.

◦ Covert-Attention Mode - the Silent Spotlight - the map biases visual cortex toward a location while the eyes stay put.

◦ Saccade-Preparation Mode - the Drawn Bow - movement-neuron activity builds toward threshold for a selected target.

◦ Saccade-Execution and Search Mode - the Leaping Eye - repeated winner-take-all target selection and gaze commands during active scanning.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Closed Observatory - the slow-wave Up-Down alternation, and the rapid-eye-movement (REM) regime in which the field helps drive the rapid eye movements that name that sleep stage.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Steady Gaze: fixation-related activity holds movement cells below threshold and leans on the superior colliculus fixation zone; high confidence.

◦ Silent Spotlight: visual and visuomovement subpopulations raise sensory gain at the attended location by feedback to visual cortex, without a motor burst, and this is the mechanism of covert attention; high confidence, single-unit and neuroimaging evidence agree that partly distinct subpopulations serve attention and movement.

◦ Drawn Bow: movement-neuron activity ramps over roughly 100 to 300 milliseconds; the saccade fires when a fixed threshold is crossed, and presaccadic attention shifts to the goal before the eye moves; high confidence.

◦ Leaping Eye: the priority map integrates parietal salience and prefrontal goals into 2 to 4 selections per second during natural search; high confidence.

◦ Closed Observatory: in REM, ponto-geniculo-occipital-linked bursts drive saccade-like commands without visual purpose; moderate confidence for the details.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A goal signal from the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex or a salience signal from the lateral intraparietal area - Steady Gaze to Silent Spotlight or Drawn Bow - roughly 100 to 200 milliseconds.

◦ Threshold crossing in movement neurons - Drawn Bow to Leaping Eye (execution) - tens of milliseconds.

◦ A stop or hold demand from the supplementary eye field and pre-supplementary motor area - Drawn Bow back to Steady Gaze - within roughly 100 milliseconds, the countermanding window.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop and the pontine REM switch - waking modes to and through the Closed Observatory - minutes, then 90-minute cycling.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a bow that cannot be un-drawn shows as failed countermanding and intrusive saccades.

◦ Watchdog: the basal ganglia hold on the superior colliculus, and the fixation-neuron population itself, are the standing brakes on premature release.


ENTRY 2.1.20. SUPRAMARGINAL GYRUS (SMG, BRODMANN AREA 40) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.20)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The supramarginal gyrus is a buffer that can be leased to different tenants, and its modes are the tenancies: on the left the sound-of-language store and the tool-use planner, on the right a post of tactile-spatial attention.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Phonological-Rehearsal Mode - the Echo Shelf - holding a string of speech sounds in short-term store while the articulatory loop refreshes it.

◦ Praxis Mode - the Tool Hand - assembling learned skilled-action representations for tool use and gesture.

◦ Tactile-Spatial Attention Mode - the Feeling Compass - right-lateralised weighting of touch and space during attentive handling.

◦ Idling Default Mode - the Empty Shelf - low-traffic rest between tasks.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Shelf at Night - the standard cortical slow-wave Up-Down alternation and the rapid-eye-movement (REM) desynchronised regime.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Echo Shelf: superior temporal sound codes are held for roughly 2 seconds unless refreshed by way of Broca's area over the arcuate fasciculus, capacity a handful of items, consistent with theta-gamma nesting in which roughly seven gamma cycles ride one theta cycle; high confidence for the store, moderate for the rhythm account.

◦ Tool Hand: retrieves stored action engrams and streams them to the premotor cortex; high confidence, left apraxia lesion evidence.

◦ Feeling Compass: raises gain on somatosensory and spatial input under cholinergic and noradrenergic tone; moderate confidence.

◦ Empty Shelf and Shelf at Night: generic cortical idling and sleep transfer functions, gated input, consolidation traffic in slow-wave sleep; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A verbal-memory or inner-speech demand from the frontal language system - any waking mode to the Echo Shelf - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Seeing or intending tool use, cued by ventral-stream object identity and prefrontal goals - to the Tool Hand - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ A tactile or spatial task, with norepinephrine alerting from the locus coeruleus - to the Feeling Compass - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ The hypothalamic sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Shelf at Night - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: an Echo Shelf that drops items in under a second marks phonological short-term-memory failure, the conduction-aphasia neighbourhood.

◦ Watchdog: none notable beyond general attentional arbitration between the tenancies.


ENTRY 2.1.21. TEMPOROPARIETAL JUNCTION (TPJ) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.21)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The temporoparietal junction is a comparator-and-interrupt with two adjacent franchises, attention-reorienting and mentalising, and its modes are which franchise holds the floor, plus a deliberately silenced state during focused search.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Vigilant-Interrupt Mode - the Fire Bell - standing watch with the ventral attention network for salient, behaviourally relevant surprises.

◦ Reorienting Burst Mode - the Bell Rung - the transient interrupt that breaks the dorsal attention network's current focus.

◦ Mentalising Mode - the Other-Minds Desk - theory of mind, the attribution of beliefs and intentions, run in a neighbouring but distinct patch.

◦ Suppressed Mode - the Muffled Bell - active down-regulation during demanding focused search, so that distractors cannot ring it.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Bell at Night - standard cortical slow-wave and rapid-eye-movement (REM) regimes.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Fire Bell: monitors multisensory input against current expectation at low output rate, filtered so that only task-relevant deviants pass; high confidence.

◦ Bell Rung: a phasic norepinephrine-coupled burst that publishes an interrupt to the frontal cortex and dorsal attention network within roughly 200 to 400 milliseconds of an unexpected relevant event; high confidence.

◦ Other-Minds Desk: integrates superior temporal social percepts into inferences about beliefs and intentions, on a slower, second-scale timescale; high confidence, with the note that imaging shows the mentalising and reorienting peaks displaced by roughly 6 to 10 millimetres, so this is a division of the construct, not a shared circuit proven.

◦ Muffled Bell: gain is lowered, and functional imaging shows right temporoparietal deactivation during high-load focused attention, which protects the current task from distraction; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ An unexpected task-relevant stimulus, with a locus coeruleus phasic broadcast - Fire Bell to Bell Rung - roughly 200 milliseconds.

◦ Interrupt handled, focus re-established by the dorsal attention network - Bell Rung back to Fire Bell - seconds.

◦ A social-inference demand, cued by faces, voices, and narrative from the superior temporal sulcus - to the Other-Minds Desk - seconds.

◦ High task load imposed by prefrontal control - Fire Bell to Muffled Bell - seconds, releasing when the load ends.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Bell at Night - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a bell that rings for everything is distractibility; a bell muffled too deep contributes to neglect-like misses of the unattended field.

◦ Watchdog: prefrontal relevance filtering is the standing gatekeeper on what may ring the interrupt.


ENTRY 2.1.22. SUPERIOR TEMPORAL SULCUS (STS) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.22)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The superior temporal sulcus is a multisensory integrator tuned to the moving social world, and its modes are set by what kind of social stream is playing and by how much the perceiver cares.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Social-Stream Parsing Mode - the People Watcher - continuous binding of dynamic faces, gaze, voices, and biological motion into social percepts.

◦ Audiovisual Speech Mode - the Lip Reader - weighting and fusing heard speech with seen mouth movements.

◦ Socially Amplified Mode - the Warmed Lens - the same parsing run at raised gain under oxytocin and attentional drive during motivated social engagement.

◦ Idling Mode - the Empty Street - low drive in the absence of agents, with default-mode-adjacent narrative traffic.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Street at Night - standard cortical slow-wave and rapid-eye-movement (REM) regimes, the latter a plausible stage for social dream content.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ People Watcher: integrates fusiform form, motion-complex kinematics, and auditory input into agent-and-action percepts on a timescale of hundreds of milliseconds; high confidence.

◦ Lip Reader: fuses audiovisual speech with visual influence strong enough to alter what is heard, the McGurk class of effects; high confidence.

◦ Warmed Lens: oxytocin and cholinergic tone raise the salience and gain of social signals; moderate confidence for mechanism, the modulation is documented but parameters are coarse.

◦ Empty Street and Street at Night: generic idling and sleep transfer functions; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Appearance of an agent, a face in motion, or a voice - Empty Street to People Watcher - roughly 100 to 300 milliseconds.

◦ Speech with a visible talker - People Watcher to Lip Reader - immediate, stimulus-bound.

◦ Social motivation, broadcast hormonally by hypothalamic oxytocin and by attentional systems - People Watcher to Warmed Lens - seconds to minutes.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop and the REM switch - waking modes through the Street at Night - minutes, then 90-minute cycling.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: parsing that fails to bind motion into agency degrades social perception, a pattern reported in autism-spectrum studies of this strip; this is a trait boundary rather than a mode.

◦ Watchdog: none notable; the amygdala downstream acts as the relevance auditor of what the sulcus reports.


ENTRY 2.1.23. MIDDLE TEMPORAL GYRUS (MTG, BRODMANN AREA 21) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.23)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The middle temporal gyrus is a semantic access integrator, and its modes are the two recognised ways meaning is reached, automatic spreading versus controlled search, plus the default-mode tenancy it holds at rest.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Automatic Access Mode - the Open Dictionary - fast, uncontrolled mapping of words and objects onto their dominant meanings.

◦ Controlled Retrieval Mode - the Deep Search - goal-directed selection of weak, remote, or context-appropriate meanings under frontal control.

◦ Narrative Default Mode - the Storyteller's Bench - default-mode-coupled semantic drift during rest, recollection, and mind-wandering.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Dictionary at Night - standard slow-wave Up-Down consolidation and rapid-eye-movement (REM) regimes.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Open Dictionary: auditory and visual word forms activate stored concepts within roughly 200 to 400 milliseconds, dominant associations first; high confidence.

◦ Deep Search: the left inferior frontal gyrus drives the posterior middle temporal gyrus to hold and re-weight candidate meanings, magnetoencephalography showing directed frontal-to-temporal oscillatory influence during controlled retrieval, with gain up under dopamine and acetylcholine; high confidence for the control loop, moderate for rhythm parameters.

◦ Storyteller's Bench: conceptual representations feed self-generated thought with the default mode network at low external gain; high confidence for membership.

◦ Dictionary at Night: gated input, slow-oscillation consolidation of the day's semantic learning; moderate confidence for content specificity.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Word or object onset - any waking mode to the Open Dictionary - roughly 200 milliseconds.

◦ Retrieval difficulty or ambiguity, detected frontally and signalled by the inferior frontal gyrus - Open Dictionary to Deep Search - roughly half a second.

◦ Task offset and rest - Deep Search to the Storyteller's Bench - seconds.

◦ The hypothalamic sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Dictionary at Night - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a Deep Search that cannot converge shows as word-finding failure with preserved comprehension of dominant meanings.

◦ Watchdog: the frontal semantic-control system is itself the watchdog, vetoing prepotent but context-wrong meanings.


ENTRY 2.1.24. FRONTOPOLAR / ANTERIOR PREFRONTAL CORTEX (BRODMANN AREA 10) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.24)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The frontal pole is a supervisory integrator whose defining trick is holding one goal pending while another runs, so its modes are bookkeeping regimes for goals rather than sensory configurations.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Single-Task Supervision Mode - the One-Ledger Desk - monitoring a single ongoing goal at low load.

◦ Branching Mode - the Held Bookmark - maintaining a pending superordinate goal across an interruption while a subtask executes.

◦ Prospective-Memory Watch Mode - the Standing Reminder - carrying a delayed intention and checking the world for its trigger.

◦ Exploration Mode - the Scout's Ledger - biasing behaviour toward sampling alternatives rather than exploiting the current best option.

◦ Metacognitive Review Mode - the Auditor of Thought - evaluating one's own confidence and strategy.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Closed Desk - slow-wave Up-Down alternation, with notable frontal deactivation persisting into rapid-eye-movement (REM).

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ One-Ledger Desk: light integrative monitoring of dorsolateral and ventrolateral prefrontal traffic; high confidence.

◦ Held Bookmark: sustains the pending goal as a slow attractor over seconds to minutes, at a documented capacity cost, dual-task branching being near the human limit; high confidence.

◦ Standing Reminder: holds an intention at low activation for minutes to hours with periodic checking; moderate confidence on mechanism.

◦ Scout's Ledger: tracks the value of unchosen options and, under norepinephrine tonic tone, tips choice toward exploration; moderate confidence, this is the standard exploration-exploitation account.

◦ Auditor of Thought: computes second-order confidence over first-order processing; moderate confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ An interruption or a deliberate subgoal launch from the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex - One-Ledger Desk to Held Bookmark - seconds.

◦ Subtask completion - Held Bookmark back to One-Ledger Desk, restoring the pending goal - seconds.

◦ Falling reward from the current policy, broadcast by dopaminergic prediction-error and rising tonic locus coeruleus norepinephrine - exploitation to the Scout's Ledger - minutes.

◦ A query about one's own performance - any mode to the Auditor of Thought - seconds.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Closed Desk - minutes, and this desk closes early and reopens late, prefrontal function being the last to return on waking, which is sleep inertia.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: too many held bookmarks at once collapses into multitasking error and forgotten intentions.

◦ Watchdog: the anterior cingulate's conflict monitor flags when the held-goal stack is failing.


ENTRY 2.1.25. PRE-SUPPLEMENTARY MOTOR AREA (PRE-SMA, ROSTRAL MEDIAL BRODMANN AREA 6) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.25)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The pre-supplementary motor area is a gate-and-selector upstream of movement, and its modes are the postures of that gate: choosing, sequencing, switching, and slamming shut.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Selection Mode - the Chooser at the Gate - weighing candidate actions before execution.

◦ Sequencing Mode - the Beadstringer - assembling actions into an ordered programme handed to the supplementary motor area.

◦ Set-Switch Mode - the Points Change - dismantling the current task set and installing another.

◦ Braking Mode - the Emergency Hand - the rapid stopping signal sent by the hyperdirect route to the subthalamic nucleus.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Gate at Night - standard slow-wave and rapid-eye-movement (REM) regimes, with REM motor commands blocked downstream by atonia.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Chooser at the Gate: integrates prefrontal goals and cingulate cost signals into a preferred action under dopaminergic gain; high confidence.

◦ Beadstringer: encodes serial order and interval, publishing rank and timing to the supplementary motor area; high confidence from primate sequence recordings.

◦ Points Change: transiently suppresses the old set and raises the new one, at a measurable switch cost of hundreds of milliseconds; high confidence.

◦ Emergency Hand: a broadband stop volley reaching the subthalamic nucleus and cancelling action within roughly 100 to 200 milliseconds, the stop-signal reaction time envelope; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A choice point presented by prefrontal goals - baseline monitoring to the Chooser at the Gate - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ A multi-step intention - Chooser to Beadstringer - immediately on selection.

◦ A rule or context change signalled by the anterior cingulate and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex - any mode to the Points Change - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ A stop imperative, external or internal - any mode to the Emergency Hand - roughly 100 milliseconds, the fastest transition in this register.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Gate at Night - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a gate biased open gives impulsive, unintended action; a gate biased shut gives hesitancy and freezing-adjacent starts.

◦ Watchdog: the subthalamic hold-your-horses circuit is the systemic brake this construct itself commands, and the basal ganglia's inhibitory default backstops it.


ENTRY 2.1.26. SUPPLEMENTARY EYE FIELD (SEF, MEDIAL BRODMANN AREA 6/8) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.26)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The supplementary eye field is a selector for gaze that is internally scheduled rather than stimulus-driven, and its modes divide by where the plan comes from and whether performance is being monitored.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Internally Timed Gaze Mode - the Metronome Eye - issuing self-paced and learned saccade plans without an external trigger.

◦ Sequence Mode - the Choreographer - running an ordered series of gaze shifts as one programme.

◦ Rule-Conflict Mode - the Anti-Look Marshal - enforcing learned rules that oppose reflex, as in the antisaccade task, where one must look away from a flashed target.

◦ Performance-Monitor Mode - the Gaze Auditor - registering errors and predicted reward after saccades and adjusting the next plan.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Dark Rehearsal - standard slow-wave and rapid-eye-movement (REM) regimes.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Metronome Eye: converts intention and elapsed-time signals into gaze commands to the superior colliculus, with dopamine setting the vigour of internally generated action; high confidence.

◦ Choreographer: encodes serial position of saccades in a learned sequence; high confidence from primate recordings.

◦ Anti-Look Marshal: pre-loads the rule and raises fixation-side control so the reflexive plan loses the race; high confidence, supplementary eye field activity predicts antisaccade success.

◦ Gaze Auditor: error-related and reward-related signals within roughly 100 to 200 milliseconds after the movement, feeding adaptation; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ An internal schedule or learned cue from prefrontal and cingulate cortex - rest to the Metronome Eye - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ A learned multi-saccade task - Metronome Eye to the Choreographer - on task engagement, seconds.

◦ An instruction that opposes reflex - to the Anti-Look Marshal - held preparatorily over the seconds before the stimulus.

◦ Movement completion - any executing mode to a Gaze Auditor pass - roughly 100 milliseconds, then return.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Dark Rehearsal - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a weak Marshal shows as antisaccade errors, reflexive glances that the rule should have vetoed.

◦ Watchdog: this construct is itself a watchdog of the frontal eye fields; its own backstop is the cingulate performance monitor beside it.


ENTRY 2.1.27. SUPERIOR PARIETAL LOBULE (SPL, BRODMANN AREAS 5 AND 7) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.27)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The superior parietal lobule is the dorsal-stream coordinate engine, and its modes are the uses that engine is put to: steering attention, steering the hand, holding locations in mind, and shifting between all of these.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Attentive Survey Mode - the Turning Beacon - sustained visuospatial attention over the scene with the dorsal attention network.

◦ Shift Mode - the Beacon Swung - the transient superior parietal signal that moves attention between locations, objects, or even tasks.

◦ Reach-and-Grasp Guidance Mode - the Hand's Pilot - continuous eye-head-hand coordinate transformation during visually guided action.

◦ Spatial Working-Memory Mode - the Held Grid - maintaining locations across a delay.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Beacon Doused - standard slow-wave Up-Down and rapid-eye-movement (REM) regimes.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Turning Beacon: maintains the current priority map at cholinergic-noradrenergic gain, alpha rhythm suppressing the ignored field; high confidence.

◦ Beacon Swung: a domain-general transient that reconfigures the map within roughly 200 to 500 milliseconds, seen for spatial, feature, and task shifts alike; high confidence.

◦ Hand's Pilot: transforms retinal target coordinates through head and body frames into reach vectors streamed to the premotor cortex, updated on-line against pulvinar and somatosensory feedback; high confidence, optic ataxia after damage is the proof.

◦ Held Grid: sustains location codes as delay-period activity over seconds; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A cue to attend, from frontal eye field and prefrontal broadcast - idle to Turning Beacon - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ A new target or task rule - Turning Beacon to Beacon Swung and back - roughly a quarter to half a second.

◦ An intended reach - any attentive mode to the Hand's Pilot - hundreds of milliseconds before movement onset.

◦ A delay imposed between seeing and acting - to the Held Grid - immediate, held for seconds.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Beacon Doused - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a Pilot running on stale coordinates shows as misreaching in peripheral vision, the optic-ataxia boundary.

◦ Watchdog: cerebellar forward-model correction by way of the corticopontine loop continuously audits the Pilot's output.


ENTRY 2.1.28. PARAHIPPOCAMPAL PLACE AREA (PPA) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.28)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The parahippocampal place area is a scene detector feeding navigation and context memory, and its modes are the directions the traffic runs: world in, memory out, and the nightly rebroadcast.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Scene-Perception Mode - the Threshold Reader - stimulus-driven recognition of places, layouts, and landmarks.

◦ Context-Encoding Mode - the Setting Stamp - tagging ongoing events with their spatial context for the hippocampus.

◦ Scene-Imagery Mode - the Remembered Room - top-down reconstruction of places during recall, imagination, and route planning.

◦ Idling Mode - the Doorway Watch - low-rate baseline with weak scene drive.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Rooms Revisited - slow-wave consolidation traffic with the hippocampal formation, and rapid-eye-movement (REM) scene imagery.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Threshold Reader: ventral-stream input is classified for scene identity and layout within roughly 150 to 250 milliseconds, cholinergic gain sharpening selectivity; high confidence.

◦ Setting Stamp: streams context codes to the entorhinal cortex and hippocampus, binding what to where; high confidence.

◦ Remembered Room: retrosplenial and hippocampal drive reactivates scene representations without a stimulus, the shared substrate of memory and imagination; high confidence.

◦ Rooms Revisited: participates in slow-oscillation-coupled replay of spatial context; moderate confidence for this area specifically.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Entry into or view of a scene - Doorway Watch to the Threshold Reader - under a quarter of a second.

◦ Ongoing event encoding, paced by hippocampal theta-coupled dialogue - Threshold Reader to the Setting Stamp - continuous during exploration.

◦ A recall or planning demand from the hippocampal-retrosplenial system - to the Remembered Room - seconds.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop, then the REM switch - waking modes to and through the Rooms Revisited - minutes, then 90-minute cycling.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a Threshold Reader that fails while faces and objects survive is the landmark-agnosia boundary, and the patient is lost in familiar streets.

◦ Watchdog: none notable; consistency is audited downstream by hippocampal match-mismatch comparison.


ENTRY 2.1.29. VISUAL WORD FORM AREA (VWFA, LEFT OCCIPITOTEMPORAL FUSIFORM) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.29)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The visual word form area is a detector built by cultural training on recycled object-recognition cortex, and its modes are reading regimes: automatic expert recognition, effortful decoding, and off-duty generic vision.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Expert Reading Mode - the Instant Scribe - fast, parallel, orientation-invariant recognition of practised letter strings.

◦ Effortful Decoding Mode - the Letter-by-Letter Lamp - serial, attention-hungry processing of unfamiliar words, scripts, or degraded print.

◦ Generic Object Mode - the Borrowed Bench - default fusiform object and shape processing when no text is present, the pre-literate tenancy this patch was recycled from.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Scribe at Rest - standard slow-wave and rapid-eye-movement (REM) cortical regimes, with slow consolidation of new orthographic learning.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Instant Scribe: maps letter strings of a learned script to word-form codes in roughly 150 to 200 milliseconds, invariant to case, font, and position, and streams them to the superior temporal and inferior frontal language cortex, with top-down phonological and semantic prediction sharpening the mapping; high confidence, this expertise account is standard.

◦ Letter-by-Letter Lamp: recruits serial attention from the dorsal system, throughput dropping from whole-word to roughly letter-rate; high confidence behaviourally, moderate on the circuit split.

◦ Borrowed Bench: responds as generic ventral shape cortex at lower selectivity; high confidence.

◦ Scribe at Rest: gated input and consolidation of the day's orthographic exposure; moderate confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Text entering fixation - Borrowed Bench to the Instant Scribe - roughly 150 milliseconds, effectively automatic in the literate adult.

◦ An unfamiliar or degraded string, flagged by recognition failure - Instant Scribe to the Letter-by-Letter Lamp - within a fixation or two.

◦ Practice over weeks to years, under cholinergic plasticity - Lamp traffic migrates to Scribe automaticity - a developmental transition, not a moment-to-moment one.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Scribe at Rest - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: loss of the Scribe with the Lamp intact is pure alexia, reading letter by letter while writing survives.

◦ Watchdog: none notable; the language network downstream simply rejects strings the Scribe mis-serves.


ENTRY 2.1.30. THE LATERAL OCCIPITAL COMPLEX AND EXTRASTRIATE BODY AREA (LOC, EBA) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.30)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This paired territory is detector cortex for shape and for bodies, and its modes are the standard sensory-cortex set: driven, imagined, adapted, and asleep.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Shape-Recognition Mode - the Contour Judge - stimulus-driven extraction of object shape by the lateral occipital complex.

◦ Body-Reading Mode - the Silhouette Clerk - recognition of body parts and postures by the extrastriate body area, feeding both social perception and one's own action system.

◦ Imagery Mode - the Inner Gallery - top-down reactivation of shape and body representations during visual imagery and action observation.

◦ Adapted Mode - the Tired Judge - repetition suppression, the reduced response to a repeated form that serves change detection and efficient coding.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Gallery Closed - standard slow-wave Up-Down and rapid-eye-movement (REM) regimes.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Contour Judge: builds cue-invariant shape from early visual and motion input within roughly 150 milliseconds, publishing to ventral temporal recognition areas; high confidence.

◦ Silhouette Clerk: selective response to bodies and parts, streamed to parietal and premotor cortex for action understanding; high confidence.

◦ Inner Gallery: frontal and parietal drive reactivates these detectors without a stimulus, at weaker amplitude than perception; high confidence.

◦ Tired Judge: response amplitude falls on repetition over seconds, recovering with novelty, a within-mode adaptation admitted here because the healthy system uses it for novelty signalling; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Stimulus onset - any waking mode to Contour Judge or Silhouette Clerk by content - roughly 100 to 150 milliseconds.

◦ An imagery or action-observation demand from frontoparietal cortex - to the Inner Gallery - seconds.

◦ Repetition of the same form - driven modes into the Tired Judge - over seconds, released by novelty.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop and REM switch - waking modes through the Gallery Closed - minutes, then 90-minute cycling.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: shape recognition failing while low-level vision survives marks the visual-form-agnosia boundary at this complex.

◦ Watchdog: none notable beyond standard cortical inhibitory balance.


ENTRY 2.1.31. SECONDARY SOMATOSENSORY CORTEX (S2, PARIETAL OPERCULUM) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.31)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The secondary somatosensory cortex is the higher touch integrator on the parietal operculum, and its modes are the jobs of expert touch: identifying by feel, attending or ignoring the skin, and forwarding what matters to feeling.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Haptic-Recognition Mode - the Reading Hand - active tactile exploration binding touch into recognised objects.

◦ Attentive-Touch Mode - the Listening Skin - raised gain on a monitored body region, bilateral fields allowing comparison across the two hands.

◦ Suppressed-Touch Mode - the Silenced Sleeve - attentional down-weighting of irrelevant tactile input, including the predicted touch of one's own movement.

◦ Affective-Relay Mode - the Feeling Ferry - weighting pain and pleasant-touch traffic onward to the insula.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Numb Watch - standard slow-wave and rapid-eye-movement (REM) regimes with strong tactile gating, salient stimuli still able to force waking.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Reading Hand: integrates primary somatosensory features across fingers and hands into object identity over seconds of exploration; high confidence, opercular damage yields tactile agnosia.

◦ Listening Skin: cholinergic and noradrenergic gain raises response to the attended site, with somatosensory rhythms over unattended skin marking suppression, the tactile analogue of visual alpha; high confidence.

◦ Silenced Sleeve: predicted self-touch is attenuated, which is why one cannot tickle oneself; high confidence.

◦ Feeling Ferry: scales nociceptive and affective-touch throughput to the insula with attention and expectation; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ An intent to identify by feel, from prefrontal and premotor drive - baseline to the Reading Hand - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ A cue to monitor a body site - to the Listening Skin - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Task load elsewhere, or a self-generated movement's forward-model prediction from the cerebellum - to the Silenced Sleeve - tens to hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Noxious or emotionally charged touch, with locus coeruleus alerting - any mode to a dominant Feeling Ferry - under a second.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Numb Watch - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a Ferry stuck at high gain is the sensitisation boundary toward chronic pain amplification.

◦ Watchdog: descending brainstem pain-modulation and predictive attenuation are the standing auditors of throughput.


ENTRY 2.1.32. THE AUDITORY BELT AND PARABELT (BRODMANN AREA 42 AND BEYOND) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.32)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The belt and parabelt are the hierarchical transform stages between tone and meaning, and their modes are listening regimes: open monitoring, selective extraction of one stream, speech-locked tracking, and the night watch.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Open-Monitoring Mode - the Wide Ear - broadband surveillance of the acoustic scene with novelty detection.

◦ Selective-Stream Mode - the Cocktail Ear - extraction of one voice or source from a mixture, the attended stream enhanced and the rest suppressed.

◦ Speech-Tracking Mode - the Syllable Loom - entrainment to the temporal envelope of speech for the language system.

◦ Music-and-Pattern Mode - the Melody Bench - analysis of pitch contour, harmony, and rhythm patterns.

◦ Sleep Sentinel Mode - the Ear That Never Sleeps - reduced but preserved processing during sleep, still able to flag salient sounds such as one's own name.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Wide Ear: builds spectrotemporal features from tonotopic core input, issuing mismatch responses to deviants within roughly 100 to 200 milliseconds; high confidence.

◦ Cocktail Ear: attentional gain reshapes responses until the attended talker dominates the population representation, under cholinergic and noradrenergic modulation; high confidence from electrocorticography.

◦ Syllable Loom: cortical oscillations entrain to the speech envelope in the delta and theta range, roughly 1 to 8 hertz, parsing the stream into syllable-scale chunks for the superior temporal language cortex; high confidence.

◦ Melody Bench: longer-window integration of pitch and rhythm patterns, right-weighted; moderate confidence on lateralisation strength.

◦ Ear That Never Sleeps: thalamic gating lowers throughput, yet evoked responses to salient or self-relevant sounds persist and can trigger arousal; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Attention to one source, directed by frontoparietal broadcast - Wide Ear to Cocktail Ear - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Speech onset in the attended stream - Cocktail Ear to the Syllable Loom - within a syllable or two.

◦ Musical structure engaging pattern analysis - to the Melody Bench - seconds.

◦ Sleep onset by the hypothalamic flip-flop with spindle-gated thalamic relay - waking modes to the Ear That Never Sleeps - minutes.

◦ A salient sound crossing the sentinel threshold - Ear That Never Sleeps to waking modes, by way of arousal-system broadcast - seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a Cocktail Ear that cannot separate streams is the hearing-in-noise failure boundary despite normal audiograms.

◦ Watchdog: the mismatch-response machinery is itself the resident watchdog, flagging deviance even when attention is elsewhere.


ENTRY 2.1.33. THE INSULA, ANTERIOR AND POSTERIOR DIVISIONS (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.1.33)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

Read with section 2.1.12, this division note registers the modes of the two-stage machine: a posterior primary interoceptive relay feeding an anterior comparator of feeling and salience, so its modes are postures of the whole body-reading pipeline.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ Quiet-Body Monitoring Mode - the Background Hum - low-salience interoceptive bookkeeping while attention faces the world.

◦ Interoceptive-Attention Mode - the Inward Ear - deliberate attention to heartbeat, breath, and gut, raising anterior readout of posterior maps.

◦ Salience-Alarm Mode - the Klaxon Desk - the anterior insula flagging a homeostatically or emotionally significant event and switching the large-scale networks.

◦ Pain-and-Threat Engagement Mode - the Hot Line - high-gain posterior nociceptive relay with strong anterior affective readout and autonomic output.

◦ Empathic-Simulation Mode - the Borrowed Ache - anterior representation of another's felt state during observed pain or emotion.

◦ Sleep Modes - the Hum at Night - continued visceral monitoring at reduced awareness, with slow-wave gating and rapid-eye-movement (REM) re-engagement of affective circuitry.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ Background Hum: posterior insula relays viscerosensory and spinothalamic traffic into primary interoceptive maps at low anterior gain; high confidence.

◦ Inward Ear: attention raises anterior gain on posterior maps, improving heartbeat detection measurably; high confidence.

◦ Klaxon Desk: the anterior insula and its large fast von Economo projection neurons broadcast a switch signal that suppresses the default mode network and engages executive control within roughly a second; high confidence for the network-switching role.

◦ Hot Line: nociceptive throughput and sympathetic output scale together, with norepinephrine and expectation setting gain, so the same injury can hurt more or less by mode; high confidence.

◦ Borrowed Ache: anterior affective circuits run without matched posterior sensory drive, which is why empathy shares the feeling but not the location of pain; high confidence.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A deliberate inward attention demand from prefrontal cortex - Background Hum to the Inward Ear - seconds.

◦ A deviant interoceptive or external salient event, with locus coeruleus phasic norepinephrine - any mode to the Klaxon Desk - under a second.

◦ Nociceptive barrage from the spinothalamic system - to the Hot Line - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Witnessing another's pain or emotion, delivered by the superior temporal social stream and amygdala - to the Borrowed Ache - seconds.

◦ The sleep-wake flip-flop - waking modes to the Hum at Night - minutes, with the Klaxon retaining waking authority for visceral emergencies.

◦ Inflammatory cytokine signalling during illness - the whole pipeline into a sickness-weighted configuration of raised interoceptive gain and lowered energetic set points - hours; admitted with a note, because the literature treats sickness behaviour as an adaptive programme.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: a Klaxon Desk that fires on benign body noise is the interoceptive miscalibration boundary toward panic and anxiety.

◦ Watchdog: reciprocal regulation by the ventromedial prefrontal cortex is the standing damper on anterior insular alarm.


ENTRY 2.2.1. THE HIPPOCAMPAL FORMATION (DENTATE GYRUS, CA3, CA2, CA1, SUBICULUM) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.1)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The hippocampus is the corpus's clearest example of a construct whose whole job configuration flips with brain state, alternating between a writing regime and a reading regime several times a second and again across the sleep-wake cycle.

Its mode is set almost entirely from outside, by the medial septal pacemaker and the ascending arousal and sleep systems.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ THETA-ENCODING mode - the Explorer's Weave - active exploration and attentive waking, when the structure writes new experience.

◦ SHARP-WAVE-RIPPLE mode - the Night Librarian - quiet rest and slow-wave sleep, when the structure replays and consolidates.

◦ rapid-eye-movement (REM)-THETA mode - the Dreamer's Reprise - rapid-eye-movement (REM) sleep theta, when circuits are re-run without new encoding.

◦ RETRIEVAL sub-phase within theta - the Reader's Half-Cycle - the opposite theta phase that reads stored patterns rather than writing them.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In THETA-ENCODING mode the local field runs at theta, roughly 4 to 8 hertz, paced from the medial septum, with nested gamma at 30 to 100 hertz; place cells phase-precess, converting a spatial sequence into a compressed firing sequence CA3 can learn, and encoding and retrieval segregate to opposite theta phases (well established).

◦ In SHARP-WAVE-RIPPLE mode CA3's recurrent network ignites brief, extraordinarily synchronous 140 to 250 hertz ripples (the manuscript gives 150 to 250 hertz), the most synchronous population events in the healthy brain, during which recent place-cell sequences replay compressed roughly twentyfold and sometimes in reverse (well established).

◦ In REM-THETA mode theta returns but the acetylcholine-high chemistry biases against consolidation, favouring circuit rehearsal (moderate confidence, mechanism still debated).

◦ In the RETRIEVAL sub-phase CA3 performs pattern completion, reconstructing a whole memory from a fragment through its autoassociative recurrent collaterals (well established).

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS confidence note: the theta-versus-ripple dichotomy is among the best-supported facts in systems neuroscience; suppressing ripples experimentally abolishes consolidation.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: onset of movement or attentive exploration, driven by cholinergic and pacemaker output of the medial septal nucleus and diagonal band by way of the fornix - from SHARP-WAVE-RIPPLE to THETA-ENCODING - within a second.

◦ Trigger: behavioural immobility, consummatory pause, or descent into slow-wave sleep, with falling acetylcholine - from THETA-ENCODING to SHARP-WAVE-RIPPLE - seconds.

◦ Trigger: the REM switch of the brainstem and the sleep-wake flip-flop - from slow-wave SHARP-WAVE-RIPPLE to REM-THETA - across the night in ninety-minute cycles.

◦ Trigger: ventral tegmental area dopamine novelty signal and amygdalar salience input, biasing whether the current experience is written at all - modulates depth of THETA-ENCODING - phasic, hundreds of milliseconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: the same recurrent CA3 ignition that makes a ripple can, if unchecked, recruit a hypersynchronous interictal spike or seizure; the mesial temporal lobe is the commonest origin of focal epilepsy.

◦ Warning condition: ripples growing into pathological fast ripples above 250 hertz signal epileptogenic tissue.

◦ Biological watchdog: feed-forward and feedback inhibition by parvalbumin and other interneurons, and cholinergic-tone gating, normally keep ignition confined to a genuine memory pattern.


ENTRY 2.2.2. THE AMYGDALA (LATERAL, BASAL, ACCESSORY BASAL, CENTRAL, MEDIAL, AND CORTICAL NUCLEI) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.2)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The amygdala is a threat-and-value machine that switches between a scanning vigilance regime, an acute defensive regime, and a learned safety regime, gated by prefrontal input.

Its modes are the discrete affective stances the whole nuclear cluster adopts, not the activity of any single nucleus.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ VIGILANCE-SCANNING mode - the Sentinel's Watch - baseline threat surveillance during ordinary waking.

◦ ACUTE-DEFENSIVE mode - the Alarm - a detected threat drives the central-nucleus output division to command freezing, sympathetic surge, and cortisol release.

◦ APPETITIVE-VALUE mode - the Appraiser - assignment of positive value to reward-predicting cues.

◦ SAFETY-EXTINCTION mode - the All-Clear - prefrontal excitation of the intercalated cell masses suppresses the fear response without erasing the memory.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In VIGILANCE-SCANNING mode the basolateral complex tonically weighs cortical and thalamic input, including the fast subcortical low road from the medial geniculate, biasing unconscious detection; basolateral activity to unconsciously presented fearful faces scales with trait anxiety (well established).

◦ In ACUTE-DEFENSIVE mode the central nucleus publishes to the periaqueductal grey for freezing, the lateral hypothalamus for sympathetic activation, the paraventricular nucleus for hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis cortisol, the locus coeruleus for arousal, and the nucleus basalis for cortical acetylcholine (well established).

◦ In APPETITIVE-VALUE mode basolateral neurons encode learned positive value passed to the nucleus accumbens and orbitofrontal cortex (well established).

◦ In SAFETY-EXTINCTION mode infralimbic and prelimbic prefrontal signalling drives the intercalated GABAergic masses, which inhibit the central nucleus and quench freezing in the presence of a safety cue (well established).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: a conditioned or innate threat cue reaching the lateral nucleus by low road (thalamo-amygdalar) or high road (cortical) - from VIGILANCE-SCANNING to ACUTE-DEFENSIVE - tens of milliseconds by the low road.

◦ Trigger: a learned safety cue, signalled by infralimbic cortex to the central amygdala - from ACUTE-DEFENSIVE to SAFETY-EXTINCTION - seconds to minutes with learning.

◦ Trigger: a reward-predicting cue with mesolimbic dopamine - from VIGILANCE-SCANNING to APPETITIVE-VALUE - phasic.

◦ Trigger: norepinephrine from the locus coeruleus and cortisol raising basolateral gain under stress - deepens VIGILANCE-SCANNING toward hair-trigger ACUTE-DEFENSIVE - minutes to hours.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: failure of prefrontal control of the intercalated masses leaves the central nucleus disinhibited, generalising fear to safe cues, the signature of anxiety and post-traumatic stress.

◦ Warning condition: sustained high basolateral gain with weak infralimbic drive predicts pathological overgeneralisation.

◦ Biological watchdog: the intercalated cell masses under prefrontal command are the built-in brake; their integrity is what distinguishes adaptive fear from disorder.


ENTRY 2.2.3. THE BED NUCLEUS OF THE STRIA TERMINALIS (BNST) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.3)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The bed nucleus of the stria terminalis is a slow-timescale anxiety integrator, the amygdala's slow cousin, and its modes are graded states of sustained apprehension rather than fast reflexes.

It handles the dread of a threat that might come, as distinct from a threat that is here now.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ QUIESCENT mode - the Calm Watch - low tonic output when the environment is predictable and safe.

◦ SUSTAINED-ANXIETY mode - the Long Dread - integration of diffuse, unpredictable, or distant threat into prolonged apprehension.

◦ STRESS-RECRUITED mode - the Braced State - corticotropin-releasing-hormone-driven engagement that couples anxiety to the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) stress axis.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In QUIESCENT mode the nucleus passes little drive to the paraventricular nucleus and periaqueductal grey, and the HPA axis idles (moderate to well established).

◦ In SUSTAINED-ANXIETY mode it integrates amygdalar, hippocampal, and prefrontal input over minutes to hours and sustains output to the paraventricular nucleus, periaqueductal grey, ventral tegmental area, and locus coeruleus, producing anxiety with no specific object (well established for unpredictable-threat paradigms).

◦ In STRESS-RECRUITED mode corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) and norepinephrine raise its gain and it drives the HPA axis, a set-point that can shift with chronic stress (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: an unpredictable or temporally distant threat, or contextual uncertainty from the hippocampus - from QUIESCENT to SUSTAINED-ANXIETY - seconds to minutes.

◦ Trigger: rising CRH and norepinephrine under stress - from SUSTAINED-ANXIETY to STRESS-RECRUITED - minutes.

◦ Trigger: restoration of predictable safety and falling stress hormones - back toward QUIESCENT - minutes to hours.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: chronic stress raises the CRH set-point (allostasis), locking the nucleus in SUSTAINED-ANXIETY and modelling generalised anxiety disorder, for which it is a principal research target.

◦ Warning condition: anxiety that persists with no identifiable object marks the maladaptive boundary.

◦ Biological watchdog: prefrontal and hippocampal contextual input that can reclassify the environment as safe is the chief off-switch.


ENTRY 2.2.4. THE BASAL GANGLIA (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.4)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The basal ganglia are a general-purpose action selector whose default state is inhibition, holding every possible action suppressed and selecting by releasing one, and their modes are the whole loop's operating regimes across motor, associative, and limbic channels.

The register below describes the modes of the entire cortex-striatum-pallidum-thalamus loop, not of its separate nuclei.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ HOLD mode - the Locked Gate - the tonic default in which internal-pallidal and reticular output inhibits the thalamus and no action is released.

◦ GO-SELECTION mode - the Chosen Channel - the direct pathway disinhibits one thalamic channel to permit a selected action.

◦ NO-GO-SUPPRESSION mode - the Global Brake - the indirect and hyperdirect pathways raise inhibition to withhold or arrest actions.

◦ GOAL-DIRECTED mode - the Deliberator - associative (dorsomedial, caudate) control by action-outcome value.

◦ HABITUAL mode - the Autopilot - sensorimotor (dorsolateral, putamen) stimulus-response control after overtraining.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In HOLD mode internal globus pallidus and substantia nigra pars reticulata tonically inhibit the ventral anterior and ventral lateral thalamus by way of the pallidothalamic tract (well established).

◦ In GO-SELECTION mode dopamine-1-receptor medium spiny neurons inhibit the inhibitor, disinhibiting a single thalamic channel; dopamine biases toward go (well established).

◦ In NO-GO-SUPPRESSION mode dopamine-2-receptor medium spiny neurons and the fast monosynaptic hyperdirect cortico-subthalamic projection raise global inhibition, the hold-your-horses signal (well established).

◦ In GOAL-DIRECTED mode the dorsomedial striatum with dorsolateral prefrontal cortex evaluates action-outcome contingency (well established).

◦ In HABITUAL mode the dorsolateral striatum with sensorimotor cortex runs cached stimulus-response policies; drug-seeking transfers from dorsomedial to dorsolateral control after extensive training (well established).

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS note: dopamine from the substantia nigra pars compacta is the switch that biases go against no-go and gates goal-directed-to-habitual transfer; it encodes reward prediction error.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: a cortical action proposal with sufficient dopaminergic go bias - from HOLD to GO-SELECTION - tens to hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Trigger: a stop signal or conflict reaching the subthalamic nucleus by the hyperdirect pathway - from GO-SELECTION to NO-GO-SUPPRESSION - tens of milliseconds.

◦ Trigger: overtraining with stable contingencies shifting dopamine-gated plasticity laterally - from GOAL-DIRECTED to HABITUAL - days to weeks.

◦ Trigger: contingency change or reward devaluation - from HABITUAL back to GOAL-DIRECTED - hours to days.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: too little output releases involuntary movement (Huntington's chorea, indirect pathway failing first); too much abolishes movement (parkinsonian akinesia after sixty to eighty percent of pars compacta neurons die); pathological beta oscillations arise in the external-pallidum-subthalamic loop.

◦ Warning condition: emergence of sustained beta-band synchrony, or loss of the go-no-go balance in either direction.

◦ Biological watchdog: dopaminergic reward-prediction-error signalling and the subthalamic hyperdirect brake normally keep selection crisp; deep brain stimulation of the subthalamic nucleus or internal pallidum is the clinical prosthesis when they fail.


ENTRY 2.2.5. THE NUCLEUS ACCUMBENS (SHELL AND CORE) AND THE VENTRAL PALLIDUM (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.5)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The nucleus accumbens is where wanting happens, the final common pathway of motivation, and its modes are the regimes by which incentive salience is assigned and translated into approach.

Shell and core carry different jobs, and the whole limbic loop's regime shifts with dopamine tone.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ UNCONDITIONED-REWARD mode - the First Taste - shell-mediated response to primary, unlearned reward.

◦ CUE-DRIVEN-SEEKING mode - the Chaser - core-mediated learned reward-seeking to conditioned cues.

◦ AVERSION-PAUSE mode - the Cold Shoulder - dopamine dips and shell circuits suppress approach to worse-than-expected outcomes.

◦ TONIC-MOTIVATION mode - the Idle Drive - baseline dopamine tone setting general vigour and willingness to work.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In UNCONDITIONED-REWARD mode the shell registers primary reward with phasic mesolimbic dopamine from the ventral tegmental area, distinguishing wanting from liking (well established).

◦ In CUE-DRIVEN-SEEKING mode the core, taking amygdalar value and hippocampal context, drives conditioned approach and relapse; it publishes to the ventral pallidum and thence mediodorsal thalamus and prefrontal cortex (well established).

◦ In AVERSION-PAUSE mode dopamine below baseline biases the loop against approach (moderate to well established).

◦ In TONIC-MOTIVATION mode slow ambient dopamine sets response vigour (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: delivery of an unexpected primary reward with a phasic dopamine burst - from TONIC-MOTIVATION to UNCONDITIONED-REWARD - hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Trigger: a learned reward-predicting cue - from TONIC-MOTIVATION to CUE-DRIVEN-SEEKING - phasic.

◦ Trigger: reward omission or punishment, dopamine dip driven by the lateral habenula - to AVERSION-PAUSE - phasic.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: every drug of abuse converges on the mesolimbic dopamine input; the core's cue-driven plasticity is the substrate of relapse.

◦ Warning condition: incentive salience captured by a single cue class at the expense of natural rewards marks addiction.

◦ Biological watchdog: prefrontal and orbitofrontal valuation input that can override cue-driven seeking is the chief regulator; its weakening is a hallmark of addiction.


ENTRY 2.2.6. THE CLAUSTRUM (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.6)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The claustrum is a thin cortex-wide sheet whose function is genuinely unresolved, so its mode register is offered provisionally and honestly.

It is modelled as a candidate global coherence hub whose regimes track the cortex-wide state it may help set.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ WAKING-BINDING mode - the Conductor - hypothesised binding of information across cortical modalities into a unified percept during attentive waking.

◦ SLOW-WAVE-SYNCHRONISING mode - the Metronome - hypothesised coordination of cortex-wide slow-wave activity in slow-wave sleep.

◦ IDLE mode - the Rest - low coordinated output between demands.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In WAKING-BINDING mode reciprocal projections to nearly every cortical region, which can equal or exceed the thalamic input to an area, may impose coherence across modalities (leading hypothesis, low to moderate confidence).

◦ In SLOW-WAVE-SYNCHRONISING mode the sheet may pace cortex-wide slow oscillations of roughly 0.5 to 4 hertz (hypothesis).

◦ In IDLE mode output is minimal (inferred).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: onset of attentive, multimodal waking driven by ascending arousal - from IDLE to WAKING-BINDING - subsecond (hypothesised).

◦ Trigger: descent into slow-wave sleep under the sleep-wake flip-flop - from WAKING-BINDING to SLOW-WAVE-SYNCHRONISING - minutes (hypothesised).

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: focal electrical stimulation of the claustrum reversibly abolished consciousness in one human patient, suggesting its disruption can collapse the integrated state, though this is a single case.

◦ Warning condition: none established, given the unresolved function.

◦ Biological watchdog: honestly, none is characterised; the construct is modelled provisionally pending evidence.


ENTRY 2.2.7. THE BASAL FOREBRAIN CHOLINERGIC COMPLEX (NUCLEUS BASALIS OF MEYNERT, MEDIAL SEPTAL NUCLEUS, DIAGONAL BAND OF BROCA) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.7)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The basal forebrain cholinergic complex is the brain's attention supply, a broadcast publisher of acetylcholine whose modes are the arousal regimes it imposes on cortex and hippocampus.

Its state is set from above by the sleep-wake and salience systems, and it in turn sets cortical mode.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ ATTENTIVE-BROADCAST mode - the Spotlight - high tonic and phasic acetylcholine enhancing signal-to-noise and gating plasticity during attentive waking.

◦ THETA-PACEMAKER mode - the Metronome - the medial septum and diagonal band pace the hippocampal theta rhythm during exploration and rapid-eye-movement sleep.

◦ QUIET-WAKE-and-SLOW-WAVE mode - the Dimmer - low acetylcholine that permits sharp-wave-ripple consolidation and cortical slow waves.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In ATTENTIVE-BROADCAST mode the nucleus basalis releases acetylcholine across the entire cortex by volume transmission, a broadcast not an address, raising signal-to-noise and telling cortex this is worth learning (well established).

◦ In THETA-PACEMAKER mode the medial septum and diagonal band drive hippocampal theta at 4 to 8 hertz by way of the fornix (well established).

◦ In QUIET-WAKE-and-SLOW-WAVE mode falling acetylcholine releases the hippocampus into ripple mode and cortex into slow oscillations (well established).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: salient or novel stimuli and amygdalar or orexinergic drive - from QUIET-WAKE to ATTENTIVE-BROADCAST - subsecond to seconds.

◦ Trigger: onset of exploration or rapid-eye-movement sleep - engages THETA-PACEMAKER - seconds.

◦ Trigger: descent into slow-wave sleep under the ventrolateral preoptic sleep switch - from ATTENTIVE-BROADCAST to QUIET-WAKE-and-SLOW-WAVE - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: degeneration of the nucleus basalis is an early and severe feature of Alzheimer's disease, collapsing the cortical acetylcholine broadcast.

◦ Warning condition: falling cortical cholinergic tone with attentional and memory decline.

◦ Biological watchdog: none intrinsic; the clinical prosthesis is the cholinesterase-inhibitor drug that props up a failing broadcast.


ENTRY 2.2.8. THE OLFACTORY BULB (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.8)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The olfactory bulb is the nose's piece of brain and the one sensory structure that does not report to the thalamus first, and its modes are respiration-locked processing regimes.

Its rhythm and gain are paced by sniffing and by acetylcholine and norepinephrine.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ RESTING-RESPIRATORY mode - the Slow Breath - passive odour sampling locked to quiet breathing.

◦ ACTIVE-SNIFFING mode - the Investigator - fast sniffing that raises sampling rate and sharpens discrimination.

◦ STATE-MODULATED-GAIN mode - the Tuned Nose - arousal and hunger raising sensitivity to salient odours.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In RESTING-RESPIRATORY mode glomerular and mitral-cell activity tracks the theta-band respiratory cycle, roughly 2 to 4 hertz at rest, transforming receptor input into an odour code sent directly to piriform cortex and amygdala (well established).

◦ In ACTIVE-SNIFFING mode sniff frequency rises to 4 to 12 hertz and gamma-band (40 to 100 hertz) synchrony sharpens odour separation (well established).

◦ In STATE-MODULATED-GAIN mode cholinergic and noradrenergic input adjusts mitral-granule inhibition, raising sensitivity to food or danger odours feeding appetite and threat drives (moderate to well established).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: a detected or sought odour prompting exploratory sniffing - from RESTING-RESPIRATORY to ACTIVE-SNIFFING - within one to two breaths.

◦ Trigger: arousal or hunger raising acetylcholine and norepinephrine - engages STATE-MODULATED-GAIN - seconds to minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: the bulb is a common early site of pathology, with anosmia an early sign in Parkinson's and Alzheimer's disease; it is also seizure-prone.

◦ Warning condition: loss of odour discrimination or emergent hypersynchrony.

◦ Biological watchdog: dense granule-cell inhibition and ongoing adult neurogenesis maintain the code.


ENTRY 2.2.9. THE PIRIFORM CORTEX (PRIMARY OLFACTORY CORTEX) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.9)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The piriform cortex forms odour objects, an auto-associative attractor network of the same family as hippocampal CA3, and its modes are encoding versus recall regimes gated by acetylcholine.

It receives its input from the bulb without a thalamic relay.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ ODOUR-BINDING mode - the Assembler - binding a combinatorial glomerular code into a single recognisable smell.

◦ PATTERN-COMPLETION mode - the Rememberer - completing a partial or noisy input to a stored odour object, a content-addressable memory for scent.

◦ ENCODING mode - the Scribe - acetylcholine-gated learning of a new odour.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In ODOUR-BINDING mode dense recurrent associative connections settle activity into an odour-object assembly (well established).

◦ In PATTERN-COMPLETION mode the recurrent attractor reconstructs a whole odour from a fragment (well established).

◦ In ENCODING mode acetylcholine biases the circuit toward storing rather than recalling, strengthening recurrent weights (moderate to well established).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: arrival of a familiar odour code from the bulb - from ENCODING to PATTERN-COMPLETION or ODOUR-BINDING - subsecond.

◦ Trigger: a novel odour with high acetylcholine from the basal forebrain - engages ENCODING - seconds.

◦ Trigger: falling cholinergic tone in quiet or sleep states - biases recall over encoding.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: the piriform's recurrent excitation makes it strongly epileptogenic, a classic kindling site.

◦ Warning condition: runaway recurrent excitation escaping inhibitory control.

◦ Biological watchdog: local feedback inhibition and cholinergic gating keep attractor dynamics confined to genuine odour objects.


ENTRY 2.2.10. THE OLFACTORY TUBERCLE (OT) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.10)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The olfactory tubercle is a hybrid of striatum and olfactory cortex that ties smell to reward, and its modes are the regimes by which odour is granted or denied incentive value.

Its state is set by dopamine tone.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ ODOUR-APPROACH mode - the Beckoner - gating approach to a rewarding smell.

◦ REWARD-LEARNING mode - the Valuer - dopamine-gated updating of an odour's incentive value.

◦ NEUTRAL-SAMPLING mode - the Bystander - low incentive output to a valueless odour.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In ODOUR-APPROACH mode ventral-striatal circuitry converts a valued odour into motivated approach, publishing to the ventral pallidum (moderate to well established).

◦ In REWARD-LEARNING mode phasic dopamine from the ventral tegmental area and substantia nigra updates the odour-value mapping (moderate confidence).

◦ In NEUTRAL-SAMPLING mode output stays low absent value (inferred).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: a food or otherwise rewarding odour with dopamine - from NEUTRAL-SAMPLING to ODOUR-APPROACH - subsecond to seconds.

◦ Trigger: pairing of an odour with reward - engages REWARD-LEARNING - trials.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: as a ventral-striatal node it can participate in maladaptive odour-cued seeking, though this is less characterised than the accumbens.

◦ Warning condition: none prominently established.

◦ Biological watchdog: shared with the wider ventral striatum, prefrontal valuation restrains cue-driven approach.


ENTRY 2.2.11. THE ANTERIOR OLFACTORY NUCLEUS (AON) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.11)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The anterior olfactory nucleus is the first associative olfactory relay and the bridge that shares smell between hemispheres, and its modes are state-dependent gating regimes.

Its gain is set by acetylcholine and norepinephrine and by brain state.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ INTERHEMISPHERIC-SHARING mode - the Bridge - relaying odour to the contralateral bulb by the anterior commissure.

◦ ASSOCIATIVE-INTEGRATION mode - the Weaver - integrating odour with olfactory context for piriform and entorhinal cortex.

◦ STATE-GATING mode - the Doorkeeper - raising or lowering olfactory throughput with arousal and sleep.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In INTERHEMISPHERIC-SHARING mode it copies bulbar output across the anterior commissure, giving both hemispheres a shared odour picture (moderate to well established).

◦ In ASSOCIATIVE-INTEGRATION mode it binds odour with context for downstream olfactory cortex (moderate confidence).

◦ In STATE-GATING mode cholinergic and noradrenergic tone raises throughput in waking and lowers it in sleep (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: waking arousal raising acetylcholine and norepinephrine - toward STATE-GATING open and ASSOCIATIVE-INTEGRATION - seconds.

◦ Trigger: sleep lowering neuromodulatory tone - narrows STATE-GATING - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: none prominently characterised as a used-versus-fault boundary.

◦ Warning condition: none established.

◦ Biological watchdog: standard olfactory inhibitory and neuromodulatory control; one honest sentence suffices here.


ENTRY 2.2.12. THE ENDOPIRIFORM NUCLEUS (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.12)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The endopiriform nucleus is a deep neighbour of the claustrum that binds olfactory and limbic information, and it is highly epileptogenic, so its modes sit close to a fault boundary.

Its regimes are integration states.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ OLFACTORY-LIMBIC-BINDING mode - the Knitter - integrating smell with limbic context into a bound percept.

◦ RECOGNITION-MEMORY mode - the Recogniser - contributing to recognition memory for odour and context.

◦ QUIESCENT mode - the Rest - low output between demands.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In OLFACTORY-LIMBIC-BINDING mode it fuses piriform and limbic-cortical input, publishing to ventral and limbic cortices and the ventral hippocampus (moderate confidence).

◦ In RECOGNITION-MEMORY mode it acts as a recognition-memory node (moderate confidence).

◦ In QUIESCENT mode output is minimal (inferred).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: an odour arriving in a salient context - from QUIESCENT to OLFACTORY-LIMBIC-BINDING - subsecond.

◦ Trigger: a familiar odour-context pair - engages RECOGNITION-MEMORY - subsecond.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: the nucleus is among the most epileptogenic in the forebrain and can seed seizures when its recurrent excitation escapes control.

◦ Warning condition: emergent hypersynchrony spreading to piriform and limbic cortex.

◦ Biological watchdog: local inhibition normally confines its integrative bursts; its low threshold makes that watchdog critical.


ENTRY 2.2.13. THE TAENIA TECTA (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.13)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The taenia tecta is an anterior olfactory-limbic cortex continuous with the hippocampal formation, and its modes are simple relay-integration regimes at the front of the limbic system.

It is a lightly characterised construct, so its register is compact and honest.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ OLFACTORY-LIMBIC-RELAY mode - the Forwarder - relaying smell into the limbic continuum.

◦ INTEGRATIVE mode - the Blender - combining olfactory and limbic signals.

◦ QUIESCENT mode - the Rest - baseline low output.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In OLFACTORY-LIMBIC-RELAY mode it forwards bulbar and anterior-olfactory-nucleus input to olfactory-limbic and hippocampal-continuation regions (low to moderate confidence).

◦ In INTEGRATIVE mode it blends olfactory with limbic activation (low confidence).

◦ In QUIESCENT mode output is minimal (inferred).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: an odour in limbic context - from QUIESCENT to OLFACTORY-LIMBIC-RELAY - subsecond.

◦ Trigger: convergent limbic drive - engages INTEGRATIVE - subsecond.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: none distinctly characterised for this small construct.

◦ Warning condition: none established.

◦ Biological watchdog: standard cortical inhibition; one honest sentence suffices.


ENTRY 2.2.14. THE ISLANDS OF CALLEJA (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.14)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The islands of Calleja are clusters of tiny dopamine-sensitive granule cells in the ventral striatum tied to grooming and mood, and their modes are dopaminergic bias states over repetitive behaviour.

Their state follows dopamine and serotonin tone.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ GROOMING-PROMOTING mode - the Preener - biasing toward self-grooming and repetitive motivated behaviour.

◦ MOOD-MODULATING mode - the Tuner - shifting affective state with local neuromodulator tone.

◦ BASELINE mode - the Rest - low modulatory output.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In GROOMING-PROMOTING mode dopaminergic input from the ventral tegmental area and substantia nigra biases local ventral-striatal circuits toward repetitive action (low to moderate confidence).

◦ In MOOD-MODULATING mode cholinergic and serotonergic input shifts affect (low confidence).

◦ In BASELINE mode output is minimal (inferred).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: rising dopamine tone - from BASELINE to GROOMING-PROMOTING - seconds to minutes.

◦ Trigger: shifting serotonergic tone - engages MOOD-MODULATING - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: implicated in excessive repetitive or compulsive-like behaviour when dopaminergic bias is excessive, though the literature is thin.

◦ Warning condition: escalation of repetitive behaviour beyond normal grooming.

◦ Biological watchdog: wider ventral-striatal regulation; characterisation is preliminary.


ENTRY 2.2.15. THE LATERAL SEPTAL NUCLEUS (LS) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.2.15)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The lateral septal nucleus carries the hippocampus's word to the hypothalamus, joining memory and space to emotion and drive, and its modes are the regimes by which it routes that integrated signal to drive centres.

Its gain is set by serotonin, dopamine, vasopressin, and oxytocin.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ SOCIAL-REGULATION mode - the Diplomat - shaping social behaviour by hippocampal and amygdalar context.

◦ STRESS-MODULATION mode - the Buffer - relaying limbic signals that temper or amplify the stress response.

◦ FEEDING-and-REWARD mode - the Provisioner - gating feeding and reward through accumbens and hypothalamic targets.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In SOCIAL-REGULATION mode it integrates hippocampal spatial and amygdalar emotional input and publishes to preoptic, paraventricular, and lateral hypothalamic areas, with vasopressin and oxytocin shaping social output (moderate confidence).

◦ In STRESS-MODULATION mode it relays limbic context to the paraventricular nucleus and bed nucleus of the stria terminalis, tuning the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis (moderate confidence).

◦ In FEEDING-and-REWARD mode it gates feeding and reward-seeking through the nucleus accumbens and lateral hypothalamus (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: a social encounter with hippocampal context - engages SOCIAL-REGULATION - seconds.

◦ Trigger: a stressor signalled from the amygdala - engages STRESS-MODULATION - seconds.

◦ Trigger: hunger or reward cues - engages FEEDING-and-REWARD - seconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: dysregulation is linked to anxiety and aggression phenotypes, but no sharp used-versus-fault line is drawn.

◦ Warning condition: none crisply established.

◦ Biological watchdog: serotonergic tone is a principal restraint on its aggression- and anxiety-related output.


ENTRY 2.3.1. THE THALAMUS (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.3.1)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The thalamus is the gateway to consciousness, and calling it a relay is an understatement, since corticothalamic feedback outnumbers the forward projection by roughly ten to one; its modes are whole-organ regimes that flip with the sleep-wake state.

The register below describes the modes of the entire thalamus, including its reticular shell, not of individual nuclei.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ TONIC-RELAY mode - the Faithful Gate - waking and rapid-eye-movement (REM) sleep, when relay cells transmit driver input linearly to cortex.

◦ BURST-ALERT mode - the Startle - a low-threshold calcium burst that flags a stimulus after silence and can trigger arousal.

◦ SPINDLE-GATED mode - the Sleep Loom - non-REM sleep, when reticular-relay loops generate sleep spindles and disconnect cortex from the world.

◦ ATTENTIONAL-SELECTION mode - the Guarded Gate - reticular inhibition, biased by prefrontal cortex, passes relevant channels and suppresses distractors.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In TONIC-RELAY mode depolarised relay cells fire in a graded, faithful manner; in waking under one percent of spikes are bursts, and the lateral geniculate transmits retinal input to primary visual cortex with high fidelity (well established).

◦ In BURST-ALERT mode a hyperpolarised relay cell de-inactivates its T-type calcium channel and fires a high-frequency burst; centromedian and centromedial tonic firing triggers rapid arousal while burst firing triggers brain-wide slow waves (well established).

◦ In SPINDLE-GATED mode reciprocal reticular-relay interaction generates 7 to 15 hertz spindles lasting about one second, coupled to hippocampal ripples and cortical slow oscillations to transfer memory; bursting rises to roughly eighteen percent of spikes in sleep (well established).

◦ In ATTENTIONAL-SELECTION mode the prefrontal cortex drives the thalamic reticular nucleus to inhibit the relay of distracting inputs, the anatomical mechanism of top-down attention (well established).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: the ascending arousal broadcast (acetylcholine from brainstem and basal forebrain, norepinephrine, histamine from the tuberomammillary nucleus, orexin) depolarising relay cells - from SPINDLE-GATED or BURST-ALERT to TONIC-RELAY - subsecond to seconds.

◦ Trigger: withdrawal of that arousal broadcast under the ventrolateral preoptic sleep switch, hyperpolarising relay cells - from TONIC-RELAY to SPINDLE-GATED - minutes.

◦ Trigger: a salient stimulus after a silent period - a single BURST-ALERT event within TONIC-RELAY - tens of milliseconds.

◦ Trigger: prefrontal task demand recruiting the reticular nucleus - engages ATTENTIONAL-SELECTION within TONIC-RELAY - hundreds of milliseconds.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: the same reticular-relay burst machinery that makes spindles can, if hypersynchronised, produce the three-hertz spike-and-wave of absence epilepsy; reticular dysfunction is implicated in the attentional deficits of schizophrenia.

◦ Warning condition: spindle machinery generalising into rhythmic three-hertz spike-and-wave.

◦ Biological watchdog: acetylcholine switching the reticular nucleus from spindle mode to relay-gating mode, and balanced reticular inhibition, normally keep bursting confined to its useful roles.


ENTRY 2.3.2. THE HYPOTHALAMUS (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.3.2)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The hypothalamus is an almond-sized structure that runs the body and is the physical location of the neuroendocrine interface, and it is the mode-richest construct in the corpus, running many discrete behavioural-state programmes that compete for control.

The register describes the whole hypothalamus as a programme selector, since its nuclei run largely mutually exclusive drive programmes.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ FEEDING programme - the Forager - arcuate hunger and satiety signals and lateral-hypothalamic drive orchestrate seeking and consuming.

◦ DEFENCE programme - the Guard - medial and ventromedial circuits command fight, flight, or freeze, prioritised over feeding when both are cued.

◦ THERMOREGULATION programme - the Thermostat - preoptic circuits defend core temperature.

◦ FLUID-BALANCE programme - the Water-Keeper - osmoreceptor-driven vasopressin release and thirst.

◦ SLEEP-WAKE programme - the Flip-Flop - the ventrolateral preoptic sleep switch against the orexin, histamine, and arousal nuclei.

◦ STRESS programme - the Mobiliser - paraventricular corticotropin-releasing-hormone drive of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis.

◦ REPRODUCTIVE and SOCIAL programme - the Progenitor - preoptic and medial circuits governing mating, parenting, and hormonal cycles.

◦ CIRCADIAN-TIMEKEEPING programme - the Clock - the suprachiasmatic master oscillator setting day against night for every other programme.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In the FEEDING programme the arcuate reads leptin, ghrelin, insulin, and glucose directly from the blood and its opposed AgRP/NPY (hunger) and POMC (satiety) populations drive the lateral hypothalamic feeding centre (well established).

◦ In the DEFENCE programme medial hypothalamic and ventromedial circuits command autonomic and behavioural defence, and lateral-hypothalamic glutamatergic input to the ventral tegmental area prioritises innate defence over feeding (well established).

◦ In the THERMOREGULATION programme preoptic thermosensitive neurons drive sweating, shivering, and vasomotor responses to defend a set-point near 37 degrees Celsius (well established).

◦ In the FLUID-BALANCE programme supraoptic and paraventricular osmoreceptors publish vasopressin when plasma osmolality rises above set-point (well established).

◦ In the SLEEP-WAKE programme the ventrolateral preoptic nucleus and the arousal nuclei form a mutually inhibitory bistable flip-flop, stabilised by lateral-hypothalamic orexin (well established).

◦ In the STRESS programme the paraventricular nucleus publishes corticotropin-releasing hormone to the anterior pituitary, driving cortisol (well established).

◦ In the REPRODUCTIVE and SOCIAL programme gonadotropin-releasing-hormone and oxytocin and vasopressin circuits govern cyclic mating and parenting behaviour (well established).

◦ In the CIRCADIAN programme the suprachiasmatic nucleus, entrained by the retinohypothalamic tract, paces roughly 24-hour rhythms and gates the pineal melatonin output (well established).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: falling energy signals (low leptin, high ghrelin) - engages the FEEDING programme - minutes to hours.

◦ Trigger: an imminent threat cue from the amygdala - preempts FEEDING with the DEFENCE programme - subsecond to seconds.

◦ Trigger: core-temperature deviation sensed by preoptic neurons - engages THERMOREGULATION - seconds to minutes.

◦ Trigger: rising plasma osmolality - engages the FLUID-BALANCE programme - seconds to minutes.

◦ Trigger: rising homeostatic sleep pressure and the circadian dusk signal - flips SLEEP-WAKE toward sleep by the ventrolateral preoptic switch - minutes.

◦ Trigger: a stressor from the amygdala and bed nucleus of the stria terminalis - engages the STRESS programme - seconds.

◦ Trigger: light and dark on the retinohypothalamic tract - resets the CIRCADIAN programme, which biases all the others - across the day.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: loss of lateral-hypothalamic orexin neurons destabilises the sleep-wake flip-flop and produces narcolepsy with cataplexy; chronic stress shifts the corticotropin-releasing-hormone set-point (allostasis); defended-weight set-point shifts underlie obesity.

◦ Warning condition: inappropriate switching between behavioural states, or a set-point that drifts and stays drifted.

◦ Biological watchdog: orexin adds hysteresis that prevents unwanted state flips, and the circumventricular sensors continually recalibrate each programme against the blood.


ENTRY 2.3.3. THE HABENULA (LATERAL AND MEDIAL) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.3.3)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The habenula is the disappointment centre, firing when an expected reward fails to arrive and punishing the brain's reward systems accordingly, and its modes are the regimes by which it delivers negative feedback to the monoamine systems.

The lateral habenula is the coordinating node of negative monoamine feedback, downregulating dopamine and serotonin together.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ REWARD-EXPECTANT mode - the Hopeful Quiet - low firing while an expected reward is still pending.

◦ OMISSION-PUNISHMENT mode - the Letdown - a burst on reward omission, punishment, or worse-than-expected outcome that suppresses the monoamine systems.

◦ STRESS-TRANSFORMED mode - the Soured Signal - under stress, formerly rewarding events produce punishment-like output.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In REWARD-EXPECTANT mode lateral-habenula firing stays low, permitting dopamine and serotonin tone (well established).

◦ In OMISSION-PUNISHMENT mode the lateral habenula, driven by the internal globus pallidus by way of the stria medullaris, fires and, through the fasciculus retroflexus and the rostromedial tegmental nucleus, inhibits ventral tegmental dopamine neurons and raphe serotonin neurons, encoding negative reward prediction error (well established).

◦ In STRESS-TRANSFORMED mode acute stress transforms reward responses into punishment-like signals and amplifies omission responses (well established, rodent).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: reward omission, punishment, or a negative prediction error - from REWARD-EXPECTANT to OMISSION-PUNISHMENT - phasic, tens to hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Trigger: return of expected or better-than-expected outcomes - back to REWARD-EXPECTANT - phasic.

◦ Trigger: acute or chronic stress - biases toward STRESS-TRANSFORMED - minutes to days.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: chronic lateral-habenula hyperactivity is a well-replicated correlate of depression, producing anhedonia and behavioural despair, and it is an emerging target for deep brain stimulation in treatment-resistant depression.

◦ Warning condition: sustained bursting and elevated tonic firing that clamps down both monoamine systems.

◦ Biological watchdog: rostromedial-tegmental gain control and prefrontal regulation normally keep the negative-feedback burst phasic and outcome-locked rather than persistent.


ENTRY 2.3.4. THE PINEAL GLAND (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.3.4)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The pineal gland transduces the neural circadian signal into an endocrine one, and its modes are simply day and night, since it is a clock-driven hormone transducer whose upward interface is entirely endocrine.

Its single output is melatonin, the hormone of darkness.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ DAY-SILENT mode - the Daylight Rest - suppressed melatonin synthesis during subjective day.

◦ NIGHT-SECRETING mode - the Dark Announcer - nocturnal melatonin release announcing that night has fallen.

◦ DAWN-SUPPRESSION mode - the Reveille - light-triggered acute shutdown of secretion at dawn.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In DAY-SILENT mode the suprachiasmatic clock holds the sympathetic drive off, so norepinephrine release and the enzyme arylalkylamine-N-acetyltransferase stay low and melatonin output is minimal (well established).

◦ In NIGHT-SECRETING mode the clock releases sympathetic norepinephrine from superior-cervical-ganglion fibres onto pinealocytes, raising arylalkylamine-N-acetyltransferase activity and converting serotonin to melatonin, released into blood and cerebrospinal fluid (well established).

◦ In DAWN-SUPPRESSION mode light reaching the suprachiasmatic nucleus by the retinohypothalamic tract acutely withdraws the sympathetic drive and shuts synthesis off (well established).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: the suprachiasmatic dusk signal by way of the paraventricular nucleus, intermediolateral column, and superior cervical ganglion - from DAY-SILENT to NIGHT-SECRETING - over the evening.

◦ Trigger: morning light acutely suppressing the clock's sympathetic output - from NIGHT-SECRETING through DAWN-SUPPRESSION to DAY-SILENT - minutes.

◦ Trigger: nocturnal artificial light - abnormally forces DAWN-SUPPRESSION at night.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: mistimed light (shift work, screens at night) suppresses night melatonin and desynchronises the circadian system, a used mechanism driven out of its normal range rather than an intrinsic fault.

◦ Warning condition: melatonin secretion that no longer tracks the dark phase.

◦ Biological watchdog: the suprachiasmatic clock itself, entrained daily by light, is the sole timekeeper that keeps the transducer on schedule.


ENTRY 2.3.5. THE SUBTHALAMUS AND THE ZONA INCERTA (ZI) (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.3.5)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The zona incerta is an under-named integrative sheet beneath the thalamus that gates arousal, attention, and movement, and its modes are gain-setting regimes over thalamocortical and behavioural circuits.

It acts largely by inhibitory gain modulation of many downstream targets.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ AROUSAL-GATING mode - the Dimmer - setting the gain of higher-order and paraventricular thalamic transmission with brain state.

◦ DEFENCE-MODULATION mode - the Restraint - rostral inhibitory neurons scaling innate flight and freezing.

◦ FEEDING-and-CONSUMMATORY mode - the Provisioner - driving feeding and consummatory behaviour.

◦ SLEEP-PROMOTING mode - the Sandman - contributing to sleep induction and non-rapid-eye-movement (non-REM) state.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In AROUSAL-GATING mode it sets the gain of thalamocortical and thalamostriatal transmission, integrating cortical, basal-ganglia, brainstem, and sensory input (moderate to well established).

◦ In DEFENCE-MODULATION mode GABAergic rostral zona-incerta neurons innervate the periaqueductal grey, and their activation reduces flight and freezing by gain modulation while suppression enhances them; activity rises during fear extinction under prefrontal drive (well established, rodent).

◦ In FEEDING-and-CONSUMMATORY mode zona-incerta populations drive feeding and other consummatory behaviour (moderate confidence).

◦ In SLEEP-PROMOTING mode subpopulations contribute to sleep induction (moderate confidence).

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Trigger: shifting arousal and attentional demand by way of cortical and brainstem input - into or out of AROUSAL-GATING - subsecond to seconds.

◦ Trigger: a threat stimulus, with medial-prefrontal drive during extinction - engages DEFENCE-MODULATION - subsecond to trials.

◦ Trigger: hunger cues - engages FEEDING-and-CONSUMMATORY - seconds.

◦ Trigger: rising sleep pressure - engages SLEEP-PROMOTING - minutes.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Boundary signature: as a deep-brain-stimulation-adjacent target near the subthalamic nucleus, aberrant zona-incerta activity can contribute to movement and arousal dysregulation, though it is thinly characterised as a fault.

◦ Warning condition: loss of balanced inhibitory gain over its many targets.

◦ Biological watchdog: prefrontal input that drives its defence-modulating and extinction-related activity is a principal regulator; overall this construct's fault map is not yet well drawn.

ENTRY 2.4.1. THE MIDBRAIN (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.4.1)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The midbrain is a composite construct whose whole-structure mode is set by the animal's overall behavioural state, because its members (the superior and inferior colliculi, the periaqueductal grey, the ventral tegmental area, the red nucleus, and the tegmental gaze and locomotor nuclei) share one governing question at any instant: orient calmly, defend against threat, or seek reward.

The register below is the register of the WHOLE midbrain, not of every nucleus separately.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ ORIENTING mode - the Scout's Sweep - the colliculi run their sight-sound-touch maps in register and throw the eyes and head toward whatever just changed.

◦ DEFENCE mode - the Cornered Animal - the periaqueductal grey seizes control and commits the body to freeze, flight, or fight while its own descending analgesia silences pain.

◦ REWARD-SEEKING mode - the Prospector - the ventral tegmental area's dopamine neurons drive approach and stamp in the actions that led toward reward.

◦ QUIESCENT-SLEEP mode - the Banked Fire - collicular orienting and dopaminergic phasic bursting fall away as the sleep switch closes down arousal.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In ORIENTING mode the superior colliculus computes a spatial error vector and issues a saccade plus a tectospinal head turn within roughly one hundred to two hundred milliseconds; the inferior colliculus keeps relaying binaural cues throughout, confidence high.

◦ In DEFENCE mode the periaqueductal grey publishes to the rostral ventromedial medulla and gates the first synapse of the pain pathway in the dorsal horn, producing analgesia deep enough for surgery under stimulation, and it commands a species-typical freeze or escape; confidence high, this is the best-characterised descending analgesia circuit in the brain.

◦ In REWARD-SEEKING mode dopamine speaks in two tenses, a fast phasic burst (a spike above baseline over tens of milliseconds) carrying reward prediction error and a slow tonic level tracking ongoing situational value; the lateral population instead fires for salience of any important event, aversive or appetitive, confidence high.

◦ In QUIESCENT-SLEEP mode phasic dopamine and collicular saccade generation are suppressed, though the cuneiform and mesencephalic locomotor region can still be recruited for an urgent arousal-driven escape.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A sudden salient event - ORIENTING to DEFENCE - hundreds of milliseconds, triggered by the amygdala's central nucleus commanding the periaqueductal grey.

◦ A better-than-expected outcome - any mode to REWARD-SEEKING - tens of milliseconds, triggered by a ventral tegmental area dopamine burst, itself disinhibited when the rostromedial tegmental nucleus brake releases.

◦ A worse-than-expected outcome - REWARD-SEEKING to a dopamine pause - tens of milliseconds, triggered by the lateral habenula driving the rostromedial tegmental nucleus.

◦ Sleep onset - waking modes to QUIESCENT-SLEEP - minutes, triggered by the ventrolateral preoptic nucleus inhibiting the ascending arousal broadcasters.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ A pathological dopaminergic drift into compulsive seeking is the addiction boundary signature, since every drug of abuse raises accumbens dopamine; the healthy watchdog is the lateral habenula to rostromedial tegmental nucleus brake, and its failure belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.4.2. THE PONS (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.4.2)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The pons is a composite construct that is simultaneously a broadcasting station (the locus coeruleus and raphe) and the great relay bridge from cortex to cerebellum, so its whole-structure mode tracks the wake-sleep axis while its relay members run continuously beneath.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ AROUSED-WAKING mode - the Ringing Bell - the locus coeruleus fires tonically and phasically, flooding the brain with norepinephrine (a monoamine transmitter of alertness).

◦ NON-rapid-eye-movement (REM)-SLEEP mode - the Dimmed Hall - aminergic firing falls and the pontine relays quieten while slow rhythms take the cortex.

◦ REM-SLEEP mode - the Silent Dreamer - the locus coeruleus and raphe go essentially silent while the cholinergic pedunculopontine and laterodorsal tegmental nuclei fire and launch the ponto-geniculo-occipital waves of dreaming.

◦ RELAY mode (state-independent) - the Great Bridge - the pontine nuclei pass twenty million corticopontine fibres onward to the cerebellum irrespective of arousal state.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In AROUSED-WAKING mode the locus coeruleus sets overall gain by tonic firing and marks surprise by phasic bursts; noradrenaline, serotonin, and histamine tone are all high, confidence high per sleep-wake recordings.

◦ In NON-REM-SLEEP mode aminergic output is low and the parabrachial and vestibular relays idle, confidence high.

◦ In REM-SLEEP mode the aminergic broadcasters are near-silent (the defining aminergic-off state) while acetylcholine drives cortical activation and ponto-geniculo-occipital waves, confidence high.

◦ In RELAY mode the pontine nuclei, superior olive, vestibular nuclei, and nucleus prepositus hypoglossi run their fixed transforms (cortex-to-cerebellum mossy fibres, binaural comparison, gaze integration) continuously.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Waking to sleep - AROUSED-WAKING to NON-REM-SLEEP - minutes, triggered by the sleep-wake flip-flop as the ventrolateral preoptic nucleus inhibits the locus coeruleus.

◦ Deep sleep to dreaming - NON-REM-SLEEP to REM-SLEEP - a rapid flip every ninety minutes or so, triggered by REM-on cholinergic cells overcoming REM-off aminergic cells, orexin stabilising the switch.

◦ A startling stimulus - any state to AROUSED-WAKING - well under a second, triggered by a locus coeruleus phasic burst that gates the awakening.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Early degeneration of the locus coeruleus is possibly the first detectable lesion of both Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease, so a progressive loss of arousal gain is the boundary signature; there is no internal watchdog, and this belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.4.3. THE MEDULLA (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.4.3)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The medulla is the autonomic and respiratory engine room, so its whole-structure mode is the autonomic balance it is currently defending, overlaid on an ever-present respiratory oscillator that must never stop.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ REST-AND-DIGEST mode - the Quiet Housekeeper - the vagal complex (nucleus tractus solitarius and dorsal motor nucleus) drives parasympathetic outflow to heart and gut.

◦ FIGHT-OR-FLIGHT mode - the Alarm Driver - the rostral ventrolateral medulla drives sympathetic outflow, raising blood pressure and heart rate.

◦ PROTECTIVE-REFLEX mode - the Bouncer - the area postrema and reticular vomiting, cough, and swallow centres seize control to expel or guard against a threat.

◦ RESPIRATORY-RHYTHM mode (always on) - the Metronome - the pre-Botzinger complex generates the breath, its rate reset by state.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In REST-AND-DIGEST mode the nucleus tractus solitarius relays baroreceptor, chemoreceptor, and gut afferents (roughly eighty percent of vagal fibres are afferent) and the nucleus ambiguus applies parasympathetic cardiac brake, confidence high.

◦ In FIGHT-OR-FLIGHT mode the rostral ventrolateral medulla sets the sympathetic pressor set-point onto spinal preganglionic neurons, confidence high.

◦ In PROTECTIVE-REFLEX mode the area postrema, a circumventricular organ lacking a blood-brain barrier, samples blood toxins and triggers emesis by way of the reticular vomiting centre, confidence high.

◦ In RESPIRATORY-RHYTHM mode the pre-Botzinger complex oscillates as a central pattern generator (an isolated slice keeps breathing) at roughly twelve to twenty breaths per minute at rest, driving phrenic motor neurons, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A fall in blood pressure - REST-AND-DIGEST to FIGHT-OR-FLIGHT - seconds, triggered by the baroreflex acting through the nucleus tractus solitarius onto the rostral ventrolateral medulla.

◦ A circulating toxin - any mode to PROTECTIVE-REFLEX - seconds to minutes, triggered by the area postrema.

◦ Rising carbon dioxide or arousal - RESPIRATORY-RHYTHM rate up - seconds, triggered by central chemoreceptors and pontine and arousal input to the pre-Botzinger complex.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Destruction of the pre-Botzinger complex is immediately fatal and there is no redundancy, so cessation of the respiratory oscillation is the terminal boundary signature; the reflexive chemoreceptor drive is the only watchdog, and failure belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.4.4. THE ASCENDING AROUSAL SYSTEM NUCLEI (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.4.4)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This construct is the ensemble that decides whether the whole brain is awake, in deep sleep, or dreaming, so its modes ARE the global brain states, and it is a flip-flop switch by design rather than a graded dial.

The members are the locus coeruleus (norepinephrine), raphe (serotonin), tuberomammillary nucleus (histamine), ventral tegmental area (dopamine), pedunculopontine and laterodorsal tegmental nuclei and basal forebrain (acetylcholine), lateral hypothalamic orexin neurons, and the parabrachial nucleus (glutamate).

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ WAKING mode - the Full Broadcast - all aminergic, cholinergic, and orexinergic members fire and hold the cortex desynchronised and conscious.

◦ NON-rapid-eye-movement (REM)-SLEEP mode - the Low Watch - the ensemble is inhibited by the ventrolateral preoptic nucleus and the cortex falls into slow waves.

◦ REM-SLEEP mode - the Split Broadcast - the aminergic members (norepinephrine, serotonin, histamine) go silent while the cholinergic members fire, activating the cortex without waking the body.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In WAKING mode norepinephrine, serotonin, histamine, and orexin tone are all high; the classic finding is that these levels are high in wake and low in both non-REM and REM sleep, confidence high.

◦ In NON-REM-SLEEP mode the aminergic and orexinergic members are suppressed together, confidence high.

◦ In REM-SLEEP mode the aminergic-off, cholinergic-on dissociation is the defining signature, with cholinergic cells firing as fast as in waking, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Sleep pressure meeting the circadian clock - WAKING to NON-REM-SLEEP - minutes, triggered by the ventrolateral preoptic nucleus inhibiting the ensemble, timed by adenosine build-up and the suprachiasmatic clock.

◦ Accumulated REM pressure - NON-REM-SLEEP to REM-SLEEP - a fast flip roughly every ninety minutes, triggered by mutual inhibition between REM-on cholinergic and REM-off aminergic populations.

◦ Any strong arousing stimulus - sleep to WAKING - under a second, triggered by a locus coeruleus and parabrachial burst.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Orexin is the stabiliser of the whole switch, and its loss produces narcolepsy, a switch that flips uncontrollably into REM-like intrusions; the boundary signature is unbidden state transitions, orexin is the watchdog, and its loss belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.5. THE CEREBELLUM (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.5)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The cerebellum runs one uniform circuit everywhere and never initiates anything, so its modes are not job-changes but learning-states of that single supervised-learning machine, plus the state-dependence it inherits from sleep.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ FORWARD-MODEL mode - the Steady Hand - the trained circuit predicts and corrects ongoing movement and cognition with the error channel quiet.

◦ ERROR-LEARNING mode - the Stern Tutor - a climbing-fibre error signal drives long-term depression at the parallel-fibre-to-Purkinje-cell synapse, reshaping the model.

◦ OFFLINE-CONSOLIDATION mode - the Night Rehearsal - during sleep the cerebellum participates in replaying and stabilising newly learned skills.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In FORWARD-MODEL mode mossy fibres carry context and intention through granule cells and parallel fibres onto Purkinje cells (the sole, inhibitory, cortical output), which sculpt deep-nuclei output with millisecond timing precision, confidence high.

◦ In ERROR-LEARNING mode each Purkinje cell's single climbing fibre from the inferior olive fires a complex spike that says the prediction was wrong, weakening the parallel-fibre synapses active at that moment (the Marr-Albus-Ito rule), confidence high.

◦ In OFFLINE-CONSOLIDATION mode cerebro-cerebellar loops are thought to rehearse skill traces during sleep; confidence moderate, this is established for motor consolidation but still under-mapped.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A movement or prediction error - FORWARD-MODEL to ERROR-LEARNING - tens of milliseconds, triggered by an inferior-olive climbing-fibre discharge.

◦ Successful repetition - ERROR-LEARNING to FORWARD-MODEL - over many trials, minutes to sessions, as the error channel falls silent.

◦ Sleep onset - waking modes to OFFLINE-CONSOLIDATION - minutes, triggered by the sleep-wake switch and slow-wave sleep.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Damage produces ataxia (incoordination, not weakness), with dysmetria, intention tremor, and dysdiadochokinesia, or, in the posterior lobe, the cerebellar cognitive affective syndrome; the boundary signature is a movement that overshoots and hunts, the inferior-olive error channel is the internal watchdog, and structural damage belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.6. THE SPINAL CORD (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.6)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The spinal cord is a computer that knows how to walk, so its modes are the behavioural jobs its own circuitry can run, from a fixed reflex to full locomotion, with descending brain commands selecting among them rather than dictating every muscle.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ REFLEX mode - the Instant Guardian - monosynaptic and withdrawal reflexes act without the brain, protecting the body in milliseconds.

◦ POSTURAL-STANDING mode - the Patient Pillar - tonic extensor drive holds the body upright against gravity.

◦ LOCOMOTOR mode - the Tireless Walker - the central pattern generator produces the alternating step rhythm autonomously.

◦ VOLUNTARY-OVERRIDE mode - the Piano Player - the corticospinal tract imposes fine fractionated movement on the local circuits.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In REFLEX mode the Ia stretch reflex is the simplest circuit in the nervous system, and its gain is task-dependent, being larger in standing than in walking or running, confidence high.

◦ In POSTURAL-STANDING mode tonic vestibulospinal and reticulospinal drive sets extensor (antigravity) muscle tone, confidence high.

◦ In LOCOMOTOR mode the lumbar and cervical central pattern generator alternates agonist and antagonist bursts and can be triggered by a simple unspecific tonic drive, with afferent feedback selecting the appropriate reflex gains phase by phase, confidence high (best characterised in animals, inferred in humans).

◦ In VOLUNTARY-OVERRIDE mode the alpha motor neuron remains Sherrington's final common path through which every command must pass.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A tendon tap or noxious touch - any mode to REFLEX - milliseconds, triggered by dorsal-root afferents locally.

◦ A brainstem go-command - POSTURAL-STANDING to LOCOMOTOR - under a second, triggered by the mesencephalic locomotor region driving reticulospinal neurons onto the pattern generator.

◦ A cortical intention - any mode to VOLUNTARY-OVERRIDE - tenths of a second, triggered by corticospinal drive; descending monoaminergic fibres from raphe and locus coeruleus set dorsal-horn pain gain throughout.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ After cord transection the isolated circuitry still generates rhythm and reflexes, but loss of descending control yields spasticity and exaggerated reflexes as the boundary signature; descending inhibition is the missing watchdog, and this belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.7.1. THE PREVERTEBRAL GANGLION INTEGRATIVE CLUSTER (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.7.1)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This construct (the celiac, superior mesenteric, and inferior mesenteric ganglia as one unit) proves that genuine neural computation happens outside the skull, so its modes are the integrative decisions it strikes between central sympathetic command and enteric feedback about the gut.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ CENTRAL-DOMINANT mode - the Chain of Command - central sympathetic drive prevails, diverting splanchnic blood flow and suppressing gut activity under stress.

◦ ENTERIC-DOMINANT mode - the Local Council - enteric afferents from the gut set the ganglionic output, coordinating motility and secretion locally.

◦ INTEGRATED-BALANCE mode - the Negotiator - the ganglionic interneurons weigh both sources and compute a compromise visceral set-point.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In CENTRAL-DOMINANT mode spinal preganglionic input drives postganglionic noradrenergic outflow that constricts splanchnic vessels and inhibits motility, confidence high (classic fight-or-flight visceral shutdown).

◦ In ENTERIC-DOMINANT mode afferent fibres running from the gut back to the ganglia dominate, producing peripheral reflex control of motility and secretion without central involvement, confidence moderate (documented in animal preparations).

◦ In INTEGRATED-BALANCE mode the resident interneurons combine both streams, which is why this is a true integrative unit and not a mere relay, confidence high on principle.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A stress or threat signal - ENTERIC-DOMINANT to CENTRAL-DOMINANT - seconds, triggered by increased spinal sympathetic preganglionic firing.

◦ A local gut distension or nutrient signal - CENTRAL-DOMINANT to ENTERIC-DOMINANT - seconds, triggered by enteric afferent traffic during quiet digestion.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ No dedicated watchdog is notable, and this is an honest one-sentence entry: the cluster's own dual-input integration is its check, and gross autonomic failure or surgical disruption belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.8. THE ENTERIC NERVOUS SYSTEM (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.8)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The enteric nervous system is legitimately the second brain, with roughly five hundred million neurons that generate coordinated peristalsis even with the vagus severed, so its modes are the digestive programmes it runs, above all the great alternation between the fasted housekeeper and the fed mixer.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ FASTED-HOUSEKEEPING mode - the Night Sweeper - the migrating motor complex sweeps residue and bacteria along the gut in cyclic waves between meals.

◦ FED-DIGESTIVE mode - the Kneading Mixer - feeding replaces the sweep with segmenting, mixing contractions that blend and propel food.

◦ SECRETORY mode - the Wetting Crew - the submucosal (Meissner's) plexus drives secretion and local blood flow.

◦ DEFENSIVE mode - the Purge - noxious contents trigger rapid propulsion (diarrhoea) or reverse peristalsis, an adaptive expulsion programme.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In FASTED-HOUSEKEEPING mode the migrating motor complex cycles in four phases (quiescence, irregular contractions, a five to ten minute burst of peak activity, and decline), recurring roughly every ninety to one hundred and twenty minutes, driven by the myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus and paced hormonally by motilin and ghrelin, confidence high.

◦ In FED-DIGESTIVE mode ingestion abolishes the complex and substitutes noncyclic intermittent postprandial contractions that mix and pump, confidence high.

◦ In SECRETORY mode the submucosal plexus matches fluid and enzyme secretion to luminal content, confidence high.

◦ In DEFENSIVE mode enterochromaffin serotonin release triggers powerful propulsive or emetic reflexes, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Arrival of a meal - FASTED-HOUSEKEEPING to FED-DIGESTIVE - minutes, triggered by luminal nutrients and vagal input (vagotomy abolishes gastric but not small-bowel cycling).

◦ Completion of absorption - FED-DIGESTIVE to FASTED-HOUSEKEEPING - hours, triggered by falling nutrient signal and rising motilin.

◦ A toxin or irritant - any mode to DEFENSIVE - seconds to minutes, triggered by mucosal enterochromaffin serotonin.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Loss of the housekeeping sweep permits small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth, the boundary signature of a stalled migrating motor complex; the fasting cycle is itself the watchdog, and its failure belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.9. THE ENDOCRINE GLANDS AS LAYER 9 STRUCTURES (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.9)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The endocrine glands are Layer 9 structures on the same footing as brain nuclei, and because they speak in hormones rather than spikes their modes are secretory regimes shared across the whole family, registered here once as reusable mode families.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ BASAL-versus-SURGE - the Idle and the Flood - a low maintenance secretion punctuated by a large stimulated release (the adrenal cortisol stress surge, the adrenal medulla's adrenaline dump, the luteinising-hormone ovulatory surge).

◦ CIRCADIAN - the Day-Night Programme - a twenty-four-hour rhythm imposed by the suprachiasmatic clock (the cortisol dawn peak, the melatonin night rise).

◦ PULSATILE-versus-CONTINUOUS - the Drummer and the Drone - hormones released in discrete pulses whose frequency carries meaning versus those held at a steady level (pulsatile gonadotropin-releasing hormone and growth hormone versus near-continuous thyroid hormone).

◦ OVARIAN FOLLICULAR-versus-LUTEAL - the Two Halves of the Month - the follicular phase of rising oestrogen and the luteal phase of progesterone dominance.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In BASAL-versus-SURGE the adrenal cortex holds a low cortisol output that can rise many-fold within minutes of an adrenocorticotropic hormone command, and adrenal medullary chromaffin cells (modified sympathetic neurons that traded their axons for the bloodstream) release adrenaline in seconds, confidence high.

◦ In CIRCADIAN the pineal releases melatonin at night under suprachiasmatic control and cortisol peaks near waking, confidence high.

◦ In PULSATILE-versus-CONTINUOUS the hypothalamic-pituitary axes read pulse frequency (slow gonadotropin-releasing-hormone pulses favour follicle-stimulating hormone, fast favour luteinising hormone), while thyroid hormone sets a slow continuous metabolic set-point for every cell, confidence high.

◦ In OVARIAN FOLLICULAR-versus-LUTEAL rising follicular oestrogen switches from negative to positive feedback to trigger the luteinising-hormone surge and ovulation, after which the corpus luteum secretes progesterone, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A stressor - BASAL to SURGE - seconds (medulla) to minutes (cortex), triggered by sympathetic preganglionic input and hypothalamic corticotropin-releasing hormone through the pituitary.

◦ Dusk and dawn - the CIRCADIAN flip - over the day, triggered by the suprachiasmatic nucleus by way of light entrainment.

◦ Mid-cycle oestrogen threshold - FOLLICULAR to LUTEAL - over hours around the luteinising-hormone surge, triggered by oestrogen positive feedback at the hypothalamus and pituitary.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Loss of pulsatility or of feedback braking is the boundary signature (continuous gonadotropin-releasing hormone paradoxically shuts the axis down); negative-feedback loops are the built-in watchdogs, and their failure belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.1. THE DEFAULT MODE NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.1)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

A large-scale network's mode is its engagement-and-coupling regime, and the default mode network is the paradigm task-negative network, so its modes are defined by when it is active, when it is suppressed, and how it couples to the outward-facing networks.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ ACTIVE-INTERNAL mode - the Inward Gaze - the network sustains and broadcasts self, memory, and imagination during rest and mind-wandering.

◦ DEACTIVATED-ANTICORRELATED mode - the Stood-Down Watch - the network is suppressed as external task-positive networks engage.

◦ SLEEP-RECONFIGURED mode - the Loosened Web - during deep sleep the network fragments and loses its long-range integration.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In ACTIVE-INTERNAL mode the medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate and precuneus, angular gyrus, and hippocampal formation sustain an internally generated state and drive the hippocampus for retrieval, confidence high.

◦ In DEACTIVATED-ANTICORRELATED mode its mean activation falls as the dorsal attention and frontoparietal networks rise, the canonical task-positive-versus-task-negative anticorrelation, confidence high.

◦ In SLEEP-RECONFIGURED mode long-range temporal dependence in the default network breaks down in deep (N3, the deepest non-rapid-eye-movement (non-REM) stage) sleep, while the anticorrelation with task-positive networks persists into REM, confidence moderate.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Onset of an external attention-demanding task - ACTIVE-INTERNAL to DEACTIVATED - one to two seconds, triggered by the salience network's switching signal recruiting the frontoparietal network.

◦ Task offset or rest - DEACTIVATED to ACTIVE-INTERNAL - one to two seconds, triggered by release as the task-positive networks disengage.

◦ Descent into deep sleep - waking modes to SLEEP-RECONFIGURED - minutes, triggered by the arousal systems and slow-wave sleep.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ A network that fails to deactivate on task is the boundary signature (seen in ageing and disease, where the anticorrelation weakens); the salience network is the switching watchdog, and persistent failure to disengage belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.2. THE SALIENCE NETWORK / VENTRAL ATTENTION NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.2)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This network detects what suddenly matters and is itself the switch between the brain's inward and outward regimes, so its modes are watch-states of a comparator that toggles the other networks.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ MONITORING mode - the Sentinel - the network scans interoceptive and sensory streams for the most salient event.

◦ SWITCHING mode - the Points-Operator - on detecting salience it interrupts the default network and recruits the executive network.

◦ SLEEP-QUIETENED mode - the Dozing Guard - salience-driven switching is blunted as arousal falls, though strong stimuli still break through.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In MONITORING mode the anterior insula (right-dominant) and dorsal anterior cingulate compute a running salience estimate from interoception, surprise, and amygdala threat and reward signals, confidence high.

◦ In SWITCHING mode it publishes a switching signal that toggles control between the default and frontoparietal networks and reorients attention, confidence high.

◦ In SLEEP-QUIETENED mode reduced norepinephrine and arousal raise the threshold for switching, confidence moderate.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A salient or unexpected event - MONITORING to SWITCHING - a few hundred milliseconds to a second, triggered by insular interoceptive and amygdalar signals, with norepinephrine marking the surprise.

◦ Return to steady scanning - SWITCHING to MONITORING - one to two seconds, after control is handed to the executive network.

◦ Sleep onset - waking modes to SLEEP-QUIETENED - minutes, triggered by falling ascending arousal tone.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Aberrant salience assignment (flagging the irrelevant as urgent) is a boundary signature linked to psychosis; the network's own interoceptive grounding is the check, and misassignment belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.3. THE DORSAL ATTENTION NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.3)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This is the network that aims attention deliberately, so its modes are the engagement regimes of a top-down spatial priority map, defined by whether it is applying goal-directed gain and how it couples against the default network.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ ENGAGED-ORIENTING mode - the Aimed Beam - the network applies top-down gain to a chosen location or feature.

◦ DISENGAGED-IDLE mode - the Lowered Beam - without a spatial goal the network idles and the default network rises.

◦ ANTICORRELATED-DOMINANT mode - the Ascendant Beam - during demanding external tasks it holds high while the default network is suppressed.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In ENGAGED-ORIENTING mode the frontal eye fields and intraparietal sulcus publish attentional gain to visual cortex at the attended location and gaze commands to the superior colliculus, confidence high.

◦ In DISENGAGED-IDLE mode gain output falls and coupling to the default network relaxes, confidence high.

◦ In ANTICORRELATED-DOMINANT mode the network runs high in reciprocal anticorrelation with the default network, modulated by the frontoparietal network, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Adoption of a spatial goal - DISENGAGED-IDLE to ENGAGED-ORIENTING - a few hundred milliseconds, triggered by goal and priority signals from the frontoparietal network, with acetylcholine setting attentional gain.

◦ A salient distractor - ENGAGED-ORIENTING interrupted - a few hundred milliseconds, triggered by the salience and ventral attention network reorienting the beam.

◦ Sustained external task - to ANTICORRELATED-DOMINANT - seconds, triggered by frontoparietal control biases.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Failure to disengage or a fixed beam (spatial neglect after parietal damage) is the boundary signature; the ventral attention network is the reorienting watchdog, and structural neglect belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.4. THE FRONTOPARIETAL CONTROL NETWORK / CENTRAL EXECUTIVE NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.4)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This is the brain's flexible controller, so its modes are the control regimes of a network that holds goals and reconfigures the others, defined by whether it is actively maintaining and manipulating information.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ ACTIVE-CONTROL mode - the Conductor - the network holds a goal in working memory and publishes control biases that reconfigure the other networks.

◦ STANDBY mode - the Waiting Conductor - between tasks it holds no active goal and its persistent activity subsides.

◦ SLEEP-DISSOLVED mode - the Silent Podium - in deep sleep its long-range coordination breaks down.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In ACTIVE-CONTROL mode the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, parietal cortex around the intraparietal sulcus, caudate head, and mediodorsal thalamus maintain persistent working-memory states and suppress the default network, confidence high.

◦ In STANDBY mode persistent delay activity falls and control biases cease, confidence high.

◦ In SLEEP-DISSOLVED mode long-range temporal integration breaks down in deep sleep alongside the attention networks, confidence moderate.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A task demand - STANDBY to ACTIVE-CONTROL - a few hundred milliseconds to a second, triggered by task-engagement signals from the salience network, with dopamine setting prefrontal gain on the inverted-U.

◦ Task completion - ACTIVE-CONTROL to STANDBY - one to two seconds, as the goal is retired.

◦ Deep sleep - waking modes to SLEEP-DISSOLVED - minutes, triggered by the arousal systems.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Too little or too much prefrontal dopamine flattens or narrows control (the inverted-U boundary), the signature of impaired working memory; the salience network gates entry and the inverted-U is the tuning watchdog, with dysregulation belonging to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.5. THE CINGULO-OPERCULAR NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.5)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This network holds a task-set steady across time, so its modes are the maintenance regimes of a tonic controller that keeps the brain on task between the moment-to-moment decisions of the executive network.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ SET-MAINTENANCE mode - the Steady Keel - the network sustains a stable task-set and tonic alertness across many trials.

◦ ERROR-ADJUST mode - the Course Corrector - on a mistake or feedback it publishes a corrective signal that tightens control.

◦ LOW-ALERT mode - the Slackening Keel - as tonic arousal falls, sustained control loosens and lapses appear.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In SET-MAINTENANCE mode the dorsal anterior cingulate, anterior insula and frontal operculum, and thalamus emit a tonic maintenance signal that holds the task-set, confidence high.

◦ In ERROR-ADJUST mode error and feedback signals transiently boost control and initiation, confidence high.

◦ In LOW-ALERT mode falling norepinephrine tone lets the maintained set decay, producing attentional lapses, confidence moderate.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Task instruction - to SET-MAINTENANCE - seconds, triggered by task instructions from the executive network.

◦ An error or feedback signal - SET-MAINTENANCE to ERROR-ADJUST - a few hundred milliseconds, triggered by performance-monitoring signals in the dorsal anterior cingulate.

◦ Drowsiness or fatigue - SET-MAINTENANCE to LOW-ALERT - minutes, triggered by declining ascending noradrenergic arousal.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Chronic loss of tonic alertness (lapses, poor sustained attention) is the boundary signature; its own error-monitoring is the watchdog, and persistent lapsing belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.6. THE LANGUAGE NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.6)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The language network turns sound and text into meaning and meaning back into speech, so its modes are the directional regimes of a single integrator-sequencer, defined by which way information is flowing through it.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ COMPREHENSION mode - the Listener - the network maps incoming speech and text onto meaning.

◦ PRODUCTION mode - the Speaker - it maps intended meaning onto articulatory programs.

◦ INNER-REHEARSAL mode - the Silent Voice - it runs the phonological loop for inner speech and verbal working memory without overt output.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In COMPREHENSION mode Wernicke's area and the temporal cortex map auditory and visual word-form input onto semantics, confidence high.

◦ In PRODUCTION mode Broca's area and the pre-supplementary motor area (linked by the frontal aslant tract) sequence articulatory programs to motor cortex, confidence high.

◦ In INNER-REHEARSAL mode the arcuate fasciculus loop supports subvocal rehearsal and self-monitoring, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Arrival of speech or text - to COMPREHENSION - a few hundred milliseconds, triggered by auditory cortex and the visual word form area.

◦ Intention to speak - to PRODUCTION - a few hundred milliseconds, triggered by executive and semantic drive to Broca's area.

◦ A working-memory demand - to INNER-REHEARSAL - a second, triggered by executive recruitment of the phonological loop.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Damage yields aphasia (comprehension loss with Wernicke lesions, production loss with Broca lesions, repetition loss with arcuate lesions), the boundary signatures; self-monitoring by the comprehension side is the watchdog, and structural aphasia belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.7. THE SENSORIMOTOR NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.7)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

This is the perception-and-action loop that senses the body and moves it, so its modes are the operating regimes of a closed sensorimotor controller, defined by whether it is executing, holding, or being reshaped.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ EXECUTION mode - the Doer - the network issues motor commands and corrects them by sensory feedback in a closed loop.

◦ POSTURAL-HOLD mode - the Bracer - it maintains posture and readiness without overt movement.

◦ MOTOR-LEARNING mode - the Apprentice - practice reshapes its cortical maps.

◦ SLEEP-OFFLINE mode - the Resting Limb - during sleep, movement is gated off and motor memories consolidate.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In EXECUTION mode primary motor cortex issues commands to the corticospinal tract while efference copies go to the cerebellum and parietal cortex and somatosensory feedback returns by the ventral thalamus, confidence high.

◦ In POSTURAL-HOLD mode tonic drive holds antigravity readiness with low phasic output, confidence high.

◦ In MOTOR-LEARNING mode use-dependent plasticity continuously reshapes the maps, confidence high.

◦ In SLEEP-OFFLINE mode motor output is suppressed (atonia in rapid-eye-movement (REM)) while newly learned skills consolidate, confidence moderate.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A movement plan - POSTURAL-HOLD to EXECUTION - tenths of a second, triggered by motor plans from the cerebellum and basal ganglia by way of the ventral thalamus.

◦ Repeated practice - EXECUTION to MOTOR-LEARNING - minutes to sessions, triggered by dopaminergic vigor and error signals.

◦ Sleep onset - waking modes to SLEEP-OFFLINE - minutes, triggered by the sleep switch and REM atonia.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Loss of feedback yields clumsy, uncorrected movement (deafferentation), the boundary signature; the cerebellar forward model is the correcting watchdog, and deafferentation belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.8. THE VISUAL NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.8)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The visual network is the seeing hierarchy from first cortical stage to object and scene recognition, so its modes are the processing regimes of a hierarchical relay-and-transform, defined by whether it is passively receiving or being modulated by attention and state.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ FEEDFORWARD-PERCEPTION mode - the Open Eye - the retinal image is transformed up the hierarchy into objects, scenes, and motion.

◦ ATTENTION-MODULATED mode - the Focused Eye - top-down gain from the dorsal attention network enhances the attended location or feature.

◦ IDLE-ALPHA mode - the Rested Eye - with eyes closed or the region disengaged, occipital alpha rises as active suppression.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In FEEDFORWARD-PERCEPTION mode primary visual cortex and the extrastriate areas transform the geniculate input along ventral (object) and dorsal (spatial) streams, confidence high.

◦ In ATTENTION-MODULATED mode pulvinar-routed and dorsal-attention gain raises the response at the attended location, confidence high.

◦ In IDLE-ALPHA mode occipital alpha (roughly eight to thirteen hertz) represents the visual cortex being actively silenced, not merely idling, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Directed attention - FEEDFORWARD to ATTENTION-MODULATED - a few hundred milliseconds, triggered by dorsal-attention gain and acetylcholine.

◦ Eye closure or disengagement - to IDLE-ALPHA - under a second, triggered by withdrawal of visual input and alpha generation.

◦ Sleep - perception modes off - minutes, triggered by thalamic gating during non-rapid-eye-movement (non-REM) sleep.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Cortical damage yields blindsight or agnosia (the surviving collicular-pulvinar route mediating blindsight), the boundary signature; attentional gain control is the tuning watchdog, and cortical lesions belong to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.9. THE AUDITORY NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.9)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The auditory network runs from tonotopic sound analysis to speech and music, so its modes are the processing regimes of a hierarchical tonotopic transform, defined by whether it is broadly monitoring or attentively parsing a stream.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ ANALYSIS mode - the Open Ear - the network analyses all incoming sound into frequency and pattern.

◦ SELECTIVE-LISTENING mode - the Cupped Ear - attention sharpens one stream, the cocktail-party selection.

◦ SLEEP-GATED mode - the Half-Shut Ear - thalamic gating attenuates input in sleep, though salient sounds still rouse.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In ANALYSIS mode primary auditory cortex (Heschl's gyrus), belt, and parabelt extract spectrotemporal structure from medial-geniculate input, confidence high.

◦ In SELECTIVE-LISTENING mode attentional gain enhances the attended talker and passes it to the language network, confidence high.

◦ In SLEEP-GATED mode the medial geniculate gates transmission down, but deviant or name-like sounds break through, confidence moderate.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A task or intention to listen - ANALYSIS to SELECTIVE-LISTENING - a few hundred milliseconds, triggered by attentional gain and acetylcholine.

◦ A deviant or salient sound - SLEEP-GATED to arousal - under a second, triggered by the salience network and locus coeruleus.

◦ Sleep onset - ANALYSIS to SLEEP-GATED - minutes, triggered by thalamic gating.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Central auditory damage yields word-deafness or auditory agnosia, the boundary signature; attentional selection is the watchdog against clutter, and central lesions belong to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.10.10. THE LIMBIC NETWORK (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.10.10)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The limbic network is where emotion, reward, and memory join cognition, so its modes are the appraisal regimes of an integrator-appraiser, defined by the valence and intensity of the value signal it is currently computing.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ APPETITIVE-APPRAISAL mode - the Beckoning - reward and positive value dominate, driving approach.

◦ AVERSIVE-APPRAISAL mode - the Recoil - threat and negative value dominate, driving avoidance and defensive autonomic output.

◦ NEUTRAL-MONITORING mode - the Even Keel - the network holds a baseline appraisal, integrating ongoing interoceptive state.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In APPETITIVE-APPRAISAL mode the orbitofrontal and ventromedial prefrontal cortex with the ventral striatum compute a positive value signal (dopamine-gated) and publish it to the executive and default networks, confidence high.

◦ In AVERSIVE-APPRAISAL mode the amygdala drives negative value and pushes drive to the hypothalamus and brainstem autonomic centres, confidence high.

◦ In NEUTRAL-MONITORING mode the insula and brainstem interoceptive input set a baseline affective tone, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A reward cue - NEUTRAL to APPETITIVE-APPRAISAL - a few hundred milliseconds, triggered by a ventral tegmental dopamine signal.

◦ A threat cue - NEUTRAL to AVERSIVE-APPRAISAL - a few hundred milliseconds, triggered by amygdala activation, serotonin and cortisol modulating the gain.

◦ Resolution of the stimulus - back to NEUTRAL-MONITORING - seconds to minutes, as value signals decay.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ A stuck aversive appraisal (chronic anxiety or depression) or a runaway appetitive one (addiction) is the boundary signature; prefrontal appraisal is the regulatory watchdog, and its failure belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.11.1. THE CORTICO-BASAL-GANGLIA-THALAMO-CORTICAL LOOPS (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.11.1)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

These are the parallel loops by which the cortex asks the basal ganglia to select an action, thought, or goal, so their modes are the selection regimes of a gate, above all the shift between deliberate selection and automatic habit.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ SELECTION mode - the Chooser - the loop releases one competing channel from tonic inhibition, permitting a deliberate action or thought.

◦ HABIT mode - the Autopilot - a well-learned action runs from the dorsolateral striatal stream with little deliberation.

◦ HOLD-ALL mode - the Gatekeeper - tonic pallidal inhibition holds every channel shut, permitting nothing until selection tips.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In SELECTION mode a cortical territory publishes to the striatum and, through the pallidum or substantia nigra reticulata to a specific thalamic nucleus and back, releases the winning channel, running in segregated motor, oculomotor, executive, and limbic loops, confidence high.

◦ In HABIT mode control migrates from ventromedial (goal-directed) to dorsolateral (habitual) striatum with over-training, confidence high.

◦ In HOLD-ALL mode tonic inhibitory output keeps the thalamus clamped so no channel is expressed, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A sufficiently strong cortical bid - HOLD-ALL to SELECTION - tenths of a second, triggered by cortico-striatal drive with dopamine setting the learning and vigor signal.

◦ Extended repetition - SELECTION to HABIT - many trials, days to weeks, triggered by dopamine-gated plasticity shifting the controlling stream.

◦ A changed contingency - HABIT back toward SELECTION - variable, triggered when outcomes stop matching and goal-directed control is re-recruited.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Dopamine loss over-strengthens HOLD-ALL (Parkinsonian akinesia) while excess frees too many channels (chorea, compulsion), the boundary signatures; the direct-indirect balance is the internal watchdog, and dopaminergic pathology belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.11.2. THE DIRECT, INDIRECT, AND HYPERDIRECT PATHWAYS (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.11.2)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

These are the three routes through the basal ganglia that say go, no-go, and stop, so this construct's modes are the balance regimes among the three pathways, since it is their sum that decides an action's fate.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ GO-DOMINANT mode - the Green Light - the direct pathway prevails and disinhibits the thalamus to release an action.

◦ NO-GO-DOMINANT mode - the Red Light - the indirect pathway prevails and further inhibits the thalamus to withhold an action.

◦ STOP-OVERRIDE mode - the Emergency Brake - the hyperdirect pathway applies a fast global brake to cancel an action already under way.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In GO-DOMINANT mode cortical glutamate drives dopamine-one striatal cells that disinhibit the thalamus, favoured by dopamine, confidence high.

◦ In NO-GO-DOMINANT mode cortical glutamate drives dopamine-two striatal cells through the external pallidum and subthalamic nucleus to deepen thalamic inhibition, favoured by low dopamine, confidence high.

◦ In STOP-OVERRIDE mode cortex drives the subthalamic nucleus directly, bypassing the striatum for a fast, global, non-selective brake, confidence high.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A rewarded opportunity - NO-GO to GO-DOMINANT - tens to hundreds of milliseconds, triggered by a dopamine burst tilting the striatal balance.

◦ A cue to withhold - GO to NO-GO-DOMINANT - hundreds of milliseconds, triggered by cortical drive to the indirect pathway.

◦ A sudden stop signal - any mode to STOP-OVERRIDE - under two hundred milliseconds, triggered by the right inferior frontal cortex and pre-supplementary motor area driving the subthalamic nucleus.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ A standing imbalance (too much no-go in Parkinsonism, too little in hyperkinetic disorders) is the boundary signature; the opposing signs of dopamine on the two pathways form the internal watchdog, and its breakdown belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.11.3. THE PAPEZ CIRCUIT (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.11.3)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The Papez circuit is the closed limbic ring that binds memory to emotion, so its modes are the circulation regimes of a reentrant loop, defined by whether memory content is being written, recirculated for consolidation, or read back.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ ENCODING-CIRCULATION mode - the Scribe's Round - fresh experience enters and circulates the ring under active theta.

◦ CONSOLIDATION-CIRCULATION mode - the Night Copyist - during rest and sleep the loop recirculates traces to stabilise them.

◦ RETRIEVAL-CIRCULATION mode - the Reader's Round - stored content is read back out through the ring.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In ENCODING-CIRCULATION mode experience passes hippocampus to fornix to mammillary bodies to anterior thalamus to cingulate and back, paced by the theta rhythm (roughly four to eight hertz) from the medial septum, confidence high on the anatomy.

◦ In CONSOLIDATION-CIRCULATION mode the ring recirculates traces during slow-wave sleep and sharp-wave ripples for cortical consolidation, confidence moderate.

◦ In RETRIEVAL-CIRCULATION mode the loop reactivates a stored trace, colouring it with its emotional tone, confidence moderate.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ A salient experience - to ENCODING-CIRCULATION - within a second, triggered by entorhinal input to the hippocampus with septal theta.

◦ Rest or slow-wave sleep - ENCODING to CONSOLIDATION-CIRCULATION - minutes, triggered by the sleep switch and hippocampal ripples.

◦ A retrieval cue - to RETRIEVAL-CIRCULATION - within a second, triggered by cortical or contextual reinstatement, modulated by acetylcholine and dopamine.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Breakage at any node produces amnesia (mammillary damage in Korsakoff syndrome), the boundary signature of a broken ring; the loop's redundancy is minimal and there is no true watchdog, so nodal damage belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.11.4. THE TRIPARTITE SYNAPSE (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.11.4)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The tripartite synapse is the synapse as three partners, the presynaptic terminal, the postsynaptic cell, and the listening astrocyte, so its modes are the gating regimes the astrocyte imposes, cross-referencing the Layer 6 and Layer 7 modes volumes on glial participation.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ SILENT-PARTNER mode - the Quiet Listener - at low activity the astrocyte merely monitors and clears transmitter without feeding back.

◦ POTENTIATING-GLIA mode - the Helping Hand - the astrocyte releases gliotransmitters (glutamate, D-serine) that permit and strengthen plasticity.

◦ SUPPRESSING-GLIA mode - the Restraining Hand - the astrocyte releases adenosine to damp transmission and raise the threshold for potentiation.

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In SILENT-PARTNER mode the perisynaptic astrocyte takes up spillover transmitter and maintains ionic homeostasis without gliotransmission, confidence high.

◦ In POTENTIATING-GLIA mode a rise in astrocytic calcium releases D-serine, a co-agonist required at the postsynaptic receptor for long-term potentiation, gating learning (see D-serine in the Chapter 7 material), confidence high.

◦ In SUPPRESSING-GLIA mode astrocytic adenosine acts presynaptically to reduce release, a brake that accumulates with wakefulness and contributes to sleep pressure, confidence moderate.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Rising synaptic activity - SILENT-PARTNER to POTENTIATING-GLIA - seconds, triggered by transmitter spillover raising astrocytic calcium.

◦ Prolonged or metabolically costly activity - to SUPPRESSING-GLIA - minutes to hours, triggered by adenosine accumulation.

◦ Sleep - a homeostatic reset - across the night, triggered by adenosine clearance restoring the potentiating regime, linking to the Layer 6 and 7 modes volumes on state-dependent gating.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Loss of D-serine supply blocks potentiation while runaway gliotransmission causes excitotoxicity, the boundary signatures; astrocytic calcium homeostasis is the watchdog, and its failure belongs to FAULT REGIMES.


ENTRY 2.11.5. THE GLIA AS COGNITIVE PARTICIPANTS (keyed to Nature's Cognitive Architecture section 2.11.5)

MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS:

The glia are the non-neuronal cells that shape cognition, one supplying chemistry, one pruning connections, one tuning conduction speed, so this construct's modes are the participation regimes of the three glial classes, cross-referencing the Layer 6 and Layer 7 modes volumes.

• THE MODE REGISTER.

◦ HOMEOSTATIC-SUPPORT mode - the Quartermaster - astrocytes supply metabolic support and gate plasticity in the resting circuit.

◦ SURVEILLANCE-and-PRUNING mode - the Sculptor - microglia survey activity and phagocytose weak synapses, shaping learning and forgetting.

◦ ADAPTIVE-MYELINATION mode - the Speed-Tuner - oligodendrocytes lay down experience-dependent myelin that sets conduction speed.

◦ REACTIVE-DEFENCE mode - the Firefighter - on injury or infection glia switch to a reactive protective programme (admitted as an adaptive used regime, with the note that sustained reactivity becomes pathological).

• PER-MODE TRANSFER FUNCTIONS.

◦ In HOMEOSTATIC-SUPPORT mode astrocytes buffer ions and transmitter and publish gliotransmitters that gate plasticity, confidence high.

◦ In SURVEILLANCE-and-PRUNING mode microglia read neural-activity and complement signals and prune weak synapses, an activity-dependent sculpting of circuits, confidence high.

◦ In ADAPTIVE-MYELINATION mode oligodendrocytes myelinate active axons, tuning conduction speed and thereby the timing of thought, confidence high.

◦ In REACTIVE-DEFENCE mode glia adopt reactive morphologies and release protective (and, if sustained, damaging) signals, confidence moderate.

• THE TRANSITION TABLE.

◦ Learning-driven activity - to ADAPTIVE-MYELINATION and PRUNING - hours to weeks, triggered by axonal activity and synaptic-weakness signals.

◦ A quiet resting circuit - to HOMEOSTATIC-SUPPORT - continuous baseline, the default participation regime.

◦ Injury, infection, or complement tagging - to REACTIVE-DEFENCE - minutes to hours, triggered by damage-associated and fractalkine signals.

• FAULT REGIMES AND WATCHDOGS.

◦ Over-pruning (excess microglial synapse loss) or chronic reactive gliosis is the boundary signature, linked to neurodegeneration; the fractalkine and complement checkpoints are the watchdogs, and sustained reactivity belongs to FAULT REGIMES.
