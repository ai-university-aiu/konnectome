HUMAN COGNITION, HUMAN COGNITIVE PROCESSES, AND HUMAN COGNITIVE ARCHITECTURES

A Complete, Comprehensive, and Thorough Outline

Compiled for the konnectome repository of D. R. Dison, Founder, Artificial Intelligence University (AIU).

Date of compilation: August 12, 2026.


HOW TO READ THIS OUTLINE

This document has four levels.

Level 1 is the PART, of which there are fifteen, numbered in words from ONE to FIFTEEN.

Level 2 is the CHAPTER, of which there are seventy, numbered continuously from 1 to 70 across the whole work.

Level 3 is the SECTION, numbered as chapter-point-section, for example 12.3.

Level 4 is the SUB-SECTION, numbered as chapter-point-section-point-subsection, for example 12.3.4.

The outline is organised on a single principle: human cognition is what a mind does when it acquires, represents, transforms, stores, retrieves, and acts upon information in the service of adaptive behaviour, and a cognitive architecture is a proposal for the fixed machinery that makes the whole of that possible at once.

Everything from Part Two through Part Twelve is the catalogue of the capacities themselves, the cognitive processes. Part One is the apparatus by which those capacities are defined and made testable. Parts Thirteen and Fourteen are the theories that try to explain the capacities as one integrated system, the cognitive architectures. Part Fifteen is the frontier where that programme meets modern machine intelligence, which is the point of contact with konnectome.

Cognition, for the purposes of this outline, means the set of processes by which a mind acquires, represents, transforms, stores, retrieves, and acts upon information.

A cognitive process, for the purposes of this outline, means one such capacity treated as an object of study in its own right, for example attention or episodic memory.

A cognitive architecture, for the purposes of this outline, means a unified computational theory that specifies the fixed structures and mechanisms an entire mind requires, within which particular knowledge and skills are the variable content.

The three terms name three grains of the same subject: the whole faculty of knowing, its separable processes, and the integrated machine that runs them.


WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS, AND WHOM IT SERVES

konnectome is built to the principles of Artificial General Intelligence and Artificial Super Intelligence. A machine that is to think in a humanlike way must be measured against the best available account of how human thinking actually works, process by process, and against the best available proposals for how those processes compose into a single mind.

This outline is that account, in map form. It is deliberately catholic: it does not commit konnectome to one architecture or one framework, because the honest state of the science is that several frameworks each explain part of the mind and none explains all of it. The outline instead lays every serious contender side by side, at a common grain, so that a later reader can see what each buys and what each still owes.

Where the companion document THE NEUROSCIENCE OF COGNITION treats cognition from the side of the nervous system, this document treats it from the side of the cognitive and computational sciences, and the two are meant to be read together.


A NOTE ON TAGS

Each chapter carries one of four tags.

(PROCESS) marks a chapter that states a cognitive capacity or the mechanism of one, the material a model of the mind must reproduce.

(FRAMEWORK) marks a chapter that supplies a theory, a mathematics, or a method by which the PROCESS chapters are made precise and checkable.

(ARCHITECTURE) marks a chapter that specifies an integrated proposal for the whole mind, or a mechanism that spans one.

(CONTEXT) marks a chapter that situates the science historically, philosophically, or at its unfinished frontier.
