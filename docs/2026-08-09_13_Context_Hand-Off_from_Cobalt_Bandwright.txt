PART SEVEN - SIGN-OFF

Two slices, and both were about the same thing from opposite ends: a quantity konnectome did not have, and a quantity konnectome had hidden.

Slice 43 was the missing one. The corpus counts in seconds and konnectome counts in ticks, and the gap had been noticed three times from three unrelated directions before anybody stopped and closed it. The number itself took a line. What took the slice was deciding that a duration which does not land on a tick must be REFUSED rather than rounded - because the promise this build was about to break was not going to be broken by anyone inventing a number. It was going to be broken by everyone rounding one, quietly, in arithmetic nobody would ever look at twice.

Slice 44 was the hidden one. konnectome's hysteresis had been sitting inside two switches as constants for dozens of slices, which is exactly where the corpus says it must not be, and slice 40 had found that and refused to fix it in the wrong place. Four slices later the right place existed and the fix took an afternoon. The waiting was the work.

And the best thing this session found, it found by being wrong. The obvious reading of the corpus's hysteresis sentence, applied to a gate that is pushed rather than filled, builds a gate that closes and never opens again - which is, word for word, one of the two ways the same corpus says such a gate BREAKS. The design and the fault were the same shape, separated only by which quantity you assumed was being compared. A test written before the code was trusted caught it in under a minute.

Write down the unit before you write down the number. Write down the mapping before you write down the rule. And when the corpus describes something in terms of a quantity your machine does not have, that gap is a decision, and a decision that is not written down has still been made - just not by anyone you can ask.
