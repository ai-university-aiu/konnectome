PART FIVE - SHARP EDGES AND HONEST NON-CLOSURES

- THE SWITCH IS NOW BOTH WRITTEN AND READ. The three-slice-old sharpest edge is CLOSED. The mind behaves differently asleep than awake. Under the default watchman a thirty-tick run sleeps on ticks nineteen through twenty-six and wakes at twenty-seven. Any slice touching the tick must now consider BOTH programs, and must keep the timing law: the selection is made from a tick's completed day and governs the NEXT tick's opening state.

- THE DEEPEST LESSON OF THIS SLICE, AND THE ONE MOST LIKELY TO REPEAT: TWO FORCES SET AGAINST EACH OTHER MUST BE MEASURED IN THE SAME UNITS. The night's replay dose was originally paid once per remembered pattern while the renormalising bound stayed one fixed step, so a growing memory store simply out-dosed the bound - measured end to end, the night was MULTIPLYING the weight by more than ten against a target of one half, the exact inverse of the renormalisation Chapter 7 specifies. The existing guard gave false assurance because it compared the night's bound to the DAY's bound, never to the replay it was set against. WORSE, AND THE PART TO INTERNALIZE: the suite's own night assertion (weight goes UP) had ENSHRINED the inversion, so the tests were green while the mechanism ran backwards. A test that asserts the direction a defect happens to push is not a test. When you add a force, write a test for its NET INTENDED EFFECT (does it move toward the target?), never merely for its direction.

- THE FIRST DRAFT'S STRONGER CLAIM WAS AN ARTIFACT, AND THAT IS ON THE RECORD. The rest-twin telling originally asserted the interleaved twin kept the old edge at or ABOVE its starting strength. After the dose fix that is false: BOTH twins end below the start, because the raised bound washes everything. The honest claim is COMPARATIVE - the forgetful twin keeps barely a third, the interleaved twin nearly two thirds, and replay DEFENDS what it refreshes. Do not restore the stronger wording.

- THE UNBOUND-WRONG-JUDGEMENT LENS KEEPS PAYING (slices 33, 34, 35, 36, 37, and now 38 - SIX in a row), and this slice it paid SIX TIMES. Its slice-38 refinements, all worth carrying: (1) a new engine that carefully judges one of its stores and not another WILL be wrong on the unjudged one - an unbound list argument to maplist/3 is silently bound to [], a partial list is silently truncated WITH THE CALLER'S TAIL MUTATED to agree, and a non-list fails silently; judge every store the same way. (2) memberchk/2 with an UNBOUND key BINDS that key to the store's first entry and returns a value belonging to something else - here a hole at an edge's endpoint invented a connection no caller declared and strengthened it; guard the key ahead of every lookup. (3) A two-clause dispatch on an atom is NOT a judgement: an unbound value unifies with the first clause head, binds itself, and LEAVES A CHOICEPOINT into the second, so one tick ran BOTH whole programs; an out-of-domain value matches neither head and fails the whole caller SILENTLY. Always judge a dispatch value against its domain BEFORE dispatching. Carry all four named lenses: unbound-wrong-judgement, key-shape aliasing, default drift, behaviour-pinned-identical.
