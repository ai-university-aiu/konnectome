KONNECTOME RUNG FIVE - THE REAL MACHINE: A DESIGN PROPOSAL FOR THE OWNER

Version 2. 2026-08-07. Status: THE OWNER HAS ANSWERED - SECTION FIVE RECORDS THE DECISIONS.
(Version 1, the conversation starter, is archived in docs/archive/.)

This document begins the design conversation the build ladder requires before any Rung Five
work can be claimed. It proposes options and names the decisions that belong to the owner.
Nothing in it authorizes a purchase, a claim, or a slice. Per the guiding book (section A6.3a,
RUNG FIVE), the rung's smoke test closes the sense-act loop "through a real machine", and no
software stand-in can round itself up to one.


SECTION ONE - WHERE THE REPOSITORY STANDS TODAY

The seam is built, tested, and told. Slice 24 shipped the two groundwork packs: simulated_body
(the honestly-named stand-in machine) and body_interface (the book's three connections as one
interface). Slice 25 taught the one runnable story to tell that groundwork without claiming the
rung. The ledger's Rung Five sub-table holds two PARTIAL rows (the smoke loop and the survival
groundwork, demonstrated in simulation only) and two PENDING rows (reaching and navigation,
which cannot begin without a machine).

What a real machine must plug into is therefore already fixed by working, pinned code. The
contract of the seam, as body_interface defines it today:

1. SENSES IN. The machine driver must present sensor readings as a list of key-value pairs
   (today: path_ahead with a value like clear or obstacle, read by body_interface_senses). A
   real camera means a perception front-end that reduces frames to such readings.

2. BODY STATE IN. The machine driver must present its physical state as key-value pairs
   including battery_charge as a fraction of full (read by body_interface_homeostasis). The
   drive system then defends it unchanged: a low battery is felt as hunger through the same
   drive_system_error as every other pain.

3. ACTIONS OUT. The machine driver must accept the named actuator commands the interface maps
   released outcomes onto (today: recharge, steer_around, hold_still) and must refuse, not
   guess at, a command it cannot enact. It must expose an inspectable log of what it enacted,
   because the tests and the record discipline read that log.

A real machine joins the family by implementing exactly this contract - a new pack (for
example: robot_body_driver) that satisfies the same call shapes simulated_body satisfies, so
body_interface runs unchanged against it. That is the entire integration plan at the software
level, and it is deliberately small.


SECTION TWO - THE THREE CANDIDATE PATHS

PATH A - A SMALL REAL ROBOT (the book's own demand, satisfied smallest).

The smallest honest machine needs: a camera or distance sensor (to see an obstacle), a
readable battery level (to feel hunger), drivable wheels (to steer and to reach a dock), and a
reachable charging dock (so recharge is a real act, not a variable assignment). Candidate
classes, in rising cost and capability:

• A wheeled kit robot with a single-board computer (for example a Raspberry-Pi-class rover
  with a battery gauge and a dock). Cheapest honest option; enough for the smoke test and the
  survival trial; reaching would wait.

• A small commercial research robot with an arm (adds the reaching-and-grasping trial).

• A vacuum-robot-class platform with an open interface (self-docking is native, which makes
  the survival trial vivid, but open programmatic control varies by model).

What Path A buys that nothing else can: the rung itself, honestly claimed, when the trials
pass on hardware.

PATH B - AN HONEST ROBOT SIMULATOR (groundwork extended, rung still not claimed).

A physics simulator (Gazebo, Webots, or PyBullet class) with a simulated camera, battery
model, and wheeled platform. This would exercise the seam against a far richer world than
simulated_body - continuous space, real navigation, noisy perception - and would prepare
every line of the eventual hardware driver. But the book's text gives no software exemption:
under Path B the rung stays unclaimed and the honest-limits line keeps naming embodiment.
Path B is a good road; it is not the destination.

PATH C - WAIT. The seam is tested and stable; nothing rots by waiting. The build ladder has
ample non-embodied work (the pending refinements, Rung Two's deepening) to continue climbing.


SECTION THREE - THE TRIALS, IN ORDER, WHEN A MACHINE EXISTS

1. THE SMOKE TEST. A change the camera sees produces, through the mind, a movement of the
   body - the loop closed through the real machine. This is the first and only gate for
   saying Rung Five is OPEN on hardware.

2. THE SURVIVAL TRIAL (the book calls it the fullest exercise of the architecture). The robot
   defends its own battery of its own accord - seeking its dock as the drive presses, exactly
   as the simulated rhythm already demonstrates. The existing pressing-threshold constant and
   drive machinery carry over unchanged; what is new is only the world's noise.

3. THE NAVIGATION TRIAL. Movement toward a goal with obstacle avoidance, using a persistent
   model of the unseen world - object permanence, load-bearing at last.

4. THE REACHING TRIAL. Arm hardware permitting: guide a grasp to a named, perceived object.

Each trial becomes a normal slice: design, test-first driver code, adversarial review,
lockstep documents, pull request. Failures and partial results are recorded as honestly as
passes; a trial that cannot pass on the chosen hardware is a finding, not an embarrassment.

SAFETY, STATED EARLY. The override controller's inviolable property (a vital drive can never
be suppressed) transfers to hardware as: the driver must expose an unconditional stop, the
survival trial runs in an enclosure with no stairs, water, or bystanders, and no trial ever
runs unattended until the owner declares otherwise. Driving-class tasks are out of scope
entirely, per the book's own safety gating.


SECTION FOUR - THE DECISIONS THAT BELONG TO THE OWNER

1. Path A, B, or C - and if A, roughly what budget class.
2. If A: does the first machine need an arm (reaching) or are wheels-plus-dock enough
   (smoke plus survival first)?
3. Where the machine lives and what enclosure counts as safe.
4. Whether Path B runs in parallel as preparation while hardware is chosen.
5. The name of the driver pack, when one exists (proposal: robot_body_driver, whole words,
   per the Twelfth Commandment).

These five decisions were answered by the owner on 2026-08-07; Section Five records them.


SECTION FIVE - THE OWNER'S DECISIONS (2026-08-07)

DECISION 1 - THE PATH: PATH B NOW, PATH A LATER. There is no physical robot today, and choosing
one needs research and shopping first. The simulator is the way to go for now - and this document
carries a STANDING REMINDER: a physical robot is to be acquired in the future, because under the
guiding book only Path A can ever claim the rung. Until then the rung stays unclaimed and the
honest-limits line keeps naming embodiment.

DECISION 2 - THE FIRST MACHINE'S SHAPE: WHEELS PLUS DOCK FIRST. When the future robot is bought,
it starts as the smallest honest machine - drivable wheels, a readable battery, a reachable
charging dock, a camera or distance sensor. The smoke test and the survival trial come first; an
arm and the reaching trial wait for a later machine or an upgrade.

DECISION 3 - WHERE IT LIVES: AN INDOOR ROOM PEN, ATTENDED. A single ground-floor room with a
simple physical pen or barrier: no stairs, no water, no bystanders; every trial attended until
the owner declares otherwise; the driver's unconditional stop always within reach. Until hardware
exists, this decision shapes the simulated world: the simulator's arena is modelled on this
enclosure, so the future real trials inherit a rehearsed geometry.

DECISION 4 - THE SIMULATOR: WEBOTS. Free and open source, runs on Linux, ships wheeled robots
with cameras and battery models, and exposes a programmatic interface a driver pack can reach
through a small bridge. The Path B groundwork builds against Webots.

DECISION 5 - THE PACK NAMES: simulator_body_driver AND robot_body_driver. Two honest names,
whole words, per the Twelfth Commandment. The Webots work lands as simulator_body_driver, keeping
the simulator-versus-real boundary visible in the name itself; robot_body_driver stays RESERVED
for the future physical machine, so no reader ever mistakes simulation for the rung.

WHAT THIS UNBLOCKS. The simulator groundwork slice can now be designed: a simulator_body_driver
pack satisfying the same seam contract simulated_body satisfies (Section One), so body_interface
runs unchanged against Webots. Rung Five itself remains PARTIAL/PENDING in the ledger until a
real machine passes the smoke test, per Decision 1's standing reminder.

End of the real-machine design proposal, version 2.
