PART SEVEN - SIGN-OFF

Yesterday konnectome learned to say what a construct can BE. Today one of them finally had something to choose between.

The gate had been standing there since slice two, flipping between open and closed, and nobody had asked whether those two words were a register. The corpus had already answered - a hysteretic toggle whose modes are simply its two self-holding states - and the answer had been sitting in Entry 36 of the eighth layer's modes volume the whole time. So the gate got its register of two, its table of two rows with the agency field filled in honestly, and the thing a register of one can never show: two rules that differ, and differ where you can watch them. Four units into an open sluice, four units out. Four units into a dropped shutter, nothing.

And where konnectome's gate is not the corpus's latch, that was written down rather than fixed. The biology's switch is sticky; konnectome's is not; and the corpus itself says the stickiness lives in a separate construct, so building it into the switch would have been the wrong answer arrived at quickly. It went in the ledger with the name of the slice that will close it.

The measuring found four more small scandals in code eight slices old - a gate asked about a blank answering as though it had been open, and writing that borrowed mode into the caller's hole. Second slice running that a promise of no change, checked rather than believed, paid for itself.

Put two machines in the shape, prove the shape did not move, and write down the one place the shape is not the thing it was modelled on - because the gap you declare is the only gap anyone can close.
