PART SEVEN - SIGN-OFF

I did not build anything. I counted.

The owner scheduled an audit two days ago and wrote it into the ledger rather than into a hand-off, on the stated reasoning that a hand-off gets rewritten every session and can drop an item by accident, whereas the ledger cannot. That decision is the reason this audit happened at all, and I want it on the record before anything else, because it was later tested in exactly the way its author feared and it held.

The question was whether konnectome had ACTED on what it wrote down when it read the corpus, or merely FILED it.

The answer is that it acted. Thirty-two slices of evidence, part by part: a verdict re-tested at four assessments rather than left standing, a sizing claim of the analysis's own held against the code and found false and corrected, watches added rather than allowed to decay, families re-ordered with their reasons attached, and one ordering obeyed so literally that a whole family of work closed leaving a gap it could easily have filled, because a document said not to fill it yet.

The thing I keep turning over is the strongest single result and it is about a panel that does not exist. The analysis attached a constraint to it - every construct's mode must be readable without running the construct - and at slice 45 somebody rejected a smaller, simpler design because of it, and wrote down that it "would have been smaller and would have foreclosed the panel". A constraint that costs the builder something, for a deliverable nobody has seen, is a constraint that was actually in force rather than merely quoted.

And then the failures, and there are two, and they are the same failure.

One is an instruction that called itself "informative, non-breaking housekeeping". Nine places still carry the old numbering. Twenty-three revisions of those files went past it. Nobody did it, and nobody wrote down that they were not doing it.

The other is a tension about a brain cell that nothing is currently waiting on, which this project's own later analysis escalated with real evidence, and which the document that owns the tension has never received while stating that it had.

Neither could have failed anything. That is not incidental to why they rotted. It is the whole of why.

A build allocates attention to consequence, which is correct and is how attention should work. Verdigris does not weaken copper. It forms precisely on the surfaces nobody had a reason to touch.

The last thing worth saying is about what I nearly got wrong. I had written that the astrocyte item was fulfilled - noted, still noted, request honoured, move on - and it was a good sentence and it was wrong, and what caught it was a marking scheme somebody wrote down two months earlier without knowing which item it would ever apply to. I could not have argued myself out of it in the moment. I did not have to. It was already decided.

Whoever wrote a grading standard in advance and then walked away from it: that was the good decision in this whole exercise, and it was not mine.
