HOW TO READ THIS OUTLINE

This document has four levels.

Level 1 is the PART, of which there are fifteen.

Level 2 is the CHAPTER, of which there are sixty-six, numbered continuously from 1 to 66 across the whole work.

Level 3 is the SECTION, numbered as chapter-point-section, for example 12.3.

Level 4 is the SUB-SECTION, numbered as chapter-point-section-point-subsection, for example 12.3.4.

The outline is organised on a single principle, which is also the question above: a brain wave is a rhythm in the electrical activity of many neurons at once, and the whole science turns on whether that rhythm does work or merely shows that work is being done.

A brain wave, for the purposes of this outline, means a neural oscillation: a repeating rise and fall in the summed electrical activity of a population of neurons, described by its frequency (how many cycles per second), its phase (where in the cycle it is at a given moment), and its amplitude or power (how large the swing is).

The neuroscience of brain waves, therefore, names the research programme that measures these rhythms, explains how they are generated, asks what if anything they compute, and, for this corpus, asks what a synthetic mind should borrow from them.

Everything before Part Twelve is the science: what the waves are, how they are measured, how they arise, what functions are claimed for them, how they behave across states and sleep and development and disease, and how they can be modulated. Part Twelve is the honest reckoning, the engine-versus-exhaust debate given its own weight. Parts Thirteen through Fifteen are the translation to computation and to konnectome, and the closing judgement.


THE ORGANISING DISTINCTION THIS OUTLINE IMPOSES: COMPUTATION, SUBSTRATE, OR CONTESTED

The corpus already mentions oscillations hundreds of times, scattered across the layer catalogues as a property of some cell, motif, or state, but nowhere does it own the functional theory of what the waves are for.

This outline supplies that missing theory, and it supplies it under a discipline the corpus already uses for contested material: every load-bearing claim that a rhythm performs a computation is graded, on the document's face, on a causal-evidence ladder.

The ladder has three rungs, and every rhythm-function pairing in Parts Seven through Eleven carries one of three gradings.

• COMPUTATION marks a function for which perturbing the rhythm specifically, most convincingly by driving or silencing it with light-activated proteins in animals, changes the cognitive outcome in the predicted way. The hippocampal sharp-wave ripple and the parvalbumin-interneuron generation of gamma are the clearest examples.

• SUBSTRATE marks a rhythm that is best read as a by-product of the underlying excitation-inhibition dynamics, real as a signal but not itself doing the work, the electrical equivalent of engine noise rather than the engine.

• CONTESTED marks a function that is supported by correlation and by some perturbation evidence but undermined by a specific technical objection, and where honest practice is to report the dispute rather than pick a side.

Three technical objections recur across the CONTESTED gradings and are named once here as a spine: gamma frequency drifts with the stimulus, so it cannot easily serve as a fixed shared reference; phase is hard to align across regions given the time it takes signals to travel; and non-sinusoidal wave shapes create spurious cross-frequency coupling that inflates the apparent evidence.


A NOTE ON TAGS

Each chapter carries one of four tags.

• (CORE) marks a chapter that states a phenomenon, a mechanism, or a function that a reader could not skip without leaving a hole in the account.

• (APPARATUS) marks a chapter that supplies the physics, the mathematics, or the method by which the CORE chapters are made checkable.

• (COMPUTATION) marks a chapter whose subject is the translation of a wave function into computational or engineering terms, the material this corpus exists to extract.

• (CONTEXT) marks a chapter that situates the science historically, clinically, or at its unfinished frontier.
