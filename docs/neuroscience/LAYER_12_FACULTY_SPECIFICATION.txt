LAYER 12, THE ORGANISM AND BEHAVIOUR LAYER: THE FACULTY SPECIFICATION

The Faculties of the Behaving, Experiencing Person, as the Acceptance-Test Catalogue for a Synthetic Mind

A design-facing document of the Certified_Neuroscientist corpus of D. R. Dison, Founder, Artificial Intelligence University (AIU).

Date of compilation: August 9, 2026.


WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

Layer 12 is where cognition is EXPERIENCED: the layer of the behaving, experiencing person - perception, action, emotion, motivation, learning, memory, language, decision, and consciousness.

The layers-needed-for-cognition sanity check assigned Layer 12 a distinct role in the engineering programme: not a parts catalogue but the SPECIFICATION - the list of faculties the assembled machine must exhibit, against which every build is verified.

This document is that specification: seventeen faculties, each stated as a capability, mapped to the Layer 10 system or coalition that implements it, and given acceptance criteria a test harness could run.

The mapping principle, established in the Layer 10 volume: the faculties named at this layer each map to exactly one system or a small coalition, and to nothing smaller.


THE SEVENTEEN FACULTIES

1. PERCEPTION.

Capability: construct a structured, stable model of the present environment from raw sensor streams, robust to noise, occlusion, and viewpoint.

Implemented by: the sensory systems (Layer 10, Chapters 1 through 7), interpreted through the predictive coding motif.

Acceptance criteria: object constancy under transformation; figure-ground segmentation; cross-modal binding of one event heard and seen; graceful degradation, not collapse, under noise.

2. ATTENTION.

Capability: select, amplify, and sustain processing of task-relevant input while suppressing the rest, and reorient within a fraction of a second to genuine surprise.

Implemented by: the dorsal attention and salience networks with the thalamic reticular gate.

Acceptance criteria: measurable selection benefit and distractor cost; involuntary capture by salient onset; restoration of goal focus after capture.

3. WORKING MEMORY.

Capability: hold and manipulate a small number of items for seconds to minutes without external storage.

Implemented by: the working memory system (Layer 10, Chapter 16) on the persistent-activity attractor motifs.

Acceptance criteria: span in the biological range (roughly four chunks); active manipulation, not mere retention; selective overwrite without wholesale loss.

4. EPISODIC MEMORY.

Capability: store one-shot records of specific events with their context, retrieve them from partial cues, and know they are memories.

Implemented by: the episodic memory system (Layer 10, Chapter 13) - separation, completion, comparison in series.

Acceptance criteria: one-trial learning; pattern completion from fragments; source discrimination between similar episodes; consolidation benefit after an offline phase.

5. SEMANTIC MEMORY.

Capability: hold generalised knowledge - concepts, facts, categories - abstracted from episodes and retrievable by meaning.

Implemented by: the semantic system's hub-and-spokes (Layer 10, Chapter 14).

Acceptance criteria: generalisation to unseen instances; graded typicality; cross-modal access to one concept from word, image, or description.

6. PROCEDURAL SKILL.

Capability: acquire, through practice, fast fluent action sequences that no longer need deliberation.

Implemented by: the procedural system - the selection loops plus the cerebellar calibrator.

Acceptance criteria: power-law improvement with practice; automaticity (low dual-task cost when skilled); persistence without episodic access to the practice.

7. LANGUAGE.

Capability: comprehend and produce unbounded structured messages mapping between sound or text and meaning.

Implemented by: the language system (Layer 10, Chapter 18).

Acceptance criteria: compositional understanding of novel sentences; production constrained by syntax and context; repair dialogue when comprehension fails.

8. DECISION AND VALUATION.

Capability: choose among options under uncertainty by learned value, balancing reward, risk, delay, and effort.

Implemented by: the reward system and selection loops (Layer 10, Chapters 9 and 17).

Acceptance criteria: reward-prediction-error learning curves; sensible discounting; exploration under uncertainty and exploitation under confidence; the capacity to stop (the hyperdirect veto).

9. EMOTION AND AFFECTIVE APPRAISAL.

Capability: appraise events for significance to the organism's goals and let the appraisal retune processing - vigilance, memory salience, decision weights - coherently.

Implemented by: the limbic coalition with the neuromodulatory broadcasts and the interoceptive system.

Acceptance criteria: appraisal-congruent reallocation of attention and memory; decay and regulation, not permanence, of affective states; interoception feeding valuation.

10. MOTOR CONTROL.

Capability: execute accurate, calibrated, adaptable movement of effectors in real time.

Implemented by: the motor systems (Layer 10, Chapters 8 through 12).

Acceptance criteria: closed-loop correction mid-action; adaptation to perturbation within tens of trials; delegation of rhythm to pattern generators.

11. SOCIAL COGNITION.

Capability: model other agents - their attention, intentions, beliefs, and feelings - and use the models in interaction.

Implemented by: the social cognition system (Layer 10, Chapter 20).

Acceptance criteria: gaze and intention following; false-belief reasoning; behaviour adjustment to another agent's knowledge state.

12. EXECUTIVE CONTROL.

Capability: maintain goals over distraction, switch task sets on demand, inhibit prepotent responses, and monitor for errors.

Implemented by: the executive system and frontoparietal network (Layer 10, Chapters 19 and 24).

Acceptance criteria: measurable switch costs that shrink with preparation; successful inhibition; post-error slowing and correction.

13. LEARNING ITSELF.

Capability: improve at all of the above from experience, without catastrophic forgetting, using both online acquisition and offline consolidation.

Implemented by: the plasticity machinery of Layers 4 through 7 under the Layer 11 schedule.

Acceptance criteria: retention of old competence as new competence is added; measurable consolidation benefit from the offline phase; graceful lifetime accumulation.

14. ACCESS CONSCIOUSNESS (THE WORKSPACE CRITERION).

Capability: make one content at a time globally available for report, reasoning, and flexible use, with the all-or-none ignition signature.

Implemented by: the global workspace dynamics (Layer 10, Chapter 44).

Acceptance criteria: capacity-limited serial report; the subliminal-versus-reportable threshold; global availability of ignited content to every other faculty.

An honest note, in the corpus tradition: this criterion specifies ACCESS consciousness - global availability - and takes no position on phenomenal experience, which remains beyond any acceptance test this document knows how to write.

Faculties 15 through 17 were added on August 12, 2026, by the konnectome build, under the owner's explicit authority of that date.

They are THIS BUILD'S WRITING, living in the corpus's directory, and they do NOT outrank the corpus's own original faculties 1 through 14, which were compiled on August 9, 2026, and which remain untouched in wording and in order.

The count was repaired in the same change, which is why this document now reads seventeen where it formerly gave the smaller count of 14, and the count repair is the only alteration made to existing text.

The material the three additions are built from is the corpus's own, cited by chapter: the cognitive map from the north-star manuscript's chapter 50.4 and its six sub-sections, metacognition from chapter 63.3 and its four sub-sections, and mental simulation from chapter 64.3 and its four sub-sections, resting on the cell chapters of the neuron catalogue (place cells 285, grid cells 286, head direction cells 287, border and boundary vector cells 288, speed cells 289, time cells 290, object vector cells 291) and on the Layer 10 system chapters named in each entry.

The acceptance criteria were checked against the published literature by web search on August 12, 2026, and where the two disagree the corpus outranks anything found that way, the outside sources serving only to make the criteria testable and to keep the measures current.

The reason they were added is a gap found by inspection of this document, which named 14 faculties and no more, so that a build could pass every one of those 14 suites while unable to navigate a space, unable to report what it does and does not know, and unable to simulate a future it has not experienced.

The middle of those three omissions is the gravest, because calibrated confidence is the mechanism by which a system declines to act, so its absence from an acceptance catalogue is a safety property left untested rather than a capability merely left out.


15. SPATIAL NAVIGATION AND THE COGNITIVE MAP.

Capability: build, hold, and use an allocentric map of an environment - a map anchored to the world rather than to the body - such that position, heading, distance, and boundary are represented continuously, and such that novel routes can be computed rather than merely replayed.

Implemented by: the hippocampal-entorhinal coalition inside the episodic memory system (Layer 10, Chapter 13), which the corpus describes as an elaboration of that navigation machinery, running on the map cell types of the neuron catalogue - place cells, grid cells, head direction cells, border and boundary vector cells, speed cells, time cells, and object vector cells, Chapters 285 through 291.

Acceptance criteria: a place code that tiles the environment and survives darkness, showing that the map is not a re-derivation from current sensory input; path integration, so that after an outbound wander with landmarks withheld the system can produce a homing vector to the start; a metric, tested by the demand for a novel shortcut across ground never traversed and for a detour when a familiar route is blocked, since a system holding only a route chain can do neither; remapping, so that a second environment receives a statistically independent assignment of units to locations rather than a distortion of the first; landmark anchoring, so that rotating the cues rotates the map coherently and as a whole; and generalisation of the same machinery to a non-spatial ordered space, the corpus's strongest claim at chapter 50.4 being that the map is a general engine for relational and predictive structure and that navigation is therefore the template for thought rather than a special case of it.

16. METACOGNITION AND CONFIDENCE.

Capability: monitor its own cognition and report, on a scale that means the same thing from one occasion to the next, how likely each of its own outputs is to be correct, and let that report control what it does next - gather more evidence, ask, defer, or decline.

Implemented by: the monitoring and control links between object level and meta level described at chapter 63.3, seated in the anterior prefrontal cortex, roughly Brodmann area 10, which the corpus reaches through the executive control system (Layer 10, Chapter 19) and the frontoparietal control network (Layer 10, Chapter 24), with the reportable content supplied by the global workspace dynamics (Layer 10, Chapter 44).

Acceptance criteria: the suite must test CALIBRATION and not the mere production of a confidence number, because a system that reports high confidence uniformly will pass any test that only asks whether a number was produced.

Specifically: reliability, measured by binning outputs by stated confidence and requiring that the empirical accuracy within each bin track the stated value across the whole range, so that the things called ninety percent likely are right about nine times in ten and the things called sixty percent likely are right about six times in ten.

Scored by a proper scoring rule, the Brier score, and reported through its decomposition into reliability, resolution, and uncertainty, rather than by expected calibration error alone, since expected calibration error is not a proper scoring rule and has trivial minima at which a system with no accuracy at all scores as perfectly calibrated.

Discrimination, measured as metacognitive sensitivity by the meta d prime statistic of signal detection theory - the first-order sensitivity d prime that an ideal observer would need in order to produce the confidence pattern actually observed - and normalised for first-order performance as the M ratio, meta d prime divided by d prime, where a value of one means the confidence signal is as informative as the evidence allows and a value below one means information available in the evidence was discarded on the way to the report.

The uniform-confidence attack must be run as an explicit negative control: a stub that returns a constant high confidence must FAIL the suite, and it will, because a constant report has no resolution, so its Brier resolution term is zero and its M ratio is zero, whereas a merely accurate system passes only if its confidence separates its own hits from its own errors.

Difficulty tracking, so that confidence falls when the items are made objectively harder without the system being told they are harder.

Selective response, tested on a risk and coverage curve: when items are sorted by the system's own stated confidence and only the most confident fraction is answered, the error rate on the answered fraction must fall monotonically as coverage is reduced, and the system must abstain rather than answer when no option clears its threshold.

And a stability check across domains, since the corpus reports at chapter 63.3 that metacognition has a domain-general component and a substantial domain-specific one, so a system calibrated on perception must be measured again on memory rather than assumed to transfer.

An honest note, in the corpus tradition: this is the faculty by which a system DECLINES TO ACT, and its absence from a build is therefore a SAFETY property rather than a capability gap, because a system with every other faculty in this catalogue and no calibrated confidence will act at full commitment on its worst outputs and will have no internal signal by which anything could stop it.

17. IMAGINATION, PROSPECTION, AND MENTAL SIMULATION.

Capability: construct experiences it has not had - a future event, an imagined scene, a counterfactual version of something that did happen - by recombining stored fragments into a coherent novel whole, and use the construction to guide present choice.

Implemented by: the default mode network (Layer 10, Chapter 21), which the corpus describes as publishing internally generated content whose output is constructive simulation, in a core network with the hippocampal machinery of the episodic memory system (Layer 10, Chapter 13), the corpus's position at chapter 64.3 being that simulation is not a separate faculty but memory operated in a different direction and that the brain does not have a separate organ for the future.

Acceptance criteria: the suite must DISTINGUISH SIMULATING A FUTURE FROM RECALLING A PAST, since the same hippocampal machinery serves both, and a system that answers a prospection probe by retrieving a stored episode and relabelling it must fail.

Specifically: novel recombination, tested by an experimental recombination protocol in which a person, a place, and an object drawn from three different stored episodes must be composed into one event that never occurred, with the composed event scored for coherence and for the presence of details from all three sources.

Content scoring on the internal and external distinction of the autobiographical interview, where internal details are the episodic content of the event itself - what happened, who was there, where and when - and external details are semantic commentary, related facts, and drift to other events, with the requirement that a simulated future carry a comparable count of internal details to a remembered past, since the corpus's evidence is that damage to the constructive machinery shows up as fragmented and spatially incoherent description rather than as silence.

A leakage test, in which the elements offered for simulation are drawn from episodes the system holds, and any output that reproduces one whole stored episode rather than a recombination is scored as a failure of prospection even though it is a success of retrieval.

Counterfactual construction with a consequence, requiring not only the alternative outcome but the comparison that separates regret from disappointment - the alternative must be one the system's own earlier choice could have produced - and requiring that the comparison feed learning, so that one outcome yields more than one lesson.

Simulation used for choice, tested by a case in which the correct action depends on an outcome the system has never observed, so that only a run-forward model can supply the answer.

And cross-modal construction, since the corpus at chapter 64.3 treats imagery as spanning vision, hearing, smell, touch, and movement rather than sight alone.

An honest note, in the corpus tradition: the corpus records at chapter 64.3 that people with aphantasia, who report no conscious depictive imagery at all, nonetheless perform normally on many imagery tasks, so what these criteria test is the constructive computation and its products and not the vividness of any accompanying experience, which lies beyond this document as phenomenal experience does at faculty 14.


HOW TO USE THIS SPECIFICATION

Each faculty is an acceptance suite: implement the Layer 10 coalition from the catalogues, schedule it under Layer 11, then test the capability against the criteria.

The build order suggested by the dependency structure: perception and motor first, then attention and working memory, then the memory pair, then valuation and executive control, then language and social cognition, with learning and the workspace criterion tested throughout.

The specification is complete when a single running instance passes all seventeen suites in one continuous session including at least one full offline consolidation cycle.
