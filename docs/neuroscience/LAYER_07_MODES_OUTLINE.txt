LAYER 7, THE SYNAPTIC AND JUNCTIONAL LAYER: MODES AND MODE TRANSITIONS - THE OUTLINE

The per-type mode registers of every synaptic and junctional type, extracted from LAYER_07_MODES_MANUSCRIPT (which holds the full entries: registers, per-mode transfer functions, transition tables, and fault regimes).

Each entry below: the junction type, its source chapter key, and its named mode register.

Date of compilation: August 9, 2026.


ENTRY 1. THE GLUTAMATERGIC FAST EXCITATORY SYNAPSE (AMPA and NMDA Receptor Synapse) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 1)

◦ Silent (N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA)-only) - The Mute Beginner - a contact with NMDA receptors but no alpha-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptors, giving no current at rest and awaiting its first strengthening.

◦ Functional-facilitating - The Eager Repeater - a low-release-probability contact (about 0.1 to 0.3) that grows across a train.

◦ Functional-depressing - The First-Strike Contact - a high-release-probability contact (about 0.5 to 0.9) that fades across a train.

◦ Potentiated maintenance - The Written Weight - a standing long-term-potentiation state with extra AMPA receptors and an enlarged spine.

◦ Depressed maintenance - The Erased Weight - a standing long-term-depression state with AMPA receptors removed.

ENTRY 2. THE KAINATE-RECEPTOR SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 2)

◦ Sub-threshold single-spike - The Ignored Tap - lone spikes barely register in the slow current.

◦ Facilitated burst-integrating - The Slow-Burn Detonator - a train summates and, at the mossy fibre address, becomes a conditional detonator.

◦ Presynaptic-facilitating autoreceptor - The Low-Glutamate Amplifier - modest spillover raises release probability.

◦ Presynaptic-depressing autoreceptor - The High-Glutamate Throttle - high glutamate cuts release.

◦ Potentiated presynaptic maintenance - The Raised-Release Weight - a lasting rise in release probability after mossy fibre long-term potentiation.

ENTRY 3. THE METABOTROPIC GLUTAMATE SYNAPSE (mGluR G-Protein-Coupled Contact) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 3)

◦ Below-threshold quiet - The Watchtower at Ease - single quanta do not reach the annulus, so the system is honestly off.

◦ Group I sensitising - The Meaning-of-More Amplifier - postsynaptic excitation and store-calcium release when heavy traffic spills over.

◦ Group II/III braking - The Terminal Damper - presynaptic reduction of release probability by tens of percent.

◦ Long-term-depression induction - The Weakening Order - group I driving mGluR-dependent long-term depression.

◦ Metaplastic set-point - The Rule-Setter - a standing adjustment of the threshold for N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) plasticity.

ENTRY 4. THE GABA-A FAST INHIBITORY SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 4)

◦ Shunting-brake - The Silent Short Circuit - near the chloride reversal the synapse divides rather than subtracts.

◦ Hyperpolarising-brake - The Downward Pull - at depolarised potentials the same synapse pulls the cell down.

◦ Tonic conductance - The Standing Damp - extrasynaptic alpha5 and delta receptors hold a background inhibition with no time signature.

◦ Depolarising (developmental or injured) - The Reversed Brake - high internal chloride makes the current excitatory.

ENTRY 5. THE GABA-B SLOW METABOTROPIC SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 5)

◦ Sub-threshold quiet - The Handbrake Off - single interneuron spikes usually do nothing.

◦ Postsynaptic potassium-damp - The Long Hold - a G-protein-gated inwardly rectifying potassium (GIRK) conductance toward about minus 90 millivolts.

◦ Presynaptic release-throttle - The Source Damper - calcium-channel suppression cutting release probability by up to half or more.

◦ Neurogliaform blanket - The One-Spike Cloud - a single neurogliaform spike evoking a reliable slow inhibitory potential over a neighbourhood.

ENTRY 6. THE GLYCINERGIC SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 6)

◦ Fast-brake (depressing) - The Reflex Veto - the one adult working mode, a reliable brief chloride inhibition.

◦ Shunting-versus-hyperpolarising - The Battery-Set Face - the same current divides near the chloride reversal and subtracts when depolarised.

◦ Depolarising (foetal) - The Early Wiring Signal - a used developmental mode before the chloride gradient matures.

ENTRY 7. THE NICOTINIC CHOLINERGIC SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 7)

◦ Safety-factor firing - The Detonator - releases far more transmitter than the minimum needed, so the target fires every time.

◦ Ganglionic relay - The Command Pass - the same one-mode reliability at autonomic ganglia.

◦ Foetal endplate form - The Slow Early Channel - the gamma-subunit receptor with longer open time before the adult switch.

ENTRY 8. THE MUSCARINIC CHOLINERGIC JUNCTION (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 8)

◦ Drowsy-relay - The Lights-Low State - low ambient acetylcholine, standing adaptation and leak currents intact, gain low, learning gate shut.

◦ Attentive-encoder - The Lights-Up State - M1-driven closure of leak and adaptation potassium currents, raised gain, learning gate open.

◦ M2/M4 quietening - The Presynaptic Brake - Gi/o-coupled dampening of release and excitability.

ENTRY 9. THE DOPAMINERGIC SYNAPSE AND VARICOSITY (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 9)

◦ Tonic-level - The Background Set-Point - a slowly drifting ambient level read mainly by high-affinity D2 receptors.

◦ Phasic-burst - The Positive Surprise - a transient surge above baseline read mainly by low-affinity D1 receptors.

◦ Phasic-pause - The Negative Surprise - a dip below baseline dropping D1 and D2 occupancy.

◦ Autoreceptor-throttled - The Self-Limited Source - D2 autoreceptors cutting further release.

◦ Potentiated or depressed maintenance - The Taught Weight - lasting cortico-striatal weight change scaled by the error.

ENTRY 10. THE NORADRENERGIC VARICOSITY (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 10)

◦ Low-tonic drowsy - The Dimmed Cortex - too little noradrenaline, gain low.

◦ Mid-tonic focused - The Sharp Peak - the optimum of the inverted-U, favouring alpha-2 then beta actions, highest signal-to-noise.

◦ High-tonic distractible - The Overdriven Stress State - lower-affinity alpha-1 recruitment degrading performance.

◦ Phasic-salience burst - The Lean-In - a transient burst flagging a salient event and tagging memory.

◦ Autoreceptor-throttled - The Self-Limited Source - alpha-2 autoreceptors depressing further release.

ENTRY 11. THE SEROTONERGIC SYNAPSE AND VARICOSITY (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 11)

◦ Ambient set-point - The Mood Baseline - a slow tonic level biasing the whole forebrain's operating point.

◦ Per-cell excitatory read - The Approach Colour - cells rich in Gq/Gs-coupled receptors lifted by the same cloud.

◦ Per-cell inhibitory read - The Withdrawal Colour - cells rich in 5-HT1 (Gi/o) receptors lowered by it.

◦ Fast 5-HT3 event - The Rare Spark - a genuine fast cation current on separate hardware.

◦ Slow adaptation - The Weeks-Long Reset - receptor and autoreceptor adjustment behind antidepressant delay.

ENTRY 12. THE HISTAMINERGIC VARICOSITY (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 12)

◦ Waking-on broadcast - The House-Lights Up - a few-hertz tonic firing holding the forebrain awake.

◦ Sleep-off silence - The House-Lights Down - essentially silent in both non-rapid-eye-movement and rapid-eye-movement sleep.

◦ Autoreceptor-throttled - The Self-Limited Source - H3 feedback holding release within a band.

ENTRY 13. THE PEPTIDERGIC (DENSE-CORE VESICLE) SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 13)

◦ Sub-threshold silent - The Held Tongue - no release at low firing rates, honestly off.

◦ Burst-release broadcast - The Slow Annotation - steeply rising release once firing exceeds roughly 10 to 20 hertz, maximal near 50 hertz.

◦ Pool-depleted depression - The Empty Larder - the finite non-recycled pool drained across repeated bursts.

ENTRY 14. THE PURINERGIC SYNAPSE (ATP-and-Adenosine Junction) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 14)

◦ Fast P2X excitation - The Energy-Molecule Spark - adenosine triphosphate (ATP)-gated cation current within milliseconds.

◦ Slow P2Y modulation - The Purine Aside - G-protein-coupled signalling over hundreds of milliseconds.

◦ Adenosine A1 brake - The Sleep-Pressure Damper - accumulating inhibition as ATP is degraded.

◦ Integrated sleep-pressure - The Wakefulness Odometer - adenosine rising over hours of waking.

ENTRY 15. THE ENDOCANNABINOID RETROGRADE SIGNAL (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 15)

◦ Resting full-transmission - The Knob at Full - no suppression, baseline release probability.

◦ Transient suppression - The Backwards Turn-Down - depolarisation-induced suppression of inhibition or of excitation, a seconds-long graded cut.

◦ Endocannabinoid long-term depression - The Lasting Turn-Down - a persistent weakening when CB1 activation coincides with presynaptic activity.

ENTRY 16. THE NITRIC OXIDE RETROGRADE GASEOUS SIGNAL (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 16)

◦ Quiescent - The Silent Gas - no coincident N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) calcium, no production.

◦ Retrograde potentiating puff - The Backwards Whisper - a puff enhancing presynaptic release at the active synapse.

◦ Neighbour-recruiting spread - The Sideways Whisper - the same puff lowering the plasticity threshold of synapses within a few micrometres.

ENTRY 17. THE AXOSPINOUS SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 17)

◦ Silent - the Mute Room - an N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA)-only contact with no working alpha-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) current, waiting to be unsilenced (catalogued fully at entry 43).

◦ Baseline - the Held Weight - the ordinary functional contact transmitting at its current strength.

◦ Potentiated - the Grown Spine - the enlarged, AMPA-rich high state after learning.

◦ Depressed - the Shrunken Spine - the weakened low state after low-activity or anti-Hebbian input.

◦ Facilitating versus depressing - the Warming versus Tiring regime - the short-term temperament set by presynaptic release probability.

ENTRY 18. THE PERFORATED SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 18)

◦ Consolidated-reliable - the Scar Held - the near-ceiling weight with a fenestrated or horseshoe density that transmits reliably.

◦ De-perforating - the Fading Scar - the depressed, re-simplified form when the memory is dismantled.

ENTRY 19. THE AXODENDRITIC SHAFT SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 19)

◦ Facilitating - the Burst Detector - the low-probability regime that clamps the branch hardest when the network runs hot (the Martinotti route).

◦ Depressing - the Early Spender - the high-probability regime that fades across a train.

◦ Homeostatic-set weight - the Slow Trim - the standing inhibitory strength adjusted over hours.

ENTRY 20. THE AXOSOMATIC SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 20)

◦ Reliable brake - the Clock Hand - the high-probability parvalbumin-basket regime that phase-locks the target to the gamma cycle.

◦ Endocannabinoid-muted - the Hushed Brake - the transient silencing of the cholecystokinin channel by retrograde lipid.

◦ Homeostatic-set weight - the Excitability Tracker - slow adjustment tracking the target's excitability.

ENTRY 21. THE AXO-AXONIC INITIAL SEGMENT SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 21)

◦ Hyperpolarising veto - the Real Brake - the mature regime where the message raises threshold.

◦ Depolarising veto - the Paradoxical Push - the immature or pathological regime where high internal chloride inverts the sign.

◦ Structurally-tuned authority - the Moved Courtroom - the slow state in which the target relocates its own initial segment relative to the cartridges.

ENTRY 22. THE PRESYNAPTIC TERMINAL-ON-TERMINAL SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 22)

◦ Tonic floor - the Idling Damp - the ongoing presynaptic inhibition present even at rest.

◦ Deepened gate - the Tightened Knob - the summating, gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA)-B-engaged block that grows with use.

ENTRY 23. THE DENDRODENDRITIC RECIPROCAL SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 23)

◦ Recurrent inhibition - the Self-Answer - gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) fed back onto the same mitral dendrite that just excited the granule spine.

◦ Lateral inhibition - the Neighbour-Hush - GABA onto neighbouring mitral dendrites, sharpening contrast.

◦ Neurogenic refresh - the Rebuilt Spine - continuous integration of adult-born granule cells refining the network over days.

ENTRY 24. THE EN PASSANT VARICOSITY (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 24)

◦ Tonic small-molecule release - the Steady Glow - the low-rate background delivery of classical transmitter.

◦ Burst peptide and monoamine release - the Flare - the high-frequency mode that recruits dense-core and monoamine cargo.

ENTRY 25. THE SERIAL AND TRIADIC SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 25)

◦ Tonic relay - the Faithful Pass - the depolarised relay-cell state that reports input linearly.

◦ Burst mode - the Wake-Up Call - the T-type-de-inactivated state good at detection but poor at fidelity.

◦ F2 gating on - the Inner Gate - the metabotropic inhibitory arm that builds under sustained input.

ENTRY 26. THE NEUROMUSCULAR JUNCTION (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 26)

◦ Safety-factor relay - the One True Word - one muscle-fibre action potential per input spike, with a margin sized to absorb depression. A register of one, stated proudly.

ENTRY 27. THE CALYX OF HELD (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 27)

◦ High-fidelity timing relay - the Faithful Grasp - one output spike per input spike, jitter tens of microseconds. A register of one, stated proudly.

ENTRY 28. THE ENDBULB OF HELD (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 28)

◦ Onset-emphasising relay - the Faithful First Word - reliable transmission that runs in a standing depressed state to emphasise onsets and changes. A register of one, stated proudly.

ENTRY 29. THE HIPPOCAMPAL MOSSY FIBRE BOUTON (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 29)

◦ Sub-threshold at rest - the Sleeping Trigger - single resting spikes that often fail entirely.

◦ Facilitated detonator - the Woken Detonator - the burst-driven multivesicular regime that reliably discharges CA3 cells.

◦ Presynaptically potentiated - the Reset Trigger - a lastingly raised basal release probability after strong bursts.

ENTRY 30. THE CLIMBING FIBRE-PURKINJE SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 30)

◦ All-or-none teaching spike - the Struck Bell - the reliable complex spike delivered at about one hertz.

◦ Graded annotation - the Loudness of the Bell - the analogue spikelet count (2 to 6) and dendritic calcium amplitude that encode error strength (good confidence, revises the strict all-or-none view).

ENTRY 31. THE PARALLEL FIBRE-PURKINJE SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 31)

◦ Baseline - the Held Grain - the ordinary low-probability functional contact.

◦ Supervised-depressed - the Blamed Grain - the weakened state after coincidence with a climbing fibre teaching spike.

◦ Potentiated - the Blameless Grain - the strengthened state after activity without the teacher.

◦ Endocannabinoid-suppressed - the Self-Hushed Grain - the transient retrograde self-quieting after strong postsynaptic activity.

ENTRY 32. THE PHOTORECEPTOR RIBBON SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 32)

◦ Dark tonic release - the Belt Running - the depolarised, high-rate steady-stream regime.

◦ Light-suppressed release - the Belt Slowed - the hyperpolarised, low-rate regime.

ENTRY 33. THE HAIR CELL RIBBON SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 33)

◦ Onset burst - the First Cry - the large transient as the docked pool releases at stimulus onset.

◦ Sustained tireless release - the Steady Speech - the lower replenishment-limited rate that does not collapse.

◦ Spontaneous release - the Quiet Hum - the resting release that sets each fibre's spontaneous rate and threshold.

ENTRY 34. THE VESTIBULAR CALYX SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 34)

◦ Quantal glutamate - the Ordinary Voice - conventional vesicular transmission.

◦ Ephaptic field coupling - the Instant Whisper - direct electrical field coupling across the enclosed cleft.

◦ Potassium accumulation - the Rising Murmur - a slow depolarising drive as cleft potassium builds.

ENTRY 35. THE CEREBELLAR GLOMERULUS (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 35)

◦ Phasic relay - the Petal Opening - transmission of mossy-fibre bursts to granule cells.

◦ Tonic inhibitory normalisation - the Divisive Brake - the standing gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA)-A conductance that scales granule-cell gain.

◦ Limited plastic weight - the Faint Trim - the modest adjustability at the mossy-fibre-to-granule-cell synapse (low as a learning site, stated honestly).

ENTRY 36. THE THALAMIC TRIAD GLOMERULUS (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 36)

◦ Tonic relay - the Open Gate - the depolarised faithful-relay state passed to cortex.

◦ Burst mode - the Alarm Gate - the T-type-de-inactivated detecting state, secure but low-fidelity.

◦ F2 feedforward inhibition - the Sharpening Frame - the local inhibitory arm timing and suppressing poorly timed inputs.

◦ Developmental weight - the Sculpted Gate - the refined retinogeniculate strength after activity-dependent pruning.

ENTRY 37. THE C-BOUTON (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 37)

◦ Gain-neutral - the Idle - the resting state with no imposed excitability shift.

◦ Gain-boosted - the Turbo On - the maintained multiplicative increase in motoneuron firing gain during locomotion and effort.

ENTRY 38. THE NEURONAL GAP JUNCTION (THE ELECTRICAL SYNAPSE) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 38)

◦ Baseline Coupled - Standing Handshake - the resting regime in which junctional conductance holds steady and a scaled low-pass copy of the neighbour's voltage crosses continuously.

◦ Potentiated - Tightened Grip - a run-up regime in which activity and calcium-calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (an enzyme that attaches phosphate groups when calcium rises) raise open probability and coupling strengthens.

◦ Depressed - Loosened Grip - a run-down regime in which coupling weakens by 10 to 50 percent for tens of minutes or longer, the electrical synapse's long-term depression.

ENTRY 39. THE MIXED CHEMICAL-ELECTRICAL SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 39)

◦ Electrical Floor - The Instant Tick - the always-on gap-junctional component delivering a zero-delay spikelet.

◦ Chemical Facilitating - Warming Voice - the glutamatergic half building up transmitter during a train (glutamate is the brain's standard excitatory messenger).

◦ Chemical Depressing - Tiring Voice - the glutamatergic half running down as vesicles deplete.

ENTRY 40. THE GLIAL GAP JUNCTION PLAQUE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 40)

◦ Open Syncytium - Full Mesh - the default high-coupling regime welding hundreds of astrocytes into one continuous interior that shares potassium, glucose, lactate, and water (a syncytium is many cells behaving as one).

◦ Down-Regulated - Throttled Mesh - a reduced-coupling regime imposed by inflammation, endothelin, or acidification, isolating a region.

◦ State-Remodelled - Dialled Mesh - a physiologically retuned coupling extent, as in the hypothalamus during lactation.

ENTRY 41. THE PANNEXIN HEMICHANNEL (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 41)

◦ Closed - Sealed Valve - the near-permanent resting state, open probability near zero, the C-terminal tail plugging the pore.

◦ Reversibly Open - Signalling Puff - a graded, self-limiting opening that publishes adenosine triphosphate (the cell's energy currency doubling as a messenger) in a brief plume.

◦ Caspase-Locked Open - Death Broadcast - the terminal state in which cleavage by caspases (the executioner enzymes of programmed cell death) removes the autoinhibitory tail and locks the channel open to release find-me signals.

ENTRY 42. THE EPHAPTIC COUPLING SITE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 42)

◦ Field-Coupled - Standing Whisper - the sole regime, in which the source's currents impose a scaled, sign-inverting voltage shift on the target with zero delay.

ENTRY 43. THE SILENT (NMDA-ONLY) SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 43)

◦ Silent - Blank Tape - the resting state carrying N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptors but no functional alpha-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptors, so output at rest is exactly zero (cross-reference Chapter 1, of which this is the AMPA-lacking mode).

◦ Unsilenced - First Words - the functional state after AMPA receptors are inserted, indistinguishable from an ordinary Chapter 1 contact.

◦ Re-Silenced - Erased Tape - the reversion state after AMPA receptors are removed during long-term depression, returning the contact to the reserve.

ENTRY 44. THE NASCENT FILOPODIAL CONTACT (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 44)

◦ Searching - Groping Thread - the actin-driven filopodium extending and sampling passing axons with little or no synaptic machinery.

◦ Stabilising - Taking Hold - the committed state in which adhesion and scaffold assemble, overlapping the silent state of Chapter 43.

◦ Retracting - Letting Go - the withdrawal state ending in demolition of the trial contact.

ENTRY 45. THE TAGGED, RETRACTING SYNAPSE (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 45)

◦ Unmarked - Spared - the protected state, carrying the do-not-eat-me signal CD47 and transmitting normally.

◦ Tagged - Condemned - the state bearing deposited complement proteins (the immune system's ancient debris labels), read as a demolition order.

◦ Retracting - Being Eaten - the disassembly state as microglia nibble the terminal piece by piece and astrocytes phagocytose in parallel.

ENTRY 46. THE AUTONOMIC NEUROEFFECTOR JUNCTION (Visceral Motor Loose Contact) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 46)

◦ Resting Tone - Idle Streetlight - the low-rate baseline in which sparse, often-failing releases set a standing tissue tone.

◦ Facilitating - Warming String - the built-up regime in which repeated firing accumulates transmitter and the sheet reads a rising command.

ENTRY 47. THE SPLANCHNIC-CHROMAFFIN SYNAPSE (Preganglionic-to-Adrenal-Medulla Contact) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 47)

◦ Basal Trickle - Idle Drip - the low-frequency regime releasing freely-soluble catecholamines through a restricted fusion pore.

◦ Flight-or-Fight Flood - Alarm Surge - the high-drive regime in which the fusion pore is driven to an expanded state and peptide co-transmitters join the catecholamine bolus.

ENTRY 48. THE NEUROHAEMAL JUNCTION (Neurosecretory-Ending-on-Bloodstream Contact) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 48)

◦ Sub-Threshold Steady - Quiet Drip - low-rate firing that releases little peptide per spike.

◦ Phasic Burst - Pulsed Release - the efficient regime of long spiking episodes that maximise hormone output.

◦ Reflex Burst - Let-Down Pulse - the intense synchronised burst, as in the oxytocin milk-ejection reflex.

ENTRY 49. THE NEUROPOD-VAGAL EPITHELIAL SYNAPSE (Gut-Sensor-to-Vagus Contact) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 49)

◦ Fast Glutamatergic - Millisecond Line - the point-to-point vesicular channel that follows luminal stimulus onset closely.

◦ Slow Peptide - Trailing Word - the dense-core co-signal (peptides such as cholecystokinin) that lags the fast report.

ENTRY 50. THE EFFERENT SYNAPSE ONTO THE HAIR CELL (Olivocochlear-to-Outer-Hair-Cell Contact) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 50)

◦ Resting Gain - Amplifier Free - low or no efferent drive, the outer hair cell left to boost faint sounds.

◦ Facilitated Inhibition - Turned Down - the built-up regime in which sustained efferent firing strengthens the hyperpolarising brake.

ENTRY 51. THE MERKEL CELL-NEURITE COMPLEX (Touch-Cell-to-Afferent Contact) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 51)

◦ Resting - Untouched - no indentation, minimal release.

◦ Sustaining - Holding the Shape - the maintained-touch regime delivering prolonged excitatory drive that keeps the slowly adapting afferent firing.

ENTRY 52. THE NEURO-IMMUNE JUNCTION (Nerve-to-Immune-Cell Contact) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 52)

◦ Quiescent Contact - Resting Watch - baseline apposition with little transmitter release and unbiased immune tone.

◦ Sympathetic Damping - Cooling Word - noradrenaline released onto lymphocytes biasing immune tone, typically restraining.

◦ Sensory Priming - Heating Word - sensory neuropeptides (substance P, calcitonin gene-related peptide) driving mast cells toward mediator release and neurogenic inflammation.

ENTRY 53. THE TRIPARTITE SYNAPSE ENSHEATHMENT (Astrocyte-Wrapped Chemical Synapse) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 53)

◦ Permissive wrap - the Green Light - high D-serine (the co-agonist that the N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor requires alongside glutamate) occupancy, so the wrapped synapse may potentiate.

◦ Restrictive wrap - the Red Light - adenosine tone dominates and clamps release, converting potentiation conditions into depression.

◦ Retracted coverage - the Withdrawn Leaflet - a life-cycle and state mode in which the leaflet pulls back and neighbouring synapses become directly juxtaposed, seen physiologically around oxytocin neurons in lactation.

ENTRY 54. THE NEURON-OPC SYNAPSE (Axon-Oligodendrocyte Precursor Synapse) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 54)

◦ Facilitating readout - the Pattern Listener - theta-burst-favouring integration of quantal drive while the precursor divides.

◦ Depressing readout - the Fatiguing Line - short-term depression under rapid trains.

◦ Proliferative standing mode - the Waiting Surveyor - integrated activity below threshold, precursor keeps dividing and listening.

◦ Differentiating mode - the Committed Wrapper - integrated calcium load crosses threshold, precursor differentiates and the synapse is dismantled (a used developmental transition, not a fault).

ENTRY 55. THE AXO-MYELINIC SYNAPSE (Axon-to-Myelin Junction) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 55)

◦ Demand-matched supply - the Fuel Line - periaxonal glutamate tracks spike rate and lactate delivery rises to match.

◦ Remodelling mode - the Speed Tuner - sustained demand nudges sheath thickness and internode geometry, adjusting delay.

◦ Idle mode - the Dumb Cable - low traffic, minimal supply, delay held.

ENTRY 56. THE PERISYNAPTIC SCHWANN CAP (Terminal Schwann Cell Ensheathment of the Neuromuscular Junction) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 56)

◦ Health mode - the Watching Foreman - sedentary supervision, reading transmission and lightly modulating release.

◦ Repair mode - the Pathfinder - after denervation the cell de-differentiates, extends processes, and guides the returning axon (a genuinely used mode, so admitted).

ENTRY 57. THE MICROGLIA-SYNAPSE CONTACT (Microglial Process-Synapse Apposition) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 57)

◦ Surveillance mode - the Hourly Round - brief contacts of about five minutes that in health enhance the contacted synapse's activity.

◦ Triage mode - the Lengthened Watch - visits lengthen and multiply within minutes when local activity rises or injury signals appear.

◦ Trogocytic mode - the Nibble - partial engulfment of a bouton, weakening but not deleting the synapse.

◦ Engulfment mode - the Condemnation - whole-synapse phagocytosis of a tagged, weak input (used developmental pruning, so admitted).

ENTRY 58. THE BETA CELL GAP JUNCTION NETWORK (Islet Connexin-36 Electrical Coupling) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 58)

◦ Coupled sheet - the Chorus - open channels synchronise voltage and calcium across the islet into one clean insulin pulse.

◦ Loosened coupling - the Scattered Crowd - reduced open-channel fraction lets heterogeneous thresholds re-emerge.

◦ Rest gating - the Silenced Sheet - at low glucose, coupling helps hold the quiet, non-secreting resting state across the islet.

ENTRY 59. THE PITUITARY GAP JUNCTION WEB (Folliculostellate Connexin-43 Coupling) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 59)

◦ Coupled web - the Gland-Spanning Relay - phosphorylated, docked channels carry a regenerative calcium wave across the whole gland.

◦ Uncoupled web - the Severed Lattice - dephosphorylated connexin-43 breaks the relay into local twitches.

ENTRY 60. THE GRANULOSA-OOCYTE GAP JUNCTION (Connexin-37 Trans-Zonal Coupling) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 60)

◦ Open coupled pipe - the Nurse Line - channels open, feeding the egg and delivering the cyclic nucleotides that hold meiosis arrested.

◦ Closed junction - the Cut Line - abrupt closure at the ovulatory surge stops the arresting signal and releases the egg.

ENTRY 61. THE PARACRINE JUNCTION (Local Diffusion Signalling) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 61)

◦ Silent neighbourhood - the Quiet Word - no secretion, no local signal.

◦ Excitatory broadcast - the Prompting Word - a messenger that raises nearby target activity.

◦ Inhibitory broadcast - the Restraining Word - a messenger that quietens neighbours, as somatostatin quietens the islet.

ENTRY 62. THE AUTOCRINE LOOP (Self-Signalling) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 62)

◦ Positive loop - the Self-Commit - the returning messenger reinforces the cell's own state and can lock it in.

◦ Negative loop - the Self-Governor - the returning messenger damps the cell toward a set point.

◦ Silent loop - the Quiet Self - no self-secretion, no feedback.

ENTRY 63. THE JUXTACRINE CONTACT JUNCTION (Membrane-Bound Ligand-Receptor Signalling) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 63)

◦ No contact - the Open Hand - membranes apart, no signal.

◦ Instructive contact - the Fate Handshake - Notch engagement changes gene expression in the receiving cell.

◦ Repulsive or attractive contact - the Sorting Handshake - Eph-ephrin engagement, bidirectional, reshaping both cells' cytoskeletons.

ENTRY 64. THE HORMONE-RECEPTOR VIRTUAL SYNAPSE (Endocrine Signalling at a Distance) (keyed to synaptics catalogue Chapter 64)

◦ Pulsatile broadcast - the Drumbeat - hormone delivered in discrete pulses that keep the receiver responsive.

◦ Continuous broadcast - the Flood - sustained delivery that drives the receiver into desensitisation.

◦ Sensitised receiver - the Open Ear - up-regulated, coupled receptors giving full response.

◦ Desensitised receiver - the Deaf Ear - uncoupled and down-regulated receptors giving a blunted response.
