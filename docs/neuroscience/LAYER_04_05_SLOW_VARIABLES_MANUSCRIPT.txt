INTRODUCTION: WHAT THE WEIGHT IS MADE OF

The upper volumes of this corpus say, again and again, that the weight is the memory.

This volume answers the question they leave open: what is the weight made of, given that every protein at a synapse turns over in hours to weeks while memories last for decades?

The answer is the SLOW VARIABLES: machinery that stores information in self-renewing arrangements of molecules - switches that re-mark themselves faster than turnover erases them, tags that allocate consolidation, structure that outlives chemistry, and chromatin that holds configuration for a lifetime.

This is deliberately not a full catalogue of Layers 4 and 5, which remain substrate; it is the dossier of their cognitive elite, in eighteen chapters and four parts: the bistable switches, the tagging and capture machinery, the structural consolidation machinery, and the nuclear and genomic machinery.

Each chapter runs a template built for storage engineering: the molecular mechanism, THE PERSISTENCE TRICK (exactly how the store outlives its molecules), the hosts above, the write, read, and erase operations, capacity and fidelity, interactions, diseases, the publish-subscribe interface, and the engineering note - because for a synthetic mind these chapters name its checkpointing, write-allocation, storage-tiering, and renormalisation subsystems.

Sixteen chapters are (COG) - the highest proportion in the corpus, by construction - and close with ENGLISH READABLE CODE and a runnable Python CODE SAMPLE; the two (NON-COG) chapters are the power supply and the honest open question.


PART ONE. THE BISTABLE SWITCHES

Molecular flip-flops that hold one bit longer than their own molecules survive.

Every protein at a synapse is torn down and rebuilt within hours to weeks, yet the memories those synapses carry can last a lifetime, so the stored bit cannot be any single molecule.

The switches of this part solve the paradox by holding their state in a self-renewing arrangement rather than in matter: a loop that keeps re-writing itself faster than turnover can wipe it.

Each is a candidate for the physical unit of memory, the smallest thing whose two settings mean remembered or not.

Read them as competing and cooperating answers to one engineering question: how do you build a latch out of parts that are all being replaced while it runs.


CHAPTER 1. THE CAMKII SWITCH (Calcium-Calmodulin-Dependent Protein Kinase Two Bistable Ring) (The Self-Rewriting Ring) (COG)

Twelve identical arms locked into a double-stacked wheel sit under a single synapse, and when a wave of calcium washes over them the arms reach across and stamp a permanent-seeming mark on one another.

The mark should fade as each arm is dismantled and replaced, but the wheel keeps re-stamping every fresh arm as it arrives, so the setting outlives every molecule that ever carried it.

Self names the trick: the enzyme is its own scribe, copying its state onto its own new parts.

Rewriting names the persistence: nothing is preserved, everything is continuously re-made.

Ring names the shape, the twelve-subunit holoenzyme that makes neighbour-to-neighbour copying geometrically possible.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

Calcium-calmodulin-dependent protein kinase two, abbreviated CaMKII, is an enzyme that attaches phosphate groups to other proteins, and in its brain form it assembles as a ring of twelve subunits arranged as two stacked six-membered wheels.

What it stores is one bit at one synapse: the ring is either mostly phosphorylated on a critical site, meaning potentiated, or mostly bare, meaning basal.

That single bit is the leading candidate for the physical engram unit, the atom of a stored memory.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

Each subunit has a catalytic head, a regulatory segment that normally folds back and blocks the head, and a hub domain that holds the ring together.

When calcium ions bind the small messenger protein calmodulin, the calcium-calmodulin complex clamps onto a subunit, pulls the blocking segment away, and switches the head on.

An active head then reaches to its immediate neighbour in the ring and attaches a phosphate to that neighbour's threonine at position 286, an event called autophosphorylation, which jams the neighbour permanently on even after calcium leaves.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The bit is held by autocatalysis fighting a phosphatase, and it is rescued from turnover by subunit exchange.

Protein phosphatase one, abbreviated PP1, continuously strips the phosphate off threonine 286, so an isolated modified subunit would eventually be reset; the ring survives because an already-active subunit re-phosphorylates any neighbour that PP1 has just cleaned, and basal calcium is enough to sustain this at the potentiated setting but not to start it.

The deeper rescue, hypothesised by John Lisman and confirmed only recently, is subunit exchange: worn subunits leave the ring and fresh unmarked ones dock in, whereupon the surviving active subunits immediately stamp the newcomers, so the on-state is copied onto brand-new protein and the memory jumps from old matter to new matter without ever being erased.

An engineer could copy the design as a latch whose gates rewrite each new gate faster than a leak current can discharge it.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

CaMKII rings sit in the postsynaptic density, the protein slab under an excitatory synapse, densest on the dendritic spines of glutamate-using principal cells.

In the Layer 6 catalogues those hosts are above all the cortical pyramidal neurons, the L2/3 IT (layer two-three intra-telencephalic) context-weaving cell chief among them, together with hippocampal CA1 and CA3 pyramidal cells and dentate granule cells.

In the Layer 7 junction catalogue the host is the mature glutamatergic spine synapse, the asymmetric axospinous contact.

TIMESCALES:

Calcium entry lasts milliseconds; autophosphorylation locks in over seconds; a single subunit's phosphate is turned over by PP1 in minutes.

Whole subunits are replaced over hours to a few days, and the ring as an object is renewed within roughly a week.

The stored bit, if the switch model holds, persists for months to years across all that turnover.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

A write is any event that drives calcium high and fast through the N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor, an ion channel that opens only when the cell is already depolarised and glutamate is present, so it reports coincidence.

A strong high-frequency burst, or the pre-before-post timing of spike-timing-dependent potentiation, pushes enough subunits over threshold that neighbour-to-neighbour stamping becomes self-sustaining and the ring flips to on.

THE READ OPERATION:

The stored setting is read out because an on ring anchors and organises the receptor slots that set synaptic strength: it holds more alpha-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid receptors, abbreviated AMPA receptors, in place, so the same glutamate release produces a larger response.

The bit is therefore read continuously and passively, simply by the synapse conducting current at its potentiated gain.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

Physiologically the switch is reset when calcium stays low long enough, or when PP1 is disinhibited, for the phosphatase to win the race against re-stamping, which is the molecular side of long-term depression.

Experimentally, inhibiting CaMKII activity or forcing the threonine-286 site to a non-phosphorylatable form prevents writing and destabilises the held state.

Erasure here is honest and graded: because the bit is a population statistic over twelve subunits, partial resets and drift are possible rather than a single clean toggle.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

The classical model treats one ring as one bit, but subunit exchange between rings lets activation spread and raises effective information retention, and modelling suggests a spine's CaMKII population can hold rather more than a single clean bit under noise.

Fidelity is set by the margin between the re-stamping rate and the PP1 stripping rate: at the potentiated setting the error rate is low enough for year-scale retention in models, though real drift rates in vivo are not firmly measured and remain an honest unknown.

Estimates of subunits per ring are firm at twelve; estimates of rings per spine run into the hundreds.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

CaMKII sets the synaptic tag of Chapter 5 by locally reorganising the cytoskeleton, so the switch and the address system share machinery.

It cooperates with the receptor slot and trafficking system by physically anchoring AMPA receptors, and it works upstream of the actin remodelling that enlarges a spine, tying the fast bit to the slower structural stores.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

The ratio of the alpha to the beta CaMKII subunit shifts across development and tunes how readily synapses potentiate, contributing to the heightened plasticity of young brains.

In ageing, changes in CaMKII regulation and in PP1 balance are associated with weaker and less stable potentiation.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Calcium-calmodulin-dependent kinases are ancient and shared across animals, but the twelve-subunit holoenzyme with efficient neighbour autophosphorylation is elaborated in the nervous systems of complex animals, where its ring geometry is exactly what makes a bistable latch possible.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Mutations in the CaMKII genes cause forms of intellectual disability, underscoring that a broken memory latch impairs cognition directly.

Dysregulated CaMKII signalling is implicated in excitotoxic neuronal death after stroke and in seizure disorders, and altered CaMKII function appears in Angelman syndrome.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Postsynaptic calcium concentration, ranging from about 0.1 micromolar at rest to tens of micromolar in a spine during a burst; calmodulin occupancy; N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor open probability.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Phosphorylation state of the AMPA receptor anchoring assembly, hence synaptic gain; a tag signal to capture machinery.

• STATE.

◦ Fraction of the twelve subunits phosphorylated at threonine 286, a number between 0 and 1 with two stable basins near 0 and near 1.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Autophosphorylation rate; PP1 concentration and its saturating activity; subunit exchange rate; basal calcium set-point.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Single-site dephosphorylation minutes; subunit turnover hours to days; ring renewal about a week; held bit months to years.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In intellectual-disability CaMKII mutations the latch cannot be set or held, and learning is impaired.

◦ Under stroke-level calcium overload the kinase drives excitotoxic damage, a memory switch turned into a death signal.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

The engineering name is bistable_weight_latch, a per-synapse component that subscribes to a calcium-coincidence signal and publishes a held weight-gain bit.

Its nearest engineering analogue is an EEPROM cell, an electrically erasable memory cell that retains its value without power, or equally a static latch refreshed by its own feedback.

The biological difference is decisive: the EEPROM cell keeps the same physical charge trap, whereas bistable_weight_latch keeps no physical part at all, copying its bit onto continuously replaced subunits so that the store survives total material turnover.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to hold one bit in a ring of twelve subunits while both random noise and whole-subunit replacement try to erase it.
Create a number variable called subunits with initial value 12.
Create a number variable called active count with initial value the number of subunits currently phosphorylated.
Create a number variable called basal calcium with initial value 0.1.
Create a number variable called stamp rate, the chance an active subunit re-phosphorylates a cleaned neighbour.
Create a number variable called strip rate, the chance protein phosphatase one removes a phosphate.
For each time step, perform the following actions.
    For each subunit, if it is bare and at least one neighbour is active, phosphorylate it with probability stamp rate.
    For each subunit, if it is active, remove its phosphate with probability strip rate.
    With a small probability, remove one subunit and dock a fresh bare subunit in its place.
    If the fresh subunit has an active neighbour, phosphorylate it with probability stamp rate.
    Set active count to the number of active subunits.
End the loop.
Report whether active count stayed above six, meaning the memory held.
The procedure is complete.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the claim is that a self-stamping loop can beat both stripping noise and subunit replacement, and a short stochastic simulation shows the held bit surviving turnover with no ceremony.

# CaMKII/PP1 bistable ring holding one bit under noise and subunit exchange.
# Active subunits re-phosphorylate cleaned neighbours faster than PP1 strips
# and than turnover replaces them (Lisman; subunit exchange, eLife 2018).
import random
N = 12
STAMP, STRIP, EXCHANGE = 0.9, 0.15, 0.05   # per-step probabilities
state = [1] * N                             # start potentiated (all active)

def neighbours_active(s, i):
    return s[(i - 1) % N] or s[(i + 1) % N]

for step in range(100000):                  # ~ far longer than one subunit lasts
    for i in range(N):
        if not state[i] and neighbours_active(state, i) and random.random() < STAMP:
            state[i] = 1                     # re-stamp a cleaned neighbour
    for i in range(N):
        if state[i] and random.random() < STRIP:
            state[i] = 0                     # PP1 strips a phosphate
    if random.random() < EXCHANGE:           # turnover: swap in fresh bare subunit
        i = random.randrange(N)
        state[i] = 1 if neighbours_active(state, i) and random.random() < STAMP else 0

print("active subunits after full turnover:", sum(state), "of", N)
print("memory held" if sum(state) > N // 2 else "memory lost")

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A twelve-armed ring that keeps a single bit by stamping its own freshly delivered parts, so the setting is years old while no molecule in it is more than a week old.


CHAPTER 2. THE PKM-ZETA LOOP (Protein Kinase M Zeta Self-Sustaining Maintenance Enzyme) (The Self-Feeding Kinase) (COG)

Most enzymes are switched on by a signal and switched off when it passes, but this one keeps making more of itself and stays on for as long as the memory it guards.

A stimulated synapse begins translating a headless, always-active kinase from a messenger that was waiting on the spot, and that kinase props open the very door that lets more of itself be made.

Self names the loop: the enzyme's product is the condition for the enzyme's own continued production.

Feeding names the persistence: the store is held not by a stable molecule but by a supply line that keeps refilling.

Kinase names the actor, an enzyme that keeps receptor numbers high by phosphorylation.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

Protein kinase M zeta, abbreviated PKM-zeta, is a truncated always-on form of an enzyme in the protein kinase C family, lacking the regulatory part that normally keeps such enzymes off.

What it stores is the maintained strength of an already-potentiated synapse: not the decision to potentiate, which the CaMKII switch of Chapter 1 makes, but the holding of that gain over days and weeks.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

At a stimulated synapse a waiting messenger ribonucleic acid, abbreviated mRNA, is translated into PKM-zeta on the spot rather than the full-length parent enzyme.

Because PKM-zeta has no regulatory brake it is constitutively active, and it acts to keep AMPA receptors trafficked into and retained at the synapse, which is what a strong synapse is made of.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The trick is a self-sustaining synthesis loop, autocatalysis at the level of production rather than chemistry.

PKM-zeta enhances its own further synthesis by maintaining the potentiated state and the trafficking that supplies the machinery for making more of it, so once the loop is running it keeps itself topped up despite the ordinary destruction of each PKM-zeta molecule.

An engineer would recognise a positive-feedback regeneration circuit: the output signal is fed back to keep the generator running, so the stored value is defined by an ongoing process, and cutting the loop for even a short window collapses the value permanently.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The loop runs in the dendritic spines of glutamatergic principal cells, prominently hippocampal CA1 pyramidal neurons and cortical pyramidal cells including the L2/3 IT context-weaving neuron.

In the Layer 7 catalogue the host junction is again the mature excitatory axospinous synapse, at its late-maintenance phase.

TIMESCALES:

Local translation of PKM-zeta ramps up over roughly the first hour after strong induction.

Each PKM-zeta molecule is short-lived, degraded within hours, which is precisely why continuous resynthesis is required.

The maintained memory the loop supports lasts from days to at least a month in rodent studies, and by inference much longer.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

A write is the same strong potentiating stimulus that flips the upstream switches, which lifts the local translational brake and starts PKM-zeta synthesis at the tagged synapse.

Once synthesis crosses the level needed to sustain the feedback, the loop latches into its running state.

THE READ OPERATION:

The setting is read as maintained synaptic gain: while the loop runs, AMPA receptor numbers stay elevated and the synapse keeps its strong response to glutamate.

Reading is passive and continuous, identical in kind to reading the CaMKII bit but sustained by a supply line rather than a stamping ring.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

Injecting a peptide called zeta inhibitory peptide, abbreviated ZIP, blocks the kinase activity and, in many studies, erases established long-term potentiation and long-term memories, which is the strongest single piece of evidence that the loop is a maintenance store.

The erasure claim carries an honest and important caveat: PKM-zeta knockout mice retain normal potentiation and memory, and ZIP still erases memory in those mice, showing ZIP is not specific and that a closely related enzyme, PKC-iota/lambda, can compensate.

The current reconciliation, supported by 2025 double-knockout work, is that PKM-zeta normally maintains memory but the atypical protein kinase C family as a whole is the real durable actor, with PKC-iota/lambda substituting when PKM-zeta is absent, so erasure is genuine but the single-molecule story was too simple.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

The loop is analog rather than digital: it holds a maintained gain level, not a clean bit, so its natural capacity is a graded strength with limited resolution.

Fidelity depends on keeping synthesis above the self-sustaining threshold; drop below it during a vulnerable window and the value is lost, so the store is robust once latched but has a definite failure edge.

Quantitative bits-per-synapse figures are not established and are an honest unknown.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

PKM-zeta depends on the local translation stations of the tagging-and-capture machinery for its on-site synthesis, and on the receptor slot system for the AMPA receptors it maintains.

It operates downstream of the CaMKII write and in parallel with structural consolidation, forming the maintenance tier above the initial switch.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

The atypical protein kinase C maintenance system matures with the synaptic apparatus and is implicated in the long-term retention that improves through childhood.

Its dysregulation is studied in age-related memory decline, though the developmental profile is less charted than that of CaMKII.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

The protein kinase C family is ancient, and the trick of generating a brakeless catalytic fragment from an internal promoter is a repurposing of that family for persistent signalling, with the atypical isoforms specialised for maintenance roles in the nervous system.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Because the loop maintains stored strength, its persistence is implicated in the durability of pathological memories, including the entrenched associations of addiction and post-traumatic stress, where a maintenance store that will not erase is the problem.

Loss of atypical protein kinase C function is studied in cognitive decline.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Local translational activation at a tagged synapse; upstream potentiation signal; substrate supply for AMPA receptor trafficking.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Maintained AMPA receptor number and hence sustained synaptic gain.

• STATE.

◦ Running PKM-zeta concentration at the synapse, a graded level with a self-sustaining threshold.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Synthesis rate; degradation rate, with molecular half-life of hours; self-sustaining threshold; compensation gain from PKC-iota/lambda.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Synthesis ramp about one hour; molecule half-life hours; maintained memory days to a month or more.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In addiction and post-traumatic stress the loop holds a maladaptive association too durably to extinguish.

◦ If synthesis dips below threshold during a vulnerable window, the maintained value collapses and the memory is lost.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

The engineering name is renewal_loop, a per-synapse maintenance daemon that subscribes to a potentiation-and-translation signal and publishes a sustained weight-gain value.

Its nearest engineering analogue is dynamic random-access memory refresh, the periodic rewriting that keeps a leaky memory cell from decaying.

The biological difference is that dynamic memory refresh copies a stored value it reads out first, whereas renewal_loop has no separately stored value to read: the value is the running process itself, so interrupting the refresh does not merely risk a bit, it destroys the only copy.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to maintain a synaptic strength by a self-feeding synthesis loop that must keep its product above a threshold or lose the memory.
Create a number variable called enzyme level with initial value 0.
Create a number variable called threshold with initial value 1.0.
Create a number variable called synthesis gain with initial value 0.4.
Create a number variable called degrade rate with initial value 0.3.
Create a truth value called inhibitor present with initial value false.
For each time step, perform the following actions.
    If inhibitor present is true, set synthesis to 0.
    Otherwise, if enzyme level is above threshold, set synthesis to synthesis gain, else set synthesis to a small seed value.
    Increase enzyme level by synthesis.
    Decrease enzyme level by enzyme level multiplied by degrade rate.
    Record whether enzyme level is still above threshold, meaning the memory is maintained.
End the loop.
Report the final maintenance state.
The procedure is complete.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the claim is that a positive-feedback synthesis loop latches above a threshold and collapses if briefly blocked, and a short loop shows both the latch and the fragility.

# PKM-zeta self-sustaining maintenance loop with a threshold and an inhibitor.
# The enzyme props open its own synthesis; a brief block collapses it for good
# (ZIP erasure; note PKC-iota/lambda compensation, eLife 2016 / 2025).
THRESH, GAIN, DEGRADE, SEED = 1.0, 0.6, 0.3, 0.05
level = 2.0                                  # start with an established memory

def step(level, inhibited):
    synth = 0.0 if inhibited else (GAIN * level if level > THRESH else SEED)
    level = level + synth
    level = level - level * DEGRADE
    return level

for t in range(60):
    inhibited = 20 <= t < 26                 # a brief ZIP-like block mid-run
    level = step(level, inhibited)
    if t in (19, 25, 59):
        print(f"t={t:2d}  level={level:5.2f}  "
              f"{'MAINTAINED' if level > THRESH else 'LOST'}")

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A headless always-on kinase that keeps memories alive by continuously re-making itself, and that a single peptide can wipe out, with the honest asterisk that a cousin enzyme can stand in.


CHAPTER 3. THE CPEB PRION-LIKE SWITCH (Cytoplasmic Polyadenylation Element Binding Protein Three Conformational Store) (The Benign Prion Stamp) (COG)

The word prion usually names a catastrophe, a misshapen protein that forces its neighbours into the same ruinous fold until tissue dies.

This chapter is about the same trick turned to a useful end: a synaptic protein that, once it clumps into a self-templating fold, keeps converting fresh copies to the same fold and thereby marks a synapse as active for as long as the clump persists.

Benign names the surprise: a prion mechanism used for memory rather than disease.

Prion names the mechanism: inheritance of a shape, not of a sequence.

Stamp names the persistence: the aggregated form is a mould that presses its own shape onto every new molecule.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

Cytoplasmic polyadenylation element binding protein three, abbreviated CPEB3, is a protein that controls whether certain messenger ribonucleic acids at a synapse are translated into protein.

What it stores is a synapse-specific mark of prior activity: whether CPEB3 at that spine is in its soluble repressing form or its aggregated activating form, which in turn sets whether local plasticity messengers are being translated.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

In its ordinary soluble state CPEB3 represses the translation of its target messengers, including messengers for proteins that build and strengthen synapses.

On synaptic stimulation CPEB3 converts into an aggregated, self-templating state that instead activates translation of those same targets, so the switch changes the sign of its own effect on protein synthesis.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The trick is conformational templating: the aggregated form is a mould that converts newly made soluble CPEB3 into more of the aggregated form, so the state is inherited by shape rather than stored in any one molecule.

The state is normally held off by a chemical brake, the small protein modifier SUMO, abbreviated from small ubiquitin-like modifier; SUMOylated CPEB3 stays soluble and repressive, and stimulation reduces SUMOylation, releasing CPEB3 to aggregate.

An engineer would see a self-propagating structural template with an inhibitory lock: normally clamped soluble, and once unlocked it seeds an aggregate that recruits and reshapes every fresh subunit, so the store outlives its molecules because the mould, not the matter, is what persists.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

CPEB3 acts in the dendrites and spines of hippocampal principal neurons, and its invertebrate counterparts ApCPEB in the sea slug Aplysia and Orb2 in the fruit fly Drosophila act at plastic synapses there.

In the Layer 6 and Layer 7 catalogues the hosts are glutamatergic principal cells and their mature excitatory spine synapses undergoing consolidation.

TIMESCALES:

De-SUMOylation and initial aggregation develop over minutes to hours after stimulation.

The aggregated template, once seeded, is self-renewing and can in principle persist indefinitely as long as fresh CPEB3 is supplied, which is the point of the mechanism.

The memories it supports are the long-lasting, protein-synthesis-dependent kind lasting days and beyond.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

A write is a synaptic stimulus strong enough to lower CPEB3 SUMOylation at that spine, permitting the first aggregation seed to form and then to template.

Because seeding is local, the write is addressed to the stimulated synapse.

THE READ OPERATION:

The state is read as a translational setting: an aggregated CPEB3 store keeps its target messengers being translated, supplying the local proteins that sustain a strengthened synapse.

Reading is therefore indirect, through the ongoing synthesis the aggregate licenses.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

Physiological erasure would require dissolving or diluting the aggregate faster than it templates, for instance by re-SUMOylation of new subunits or by clearance machinery removing the seed.

Experimentally, blocking CPEB3 aggregation, or forcing a permanently SUMO-fused CPEB3 that cannot aggregate, prevents the persistent form and impairs memory persistence.

Honest note: prion-like stores are inherently hard to erase cleanly, and the routes and rates of physiological dissolution in the mammalian brain are not well quantified.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

The switch is effectively a local one-bit store, aggregated or not, per pool of CPEB3 at a synapse.

Fidelity is high for maintenance because templating strongly favours the aggregated state once seeded, but the fidelity of not seeding by accident, that is, the spontaneous false-write rate, is the critical unknown and is presumably kept low by the SUMO brake.

Bits-per-synapse and drift rates are not established.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

CPEB3 controls the supply of the very plasticity products, the capture cargo of Chapter 6, that other slow variables consume, so it sits upstream of local rebuilding.

It cooperates with the synaptic tag and the local translation stations, and its output feeds the structural consolidation machinery that turns strength into shape.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

CPEB-family translational control operates from early development in patterning and synapse formation, and is redeployed for memory in the mature brain.

Its long-lifespan behaviour, as a candidate for truly durable maintenance, is one reason it is studied as a substrate of remote, decades-old memory.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

The functional-prion strategy for memory is conserved across a sea slug, a fly, and a mouse, which is strong evidence that a self-templating conformational store was recruited for memory very early and retained, a rare example of a beneficial prion mechanism.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

The same aggregation chemistry that serves memory borders the pathological aggregation of neurodegeneration, and understanding what keeps CPEB3 aggregation benign and reversible bears on why other aggregates are toxic.

Disrupted CPEB3 function is studied in memory impairment.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Synaptic stimulation lowering SUMOylation; supply of soluble CPEB3; local target messengers.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Translational activation of plasticity-related messengers at the spine.

• STATE.

◦ Fraction of local CPEB3 in the aggregated form, effectively a bit with a soluble basin and an aggregated basin.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ SUMOylation rate as the inhibitory lock; seeding rate; templating conversion rate; soluble CPEB3 supply rate.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Aggregation onset minutes to hours; templated maintenance open-ended given fresh supply; supported memory days and longer.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ If aggregation cannot form, as with SUMO-locked CPEB3, memory fails to persist.

◦ If aggregation seeds spuriously or spreads unchecked, it borders pathological aggregation.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

The engineering name is conformational_template_store, a per-synapse durable store that subscribes to a de-repression signal and publishes a translational-activation state that it copies onto fresh subunits by shape.

Its nearest engineering analogue is a write-once seed that thereafter self-replicates its pattern, similar to how a crystallisation seed fixes a phase.

The biological difference is that the store is inheritable by conformation with an active inhibitory lock, so it is both self-propagating and gated, unlike an inert seed.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to keep a synaptic bit by letting an aggregated form convert fresh soluble copies to itself, held off until an inhibitory lock is released.
Create a number variable called soluble with initial value 100.
Create a number variable called aggregated with initial value 0.
Create a truth value called lock released with initial value false.
Create a number variable called template rate with initial value 0.2.
Create a number variable called supply per step with initial value 5.
For each time step, perform the following actions.
    Add supply per step fresh molecules to soluble.
    If lock released is true and aggregated is greater than zero, convert a fraction template rate of soluble into aggregated.
    If lock released is true and aggregated is zero, with a small seeding chance convert one soluble molecule into aggregated.
    Record whether aggregated is greater than soluble, meaning the switch is on.
End the loop.
Report the final switch state.
The procedure is complete.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the claim is that a seed plus templating converts an ever-refreshed soluble pool into a stable aggregated majority, and a short loop shows the pool turning over while the state stays on.

# CPEB3 prion-like conformational switch: aggregated form templates fresh copies.
# A SUMO-like lock gates seeding; once seeded, templating holds the state
# across full replacement of the soluble pool (Fioriti/Kandel; Cell Reports 2015).
import random
soluble, aggregated = 100.0, 0.0
TEMPLATE, SUPPLY, TURNOVER = 0.25, 6.0, 0.1

for t in range(200):
    soluble += SUPPLY                                   # fresh CPEB3 made
    soluble -= soluble * TURNOVER                       # soluble pool degrades
    lock_released = t >= 20                             # stimulation frees it
    if lock_released and aggregated == 0 and random.random() < 0.3:
        aggregated, soluble = 1.0, soluble - 1.0        # first seed
    if lock_released and aggregated > 0:
        conv = soluble * TEMPLATE
        soluble, aggregated = soluble - conv, aggregated + conv
    if t in (19, 40, 199):
        print(f"t={t:3d}  soluble={soluble:6.1f}  aggregated={aggregated:6.1f}  "
              f"{'ON' if aggregated > soluble else 'off'}")

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A protein that uses the prion trick for good, turning a stimulated synapse permanently on by pressing its own aggregated shape onto every fresh copy that arrives.


CHAPTER 4. THE CREB GATE (Cyclic Adenosine Monophosphate Response Element Binding Protein Threshold Gate) (The Graduation Gate) (COG)

Not every fleeting synaptic change deserves to become a lasting memory, and something must decide which ones are promoted from a passing chemical adjustment to a rewritten gene programme.

This chapter is that decision maker: a transcription factor that integrates signals arriving at the nucleus and, when they cross a threshold, opens the door to the gene expression that builds durable memory, and that also biases which neurons are recruited into a memory in the first place.

Graduation names the decision: promotion from short-term to long-term, from cytoplasm to genome.

Gate names the mechanism: a threshold that passes some events and blocks others.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

Cyclic adenosine monophosphate response element binding protein, abbreviated CREB, is a transcription factor, a protein that binds specific sites in DNA to switch nearby genes on.

What it stores, in the moment, is a decision: whether the pattern of activity a neuron just experienced is strong or repeated enough to trigger the memory gene programme, and, across a population, which neurons are made eligible to join the engram.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

Signals from active synapses, carried by calcium waves and by the second messenger cyclic adenosine monophosphate, activate kinases that phosphorylate CREB at a critical serine.

Phosphorylated CREB recruits a co-activator and switches on target genes, among them the immediate-early genes whose products begin the long-term structural and molecular changes of memory.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

CREB itself is not a long-term store; its persistence trick is to convert a transient event into a durable one by triggering renewal loops downstream, so it is the switch that starts the self-sustaining stores rather than a self-sustaining store itself.

The threshold nature is the essence: CREB integrates multiple, repeated, or coincident signals and fires the gene programme only when they sum past a set point, which is why spaced repetition consolidates memory better than massed repetition, because spacing lets CREB activation accumulate and outlast its own inhibitors.

An engineer would see an integrate-and-fire gate with hysteresis feeding a one-shot trigger that arms slower, self-holding stores.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

CREB operates in the nucleus of principal neurons throughout the memory circuits, prominently the lateral amygdala pyramidal cells in fear learning studied by Sheena Josselyn and colleagues, and hippocampal and cortical pyramidal cells including the L2/3 IT context-weaving neuron.

In the Layer 7 catalogue its relevant interface is not a synapse but the synapse-to-nucleus relay that delivers activity signals to the genome.

TIMESCALES:

CREB phosphorylation rises within minutes of activity and its target immediate-early genes are transcribed within minutes to tens of minutes.

The heightened excitability that biases allocation lasts hours, defining a window during which a neuron is preferentially recruited.

The gene programme CREB launches then supports memories lasting days to a lifetime.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

A write is any activity pattern that drives enough calcium and cyclic adenosine monophosphate signalling to push phosphorylated CREB past threshold, especially spaced training that lets activation accumulate.

Raising CREB levels experimentally lowers the threshold and biases that neuron toward inclusion in the engram.

THE READ OPERATION:

The gate's decision is read as the presence or absence of downstream gene expression, and its allocation bias is read as which neurons carry the engram, testable because artificially raising CREB in a subset of neurons, then reactivating just those neurons, drives recall.

Reading the allocation is thus done by activity marks such as the immediate-early gene products the gate switched on.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

The gate itself is reset simply when phosphorylation is removed and activity subsides, closing the window.

Blocking the CREB-driven rise in excitability, for example by co-expressing an inward-rectifier potassium channel that clamps the cell, prevents preferential allocation, and selectively deleting or silencing the high-CREB neurons after learning erases that specific memory.

Erasure at the gate is therefore about the allocation decision and the downstream programme, not about a maintained bit the gate holds.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

As a per-neuron decision the gate carries essentially one graded go or no-go value at a time, but its population role sets the size and identity of an engram, on the order of a small percentage of neurons in a region winning allocation.

Fidelity is the reliability of promoting salient events and rejecting noise; spaced-versus-massed effects show the threshold is tuned, and the false-promotion rate is controlled by inhibitors that limit CREB activation between bouts.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

CREB sits atop the nuclear and genomic machinery, triggering the immediate-early gene response and, through it, the supply of capture cargo.

It sets which neurons and, with the synaptic tag, which synapses will consolidate, and its downstream programme feeds every structural store, making it the allocation policy for the whole dossier.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

CREB signalling is essential in development for neuronal survival and circuit formation, and its efficiency in gating consolidation changes with age, contributing to differences in learning rate across the lifespan.

Declining CREB function is one proposed contributor to age-related memory weakening.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

CREB-dependent, cyclic adenosine monophosphate-driven transcription for long-term memory is conserved from the sea slug Aplysia and the fruit fly through mammals, one of the most strongly conserved memory mechanisms known, which is why invertebrate work translated so directly.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome, an intellectual-disability disorder, is caused by mutations in the CREB co-activator CREB-binding protein, tying the gate directly to human cognitive disability through the histone connection.

Dysregulated CREB signalling is implicated in the entrenched engrams of addiction and in mood disorders.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Calcium-wave and cyclic adenosine monophosphate signals from active synapses; kinase activity phosphorylating CREB; the neuron's recent excitability.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Transcription of immediate-early and memory genes; a raised-excitability allocation bias.

• STATE.

◦ Level of phosphorylated CREB relative to threshold; the neuron's eligibility flag for engram inclusion.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Phosphorylation threshold; integration and decay time of the signal; basal CREB level, which sets the set-point; inhibitor strength.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Phosphorylation minutes; immediate-early transcription tens of minutes; allocation window hours; launched memory days to lifelong.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome the CREB co-activator is deficient and consolidation and cognition are impaired.

◦ In addiction the gate over-promotes drug-associated engrams, a persistence pathology.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

The engineering name is write_allocation_gate, a per-neuron admission controller that subscribes to integrated activity signals and publishes both a commit-to-long-term signal and an eligibility flag for which cell records the memory.

Its nearest engineering analogue is a write-ahead-log commit gate combined with an admission-control policy that decides which transactions are durably logged and on which node.

The biological difference is that the gate also raises the host cell's future excitability, biasing which unit wins the next write, a feedback an ordinary commit gate does not have.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to decide whether a burst of activity graduates to the long-term gene programme by integrating signals until they cross a threshold, and to bias allocation by the neuron's baseline.
Create a number variable called creb signal with initial value 0.
Create a number variable called threshold with initial value 5.0.
Create a number variable called baseline with initial value the neuron's resting creb level.
Create a number variable called decay with initial value 0.6.
For each training event in the session, perform the following actions.
    Increase creb signal by the event strength plus baseline.
    Multiply creb signal by decay to model dispersal between events.
    If creb signal is greater than threshold is true, then record that this event graduated to gene expression.
End the loop.
Report whether the session graduated and how high the baseline pushed the odds.
The procedure is complete.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the point is that spaced events accumulate past a threshold while a high baseline biases allocation, and a short loop shows both spacing and baseline changing the outcome.

# CREB threshold gate: integrate activity, fire the gene programme past threshold,
# and let a raised baseline bias which neuron is allocated to the engram
# (Josselyn/Silva; CREB, excitability and allocation, Science 2009 and after).
THRESHOLD, DECAY = 5.0, 0.6

def session(event_strengths, baseline, spaced):
    signal, graduated = 0.0, False
    for s in event_strengths:
        signal += s + baseline
        if spaced:
            signal *= DECAY          # spacing lets inhibitors disperse; net accrual
        if signal > THRESHOLD:
            graduated = True
    return graduated, round(signal, 2)

events = [2.0, 2.0, 2.0, 2.0]
for baseline in (0.0, 1.0):          # low- vs high-CREB (more excitable) neuron
    for spaced in (False, True):
        g, final = session(events, baseline, spaced)
        print(f"baseline={baseline}  spaced={spaced}  "
              f"final={final}  {'GRADUATED' if g else 'stayed short-term'}")

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

The nuclear gatekeeper that decides both whether an experience becomes a lasting memory and which neurons get to hold it, tuned so that spaced practice and a more excitable cell win the write.


PART TWO. THE TAGGING AND CAPTURE MACHINERY

The switches of Part One can flip, but a neuron makes many of the ingredients for lasting change in one shared batch, so it needs an addressing system to deliver them only where they are earned.

This part is that system: a transient local mark that stamps a stimulated synapse, and a cell-wide shipment of plasticity products that only tagged synapses are allowed to seize.

Together they let a weak event borrow the durable machinery triggered by a strong one nearby, and they explain how consolidation is allocated across thousands of synapses from a single nuclear response.

Read them as the write-allocation policy of memory: the tag is the address, the cargo is the payload, and capture is the delivery.


CHAPTER 5. THE SYNAPTIC TAG (Frey-Morris Local Capture Mark) (The Loading Dock) (COG)

A neuron that decides to consolidate does not know in advance which of its thousands of synapses earned the change, and it cannot afford to strengthen all of them.

The solution is a temporary flag set right at the stimulated synapse, a molecular loading dock that lasts an hour or two and entitles that synapse, and only that synapse, to grab the plasticity proteins the cell ships out.

Loading names the function: it is where cargo is taken on board.

Dock names the locality: a specific, addressed place, not the whole warehouse.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The synaptic tag is a transient, protein-synthesis-independent local state set at a stimulated synapse, described by Uwe Frey and Richard Morris.

What it stores is a short-lived address: the fact that this particular synapse recently underwent early-phase plasticity and is therefore eligible to capture plasticity products, holding that eligibility for roughly one to two hours.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

Induction sets the tag through local kinase activity, prominently CaMKII, and through reorganisation of the spine's actin cytoskeleton, producing a captured local configuration that can bind and retain incoming plasticity proteins.

The tag does not require new protein synthesis to be set, which is why weak stimulation can set a tag even though it cannot by itself make the proteins to consolidate.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The tag is deliberately not persistent; its trick is the opposite, a timed local mark whose short life is the whole point, because it converts the problem of durable storage into a problem of addressing.

By existing only briefly and only at the stimulated synapse, the tag ensures that the durable stores, made elsewhere and shared cell-wide, are installed at exactly the synapses that earned them and nowhere else, so persistence is achieved by pairing a fleeting address with a durable payload.

An engineer would see an eligibility trace: a decaying local marker that gates when and where a slower reward or product signal is allowed to act, exactly the three-factor learning rule of modern theory.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

Tags are set on the dendritic spines of glutamatergic principal cells, classically hippocampal CA1 pyramidal neurons and cortical pyramidal cells including the L2/3 IT context-weaving neuron.

In the Layer 7 catalogue the host is the individual mature excitatory axospinous synapse, considered one at a time against its neighbours.

TIMESCALES:

The tag is set within minutes of stimulation.

It decays in under about three hours, with most experiments placing the useful window at one to two hours.

That window defines how far apart in time a weak and a strong event can be and still share consolidation, the phenomenon of behavioural tagging.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

A write is the induction of early-phase plasticity at a synapse, weak or strong, which sets the local tag through kinase and cytoskeletal changes.

Crucially a weak input that would otherwise decay can set a valid tag, so the write of an address is cheaper than the write of a durable store.

THE READ OPERATION:

The tag is read by the plasticity products as they diffuse through the dendrite: a tagged synapse binds and retains them, an untagged one does not, so reading is the selective capture itself.

The stored eligibility is thus read out by whether cargo sticks.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

The tag erases itself by decay within a couple of hours if no capture occurs, resetting the synapse to ineligible.

Interfering with the kinase activity or actin dynamics that set the tag prevents it experimentally, and strong depotentiating stimuli can clear a set tag.

Erasure here is a feature, not a loss: an unused address must expire so the synapse does not wrongly capture a later, unrelated shipment.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

Capacity is one eligibility flag per synapse, so a neuron can hold thousands of independent, simultaneously decaying tags, which is what gives tagging its addressing power.

Fidelity is the spatial and temporal precision of capture: tags are local to the stimulated spine and its immediate vicinity, and the main error mode is cross-capture by a nearby tagged synapse within the same window, a real but bounded source of associative interference.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The tag is set by the same CaMKII activity as the Chapter 1 switch and by the actin skeleton of the structural machinery, and it gates the capture cargo of Chapter 6.

It is the mechanistic link between the nuclear allocation decision of CREB and the synapse-level installation of durable change, so it sits at the centre of the consolidation pipeline.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Tagging efficiency tracks the plasticity of the developing brain and declines with age as the kinase and cytoskeletal machinery that set tags become less responsive.

Impaired tagging is one proposed substrate of age-related and disease-related consolidation deficits.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

The tag-and-capture strategy solves a general problem faced by any neuron that makes shared plasticity products, and homologous synaptic tagging phenomena appear across species with dendritic protein synthesis, indicating an old and general solution to write allocation.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

In Alzheimer disease, amyloid-beta disrupts the setting and maintenance of synaptic tags and the capture of plasticity products, contributing to the failure to form new lasting memories even before synapses are lost.

Disrupted tagging is also studied in stress-related and developmental cognitive disorders.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Local early-phase plasticity induction; CaMKII activity; actin remodelling signals.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ A local eligibility state that permits capture of plasticity products at that spine.

• STATE.

◦ Tag strength at each synapse, a decaying value between 0 and 1, one per synapse.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Tag-setting threshold; decay time constant of roughly one to two hours; spatial capture radius along the dendrite.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Setting minutes; decay one to two hours, under three hours at most; capture window bounded by that decay.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In Alzheimer disease amyloid-beta prevents tags being set or captured, blocking new durable memory.

◦ Overlapping tags within one window cross-capture cargo, producing associative interference.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

The engineering name is write_allocation_tag, a per-synapse decaying eligibility trace that subscribes to a local induction signal and publishes a capture-permission flag scoped to that synapse.

Its nearest engineering analogue is an eligibility trace in reinforcement learning, or a short-lived write token that authorises a later bulk delivery to a specific address.

The biological difference is that the tag is set by the same event it later helps consolidate and expires on a wall-clock timer measured in hours, coupling addressing to a strict biochemical deadline.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to let each synapse hold a decaying eligibility tag so that shared plasticity products are captured only where a tag is still alive.
Create a list called tags with one decaying value for each synapse, all starting at 0.
Create a number variable called decay per step with initial value 0.8.
Create a number variable called capture threshold with initial value 0.2.
When a synapse is stimulated, set its tag value to 1.
For each time step, perform the following actions.
    For each synapse, multiply its tag value by decay per step.
    If plasticity products are present this step, then for each synapse whose tag value is above capture threshold, install a durable weight increase at that synapse.
End the loop.
Report which synapses captured a durable change.
The procedure is complete.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the point is that only synapses whose tag is still above threshold when cargo arrives get consolidated, and a short simulation shows timing decide the winners.

# Synaptic tag-and-capture: decaying local tags gate a cell-wide product wave.
# Only synapses still tagged when products arrive consolidate
# (Frey and Morris 1997; Redondo and Morris, Nat Rev Neurosci 2011).
N = 5
tags = [0.0] * N
DECAY, THRESH = 0.8, 0.2
stim_times = {0: 0, 1: 0, 2: 3}          # synapses 0,1 tagged early; 2 later
product_wave_at = 6                       # strong event ships products here
durable = [0.0] * N

for t in range(10):
    for syn, when in stim_times.items():
        if when == t:
            tags[syn] = 1.0               # set tag on stimulation
    tags = [v * DECAY for v in tags]      # tags decay each step
    if t == product_wave_at:
        for syn in range(N):
            if tags[syn] > THRESH:
                durable[syn] += 1.0       # capture only where tag survives

print("tag levels at delivery :", [round(v, 3) for v in tags])
print("captured durable change:", durable)

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A fleeting local flag that turns the hard problem of durable storage into the easy problem of addressing, letting a weak synapse borrow a strong neighbour's consolidation if it acts within the hour.


CHAPTER 6. THE PLASTICITY PRODUCTS (THE CAPTURE CARGO) (Activity-Triggered Plasticity-Related Proteins) (The Cell-Wide Shipment) (COG)

When a neuron commits to consolidation, it does not manufacture a bespoke parcel for each earned synapse; it produces one batch of plasticity proteins and broadcasts them through the whole dendritic tree.

The batch travels everywhere but is seized only at synapses flying a valid tag, and the strangest item in it, the protein Arc, packages messenger ribonucleic acid inside a shell that looks and behaves like a virus and can carry that cargo between cells.

Cell-wide names the delivery: broadcast, not point-to-point.

Shipment names the contents: a mixed load of proteins and messengers dispatched together.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The plasticity products, also called plasticity-related proteins, are the activity-triggered wave of newly made molecules that consolidate synaptic change, including the activity-regulated cytoskeleton-associated protein, abbreviated Arc, the growth factor brain-derived neurotrophic factor, abbreviated BDNF, and their companions.

What they store is not a bit but the raw material of durable change; the store is written where they are captured, so the cargo is the payload the tag addresses.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

A strong plasticity event drives CREB and the immediate-early gene response, which transcribes and translates the plasticity products, some in the cell body and some by local translation in the dendrites.

The products diffuse or are transported through the dendritic tree, and at tagged synapses they are captured and used to build the enlarged, receptor-rich, structurally consolidated synapse.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The products are themselves short-lived, so their persistence trick is indirect: they convert a transient tag into a permanent structural and molecular change that then persists by the mechanisms of the other slow variables, meaning the cargo is spent to build something durable rather than being durable itself.

The Arc component adds a remarkable twist: Arc self-assembles into a virus-like capsid, derived evolutionarily from a Ty3/gypsy retrotransposon Gag protein, encapsulates its own and other messenger ribonucleic acids, and is released in extracellular vesicles that transfer that RNA into neighbouring cells, so some cargo is delivered between neurons like a domesticated virus.

An engineer would see broadcast delivery with addressed capture, plus an optional inter-node courier that packages payloads in self-assembling containers.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The products are made and captured in glutamatergic principal neurons, prominently hippocampal and cortical pyramidal cells including the L2/3 IT context-weaving neuron, and in the Drosophila neuromuscular junction the Arc courier famously ships from motor neuron to muscle.

In the Layer 7 catalogue the capture sites are the mature excitatory spine synapses, while the Arc vesicle route crosses to postsynaptic partners.

TIMESCALES:

The synthesis wave rises over minutes to about an hour after induction and continues over the following hours.

Individual products are degraded within hours, so the delivery window overlaps the one-to-two-hour life of the synaptic tag by design.

The structural change they build then lasts days to a lifetime.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

A write is the strong, CREB-engaging event that triggers the immediate-early gene response and launches the product wave into the dendritic tree.

Because the write is cell-wide, one strong event can supply many tagged synapses, which is the economy the whole system exploits.

THE READ OPERATION:

The products are read out by capture at tagged synapses, where they are consumed to install durable strength, so reading is the conversion of shared cargo into a local, permanent change.

The Arc RNA-transfer route is read out in the recipient cell, where delivered messengers can be translated to influence its plasticity.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

Uncaptured products are simply degraded within hours, clearing the broadcast so it cannot consolidate the wrong synapse later.

Blocking the synthesis of plasticity products, for instance with protein-synthesis inhibitors during the consolidation window, prevents late-phase potentiation and blocks long-term memory, a classic result; disrupting Arc capsid formation or vesicle transfer blocks the plasticity that depends on it.

Erasure of the cargo is thus rapid and total by default, which is why timing relative to the tag is everything.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

The cargo carries no addressing information of its own; its fidelity comes entirely from the tag, so capacity is set by how many tagged synapses one product wave can supply before it is degraded.

The main error modes are supplying a spuriously tagged synapse, producing cross-consolidation, and, for the Arc route, off-target RNA transfer, whose physiological scope and specificity in the mammalian brain remain an active and honest unknown.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The products are triggered by the CREB gate and the immediate-early gene response, captured under the control of the synaptic tag, and consumed by the receptor slot system and the actin skeleton to build structural change.

They are the physical bridge between the nuclear machinery of Part Four and the synapse-level stores, making this chapter the hinge of the whole dossier.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Arc and BDNF signalling shape synapse formation and refinement in development, and their activity-dependent expression underlies experience-dependent plasticity across life.

Declining BDNF is associated with age-related cognitive decline, and the products' regulation changes with ageing and activity.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Arc is a striking case of molecular domestication: a retrotransposon Gag gene captured by the vertebrate lineage and repurposed for intercellular RNA transfer in the nervous system, with an independent parallel domestication in flies, one of the clearest examples of a virus-like element turned to a neural function.

BDNF and the broader neurotrophin system are ancient signalling molecules elaborated for plasticity.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Arc dysregulation is implicated in Angelman syndrome, fragile X syndrome, and Alzheimer disease, where the products' machinery malfunctions and consolidation suffers.

Altered BDNF signalling is linked to depression, and blocked plasticity-product synthesis is a general route to failed memory formation.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ CREB-driven immediate-early gene transcription; local and somatic translation capacity; strong-plasticity trigger.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ A cell-wide pool of plasticity proteins for capture; Arc-vesicle RNA delivered to neighbouring cells.

• STATE.

◦ Concentration of available products in the dendritic tree, a decaying broadcast pool.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Synthesis rate and onset; degradation half-life of hours; diffusion and transport range; Arc capsid and vesicle release rate.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Synthesis onset minutes to an hour; product half-life hours, matched to the tag window; built structure days to lifelong.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ Protein-synthesis block during consolidation prevents late-phase potentiation and long-term memory.

◦ In fragile X and Angelman syndromes Arc regulation is disturbed and plasticity is abnormal.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

The engineering name is capture_cargo_broadcast, a per-neuron producer that subscribes to a commit signal and publishes a decaying pool of plasticity payloads for capture by write_allocation_tag, with an optional inter-node courier for the Arc route.

Its nearest engineering analogue is a publish-subscribe broadcast of shared resources with address-filtered delivery, plus a sidecar that ships payloads in self-assembling containers between nodes.

The biological difference is that the courier container is an evolved viral capsid, so the system literally reuses a domesticated pathogen's packaging to move information between cells, a mechanism no ordinary message bus has.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to broadcast a decaying pool of plasticity products through a dendrite and let only tagged synapses capture them, with a fraction packaged for transfer to a neighbour.
Create a number variable called product pool with initial value 0.
Create a number variable called synthesis burst with initial value 10.
Create a number variable called degrade rate with initial value 0.3.
Create a list called tags with one value per synapse.
Create a number variable called capture threshold with initial value 0.2.
When a strong event fires, add synthesis burst to product pool.
For each time step, perform the following actions.
    Decrease product pool by product pool multiplied by degrade rate.
    For each synapse whose tag is above capture threshold, move a share of product pool into a durable change at that synapse.
    Set aside a small fraction of product pool as an Arc vesicle destined for a neighbouring cell.
End the loop.
Report durable changes and the amount shipped to neighbours.
The procedure is complete.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the point is that one shared decaying pool consolidates only tagged synapses while a slice is exported, and a short loop shows broadcast, addressed capture, and inter-cell shipment together.

# Plasticity products: one cell-wide pool, captured only at tagged synapses,
# with an Arc-capsid slice exported to a neighbour cell
# (Frey/Morris capture; Pastuzyn et al., Arc as domesticated Gag, Cell 2018).
pool = 0.0
BURST, DEGRADE, CAP_SHARE, ARC_SHARE, THRESH = 10.0, 0.3, 0.25, 0.05, 0.2
tags = [0.9, 0.05, 0.5, 0.0]      # synapses 0 and 2 are tagged; 1 and 3 are not
durable = [0.0] * len(tags)
exported = 0.0

for t in range(8):
    if t == 0:
        pool += BURST                       # strong event ships the batch
    pool -= pool * DEGRADE                   # uncaptured cargo degrades
    for i, tag in enumerate(tags):
        if tag > THRESH:
            grab = pool * CAP_SHARE * tag
            durable[i] += grab               # tagged synapse captures its share
            pool -= grab
    slice_out = pool * ARC_SHARE
    exported += slice_out                     # Arc vesicle to a neighbour
    pool -= slice_out

print("durable change per synapse:", [round(d, 2) for d in durable])
print("total RNA/cargo exported to neighbour:", round(exported, 2))

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A cell-wide broadcast of plasticity proteins seized only at flagged synapses, carrying as its oddest member a domesticated virus that packs RNA in a capsid and ferries it to the cell next door.


CHAPTER 7. THE LOCAL TRANSLATION STATIONS (Dendritic on-site protein synthesis machinery) (On-Site Fabricator) (COG)

A synapse a millimetre from the cell body cannot afford to send a courier to the nucleus and wait for a part to arrive whenever it needs one.

So it keeps a workshop on the doorstep: ribosomes, a private stock of blueprints, and the tools to read them, all parked under the spine.

When the synapse is used hard enough, that workshop wakes and builds the proteins that make the change stick, on the spot and within minutes.

This is manufacturing moved to the point of consumption, and it is one of the quiet reasons a memory can be written faster than the nucleus could ever ship the goods.

On-Site names the trick: synthesis happens at the synapse, not at the distant cell body.

Fabricator names the function: it makes protein, the raw material of a strengthened synapse.


WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The local translation station is the collection of machinery, sitting in the dendrite beneath and around a synapse, that turns messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA, the working copy of a gene) into protein without involving the cell body.

It stores two things at once.

It stores a standing inventory of silenced blueprints - specific mRNAs pre-positioned in the dendrite, held translationally dormant until needed.

And, through what it builds, it stores the local capacity to convert a brief electrical event into a durable material change at one synapse.


THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

mRNAs made in the nucleus are packaged with ribonucleic-acid-binding proteins (RBPs, proteins that grip ribonucleic acid) into travelling parcels called ribonucleoprotein granules, or ribonucleic-acid granules.

Inside a granule the mRNA is folded up and gagged, held silent by its binding proteins so that not a single protein is made during the journey.

Motor proteins, the cell's cargo haulers - kinesin walking outward and dynein walking back - drag these granules along microtubules, the dendrite's rail tracks, out to the synapses.

At the destination sit ribosomes, the two-part molecular machines that read mRNA and assemble protein, some free in the dendrite and some clustered as polyribosomes at the base of spines.

A synaptic signal, chiefly a rise in calcium and activation of glutamate receptors, unlocks the granule, frees the mRNA, and lets the waiting ribosomes translate it.

Classic mRNAs held this way include those for the calcium-calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II alpha subunit (a plasticity kinase), the activity-regulated cytoskeleton-associated protein (Arc), and components of the postsynaptic scaffold.


THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The trick here is pre-positioned dormant inventory plus on-demand release, so that the durable part is manufactured locally the instant it is authorised.

A distant nucleus cannot address one synapse out of tens of thousands; by the time bulk protein diffused out from the soma the plasticity window would be shut.

By staging silent blueprints at the synapse in advance, the cell converts a slow logistics problem into a fast local switch: the mRNA is already there, so only the act of translation, not the act of delivery, has to be triggered.

Persistence follows because the freshly made proteins - kinases, scaffolds, cytoskeletal parts - are exactly the components that lock a strengthened synapse into a self-renewing state, and the station can rebuild them again whenever they turn over.

An engineer would call this just-in-time manufacturing from a local parts bin, in deliberate contrast to warehouse shipping from a central depot.


WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The stations operate in the dendrites of every principal excitatory neuron catalogued in the Layer 6 volumes, most heavily in the highly plastic layer two and three intratelencephalic pyramidal cells and in the layer five pyramidal projection cells.

They are dense in the hippocampal pyramidal cells of the cornu ammonis fields, where dendritic translation was first proven.

At the Layer 7 junction level they cluster at excitatory glutamatergic spine synapses, in the dendritic shaft just below the spine and, for a subset of mRNAs, inside the spine head itself.

Inhibitory interneurons translate locally too, but the machinery is most elaborate at the plastic excitatory synapse.


TIMESCALES:

Granule transport from soma to distal dendrite takes minutes to a few hours, moving at roughly a tenth to one micrometre per second along the tracks.

Unlocking and the first burst of local synthesis follow a plasticity trigger within one to fifteen minutes.

Individual proteins made this way turn over on the scale of hours to a few days, so the station must run repeatedly to hold a synapse strong.

The stored dormant inventory itself can sit in place for hours to days before it is ever called upon.


THE WRITE OPERATION:

The write is a strong synaptic event - high-frequency stimulation or paired pre- and postsynaptic firing - that raises local calcium and activates the target-of-rapamycin and related translation-control pathways.

This does two things: it releases specific mRNAs from their binding proteins, and it switches ribosomes from idling to active reading.

The result is a local pulse of new protein delivered to precisely the synapse that earned it.


THE READ OPERATION:

The stored setting is read whenever the freshly synthesised proteins take up their posts.

New scaffold proteins add receptor-holding capacity, new kinases sustain the phosphorylation state of the strengthened synapse, and new cytoskeletal parts feed spine enlargement.

The synapse's ongoing strength is, in part, a continuous readout of how much local translation it can command.


THE ERASE OPERATION:

Erasure comes by re-silencing and degradation: when the trigger fades, binding proteins re-gag their mRNAs, and the proteins already made are removed by normal turnover through the ubiquitin-proteasome system, the cell's tagged-protein shredder.

Blocking translation with drugs such as anisomycin or cycloheximide during the plasticity window erases the durable phase of potentiation while leaving the brief early phase intact, which is the experimental proof that on-site synthesis is required.

Honest caveat: because these drugs are cell-wide, they cannot by themselves show that the required synthesis was strictly local rather than somatic, though dendrite-severing and local-uncaging experiments have since supplied that evidence.


CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

Capacity is best thought of as the size and diversity of the local mRNA pool: dendritic transcriptomes in hippocampal neurons run to thousands of distinct mRNA species, with hundreds enriched in the neuropil where synapses sit.

Per synapse, only a modest number of copies of any given mRNA are staged, so output is limited and somewhat stochastic, which is a fidelity cost.

Fidelity is aided by the addressing: many dendritic mRNAs carry zip-code sequences in their untranslated regions that target them to the right compartment.

Honest unknown: the true number of translation events per synapse per plasticity episode is not firmly measured and is an active question.


INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

This station is the supply depot for the tag-and-capture machinery of Part Two: the plasticity products captured at a synaptic tag are in large part locally translated here.

It feeds the slot system of Chapter 8 by making scaffold and receptor-associated proteins, and it feeds the actin skeleton of Chapter 9 by making cytoskeletal regulators.

It is powered by the mitochondria of Chapter 12, which supply the energy and, at times, sit beside the granules.

It is downstream of the nuclear machinery of Part Four, which decides which mRNAs are exported to the dendrite in the first place.


DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Dendritic translation is heaviest during development, when synapses are being built and refined in bulk.

It remains available lifelong as the engine of adult plasticity, though the standing inventory and the efficiency of unlocking decline with age.

The composition of the local mRNA pool is itself shaped by experience across the lifespan.


EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Localised translation is ancient and general: cells across eukaryotes position mRNAs to sub-cellular sites, from the budding yeast bud tip to the fly egg's body axes.

Neurons co-opted this universal logistics trick and scaled it up to serve synapse-specific memory, which is a specialisation of a very old capability rather than an invention.


DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Fragile X syndrome, the commonest inherited intellectual disability, is a disease of this machinery: the fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein normally brakes local translation, and its loss leaves dendritic synthesis running unchecked, distorting plasticity.

Several autism-linked and intellectual-disability genes encode ribonucleic-acid-binding proteins or translation regulators.

Dysregulated local translation is also implicated in the transport-and-aggregation defects of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and related motor-neuron disease.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In fragile X syndrome, loss of the fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein removes the brake on dendritic translation, causing exaggerated synthesis, immature elongated spines and intellectual disability.

◦ Aggregation of ribonucleic-acid-binding proteins in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis strands granules and starves distal synapses of parts.

CHAPTER 8. THE SLOT AND TRAFFICKING SYSTEM (Postsynaptic receptor slot scaffold) (Weight Rack) (COG)

If the switches of Part One hold the memory as a stored number, this is the hardware the number is actually written into.

The strength of an excitatory synapse is, to a first approximation, how many receptors are parked in its receiving surface, and that count is set by a rack of scaffold proteins that provides holding slots and by a constant two-way traffic of receptors in and out.

Add slots and trap more receptors and the synapse grows stronger; take them away and it weakens.

Everything the bistable switches, the tags, and the local factory do converges here, on the number of receptors held in the rack.

Weight names what it is: the synaptic weight, the strength value the rest of the corpus treats as the memory.

Rack names how: physical slots that hold receptors in place until traffic changes their number.


WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The slot and trafficking system is the postsynaptic scaffold, built around the postsynaptic density protein 95 (a slot-forming scaffold protein), together with the receptor traffic that fills and empties it.

It stores one thing with unusual directness: the number of alpha-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic-acid receptors (AMPA receptors, the fast excitatory glutamate receptors) held at the synapse.

That number is the physical operand of the synaptic weight - the quantity the switches of Part One work to keep steady.


THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

Postsynaptic density protein 95 belongs to a family of scaffold proteins that pack into a dense lattice, the postsynaptic density, just under the receiving membrane.

AMPA receptors do not bind this scaffold directly; they are held by auxiliary subunits called transmembrane AMPA-receptor regulatory proteins, chief among them stargazin, which grip the scaffold like a handle fitting a slot.

A receptor drifting in the membrane by lateral diffusion, its random sideways wandering, is captured when its stargazin handle catches a free postsynaptic density protein 95 slot, and is held by this diffusional trapping.

The count is set by three flows: lateral diffusion in and out of the synapse, exocytosis (insertion of receptors from internal stores into the membrane) and endocytosis (their removal back into the cell).

More slots trap more receptors; fewer slots or more endocytosis lowers the count.


THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The trick is a physical slot count that holds receptors by trapping, kept refreshed against turnover by continual traffic through a reserve pool.

Any individual receptor stays at the synapse only for minutes to hours before it diffuses away or is internalised, so the specific molecules are never the memory.

What persists is the number of occupied slots, because as one receptor leaves another is trapped from the local surface pool, and the slot scaffold itself is replenished under the control of the upstream switches.

An engineer would recognise this as a fixed number of register bits whose contents are constantly rewritten from a buffer: the value survives even though every bit is refreshed, exactly as memory-cell contents survive refresh in dynamic random-access memory.

The biological difference is that the switches of Part One, not a clock, decide the set point the traffic maintains.


WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The system operates at every excitatory glutamatergic spine synapse catalogued in the Layer 7 junction volumes, on the principal pyramidal cells of the Layer 6 catalogues.

It is densest and most studied at the Schaffer-collateral synapses onto hippocampal cornu ammonis field one pyramidal cells, and at the spine synapses of cortical layer two, three and five pyramidal cells.

The relevant host structure is the postsynaptic density itself, the disc-shaped protein thickening at the tip of the spine.


TIMESCALES:

Single-receptor dwell time in a slot is seconds to minutes.

Exocytic and endocytic exchange with internal pools runs over minutes.

Changes in slot number that follow potentiation or depression establish over minutes to an hour and are then held for hours to days by the upstream switches.

The scaffold proteins themselves turn over across hours to days and are continually replaced.


THE WRITE OPERATION:

Long-term potentiation writes an increase: calcium entry through the N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (a coincidence-detecting glutamate receptor) triggers insertion of AMPA receptors by exocytosis and the recruitment of additional postsynaptic density protein 95 slots, raising the trapped count.

Long-term depression writes a decrease by driving endocytosis of receptors and loss of slots.

The direction is set by the amount and timing of calcium: large brief rises write up, smaller sustained rises write down.


THE READ OPERATION:

The stored count is read on every incoming pulse of glutamate.

More receptors in the rack means a larger excitatory current for the same neurotransmitter release, which is precisely what a stronger synapse means.

The synapse's ongoing contribution to its neuron's firing is a direct, moment-to-moment readout of the slot count.


THE ERASE OPERATION:

Physiological erasure is receptor internalisation and slot loss, as in long-term depression and in the forgetting that follows.

Experimentally, peptides that block the stargazin-to-scaffold interaction, or that interfere with receptor insertion, collapse the count and erase potentiation.

Honest note: because the count is maintained by the Part One switches, notably the protein kinase M zeta loop said to hold AMPA-receptor numbers high, the erasure debate around that kinase and its inhibitory peptide applies here too; whether blocking it truly empties the rack or merely stops refilling it is not fully settled.


CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

A single hippocampal synapse holds on the order of tens to low hundreds of AMPA receptors, with a mature postsynaptic density presenting roughly tens of slots.

If one treated slot occupancy as graded, the analog capacity is a few bits per synapse; measured across the range of spine sizes, synaptic strengths span at least an order of magnitude and cluster into a handful of reliably distinguishable levels, implying roughly two to five bits.

Fidelity is limited by the stochastic nature of trapping and traffic, so the count drifts and must be actively held.

Honest unknown: the exact usable bit depth per synapse is estimated, not measured, and depends on how reproducibly levels can be read.


INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

This is the operand that the CaMKII switch and the protein kinase M zeta loop of Part One act upon: their job is to keep this count from decaying.

It is filled by proteins from the local translation stations of Chapter 7 and captured at the synaptic tag of Chapter 5.

It is physically braced by the spine actin skeleton of Chapter 9, whose enlargement makes room for more slots, and by the trans-synaptic adhesion complex of Chapter 10, which keeps the rack aligned with the transmitter release site opposite.

The homeostatic set-point machinery of Chapter 18 scales the whole rack up or down to hold the neuron's average activity steady.


DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Immature synapses are often silent, carrying N-methyl-D-aspartate receptors but few or no AMPA receptors; the first write of this system is the unsilencing that installs AMPA receptors during development.

Slot number and traffic remain plastic lifelong, and their dysregulation contributes to age-related synaptic loss.


EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Ionotropic glutamate receptors and membrane-associated scaffolds of this family are ancient, present across bilaterian animals and with deep roots in simpler nervous systems.

The particular trick of a stargazin-like auxiliary subunit handing receptors to a scaffold slot is a refinement that let synapses become rapidly and reversibly tunable.


DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Antibodies against AMPA-receptor and scaffold components cause forms of autoimmune encephalitis with memory disturbance.

Mutations and copy-number changes in postsynaptic density protein 95 family genes and their partners appear in intellectual disability, autism and schizophrenia.

The synaptic failure of early Alzheimer disease is in part a loss of AMPA receptors from slots driven by amyloid-beta.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Excitatory postsynaptic current amplitude; synaptic weight value read by the host neuron.

◦ Number of occupied slots; number of free slots; local surface and internal receptor pool sizes.

◦ Loss-of-function in postsynaptic scaffold genes such as those of the discs-large family produces intellectual disability by destabilising the rack.

# The slot count is the weight's operand: receptors leave and are
# retrapped from the surface pool, but occupancy holds near the set point
# fixed by the upstream switches (Opazo and Choquet, Curr Opin Neurobiol 2011).
import random
total_slots, trapped, pool = 40, 30, 200
set_point = 30                      # slots the Part One switch defends

for step in range(8):
    trapped -= random.randint(2, 6)                 # diffusion + endocytosis
    free = total_slots - trapped
    want = min(free, pool, max(0, set_point - trapped) + random.randint(2, 6))
    trapped += want                                 # retrapping + exocytosis
    weight = trapped / total_slots
    print(f"step {step}: trapped={trapped:2d}  weight={weight:.2f}")

PART THREE. THE STRUCTURAL CONSOLIDATION MACHINERY

The switches of Part One held a number and the tags of Part Two decided which synapses received the goods; this part is about making the change a matter of shape.

Where a chemical mark can be undone by chemistry, a rebuilt structure resists erasure simply by being built, and each of the mechanisms here converts a fleeting signal into architecture that outlives it.

The spine grows a new skeleton, the two sides of the junction are bolted into register, and a lattice condenses around whole cells to fence the mature wiring in place.

Together they are the reason a consolidated memory feels less like a stored value and more like a healed structure.


CHAPTER 9. THE SPINE ACTIN SKELETON (Regulated spine cytoskeleton) (Shape Lock) (COG)

A strengthened synapse does not just hold more receptors; the little knob that carries it physically swells, and stays swollen.

The swelling is done by actin, the same self-assembling protein fibre that gives every cell its shape, driven to build more filament and to cross-brace it into a stiffer, larger frame.

The chemistry that ordered the change is gone within minutes, but the enlarged spine remains for hours, days, sometimes far longer, because geometry, once built, does not need the original signal to persist.

This is memory turned into structure - the point at which a synapse stops holding a number and starts holding a shape.

Shape names what it stores: the enlarged geometry of the spine.

Lock names the trick: the shape holds itself after the chemistry that made it has faded.


WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The spine actin skeleton is the network of actin filaments (self-assembling protein rods) that fills a dendritic spine and sets its size and shape.

It stores the volume of the spine, which is tightly correlated with synaptic strength: a bigger spine head holds a bigger postsynaptic density and more receptor slots.

So the store is geometric - the enlarged, cross-braced shape that a potentiation event builds and leaves behind.


THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

Actin exists in two forms: free subunits (globular actin) and assembled filaments (filamentous actin), and it continually treadmills, adding subunits at one end while losing them at the other.

On potentiation, a calcium signal recruits actin, the severing protein cofilin, and the branch-nucleating complex Arp2/3 into the spine.

Cofilin is briefly inactivated, then acts to sever existing filaments, creating many new free ends that rapidly grow, while Arp2/3 nucleates branches, together expanding the network and pushing the membrane outward.

The spine settles into distinct actin pools: a fast-treadmilling dynamic pool at the tip that turns over in tens of seconds, a slow stable pool at the base turning over in about seventeen minutes, and, after potentiation, a new enlargement pool.

The lasting enlargement depends on growth of a stable, cross-linked pool of filaments, braced by cross-linkers such as alpha-actinin, drebrin and cortactin that slow the network's dynamics and hold the new size.


THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The trick is structural lock-in through a cross-linked stable filament pool that self-maintains by treadmilling, so the shape outlives the signal that built it.

Actin subunits are exchanged ceaselessly, so no individual filament is permanent, yet the cross-braced stable pool holds a fixed larger volume because subunits leaving are replaced in place, and the cross-linkers resist disassembly.

The chemical trigger - the calcium and kinase activity - lasts only minutes, but the enlarged geometry it commissions persists for hours and can be maintained indefinitely by continued treadmilling within the braced frame.

An engineer would call this casting a part and then leaving it: once the cross-linked scaffold is set, holding the shape costs only routine maintenance, not the original forming pressure.


WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The skeleton operates in the dendritic spines of the principal excitatory neurons of the Layer 6 catalogues, most vividly in the plastic pyramidal cells of cortical layers two, three and five and in hippocampal pyramidal cells.

At the Layer 7 junction level it is the internal machinery of every excitatory spine synapse.

Its enlargement is the structural signature of long-term potentiation at those junctions.


TIMESCALES:

The first actin remodelling begins within seconds to two minutes of a plasticity trigger.

The transient enlargement phase lasts several minutes; conversion to the stable enlarged state takes tens of minutes.

The stable pool then holds the new size for hours to days, and enlarged spines can persist for months in vivo, matching the lifetime of the memories they carry.


THE WRITE OPERATION:

The write is a potentiating stimulus that raises spine calcium and activates the small guanosine-triphosphate-hydrolysing switch proteins (Rho-family, the actin traffic controllers) and their downstream kinases.

These recruit actin, cofilin and Arp2/3 and drive the burst of polymerisation that enlarges the spine.

Consolidation of the enlargement into the stable pool completes the durable write.


THE READ OPERATION:

The stored shape is read as synaptic strength: spine volume sets the area of the postsynaptic density and therefore the number of receptor slots available to Chapter 8.

A larger spine sustains a larger excitatory current, so the geometry is continuously read out as the synapse's weight.


THE ERASE OPERATION:

Physiological erasure is spine shrinkage: long-term depression drives net actin disassembly, cofilin-mediated severing without regrowth, and loss of the stable pool, returning the spine to a smaller size.

Experimentally, actin-depolymerising drugs such as latrunculin applied during the window prevent or reverse the enlargement and block the durable phase of potentiation.

Honest note: whether a shrunken spine has truly erased the memory or merely down-weighted a synapse that could be re-enlarged is not fully resolved.


CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

Spine volume is a graded, continuous variable spanning roughly a tenfold range, so in principle a spine could encode several bits of strength.

In practice fidelity is limited by ongoing spine-size fluctuations that occur even without plasticity, a spontaneous drift that sets a floor on how finely a stable size can be read.

Reliable long-term storage therefore probably amounts to a few distinguishable levels, on the order of two to three bits per spine.

Honest unknown: how much of the observed spontaneous volume fluctuation is noise versus ongoing low-level learning is debated.


INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The skeleton is fed by the local translation stations of Chapter 7, which make cytoskeletal regulators on site, and by the plasticity product Arc, which regulates actin and receptor traffic.

It provides the physical volume that the slot system of Chapter 8 fills with receptors.

Stable actin has itself been proposed as a component of the synaptic tag of Chapter 5, marking which spine has earned capture.

It is braced from outside by the trans-synaptic adhesion complex of Chapter 10, and its enlargement is fuelled by the mitochondria of Chapter 12.


DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Spine actin dynamics are fastest in the developing brain, when spines are born, tested and pruned in vast numbers.

Turnover slows with maturation as the stable pool comes to dominate, which is part of why adult wiring is harder to change.

Age brings loss of spines and of the machinery that enlarges them.


EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Actin is one of the most ancient and conserved proteins in all of biology, its sequence nearly identical from yeast to human.

The regulators that neurons use to sculpt spines - cofilin, the Arp2/3 complex, the Rho-family switches - are the same general cell-shape toolkit used by crawling cells everywhere, redeployed to build and hold synaptic geometry.


DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Many intellectual-disability and autism genes encode regulators of spine actin, including the Rho-family switches and their controllers, and they produce abnormal spine shape and density.

Fragile X syndrome yields immature, elongated spines partly through dysregulated actin control.

Spine loss driven by actin collapse is an early feature of Alzheimer disease and of chronic stress.

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Spine calcium transient; Rho-family switch state; cofilin activity; free actin subunit supply; energy from local mitochondria.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Spine head volume; postsynaptic-density area available for receptor slots; mechanical bracing of the synapse.

• STATE.

◦ Filamentous-to-globular actin ratio; sizes of the dynamic, stable and enlargement pools; cross-linker occupancy.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Dynamic-pool turnover roughly 40 seconds; stable-pool turnover roughly 17 minutes; spine volume range about tenfold.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ First remodelling seconds to 2 minutes; stabilisation tens of minutes; enlarged state hours to months.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In fragile X syndrome and related intellectual disabilities, faulty Rho-family and cofilin control yields thin, immature spines that cannot lock in strength.

◦ Amyloid-beta and chronic stress drive actin collapse and spine loss, erasing structural memory in Alzheimer disease and depression.

A synaptic knob that physically swells and stays swollen, its size held by a cross-braced pool of the most ancient protein in biology, long after the calcium that ordered the change has drained away.


CHAPTER 10. THE TRANS-SYNAPTIC ADHESION COMPLEX (Cleft-spanning adhesion machinery) (Cross-Cleft Bolts) (COG)

A synapse is two half-machines facing each other across a gap barely twenty nanometres wide, and something must keep them aligned so that transmitter released on one side lands on receptors on the other.

That something is a set of adhesion proteins that reach across the cleft and clasp hands, holding the sending and receiving specialisations in register and signalling in both directions through the join.

Because the alignment is itself part of what makes the synapse strong and specific, these bolts store a kind of structural memory: the fact that this partner was chosen and locked to that one.

They are also, not by coincidence, one of the densest clusters of autism-risk genes in the genome, because a synapse that cannot register is a synapse that cannot compute.

Cross-Cleft names where they act: spanning the gap between the two cells.

Bolts names what they do: hold the two sides fastened in exact register.


WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The trans-synaptic adhesion complex is the set of cell-surface proteins, chiefly the presynaptic neurexins and the postsynaptic neuroligins (a matched pair of adhesion proteins), that bridge the synaptic cleft.

It stores the identity and registration of a synaptic connection: which presynaptic terminal is bound to which postsynaptic site, and the precise nanometre alignment of transmitter release to receptor cluster.

That stored registration is a structural record that a particular partnership was made and stabilised.


THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

Neurexins sit in the presynaptic membrane and neuroligins in the postsynaptic membrane, and their extracellular domains clasp across the cleft to form an adhesive bridge.

On the inside, neuroligins bind postsynaptic scaffold proteins and neurexins bind presynaptic release machinery, so a single bridge mechanically couples the transmitter-release site to the receptor rack opposite.

Many other adhesion families join them - the leucine-rich repeat transmembrane proteins, the synaptic-cell-adhesion molecules, the cadherins - forming a code of matching surfaces that specifies and holds a connection.

This coupling organises trans-synaptic nanocolumns, aligned pre- and postsynaptic patches that place release directly over receptors.


THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The trick is mechanical registration held by a redundant, continually re-clasped adhesion bond across the cleft, so the alignment survives the turnover of any single bolt.

Each adhesion protein turns over on the scale of hours to days, but the join persists because many bonds act in parallel and each vacated site is re-clasped from a local surface pool before the whole bridge can slip.

The stored quantity is not a molecule but a geometric relationship - release site over receptor cluster - and that geometry is self-restoring as long as the adhesion code on the two faces still matches.

An engineer would call this a bolted flange whose bolts are replaced one at a time while under load, so the joint never loses register even though no bolt is original.


WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The complex operates at every chemical synapse in the Layer 7 junction catalogues, excitatory and inhibitory alike, with different neurexin and neuroligin variants specifying each type.

Neuroligin one and the leucine-rich repeat transmembrane proteins mark excitatory synapses, while neuroligin two marks inhibitory ones, on the principal cells and interneurons of the Layer 6 catalogues.

The host structure is the cleft itself and the paired membranes that face it.


TIMESCALES:

Bond formation and initial synapse assembly occur over minutes to hours during synaptogenesis.

Individual adhesion proteins turn over across hours to days.

The registered alignment, once mature, is held for the lifetime of the synapse, which can be months to years.


THE WRITE OPERATION:

The write is synapse formation and stabilisation: a matching neurexin and neuroligin pair clasp, recruit scaffolds and release machinery to their two sides, and convert a tentative contact into an aligned junction.

Activity and plasticity can adjust the amount and composition of adhesion molecules, tuning how firmly a synapse is bolted and thereby how readily it is kept or pruned.


THE READ OPERATION:

The stored registration is read on every transmission event: because release is held over the receptor cluster, a given amount of transmitter produces a reliable postsynaptic response.

Loss of registration is read as reduced reliability and strength, so the alignment is continuously expressed in how faithfully the synapse transmits.


THE ERASE OPERATION:

Physiological erasure is synapse elimination: adhesion bonds are broken, often with the help of microglia and complement tagging, and the junction is disassembled during developmental pruning or forgetting.

Experimentally, cleaving neurexin-neuroligin binding, or knocking out the partners, destabilises synapses and reduces their number.

Honest note: adhesion loss and synapse loss are hard to separate cause from effect, so whether breaking the bolts erases a memory or merely marks an already-condemned synapse is often ambiguous.


CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

The information here is combinatorial: alternative splicing of neurexins alone generates thousands of variants, and the matching of surface codes across families gives an enormous space for specifying which partner binds which.

Per synapse, the stored quantity is closer to an identity label and an alignment than to a graded weight, so its capacity is best measured as connection specificity rather than bits of strength.

Fidelity of alignment is high - nanocolumns place release within tens of nanometres of receptors - but the matching code tolerates some redundancy.

Honest unknown: how much of the vast splice diversity is functionally read versus merely permitted is not settled.


INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The complex mechanically braces the slot system of Chapter 8 from the outside, keeping the rack aligned with the release site.

It anchors the spine actin skeleton of Chapter 9 through its scaffold connections, coupling adhesion to spine shape.

It works with the perineuronal net of Chapter 11, which stabilises whole-cell wiring once these junctions are set.

Its signalling feeds the synapse-to-nucleus relay of Part Four when connections are made or lost.


DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Adhesion molecules are central to building the nervous system: they help axons find partners and induce synapse formation during development.

Their composition matures as circuits stabilise, contributing to the closing of plastic windows.

In adulthood they maintain and gate the remodelling of existing synapses, and their decline accompanies age-related synapse loss.


EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Cell-adhesion molecules are among the oldest tools of multicellular life, used everywhere to hold cells together.

Neurexins and neuroligins are conserved across animals with nervous systems, and their elaboration - especially the splice diversity - tracks the growing complexity of brains, making them an old adhesion system specialised for synaptic specificity.


DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

This complex is one of the strongest genetic hotspots for autism spectrum disorder: mutations in neurexin one, in neuroligins three and four, and in the downstream scaffold Shank family are recurrently found, including the neuroligin-3 substitution of arginine to cysteine at position 451.

Neurexin and neuroligin variants also appear in intellectual disability and schizophrenia.

The common thread is a synapse that forms or aligns poorly, degrading the brain's ability to compute.


SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

◦ Matching surface adhesion code on the partner membrane; activity level; scaffold and release-machinery availability.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Mechanical registration of release site over receptor cluster; trans-synaptic organising signals to both sides.

• STATE.

◦ Number and identity of engaged neurexin-neuroligin bonds; nanocolumn alignment; splice-variant composition.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Cleft width roughly 20 nanometres; neurexin splice variants in the thousands; alignment precision tens of nanometres.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Assembly minutes to hours; adhesion-protein turnover hours to days; registered junction lifetime months to years.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In autism spectrum disorder, neurexin-1, neuroligin-3 and neuroligin-4 and Shank mutations impair synapse formation and trans-synaptic signalling.

◦ Loss of neuroligins globally destabilises synapse organisation and can be lethal by disrupting essential circuits.


THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

The trans-synaptic adhesion complex maps to a engineering module named edge_registration_bolts.

It subscribes to partner identity signals across the cleft and publishes the mechanical and molecular registration that keeps an edge's two endpoints matched.

The nearest analogue is schema typing that holds both ends of a connection to one contract - the biological difference is that the type system is adhesive, literally.

The purpose of this procedure is to keep the two sides of a synapse in register by re-clasping adhesion bolts one at a time, so the alignment holds even as individual bolts turn over.

Create a number variable called clasped bolts with initial value 12.

Create a text variable called registered with initial value yes.

    Set clasped bolts to the smaller of total bolts and clasped bolts plus the number re-clasped from the surface pool.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the point is that structural registration is held across the cleft while bolts are swapped, and a short loop shows the alignment surviving continual turnover.

# Structural registration held across the cleft: adhesion bolts turn over,
# but parallel redundancy and re-clasping keep release aligned to receptors
# (Sudhof, Nature 2008; nanocolumn model, Tang et al., Nature 2016).
import random
total_bolts, clasped, code_matches = 12, 12, True

for step in range(8):
    clasped -= random.randint(1, 4)                 # bolts turn over
    if code_matches:
        clasped = min(total_bolts, clasped + random.randint(2, 4))  # re-clasp
    registered = clasped >= 8                        # enough to hold register
    print(f"step {step}: clasped={clasped:2d}/12  registered={registered}")

A handful of proteins reaching across a twenty-nanometre gap to clasp the two halves of a synapse into exact register, replaced one bolt at a time so alignment never slips - and the genome's densest cluster of autism risk.


CHAPTER 11. THE PERINEURONAL NET (Chondroitin-sulphate perisomatic lattice) (Plasticity Fence) (COG)

Around certain neurons, as childhood learning closes, a mesh condenses like a cast setting around a healed bone.

It is a lattice of sugar-coated proteins that wraps the cell body and proximal dendrites, and once it is in place the wiring it encloses becomes hard to change.

This is not a store of any one memory but a brake on rewriting them - a fence thrown up to protect mature circuits from further edits, and, remarkably, a fence that an enzyme can cut to reopen the plasticity of youth.

To lock learning in by building a wall around it, and to be able to take the wall down again, is one of the most striking persistence tricks in the brain.

Fence names what it is: a barrier around the cell that keeps edits out.

Plasticity names what it gates: the very ability of the enclosed synapses to change.


WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The perineuronal net is a dense lattice of the extracellular matrix, built from chondroitin-sulphate proteoglycans (sugar-decorated matrix proteins) held on a hyaluronan backbone, that condenses around mostly parvalbumin-expressing inhibitory interneurons.

It stores a set-point rather than a value: the closed-or-open state of a circuit's plasticity, fixed at the end of a developmental critical period.

What it holds is the decision that this wiring is now mature and protected, a durable configuration of the enclosing tissue.


THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

The net is assembled from aggrecan and related chondroitin-sulphate proteoglycans, linked to long hyaluronan chains and cross-tied by link proteins and the glycoprotein tenascin-R.

Its sugar chains bind and hold plasticity-limiting signals at the cell surface, including molecules that stabilise existing synapses and repel new ones.

One key route: the net's components engage the receptor protein tyrosine phosphatase sigma, which restrains the neurotrophin receptor tropomyosin-receptor-kinase B in parvalbumin interneurons, keeping the brake on.

By enwrapping the fast-spiking interneurons that set cortical inhibition, the net also locks in the inhibitory tone that governs when plasticity is permitted.


THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The trick is a stable structural lattice of slow-turnover matrix that physically fences synapses, holding the closed state until the fence is enzymatically removed.

The extracellular matrix turns over far more slowly than intracellular proteins, so the net is one of the most durable structures at the cell surface, and its very presence maintains the low-plasticity state.

Persistence is therefore by construction: the configuration is held not by a renewing chemical loop but by a long-lived physical barrier, and it stays closed by default.

An engineer would call this a hardware write-protect tab: set it and the memory beneath cannot be rewritten; the striking biological addition is that the tab can be dissolved to re-enable writing.


WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The net operates around the parvalbumin-expressing fast-spiking interneurons catalogued in the Layer 6 volumes, most prominently the basket cells of the cerebral cortex, and around selected cells in the hippocampus, amygdala and deeper structures.

It is a perisomatic structure, wrapping the cell body and proximal dendrites and leaving windows at synaptic contacts.

Its closure coincides with the maturation of these interneurons and the end of the visual and other cortical critical periods.


TIMESCALES:

The net condenses over the weeks of late development that close each critical period.

Once formed, it persists for the life of the animal, with slow, partial turnover.

Enzymatic removal reopens plasticity within days, and the net can slowly re-form thereafter.


THE WRITE OPERATION:

The write is developmental: sensory experience matures the parvalbumin interneurons, which triggers condensation of the net and closes the critical period.

The write can also be partly re-run in adulthood, since experience, exercise and some drug treatments modulate net density.


THE READ OPERATION:

The stored closed state is read as the reduced plasticity of the enclosed circuit: synapses inside a mature net resist the structural changes of Chapters 9 and 10.

Whenever the circuit fails to rewire in response to a stimulus that would have reshaped a juvenile brain, the net's setting is being read out.


THE ERASE OPERATION:

Physiological loosening occurs with certain experiences and states that reduce net density and locally lift the brake.

The classic experimental erase is the enzyme chondroitinase ABC (a bacterial enzyme that digests chondroitin-sulphate chains), which strips the net and reopens critical-period-like plasticity in adult cortex, for example restoring ocular-dominance plasticity.

Antidepressant drugs act partly by the same route, releasing tropomyosin-receptor-kinase B from the phosphatase brake in parvalbumin interneurons.

Honest note: reopening plasticity is not automatically beneficial, since the net also protects against maladaptive rewriting and against excitotoxicity, so its removal is a double-edged intervention.


CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

The net stores essentially one graded variable per enclosed cell - the degree of plasticity closure - rather than a multi-bit pattern, so its per-cell information content is low, perhaps around one bit for open versus closed with some intermediate grading.

Its fidelity as a brake is high: mature nets reliably hold the closed state for years.

Honest unknown: the precise mapping from net density and sulphation pattern to the exact amount of residual plasticity is not quantitatively pinned down, and the sulphation code may carry more information than a single bit implies.


INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The net gates the structural machinery of Chapters 9 and 10, permitting or forbidding the spine enlargement and adhesion remodelling that rewrite connections.

By enwrapping parvalbumin interneurons it sets the inhibitory context in which the switches and tags of Parts One and Two can operate.

It works alongside the epigenetic stores of Part Four, which also help close developmental windows, so that critical-period closure is enforced at both the chromatin and the extracellular-matrix levels.


DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

The net is fundamentally a developmental clock's output, marking the transition from a plastic juvenile circuit to a stable adult one.

It persists across adult life as a guardian of that stability.

In ageing, net changes are linked to both rigidity and, where nets degrade, to vulnerability, and net manipulation is being explored to restore youthful plasticity in disease.


EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Chondroitin-sulphate proteoglycans and hyaluronan are ancient components of the extracellular matrix used throughout the body for structure and signalling.

Their condensation into perineuronal nets that gate neural plasticity is a vertebrate elaboration, repurposing a general matrix system as a switch that closes learning windows.


DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Perineuronal-net abnormalities are reported in schizophrenia, where reduced nets around cortical parvalbumin interneurons accompany the disorder's inhibitory deficits.

Nets are implicated in the persistence of drug-associated memories in addiction, and their manipulation can weaken such memories in animal models.

They also form part of the inhibitory scar that blocks regeneration after spinal-cord injury, which is why chondroitinase is studied as a repair treatment, and they are altered in epilepsy and Alzheimer disease.


SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Developmental stage; sensory experience; parvalbumin-interneuron maturation; enzymatic and pharmacological modulators.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Plasticity-closed-or-open state of the enclosed circuit; stabilised inhibitory tone; surface-held plasticity-limiting signals.

• STATE.

◦ Net density; sulphation pattern of the chondroitin-sulphate chains; occupancy of protein tyrosine phosphatase sigma.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Condensation over weeks at critical-period close; reopening within days of chondroitinase; slow re-formation over weeks.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Formation weeks; maintenance years; enzymatic removal days; matrix turnover slow relative to intracellular proteins.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In schizophrenia, reduced perineuronal nets around cortical parvalbumin interneurons accompany impaired inhibition.

◦ In addiction, over-stable nets help lock in drug-associated memories; in spinal-cord injury, net-rich inhibitory scars block regeneration.


THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

The perineuronal net maps to a engineering module named plasticity_freeze_fence.

It subscribes to maturation and criticality signals and publishes a write-protection state over converged subnetworks.

The nearest analogue is freezing trained layers - the biological difference is that the fence is enzymatically removable, so the freeze is a policy, not a fate.

Set net density to the current measured density of the net.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the mechanism is a removable brake, and a short function makes plain that plasticity is closed by default and reopens only when the fence is cut.

# Plasticity brake with removable fencing: an intact perineuronal net holds
# the circuit closed; chondroitinase ABC digests it and reopens juvenile
# plasticity (Pizzorusso et al., Science 2002).
def circuit_plasticity(net_density):
    # net_density 1.0 = mature intact net, 0.0 = fully digested
    if net_density > 0.4:
        return "closed (write-protected)"
    return "open (rewriting enabled)"

timeline = [("critical period close", 1.0),
            ("adult, untreated", 0.9),
            ("day 3 after chondroitinase", 0.2),
            ("weeks later, re-formed", 0.7)]
for label, density in timeline:
    print(f"{label:28} density={density:>3}  -> {circuit_plasticity(density)}")

A sugar-and-protein mesh that condenses around a cell as childhood learning ends and write-protects the wiring inside it for life - unless an enzyme dissolves the fence and reopens the plasticity of youth.


CHAPTER 12. THE MITOCHONDRIAL PROVISIONING SYSTEM (Synaptic mitochondrial energy and calcium service) (Power Dock) (NON-COG)

Every mechanism in this dossier runs on energy, and much of it runs on the careful handling of calcium, and neither is free at a synapse hundreds of micrometres from the cell body.

So the neuron docks mitochondria, its power plants, at the places where the demand is - beneath active spines and along busy stretches of dendrite and axon - where they burn fuel into the cell's energy currency and soak up excess calcium.

They do not store a memory, and they do not compute; they make the writing, reading and holding of memory physically possible.

This chapter is the dossier's supporting act: the power supply that every entry above quietly depends on.

Power names what it delivers: energy and calcium handling.

Dock names how: mitochondria are parked and anchored exactly where the load is.


WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The mitochondrial provisioning system is the positioning and anchoring of mitochondria (the cell's energy-producing organelles) at sites of high synaptic demand, together with the energy and calcium services they provide there.

It does not store cognitive information; what it holds is a spatial arrangement - which synapses currently have a power dock nearby.

That arrangement is a supporting resource, not a memory, which is why this entry is tagged as not directly cognitive.


THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

Mitochondria are hauled along microtubules by motor proteins, linked to the outward-walking kinesin motor through an adaptor complex containing the calcium-sensing protein Miro (a mitochondrial outer-membrane protein with calcium-binding domains).

When a synapse is active and local calcium rises, calcium binds Miro's sensing domains and releases the motor, so the mitochondrion halts precisely where the activity is.

Anchoring proteins such as syntaphilin then fix it in place, docking it beneath the base of the spine or along the active axon.

There it produces adenosine triphosphate (ATP, the cell's chemical energy currency) by oxidative phosphorylation, and it buffers calcium by taking it up through the mitochondrial calcium uniporter, shaping the local calcium signal.


THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

This section is present but limited, because the mechanism's role is supporting rather than a store of cognitive information.

There is a persistence-relevant behaviour worth stating plainly: by parking a long-lived, self-renewing organelle at a demanding synapse, the system provides a durable local supply of energy and calcium handling that outlasts any single burst of activity.

Mitochondria themselves persist and renew by fission, fusion and the import of fresh proteins, so a docked power supply can be maintained indefinitely, but what persists is provisioning capacity, not a learned value.

An engineer would call this stationing a generator at a substation: it keeps the lights on so the real computation elsewhere can run, without itself holding any of the data.


WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The system operates throughout the neurons of the Layer 6 catalogues, docking mitochondria at the pre- and postsynaptic sites of the Layer 7 junctions.

Presynaptically it services transmitter release and vesicle recycling at active terminals; postsynaptically it docks beneath the base of dendritic spines.

It is especially demanding in the fast-spiking parvalbumin interneurons, whose high firing rates make them heavily dependent on mitochondrial supply.


TIMESCALES:

Mitochondrial transport and repositioning occur over seconds to minutes in response to activity.

Docking at an active site can last minutes to hours.

The organelles themselves persist for days to weeks, renewed continuously by fission, fusion and protein import.


THE WRITE OPERATION:

There is no cognitive write; the closest analogue is activity-driven repositioning, in which local calcium halts and anchors a mitochondrion at a newly demanding synapse.

This adjusts where provisioning is concentrated but does not encode a specific memory.


THE READ OPERATION:

There is no cognitive read; the system's output is read only in the sense that nearby machinery draws on the ATP and calcium buffering it supplies.

A synapse with a docked mitochondrion can sustain the energy-hungry work of plasticity that a poorly provisioned one cannot.


THE ERASE OPERATION:

There is no memory to erase; the analogous operation is undocking and removal, in which a mitochondrion is released and transported away, or cleared by mitophagy (the targeted degradation of worn-out mitochondria) when damaged.

Loss of provisioning does not erase a stored value but can starve the machinery that maintains one.


CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

There is no cognitive capacity to quote; the relevant measures are provisioning quantities.

A single mitochondrion supplies large numbers of ATP molecules per second and can buffer micromolar calcium loads, and the density of dendritic mitochondria correlates with local synaptic and metabolic demand.

Honest unknown: precisely how mitochondrial positioning maps onto which synapses are strengthened is an active question, and the causal direction is not fully resolved.


INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

This system powers essentially every mechanism in the dossier: the local translation stations of Chapter 7, the receptor traffic of Chapter 8, the actin remodelling of Chapter 9, and the transcription of Part Four all draw on mitochondrial ATP.

Its calcium buffering shapes the very calcium signals that write the switches of Part One and set the direction of plasticity at the slot rack.

It is the shared precondition that the cognitive entries take for granted, which is exactly why it is catalogued here as their supporting act.


DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Mitochondrial supply is critical during development, when building synapses is metabolically expensive.

Provisioning must keep pace with a neuron's activity throughout life, and declining mitochondrial function is a hallmark of brain ageing.

Failures of mitochondrial transport and quality control accumulate with age and disease.


EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Mitochondria are descended from an ancient free-living bacterium taken up by an early eukaryotic cell, one of the foundational events of complex life.

Neurons inherited this universal power plant and evolved specialised transport and anchoring machinery to station it at synapses, a neural specialisation of a system shared by all complex cells.


DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Mitochondrial dysfunction is implicated across neurodegeneration: impaired transport and quality control feature in Parkinson disease, where the genes PINK1 and parkin govern mitochondrial clearance, and in Alzheimer disease and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.

Primary mitochondrial diseases cause encephalopathies with prominent cognitive and neurological impairment.

Because parvalbumin interneurons are so energy-dependent, mitochondrial deficits there are linked to the inhibitory failures of schizophrenia.


SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Local calcium level sensed by Miro; activity level of the nearby synapse; motor and anchor availability; substrate and oxygen supply.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Local adenosine triphosphate; local calcium buffering; docked-mitochondrion positioning.

• STATE.

◦ Mitochondrial position and docked-or-motile status; membrane potential of the organelle; local ATP and calcium levels.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Transport speed on the order of a tenth to one micrometre per second; halt triggered near micromolar calcium; ATP output large per organelle.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Repositioning seconds to minutes; docking minutes to hours; organelle renewal days to weeks.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In Parkinson disease, PINK1 and parkin failures leave damaged mitochondria uncleared and mispositioned, starving synapses.

◦ In parvalbumin interneurons, mitochondrial deficits impair fast spiking and contribute to the inhibitory dysfunction of schizophrenia.

The mitochondrial provisioning system maps to a engineering module named local_power_provisioning.

It subscribes to activity and demand signals and publishes positioned energy and calcium services at the busiest edges.

The nearest analogue is a resource scheduler co-locating power with compute - the biological difference is that provisioning is itself activity-dependent and slowly learned.

PART FOUR. THE NUCLEAR AND GENOMIC MACHINERY

The four mechanisms of Parts One through Three all live in the cytoplasm and the membrane, close to the synapse that they serve, and none of them touches the master copy of the cell's instructions.

This part descends into the nucleus, the walled inner chamber where the deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) is kept, and asks how an event at a synapse ten thousand times its width away can reach in and change what the genome reads out.

These are the slowest stores in the whole dossier: a synaptic tag lasts an hour, a spine's actin lasts days, but a methyl group written onto DNA can outlast the cell's every other molecule and, in some cases, the better part of a human life.

Here the storage question sharpens to its hardest form, because chromatin is written once and read many times, and the write must be selective enough that an ordinary Tuesday does not overwrite a childhood.


CHAPTER 13. THE SYNAPSE-TO-NUCLEUS SIGNALLING RELAY (The Retrograde Petition Channel) (Long-Runner Courier) (COG)

A synapse that has just learned something faces a delivery problem no postal service ever solved: it must send a signed request the whole length of a dendrite, past a thousand rival synapses, to a nucleus that will never come to visit.

The message cannot shout, because the cytoplasm damps electrical and chemical signals within microns, so instead the synapse writes a letter and hands it to a courier that walks the entire distance on foot.

The courier is a shuttle protein; the road is a microtubule; and the letter, once delivered, either opens or shuts the genome's willingness to answer.

Long-Runner names the courier that survives the whole retrograde journey. Courier names its single job: carry one signed request from the membrane to the chromatin.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The synapse-to-nucleus signalling relay is the set of mobile proteins and calcium signals that carry news of a plasticity event from a stimulated synapse to the cell nucleus, so that the genome can be recruited into a long-term memory.

It does not itself store the memory; what it stores, briefly, is a routed request - the identity and strength of a synaptic event, encoded in the phosphorylation state and cargo of a travelling protein.

Think of it as the request channel of consolidation: the thing that decides whether a synaptic whisper is loud enough, and coherent enough, to be worth waking the nucleus for.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

The best-worked example is a protein called Jacob, which sits near N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptors, the calcium-passing glutamate receptors that detect coincident activity.

When those receptors open, calcium enters, and a kinase called calcium-calmodulin-dependent protein kinase two alpha (CaMKII-alpha) together with extracellular-signal-regulated kinase (ERK) phosphorylates Jacob at a site called serine 180, forming a stable three-part complex of Jacob, phosphorylated ERK, and importin.

Importin-alpha and importin-beta are the standard cellular carriers that recognise a nuclear localisation signal - a short address tag on a protein that means "deliver to nucleus" - and hand their cargo to the motor protein dynein, which walks it backward along microtubules toward the cell body.

A calcium-binding protein called caldendrin can grab Jacob's address tag in a calcium-dependent way and hold it at the synapse, so the decision to send is itself gated by how much and what kind of calcium arrived.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

This mechanism is not itself a long-term store, so its trick is a transport trick rather than a persistence trick: it keeps the message alive long enough to survive a journey that takes hours.

The signed cargo is protected in transit by being locked into a trimeric complex, so that the phosphate mark on ERK and on Jacob - which a passing phosphatase would otherwise strip in seconds - is shielded until arrival.

The engineering lesson an engineer could copy is encapsulation for transport: wrap the fragile payload and its checksum together in a carrier that also encodes the destination, so the whole packet either arrives intact or fails cleanly, never half-delivered.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The relay runs in principal excitatory neurons that carry NMDA receptors and long dendrites, above all the layer two and three intratelencephalic (L2/3 IT) and layer five pyramidal cells of the neocortex catalogued in the corpus's Layer 6 volume, and the CA1 and dentate granule cells of the hippocampus.

The hosting junctions are the glutamatergic excitatory synapses of Layer 7, specifically the NMDA-receptor-bearing postsynaptic densities on dendritic spines, from which the retrograde journey begins.

TIMESCALES:

Calcium entry lasts milliseconds; complex assembly takes seconds to minutes; the retrograde walk from a distal dendrite to the nucleus takes on the order of thirty minutes to several hours depending on distance.

The window matters because it sets the shortest time in which a synaptic event can begin to influence transcription, which is why the earliest immediate-early gene messenger ribonucleic acid appears within about five to fifteen minutes but full protein answers take an hour or more.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

The request is written by coincident, strong synaptic activity that opens enough NMDA receptors to raise local calcium past the threshold for CaMKII-alpha and ERK activation.

The physiological writing condition is the same one that induces long-term potentiation: high-frequency or appropriately timed input, sufficient to phosphorylate the courier and expose its nuclear address.

Crucially, the source of calcium biases the message - synaptic NMDA receptors and extrasynaptic ones send different-signed requests, so the relay carries not just "something happened" but a coarse judgement of what kind of event it was.

THE READ OPERATION:

The stored request is read at the nucleus, where the arriving courier releases its cargo and modifies transcription factors, most notably by acting on cyclic adenosine monophosphate response element binding protein (CREB), the gate catalogued in Part One entry four.

Depending on the courier's phosphorylation state and origin, the read-out either promotes CREB-driven transcription of plasticity genes or drives CREB shut-off, a silencing that simplifies the cell's branching architecture.

The read is therefore not passive: the nucleus interprets the incoming message and only then decides whether to answer with gene expression.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

There is no dedicated erase, because the relay holds no durable state; the message is consumed on delivery.

Physiologically, the request decays if phosphatases strip the courier before it arrives, or if caldendrin sequesters it at the synapse, so the erasure route is simply failure to deliver.

Experimentally, blocking dynein-based transport, or mutating the nuclear address tag, prevents delivery and so erases the request in flight, which is how the pathway was proven.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

Capacity is low by design: a single delivered request carries roughly the information of "a strong coincident event of a given sign occurred on this cell", perhaps a few bits, aggregated over many synapses.

Fidelity is limited by the fact that many synapses share one nucleus, so the relay cannot address a single synapse; it reports a cell-wide summary, and synapse-specific addressing is handled downstream by the tagging machinery of Part Two.

Honest unknown: nobody has measured a true error rate for synapse-to-nucleus delivery in a living human neuron, and the numbers here are inferred from rodent live-imaging of labelled couriers.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The relay is the on-ramp to the genomic machinery: its delivered request triggers the immediate-early gene response of Chapter 14, which in turn recruits the histone and DNA-methylation writers of Chapters 15 and 16.

Upstream, it depends on the same CaMKII activity that runs the bistable switch of Part One, so a single calcium event both flips a local switch and dispatches a nuclear courier.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Synapse-to-nucleus signalling is heavily used during development, when activity-dependent transcription shapes wiring, and caldendrin and Jacob expression track the maturation of glutamatergic synapses.

In ageing, transport slows as microtubule networks and motor function decline, which is one proposed contributor to the weaker consolidation seen in older brains.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Importins and dynein are ancient, universal to eukaryotic cells, where they have always carried cargo into and along the cell; neurons did not invent them but repurposed a general logistics system for the special problem of a very long cell.

The neuron-specific couriers such as Jacob are evolutionary latecomers, adapting the old nuclear-import address system to report synaptic events.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Excessive extrasynaptic NMDA-receptor signalling drives Jacob-mediated CREB shut-off and dendritic simplification, a pathway implicated in the neuronal loss of Alzheimer disease and in excitotoxic damage after stroke.

Disrupted synapse-to-nucleus signalling is also proposed in intellectual-disability and neurodegeneration syndromes where transport or transcription-coupling genes are mutated.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Local synaptic calcium concentration, transient rise from roughly 0.05 micromolar at rest to 1 to 10 micromolar during activity.

◦ NMDA-receptor open state and its synaptic versus extrasynaptic origin.

◦ CaMKII-alpha and ERK activity state.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ A phosphorylated nuclear courier delivered to the nucleus.

◦ A CREB activation or CREB shut-off signal.

• STATE.

◦ Courier phosphorylation state at serine 180; bound-versus-free importin; retrograde position along the dendrite.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Retrograde dynein transport speed roughly 1 micrometre per second; dendritic path length up to several hundred micrometres.

◦ Calcium threshold for CaMKII-alpha activation near 1 micromolar.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Calcium transient 10 to 100 milliseconds; complex assembly seconds to minutes; delivery 30 minutes to several hours.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In Alzheimer disease, amyloid-beta drives extrasynaptic NMDA-receptor activation and Jacob-mediated CREB shut-off, contributing to dendritic simplification and synapse loss.

◦ In stroke and excitotoxicity, the same route drives cell degeneration.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

In engineering terms this mechanism is the write_request_channel: it subscribes to per-synapse activation events and publishes a single routed, signed consolidation request to the nucleus module.

The nearest engineering analogue is a remote procedure call carrying an authentication token across a slow, lossy link: the payload must be signed (phosphorylated), addressed (nuclear localisation signal), and may be dropped in transit.

The biological difference is that the "network" is physical microtubule transport at one micrometre per second, so latency is measured in minutes to hours, not milliseconds, and there is no retransmission - a dropped request is simply lost.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to decide whether a synaptic event is strong enough to dispatch a courier to the nucleus, and to deliver it only if it survives transport.

Create a number variable called calcium level with initial value set from the synaptic calcium reading in micromolar.

Create a number variable called calcium threshold with initial value 1.0.

Create a number variable called phosphatase strip chance with initial value 0.3.

Create a text variable called delivery result with initial value "no request sent".

Check if calcium level is greater than or equal to calcium threshold.

If that is true, then perform the following actions.

    Set delivery result to "courier phosphorylated and dispatched".

    Draw a random fraction between 0 and 1 and call it strip roll.

    Check if strip roll is less than phosphatase strip chance.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the point is a thresholded, probabilistic delivery - fire only above threshold, then survive transport or not - and Python expresses that gate and coin-flip in a few plain lines.

# Synapse-to-nucleus petition-and-response relay with thresholding.
# A strong calcium event phosphorylates a courier (Jacob) that dynein
# walks to the nucleus; phosphatases may strip it in transit.
# (Karpova et al., Cell 2013; Dieterich et al., PLoS Biology 2008.)
import random

CA_THRESHOLD = 1.0       # micromolar, CaMKII-alpha activation point
STRIP_CHANCE = 0.30      # probability the phosphate is stripped in transit

def relay(calcium_uM):
    if calcium_uM < CA_THRESHOLD:
        return "below threshold: no request sent"
    # dispatched; now survive the retrograde walk
    if random.random() < STRIP_CHANCE:
        return "request lost in transit"
    return "request delivered to nucleus -> CREB engaged"

for ca in (0.2, 0.9, 3.0, 8.0):
    outcomes = [relay(ca) for _ in range(1000)]
    delivered = sum("delivered" in o for o in outcomes)
    print(f"calcium {ca:4.1f} uM: {delivered:4d}/1000 requests delivered")

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A protein courier that is phosphorylated at a synapse, wrapped with importin and a checksum, and walked by hand the entire length of a dendrite to hand the genome a single signed request - the only mechanism in the cell whose job is a slow, physical, long-distance letter.


CHAPTER 14. THE IMMEDIATE-EARLY GENE RESPONSE (The First Genomic Answer) (Fast Ledger) (COG)

Ask the nucleus a question and, within a few minutes, it answers - not with the finished proteins of memory, but with a first, fast wave of transcription that both begins the work and, incidentally, leaves a mark neuroscientists have learned to read.

That first wave is the immediate-early genes, so named because they turn on without needing any new protein to be made first: the switch to read them is already in the room.

They are, at once, the opening move of the genomic write and the timestamp that lets a scientist ask, months later, "which cells were active that afternoon?"

Fast names how quickly they answer, minutes not hours. Ledger names what they leave behind: a durable, readable record of which neurons fired.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The immediate-early gene response is the rapid, protein-synthesis-independent burst of transcription that a strong synaptic event triggers in the nucleus, producing genes such as c-Fos, Arc (activity-regulated cytoskeleton-associated protein), Egr1 (early growth response one), and Npas4 (neuronal PAS domain protein four).

What it stores, transiently, is a record of recent activity - the fact that this particular neuron was strongly driven in the last hour - written as a pulse of messenger ribonucleic acid and then protein.

Because that pulse can be captured by genetic tricks, the immediate-early gene response has become the standard tool for labelling engram cells, the sparse neuron populations that carry a specific memory.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

Immediate-early genes sit in the genome already poised, with paused ribonucleic-acid polymerase and permissive chromatin, so that when an incoming signal activates transcription factors such as CREB and serum response factor, transcription begins at once without waiting for any new protein.

The relay of Chapter 13 delivers the activating signal; CREB and its partners bind the promoters; and within five to fifteen minutes the first immediate-early messenger ribonucleic acid appears.

Some immediate-early gene products, such as c-Fos and Egr1, are themselves transcription factors that then switch on a slower second wave of "late-response" genes, so the fast answer sets up the durable one.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The immediate-early gene response is fast and self-limiting, not persistent, so on its own it holds nothing for long - its messengers and proteins are gone within hours.

Its contribution to persistence is indirect and it works by feed-forward hand-off: the fast wave switches on transcription-factor products that recruit the enduring writers, so a minutes-long pulse seeds an hours-to-lifelong change downstream.

The trick an engineer could copy is the pre-armed trigger: keep the first-response machinery loaded and poised at all times so the answer costs no start-up latency, and use that instant answer only to launch the slow, durable commit.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The response runs in the same principal excitatory neurons as the relay - neocortical L2/3 IT and layer five pyramidal cells, hippocampal CA1, CA3 and dentate granule cells - and also in some inhibitory interneurons, especially for Npas4.

The hosting junctions are again the Layer 7 glutamatergic excitatory synapses whose NMDA-receptor activity drives the calcium signal, though the response is a whole-cell nuclear event rather than a synapse-local one.

TIMESCALES:

Messenger ribonucleic acid appears within about 5 to 15 minutes, peaks near 30 to 60 minutes, and decays over 1 to 4 hours; the protein products lag by roughly 30 to 90 minutes and clear within several hours.

This is the fastest genomic timescale in the dossier, and it defines the temporal resolution with which activity-tagging methods can timestamp a memory.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

The response is written by any event strong enough to raise nuclear calcium and activate CREB - novel experience, strong sensory input, learning, or seizure.

In the laboratory, the write is deliberately harnessed: a fos-promoter drive coupled to a drug-gated switch, as in targeted recombination in active populations (TRAP), opens a permanent genetic label only in neurons that expressed the immediate-early gene during a chosen time window.

THE READ OPERATION:

Physiologically, the immediate-early gene products are read by their own downstream targets: Arc protein is trafficked to synapses to regulate receptor internalisation, and the transcription-factor products bind late-gene promoters.

Experimentally, the read is done by the scientist, who visualises the labelled cells or reactivates them optogenetically to test whether that population is the memory - the read that turned immediate-early genes into the workhorse of engram research.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

The natural erase is simple degradation: the messengers and proteins are unstable and clear within hours, resetting the cell for the next event.

There is no need to erase the ledger deliberately, and experimental labels installed via the fos promoter are, by contrast, made permanent on purpose so the record persists for the experimenter.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

As a per-cell activity marker the immediate-early gene response is close to one bit: strongly active recently, or not, though graded expression carries somewhat more.

Fidelity is imperfect: c-Fos and Arc label overlapping but non-identical populations, with fewer than half of tagged cells expressing both markers in some memory tasks, so any single immediate-early gene is a noisy readout of "the engram".

The honest caveat is that immediate-early gene expression marks strong activity, which correlates with but is not identical to memory encoding, so the ledger reads activity, and the experimenter infers memory.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The response is triggered by the relay of Chapter 13 and it is the first stage of the genomic write, handing off to the histone code of Chapter 15 and the DNA methylation store of Chapter 16.

Arc, one of its products, is also the strange retrovirus-like courier catalogued as a plasticity product in Part Two entry six, so this chapter and that one describe the same molecule from two angles.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Immediate-early genes are central to activity-dependent development, where they translate patterned firing into the transcription that refines circuits during critical periods.

Across life their induction stays robust, though baseline and evoked levels shift with age and with sleep, which strongly modulates immediate-early gene expression.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

The immediate-early gene programme is an ancient, general cellular stress-and-growth response - c-Fos and Egr1 turn on in many cell types after growth-factor stimulation - that neurons co-opted for activity-dependent plasticity.

Their pre-poised promoter architecture is a widespread eukaryotic solution to the need for fast, standing-start transcription.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Altered immediate-early gene expression appears across psychiatric and neurological disease - blunted induction in some models of depression and schizophrenia, aberrant induction in epilepsy where seizures drive massive c-Fos expression, and dysregulation in addiction, where drug-evoked immediate-early genes help entrench compulsive associations.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Nuclear calcium and delivered courier signal from the relay.

◦ CREB, serum response factor, and mitogen-activated protein kinase activity.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Pulses of c-Fos, Arc, Egr1, Npas4 messenger ribonucleic acid and protein.

◦ A second-wave transcription-factor drive to late-response genes.

• STATE.

◦ Per-gene transcription level, currently rising, peaked, or decaying.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Induction threshold set by CREB activation; messenger half-life roughly 30 to 60 minutes.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Onset 5 to 15 minutes; peak 30 to 60 minutes; decay 1 to 4 hours.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In epilepsy, seizures drive pathological, massive immediate-early gene induction.

◦ In addiction, repeated drug-evoked c-Fos and a stable truncated Fos variant (delta-FosB) accumulate and entrench compulsive associations.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

In engineering terms this mechanism is the activity_logger: it subscribes to nuclear activation events and publishes a fast, timestamped, decaying record of which unit was strongly driven, plus a trigger to the durable-write path.

The nearest engineering analogue is a write-ahead log or an append-only event ledger: a cheap, fast record written immediately so the slower durable commit can follow.

The biological difference is that the log entries self-erase within hours unless a downstream durable writer copies them forward, and that the same entries double as an externally readable activity tag - the basis of engram labelling.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to log a fast activity pulse when a neuron is strongly driven, let it decay over time, and flag whether it crossed the threshold to launch a durable write.

Create a number variable called drive with initial value set from the neuron's recent activation.

Create a number variable called induction threshold with initial value 1.0.

Create a number variable called ieg level with initial value 0.

Create a text variable called durable write with initial value "not launched".

If that is true, then perform the following actions.

    Set ieg level to the value of drive.

    Set durable write to "launched: recruit histone and methylation writers".

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the mechanism is a fast pulse that decays while flagging a durable follow-up, and Python makes the pulse-and-decay loop and the one-shot flag immediately legible.

# Immediate-early gene response as a fast activity logger.
# A strong drive writes a decaying c-Fos/Arc pulse and, above threshold,
# launches the durable genomic write. (Minatohara et al., Front Mol Neurosci 2016.)
INDUCTION = 1.0     # activation units needed to induce
HALF_LIFE_H = 1.0   # hours; pulse halves each hour

def ieg_pulse(drive, hours=5):
    launched = drive >= INDUCTION
    level = drive if launched else 0.0
    trace = []
    for h in range(hours + 1):
        trace.append(round(level, 3))
        level *= 0.5 ** (1 / HALF_LIFE_H)   # exponential decay
    return launched, trace

for drive in (0.4, 1.2, 3.0):
    launched, trace = ieg_pulse(drive)
    tag = "durable write LAUNCHED" if launched else "sub-threshold, no write"
    print(f"drive {drive:3.1f}: {tag}; pulse over hours = {trace}")

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A pre-armed set of genes that answer the nucleus's question in minutes with no start-up cost, seed the durable change, and leave behind the only timestamp precise enough to let a scientist point at a memory's cells months later.


CHAPTER 15. THE HISTONE CODE (The Readability Settings) (Spool-Tension Store) (COG)

The two-metre thread of DNA in every nucleus is not loose; it is wound onto millions of protein spools, and how tightly each spool is wound decides whether the gene beneath it can be read at all.

Learning reaches in and adjusts the tension of particular spools, loosening some so their genes become easy to read, and those adjustments - chemical tags on the spool proteins - can hold for hours to days, gating whether a memory consolidates.

This is the histone code: a layer of reversible settings that sits on top of the genes without changing a single letter of them, controlling access rather than content.

Spool names the histone protein around which DNA is wound. Tension names the acetylation and methylation marks that set how readable each spool's gene is.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The histone code is the set of reversible chemical marks - most importantly acetylation and methylation - placed on histones, the spool proteins around which DNA is wound to form chromatin.

What it stores is gene readability: an acetylation mark loosens the wrap and makes a gene easier to transcribe, while certain methylation marks tighten it and silence the gene.

By setting which plasticity genes are readable, the histone code stores a cell's recent instruction about which parts of the genome should be available for the next wave of consolidation.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

DNA is wound around histone octamers, and enzymes add or remove small chemical groups on the histone tails that protrude from each spool.

Histone acetyltransferases such as CREB-binding protein (CBP) and its relative p300 add acetyl groups, which neutralise the positive charge of the histone tail, weaken its grip on the negatively charged DNA, and open the chromatin for transcription; histone deacetylases (HDACs) remove them and re-tighten it.

Because CBP is recruited by activated CREB, the same synaptic signal that opens the CREB gate also drives histone acetylation at plasticity genes, coupling the events of Chapters 13 and 14 to a durable readability setting.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

An individual acetyl mark is not intrinsically permanent, so the histone code's persistence comes from a maintained balance and, at longer range, from reinforcing loops with DNA methylation.

The centrepiece trick is dynamic equilibrium held off-centre: writers and erasers both act continuously, and a plasticity event shifts the balance so that acetylation at a given gene sits higher, and stays higher, as long as the writers keep winning locally - a self-sustaining setting rather than a frozen one.

An engineer could copy this as a maintained set-point: rather than writing a value once and hoping it survives, run the writer and eraser continuously and store the memory in where their balance settles, so molecular turnover of any single mark does not erase the state.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The histone code operates in the nuclei of all neurons but is best characterised for memory in hippocampal CA1 and dentate cells and in neocortical pyramidal cells of the Layer 6 catalogue.

There is no junction-level host, because chromatin is nuclear, but the marks are driven by activity at the Layer 7 excitatory synapses whose signals reach the nucleus through the relay of Chapter 13.

TIMESCALES:

Acetylation changes appear within roughly 30 minutes to a few hours of learning and can persist for hours to a few days at consolidating genes.

This places the histone code between the minutes of the immediate-early gene response and the potentially lifelong DNA methylation store, as the middle tier of the genomic write.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

The write is driven by learning-evoked CREB activation recruiting CBP and p300 to acetylate histones at plasticity-gene promoters.

Experimentally, the write is amplified by HDAC inhibitors - drugs that block the erasers, so acetylation accumulates - which enhance memory and long-term potentiation in animal models, an effect shown to require CREB and CBP.

THE READ OPERATION:

The stored readability setting is read by the transcription machinery: an acetylated, open region is bound more readily by transcription factors and polymerase, so the gene beneath it is expressed more.

Reader proteins with bromodomains specifically recognise acetyl marks and recruit further machinery, so the mark is read both by making DNA physically accessible and by being a docking signal.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

The physiological erase is straightforward: HDACs remove acetyl groups and re-tighten the chromatin, and demethylases remove methyl marks, returning genes to a default readability.

This makes the histone code the most readily reversible of the genomic stores, which is exactly why HDAC inhibition - preventing erasure - so reliably boosts memory in the laboratory.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

Capacity is large in principle, because each of many histone sites can carry several possible marks, giving a combinatorial "code", though how much of that combinatorial space carries independent information in a real neuron is unknown.

Fidelity is moderate: marks are noisy and continuously turned over, so the histone code is better suited to a maintained, self-correcting setting than to a precise, long-frozen record - the durable-record role falls to DNA methylation.

Honest unknown: the true information capacity of the histone code in a memory-encoding neuron has not been measured, and claims of a rich "code" remain partly interpretive.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The histone code is driven by the CREB gate and the immediate-early gene response upstream, and it reinforces and is reinforced by the DNA methylation store of Chapter 16, the two epigenetic layers stabilising each other.

It also gates the expression of the plasticity-product genes of Part Two, so its readability settings decide how much capture cargo an event can manufacture.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Histone acetylation is central to developmental gene programmes, and CBP dosage matters so much that losing one working copy causes a developmental syndrome (below).

Across the lifespan, a shift toward greater HDAC activity and reduced acetylation is associated with age-related memory decline, part of the rationale for testing HDAC inhibitors as cognitive enhancers.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Histones and their modifying enzymes are ancient and universal to eukaryotes, where chromatin marking has always regulated gene access; neurons inherited the whole toolkit and pointed it at plasticity genes.

The coupling of CBP to a synaptic transcription factor is the neural specialisation on top of a deeply conserved system.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

The signature disease is Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome, a neurodevelopmental disorder caused by mutations in CREBBP or EP300 - the genes for CBP and p300 - producing intellectual disability; mouse models with one disabled CBP copy show impaired chromatin acetylation, long-term potentiation, and memory, all partly rescued by HDAC inhibitors.

Altered histone acetylation is also implicated in Alzheimer disease, depression, and addiction, where it helps entrench maladaptive gene-expression states.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Activated CREB and recruited CBP/p300 acetyltransferase activity.

◦ HDAC eraser activity level.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Per-gene chromatin readability, raising or lowering transcription of plasticity genes.

• STATE.

◦ Acetylation and methylation level at each regulated histone site.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Writer-to-eraser balance; drug-tunable via HDAC inhibitors.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Mark deposition minutes to hours; persistence hours to a few days; turnover continuous.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome, loss of one CBP or p300 copy impairs acetylation, long-term potentiation, and memory.

◦ In age-related decline, excess HDAC activity lowers acetylation and weakens consolidation.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

In engineering terms this mechanism is the readability_gate: it subscribes to consolidation-request signals and publishes a per-gene access-permission that raises or lowers how strongly the durable-write path can express a change.

The nearest engineering analogue is a file-permission or access-control layer that turns read access on or off without altering the file's contents.

The biological difference is that the permission bit is itself a chemical equilibrium under continuous writer/eraser action, so it is inherently reversible and drug-tunable, and it drifts unless maintained - unlike a permission flag stored once in a table.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to set a gene's readability from the balance of acetylation writers and erasers, and to decide whether consolidation of that gene is permitted.

Create a number variable called writer activity with initial value set from CBP recruitment.

Create a number variable called eraser activity with initial value set from HDAC level.

Create a number variable called acetylation with initial value 0.5.

Create a number variable called readability threshold with initial value 0.6.

Set acetylation to writer activity divided by the sum of writer activity and eraser activity.

Create a text variable called gate with initial value "consolidation blocked".

Check if acetylation is greater than or equal to readability threshold.

Display acetylation and gate to the console.

The procedure is complete.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the state that matters is a writer-versus-eraser balance point that gates a downstream permission, and Python renders that ratio and threshold in a few transparent lines.

# Histone code as readability settings gating consolidation.
# Acetylation sits where writers (CBP/p300) and erasers (HDACs) balance;
# HDAC inhibitors lower eraser activity and open the gate.
# (Vecsey et al., J Neurosci 2007; Alarcon et al., Neuron 2004.)
READABILITY_THRESHOLD = 0.6

def readability(writer, eraser):
    return writer / (writer + eraser)   # fraction acetylated at balance

scenarios = [
    ("baseline",           1.0, 1.0),
    ("learning (more CBP)", 2.0, 1.0),
    ("HDAC inhibitor",      1.0, 0.3),
    ("Rubinstein-Taybi",    0.4, 1.0),   # one CBP copy lost
]
for name, writer, eraser in scenarios:
    a = readability(writer, eraser)
    gate = "PERMITTED" if a >= READABILITY_THRESHOLD else "blocked"
    print(f"{name:22s} acetylation={a:4.2f}  consolidation {gate}")

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A layer of chemical tags that changes not one letter of a gene but only how tightly its spool is wound, held at a maintained balance of writers and erasers, and so reversible that blocking the erasers alone measurably sharpens memory.


CHAPTER 16. THE DNA METHYLATION STORE (The Durable Covalent Store) (Lifelong Ink) (COG)

Every store so far can be undone: a switch can be reset, a spool re-tightened, a tag stripped in an hour.

The DNA methylation store is different in kind, because the mark is written directly onto the DNA letters themselves, a small methyl group locked on by a covalent chemical bond, and once set it can be copied faithfully forward for the rest of a cell's life.

This is the framework's candidate for truly lifelong cellular configuration: the mechanism by which an early experience, or a single episode of learning, can leave a durable instruction that shapes a gene's activity for decades.

Lifelong names the persistence, potentially the whole life of the cell. Ink names the covalent chemistry: written on, not merely resting against, the DNA.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The DNA methylation store is the set of methyl groups attached directly to cytosine bases in DNA, most often at cytosine-guanine dinucleotide sites called CpG sites, which durably lower the activity of the genes they cover.

What it stores is a long-term configuration setting: whether a given gene is kept quiet or available, written into the DNA itself and therefore, in principle, the most durable memory mark in the cell.

It is the framework's steroid-channel terminus - the mechanism through which hormonal and early-life experience programme lifelong differences in stress-gene activity - and the leading candidate for lifelong cellular configuration.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

Enzymes called DNA methyltransferases add a methyl group to cytosine: DNMT3A and DNMT3B write new (de novo) marks, and DNMT1 copies existing marks onto the new strand each time DNA is replicated, maintaining them.

A methylated promoter recruits methyl-binding proteins and represses the gene, so methylation typically silences; removal happens through an active, multi-step demethylation pathway rather than a single eraser.

In neurons, DNMT3A and DNMT3B are upregulated in the hippocampus after fear learning, and blocking DNMT activity prevents both memory formation and long-term potentiation, showing the store is written by experience and required for it.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The methyl mark is held by two reinforcing tricks: a covalent bond that does not spontaneously fall off, and a copying enzyme that re-writes the mark after every DNA replication.

The centrepiece is template-guided self-renewal: after replication one strand still carries the mark, DNMT1 reads the half-methylated site and restores the mark on the fresh strand, so the pattern re-templates itself indefinitely - the same self-templating logic as a prion, but written in covalent chemistry on the master copy.

An engineer could copy this as a checksum-restored durable store: keep the record in a form that is chemically stable, and pair it with a maintenance process that detects a half-copied record and completes it, so the value survives both molecular turnover and copying.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

The store operates in neuronal nuclei throughout the brain, with memory roles best shown in hippocampal CA1 and dentate cells and in prefrontal cortical neurons; the maternal-care programming was localised to hippocampal neurons.

As with the other nuclear stores there is no junction host, but the marks are driven by activity and by hormonal signals - notably glucocorticoids, the stress steroids - that reach the nucleus and set gene methylation.

TIMESCALES:

Methylation changes appear within hours of learning and, unlike histone marks, can persist for the lifetime of the cell; the maternal-care mark on the glucocorticoid-receptor gene persists from infancy into adulthood.

This makes DNA methylation the slowest, most durable store in the entire dossier, the endpoint of the fast-to-slow storage tiering.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

The write is driven by experience-evoked and hormone-evoked recruitment of DNMTs to specific genes, as in fear conditioning, and by early-life experience during sensitive periods.

The classic write is the Meaney laboratory's finding that high levels of maternal licking, grooming, and arched-back nursing in rats demethylate the glucocorticoid-receptor gene promoter in the pups' hippocampus, raising receptor expression and lowering lifelong stress reactivity - an environmental write that lasts a lifetime and can be experimentally reversed.

THE READ OPERATION:

The stored mark is read continuously by the transcription machinery and by methyl-binding proteins: a methylated gene is bound by repressors and read out as low expression, so the memory is expressed as a persistent bias in how much of that gene's product the cell makes.

In the maternal-care case, the read-out is a lifelong difference in glucocorticoid-receptor levels and therefore in the animal's stress response.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

There is no simple eraser; removal requires active demethylation through an enzymatic pathway that oxidises and excises the mark, which is why the store is so durable.

Experimentally, the maternal-care mark can be shifted pharmacologically - methionine treatment raises methylation and reverses the licking-and-grooming effect, while an HDAC inhibitor shifts it the other way - showing the mark is durable but not absolutely fixed.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

Capacity is potentially large, since millions of CpG sites can each be methylated or not, though only a small, gene-specific subset carries experience-driven memory information.

Fidelity is the highest of the stores: covalent chemistry plus template-guided maintenance gives very low spontaneous loss, and DNMT1 copying keeps error rates low across cell divisions, though neurons rarely divide so copying is less relevant post-development.

Honest unknown: how much of adult memory actually resides in neuronal DNA methylation, as opposed to being a developmental and homeostatic setting, remains debated, and single-neuron methylation-memory measurements are still scarce.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The DNA methylation store reinforces and is reinforced by the histone code of Chapter 15, the two epigenetic layers locking each other in, and it is downstream of the CREB gate and immediate-early gene response that first mark a gene for durable change.

It is the terminus toward which the whole genomic write tends: fast immediate-early pulse, then histone readability, then, for the events that matter most, durable methylation.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Methylation is laid down massively during development to set cell identity, and the sensitive-period maternal-care programming shows that early life is a privileged writing window whose marks persist for life.

Across the lifespan, genome-wide methylation drifts with age - the basis of "epigenetic clocks" - and this drift is associated with cognitive change.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Cytosine methylation is an ancient genome-regulation and genome-defence system, used across life to silence transposable elements and set gene programmes; the vertebrate brain co-opted this deeply conserved silencing chemistry for experience-dependent gene regulation.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Methylation machinery is central to several disorders: Rett syndrome is caused by mutation of MECP2, a methyl-binding reader protein, and disordered methylation is implicated in Alzheimer disease, in stress and depression via glucocorticoid-receptor programming, and in the lasting effects of early-life adversity.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ DNMT3A and DNMT3B recruitment driven by learning and by glucocorticoid and other hormonal signals.

◦ Active-demethylation pathway activity.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Durable per-gene expression bias, most often silencing.

• STATE.

◦ Methylation status at each regulated CpG site.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Spontaneous loss rate very low; maintenance by DNMT1 at replication.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Write within hours; persistence months to a lifetime; erasure only by active enzymatic demethylation.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In Rett syndrome, mutation of the methyl-reader MECP2 disrupts read-out of the methylation store, causing severe neurodevelopmental regression.

◦ In early-life adversity, aberrant glucocorticoid-receptor methylation locks in lifelong heightened stress reactivity.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

In engineering terms this mechanism is the durable_configuration_store: it subscribes to durable-write requests approved by the readability gate and publishes a long-lived per-gene expression bias that survives turnover and, in principle, the cell's whole life.

The nearest engineering analogue is electrically-erasable programmable read-only memory (EEPROM) or write-once durable storage: written deliberately and rarely, read constantly, hard to erase.

The biological difference is that the store is written directly onto the master copy (the DNA), maintained by a template-copying enzyme rather than refreshed electrically, and erased only by an active multi-step chemical pathway, which is why an infant's experience can still be read from it in adulthood.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to write a durable methylation mark from experience, maintain it across time and copying, and read out the resulting gene expression bias.

Create a number variable called methylation with initial value 0.

Create a number variable called experience strength with initial value set from the learning or care signal.

Create a number variable called write threshold with initial value 0.5.

Check if experience strength is greater than or equal to write threshold.

If that is true, then set methylation to 1 to represent a durable mark written.

For each maintenance cycle, if methylation is 1 then keep methylation at 1 to represent template-guided copying.

Create a text variable called gene output with initial value "gene active".

Check if methylation is equal to 1.

If that is true, then set gene output to "gene silenced for life".

Display gene output to the console.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the essence is a covalent bit that, once set, is re-copied faithfully and resists noisy erasure, and Python lets us run the maintenance loop against random perturbation in a few lines.

# DNA methylation as a durable covalent configuration store.
# A strong experience writes a methyl mark; DNMT1-style maintenance
# re-templates it after replication, so it survives turnover.
# (Meaney & Szyf; Miller & Sweatt, Neuron 2007; Weaver et al., Nat Neurosci 2004.)
import random

WRITE_THRESHOLD = 0.5
SPONTANEOUS_LOSS = 0.02   # small chance a mark is lost per cycle
MAINTENANCE = True        # DNMT1 re-copies half-methylated sites

def durable_store(experience, cycles=50):
    mark = 1 if experience >= WRITE_THRESHOLD else 0
    for _ in range(cycles):
        if mark == 1 and random.random() < SPONTANEOUS_LOSS:
            mark = 0                     # lost this cycle
        if MAINTENANCE and mark == 0 and random.random() < 0.9:
            mark = mark                  # unmarked stays unmarked
        if MAINTENANCE and mark == 1:
            mark = 1                     # re-templated, restored
    return mark

for exp, label in ((0.9, "high maternal care"), (0.2, "low signal")):
    survived = sum(durable_store(exp) for _ in range(1000))
    print(f"{label:20s}: mark present in {survived:4d}/1000 cells after 50 cycles")

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A memory written not beside the genome but onto it, as a covalent methyl group re-copied faithfully after every replication, so durable that a rat pup's mothering can still be read from its stress genes in old age.


CHAPTER 17. THE TRANSPOSON AND SOMATIC MOSAICISM QUESTION (The Open Genomic Frontier) (The Jumping-Gene Question) (NON-COG)

Every chapter until now has described a mechanism that stores information; this one describes a phenomenon that might, and honesty requires it be catalogued as a question rather than an answer.

Neurons, it turns out, are not all genetically identical: mobile genetic elements can copy themselves and paste into new genomic locations during brain development, so that individual neurons carry small, private differences in their DNA - somatic genomic mosaicism.

The tantalising idea is that this jumping might diversify neurons in a way that matters for cognition; the sober reality is that the evidence for how often it happens, and whether it does anything useful, is contested and still being cleaned up.

Jumping-Gene names the transposable element that copies and reinserts itself. Question marks the honest status: a real biological phenomenon whose cognitive role is unproven.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The subject is somatic retrotransposition in neurons, chiefly of an element called long interspersed nuclear element one (LINE-1, or L1), a mobile stretch of DNA that can copy itself through an RNA intermediate and paste the copy into a new site in the genome of a single cell.

Because these insertions happen in individual cells during development, different neurons end up with slightly different genomes, a condition called somatic mosaicism.

What, if anything, this "stores" is precisely the open question: it creates durable, cell-specific genomic differences, but whether those differences encode or support any information the brain uses is unknown.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

A LINE-1 element is transcribed into RNA, which encodes proteins that reverse-transcribe it back into DNA and nick the genome to insert the new copy, a process called target-primed reverse transcription.

In the brain, LINE-1 activity is reported to be elevated during neurogenesis, when neural progenitor cells are dividing and differentiating, which is proposed as the window in which most somatic insertions occur.

Other sources of somatic mosaicism - copy-number changes and single-nucleotide mutations - also occur, so retrotransposition is one contributor among several to neuronal genomic diversity.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

If it happens, the persistence is trivially total: an insertion is a permanent change to that cell's DNA sequence, inherited by any daughter cells and never reversed, which is the most durable change imaginable.

But this durability is the durability of a scar, not of a designed store, so the mechanism has no write-read-erase discipline; its permanence is a by-product of editing the master copy, not a feature engineered for memory.

The honest engineering framing is that there is no persistence trick to copy, because there is no evidence this is a controlled store rather than an incidental, irreversible mutation.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

Reported hosts are neurons and neural progenitor cells, with the strongest claims for hippocampal neurons and cerebral cortical neurons of the Layer 6 catalogue.

There is no junction-level host; this is a nuclear, genomic phenomenon of the cell body, unrelated to any Layer 7 synapse type.

TIMESCALES:

Most insertions are thought to occur during development, in progenitors and young neurons, and then persist unchanged for the life of the cell.

So the write window is developmental and brief, and the "store", such as it is, is lifelong by default.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

The write is a retrotransposition event during neurogenesis, apparently promoted by the chromatin and transcription-factor environment of developing neurons.

There is no evidence of an experience-driven, memory-specific write; unlike methylation, no one has shown that learning directs an insertion to a useful place, which is central to why the entry is tagged non-cognitive.

THE READ OPERATION:

There is no established read: no machinery is known to interpret a neuron's private insertion pattern as information, and any effect is limited to whatever the insertion happens to do to nearby genes in that one cell.

This absence of a read operation is the strongest single argument that the phenomenon, real as it is, is not a memory store.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

There is no erase; a genomic insertion is permanent, which again marks it as mutation rather than a managed store.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

This is where the controversy is sharpest, and the numbers must be given with their dispute attached.

One influential single-cell study using retrotransposon-capture sequencing estimated about 13.7 somatic L1 insertions per hippocampal neuron, implying substantial mosaicism.

However, later work argued that much of that count was artefact: aspects of the single-cell whole-genome amplification, the bioinformatics, and the validation were shown to inflate the apparent rate by more than fiftyfold, and reanalysis with stricter filtering yielded a consensus that retrotransposition does occur in the developing brain but at a far lower rate, on the order of much less than one confirmed insertion per neuron.

So the fidelity of the phenomenon as a store is unknowable because its very frequency is unsettled, and the honest grade is "real but rare, and quantitatively contested".

EVIDENCE GRADE:

Graded honestly: that somatic L1 retrotransposition occurs in the human brain is well supported and no longer seriously doubted (high confidence).

That it occurs at the high, cognitively interesting rates first reported is not supported, and the high estimates are substantially artefactual (high confidence that the early rates were inflated).

That neuronal somatic mosaicism plays any functional role in normal cognition is unproven and speculative (low confidence, no direct evidence of a read-out).

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

The main real interaction is defensive: DNA methylation (Chapter 16) and other silencing systems normally keep transposable elements switched off, so this chapter is partly the story of what the methylation store is guarding against.

When that silencing weakens, transposon activity can rise, which links transposon control to genome-stability roles rather than to cognitive storage.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

The proposed activity is developmental, and some reports suggest transposon de-repression rises again in ageing and in neurodegeneration as silencing machinery falters, though this too is an active research area rather than settled fact.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

LINE-1 and related elements are ancient genomic parasites that make up a large fraction of the mammalian genome; the cell's methylation and small-RNA defences evolved largely to contain them.

Any neuronal role would be a very recent and unproven co-option of a parasite the genome otherwise works hard to suppress.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Dysregulated transposon activity is implicated, with varying strength of evidence, in Rett syndrome, in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and other neurodegenerations, and in ageing-related genome instability - roles that are pathological, not cognitive, consistent with the non-cognitive tag.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Developmental chromatin state and transposon-silencing status (methylation, small RNAs).

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ Permanent, cell-specific genomic insertions of unknown functional consequence.

• STATE.

◦ The set of somatic insertions carried by a given neuron.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Insertion rate per neuron: disputed, from about 13.7 (contested, likely inflated) down to well under 1 confirmed per neuron.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Write during neurogenesis; persistence lifelong; no erase.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ De-repressed transposon activity is associated with genome instability in neurodegeneration and ageing, not with a memory failure.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

In engineering terms this is not a store but a noted hazard: a genome_mutation_event that the durable_configuration_store's silencing is partly designed to suppress.

The nearest engineering analogue is a rare, uncorrectable bit-flip in read-only firmware caused by a self-replicating fragment - a fault to be contained, not a feature to be used.

The biological difference from every other entry in this dossier is that there is no evidence of a controlled subscribe/publish contract for cognition here at all, which is exactly why it is tagged non-cognitive and presented as an open question.

CHAPTER 18. THE HOMEOSTATIC SET-POINT MACHINERY (The Multiplicative Renormaliser) (The Fair Governor) (COG)

Learning has a runaway problem: every time a synapse strengthens, the neuron fires a little more, which tends to strengthen it further still, and left unchecked the cell would saturate into a useless roar or fall silent.

The homeostatic set-point machinery is the slow governor that prevents this, watching the neuron's average firing rate over hours to days and, when it drifts, turning every synapse up or down by the same factor to bring the average back to target.

The subtlety - and the reason it deserves a place among the memory machinery rather than opposing it - is that scaling everything by the same factor preserves the relative differences between synapses, so it stabilises the cell without erasing what learning wrote.

Fair names the multiplicative rule that spares relative weights. Governor names the negative-feedback job: hold the average, whatever the traffic.

WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT STORES:

The homeostatic set-point machinery is synaptic scaling: a slow, cell-wide mechanism that adjusts the strength of all of a neuron's excitatory synapses together to keep its average firing rate near a stable target, the firing-rate set point.

What it stores is that set point - a target activity level the cell defends - and, implicitly, the current global gain it is applying to hold there.

It is the cellular partner of the sleep-dependent renormalisation catalogued in the Layer 11 volume: where sleep renormalises across the whole brain over a night, this machinery renormalises one cell over hours to days.

THE MOLECULAR MECHANISM:

The cell senses its own average activity through calcium-dependent signals that report firing over long windows, and translates a persistent deviation into a change in the number of AMPA-type glutamate receptors at every synapse.

When activity is chronically too low, the cell scales receptors up; when chronically too high, it scales them down - and the up-scaling in particular is mediated in part by a glia-derived signal, the cytokine tumour-necrosis-factor alpha (TNF-alpha), released by glial cells when network activity falls.

The receptor-number change is the same physical operand - AMPA-receptor slots - that the switches of Part One hold and the tag-and-capture machinery of Part Two fills, so scaling and learning write to the same ledger.

THE PERSISTENCE TRICK:

The persistence here is the persistence of a defended set point rather than of a fixed value, and the centrepiece trick is negative feedback around a target: the machinery does not remember a particular weight, it remembers a target rate and continuously corrects toward it.

Its cleverest feature is that the correction is multiplicative - every weight is multiplied by the same factor - so the relative pattern that learning wrote is preserved exactly while the overall level is renormalised, the way rescaling a photograph's brightness keeps every relative contrast intact.

An engineer could copy this as gain normalisation that preserves ratios: to keep a system in range without destroying its learned pattern, multiply the whole weight vector by one scalar chosen to hit a target, never add or clip individual weights, because multiplication alone leaves the relative code untouched.

WHERE IT OPERATES (THE HOSTS ABOVE):

Synaptic scaling operates in principal excitatory neurons throughout the cortex and hippocampus - the L2/3 IT and layer five pyramidal cells and hippocampal pyramidal cells of the Layer 6 catalogue - and comparable homeostatic rules operate in other cell classes.

The hosting junctions are the Layer 7 glutamatergic excitatory synapses whose AMPA-receptor number is the scaled quantity, with glial cells supplying the TNF-alpha signal for up-scaling.

TIMESCALES:

Scaling is deliberately slow, operating over many hours to days, far slower than the seconds-to-minutes of the learning it stabilises, so that it corrects long-term drift without cancelling moment-to-moment plasticity.

The up-scaling response to prolonged activity blockade becomes TNF-alpha-dependent for its maintenance beyond roughly a day, marking the crossover from fast induction to slow maintenance.

THE WRITE OPERATION:

The set point is written developmentally and refined by experience; the scaling response itself is "written" whenever average activity deviates persistently from target, driving a global receptor-number change.

Experimentally, the write is induced by chronically silencing a network (which triggers up-scaling) or chronically over-driving it (which triggers down-scaling), the classic manipulations from which synaptic scaling was discovered.

THE READ OPERATION:

The stored global gain is read out continuously as the multiplier applied to every synapse's strength, setting how loudly the whole cell responds to a given input pattern.

The set point is read whenever the cell compares its recent average firing against target and computes the correction, an ongoing background read rather than a discrete one.

THE ERASE OPERATION:

There is no erase in the memory sense; the machinery instead continuously re-corrects, so any particular scaling state is transient and overwritten as conditions change.

If the sensing or effector machinery is disabled - for instance by blocking TNF-alpha signalling or the activity sensor - scaling fails to engage, which is the experimental route to removing the correction.

CAPACITY AND FIDELITY:

As a store the machinery holds essentially one slow analogue value per cell - the global gain defending the set point - so its capacity is low, by design, because its job is stabilisation, not encoding.

Its fidelity to the multiplicative ideal is good at the population level but imperfect at single synapses: recent work shows scaling is multiplicative across the population yet variable synapse by synapse, so relative weights are preserved on average but not perfectly at every contact.

Honest unknown: the exact biological set point and how tightly it is defended in human neurons in vivo are not precisely measured, and much of the quantitative picture comes from rodent culture and slice work.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE OTHER SLOW VARIABLES:

Synaptic scaling acts on the very AMPA-receptor slots that the bistable switches of Part One hold and the tag-and-capture machinery of Part Two fills, so it renormalises the operand that all the fast learning writes - and because it is multiplicative, it does so without erasing their relative pattern.

It is the cellular counterpart of the brain-wide, sleep-dependent synaptic renormalisation catalogued in the Layer 11 volume, and the two are thought to cooperate, with sleep providing a privileged window for down-scaling.

DEVELOPMENT AND LIFESPAN:

Homeostatic scaling is critical during development for setting and stabilising firing-rate set points as circuits wire up, and for holding networks in a workable range through the huge activity changes of early life.

Across the lifespan it continues to stabilise networks, and its weakening or miscalibration is proposed to contribute to the network instability of ageing and disease.

EVOLUTIONARY PROVENANCE:

Negative-feedback homeostasis is one of the oldest and most universal control strategies in biology, from bacterial gene circuits upward; neurons applied this deeply conserved principle to firing rate, adding the multiplicative rule that specifically protects a learned pattern.

The recruitment of a glial immune cytokine (TNF-alpha) as the up-scaling signal is a striking neural-glial specialisation on top of the general feedback logic.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS:

Failed or maladaptive homeostatic scaling is implicated in epilepsy, where loss of the brake permits runaway excitation, and dysregulated set points are proposed in autism-spectrum disorders, in Alzheimer disease, and in the network effects of chronic inflammation, given the TNF-alpha link.

Because TNF-alpha is an inflammatory cytokine, systemic inflammation can perturb scaling, one proposed route from illness to cognitive fog.

SYSTEMS INTERFACE:

• SUBSCRIBES (INPUTS).

◦ Long-window average firing rate, sensed via calcium-dependent signalling over hours.

◦ Glia-derived TNF-alpha for up-scaling maintenance.

• PUBLISHES (OUTPUTS).

◦ A single global multiplicative gain applied to all excitatory synapses, via AMPA-receptor number.

• STATE.

◦ The firing-rate set point and the current global gain.

• PARAMETERS.

◦ Set-point target rate (cell-specific, on the order of a few hertz); scaling is multiplicative by one shared factor.

• TIME CONSTANTS.

◦ Sensing and correction over many hours to days; TNF-alpha-dependent maintenance beyond about 24 hours.

• FAILURE MODE.

◦ In epilepsy, loss of homeostatic braking permits runaway recurrent excitation and seizures.

◦ Under chronic inflammation, excess TNF-alpha can misdrive scaling and destabilise networks.

THE ENGINEERING NOTE:

In engineering terms this mechanism is the renormalisation_daemon: it subscribes to a cell's long-window average activity and publishes one scalar gain that multiplies the whole weight vector to hold the average at target.

The nearest engineering analogue is automatic gain control or weight normalisation in a neural network - divide the weight vector by its norm to keep it in range - which keeps a unit's output bounded without changing the direction of its weight vector.

The biological difference is that the daemon runs on a deliberately slow clock (hours to days) so it never competes with fast learning, and it recruits a separate cell type (glia, via TNF-alpha) as part of the feedback loop, so the governor is partly outsourced to the cell's support system.

ENGLISH READABLE CODE:

The purpose of this procedure is to renormalise all of a neuron's synaptic weights by one shared factor so that its average firing returns to target, while preserving the relative pattern that learning wrote.

Create a list variable called weights holding the current synaptic strengths.

Create a number variable called target rate with initial value 5.0.

Create a number variable called measured rate with initial value set from the recent average firing.

Create a number variable called scale factor with initial value 1.

Set scale factor to target rate divided by measured rate.

For each weight in weights, multiply that weight by scale factor and store the result back.

Note that because every weight is multiplied by the same scale factor, the ratio between any two weights is unchanged.

Display the rescaled weights and the scale factor to the console.

The procedure is complete.

CODE SAMPLE:

Python is chosen because the whole point is that one shared multiplier changes the sum while leaving every pairwise ratio identical, and Python lets us print the weights, their ratios, and the restored rate side by side to prove it.

# Homeostatic synaptic scaling: multiplicative renormalisation.
# One shared factor pulls average firing back to target while leaving
# relative weights (the learned pattern) untouched. The cellular partner
# of sleep-dependent renormalisation in the Layer 11 volume.
# (Turrigiano et al., Nature 1998; Stellwagen & Malenka, Nature 2006.)
TARGET_RATE = 5.0   # hertz, the defended set point

def rate_from(weights):
    return sum(weights)            # toy: firing proportional to total drive

def rescale(weights):
    factor = TARGET_RATE / rate_from(weights)
    return [w * factor for w in weights], factor

learned = [0.2, 0.6, 1.0, 1.8]    # a learned pattern; total = 3.6 -> too quiet
before_ratios = [round(w / learned[0], 2) for w in learned]
scaled, factor = rescale(learned)
after_ratios = [round(w / scaled[0], 2) for w in scaled]

print(f"before: rate={rate_from(learned):.1f} Hz  ratios={before_ratios}")
print(f"factor applied to every synapse: {factor:.3f}")
print(f"after:  rate={rate_from(scaled):.1f} Hz  ratios={after_ratios}")
print("relative pattern preserved:", before_ratios == after_ratios)

WHAT MAKES IT UNMISTAKABLE:

A slow governor that watches a cell's average firing for hours and, when it drifts, multiplies every synapse by one shared number to restore the target - the only memory-adjacent mechanism whose defining feature is that it changes the whole while leaving every relative weight, and so the learned pattern, exactly intact.
