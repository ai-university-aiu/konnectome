THE GAP THIS FILLS

The guiding manuscript, LAYER_09_NATURES_COGNITIVE_ARCHITECTURE_MANUSCRIPT, carries zero time constants across its 139 numbered regional headings.

The layers on either side of it do carry them. Layer 7 (LAYER_07_SYNAPTICS_MANUSCRIPT, 64 junctions), Layer 8 (LAYER_08_MICROCIRCUITS_MANUSCRIPT, 71 motifs), Layer 10 (LAYER_10_SYSTEMS_MANUSCRIPT, 44 systems, plus its addendum) and Layer 11 (LAYER_11_GLOBAL_STATES_MANUSCRIPT, 26 states) each state a characteristic timescale on every entry.

A region sits between the junctions and motifs it is built from and the systems and states it participates in. Its operating timescale is therefore often derivable from the layers on either side of it, without inventing anything.


METHOD: THE FOUR MARKINGS

Every timescale bullet in this companion ends with one of four markings, and the marking is the honesty of the bullet.

• CITED means a corpus layer states the value, and the layer and the entry inside it are named. This is the strongest marking, because the value is already in the corpus.

• DERIVED means the value is computed from corpus values of the layers on either side of the region, with the working shown in the entry. The corpus supplies the inputs; the arithmetic or the bracketing is the build's.

• LITERATURE means the value is established by a web search outside the corpus, with the source named. The corpus outranks any LITERATURE value, and where the literature and the corpus disagree the corpus wins.

• REFUSED means no honest value can be stated, and the bullet says why. A slot filled with a plausible number is this project's named worst failure, and refusing is the correct move when the evidence is not there.

A note on the count, stated on this document's face: a companion that managed to state a timescale for all 139 headings would be a companion that invented some. Some headings are grouping headings, some name structures whose characteristic operating timescale is genuinely unsettled, and some name hubs that have no single timescale of their own. The refusals in this companion are deliberate. They are not gaps to be filled later with guesses.

A second note: where the strongest available value is from rodent recordings, the bullet says so, because rodent timings are not silently the human's.

2.1 THE CEREBRAL CORTEX


ENTRY: 2.1 THE CEREBRAL CORTEX

TIME CONSTANTS:
• This is a grouping heading for the whole cortical mantle, whose shared building block is the canonical cortical column: loop traversal about 10 milliseconds, AMPA 2 milliseconds, NMDA 100 milliseconds, GABA-A 5 to 10 milliseconds, adaptation 100 to 300 milliseconds, critical periods months to years (CITED, Layer 8, Chapter 37 The Canonical Cortical Column).
• The cortex has no single operating timescale of its own beyond this shared tile; the per-area constants are stated in the entries below rather than invented for the mantle as a whole (REFUSED for a single cortex-wide value: a grouping heading has no one timescale).


ENTRY: 2.1.1 PRIMARY VISUAL CORTEX (V1, STRIATE CORTEX, BRODMANN AREA 17)

TIME CONSTANTS:
• First spikes arrive in V1 about 40 to 60 milliseconds after the stimulus, and the feedforward sweep to which V1 contributes runs 100 to 150 milliseconds (CITED, Layer 10, Chapter 1 The Visual System, whose time constants give the feedforward sweep as 100 to 150 milliseconds).
• The canonical cortical column that V1 is built from traverses its loop in about 10 milliseconds, with AMPA at 2 milliseconds, NMDA at 100 milliseconds, GABA-A at 5 to 10 milliseconds, and adaptation over 100 to 300 milliseconds (CITED, Layer 8, Chapter 37 The Canonical Cortical Column).
• Ocular-dominance and orientation plasticity is confined to a critical period of months to years (CITED, Layer 8, Chapter 37 The Canonical Cortical Column, critical periods months to years).


ENTRY: 2.1.2 SECONDARY AND HIGHER VISUAL AREAS (V2, V3, V4, V5/MT)

TIME CONSTANTS:

• Contrast and surround gain settles over the divisive-normalization window of shunting 5 to 20 milliseconds, synaptic depression 50 to 300 milliseconds, and contrast adaptation seconds to tens of seconds (CITED, Layer 8, Chapter 18 Divisive Normalization).

ENTRY: 2.11.1 THE CORTICO-BASAL-GANGLIA-THALAMO-CORTICAL LOOPS (CBGTC)

TIME CONSTANTS:
• Hyperdirect brake about 10 milliseconds; full selection tens to hundreds of milliseconds; dopamine teaching bursts under a second; habit formation over many repetitions across days to months (CITED, Layer 10, The Cortico-Basal-Ganglia-Thalamo-Cortical Loop System).

ENTRY: 2.11.2 THE DIRECT, INDIRECT, AND HYPERDIRECT PATHWAYS

TIME CONSTANTS:
• Hyperdirect latency 5 to 8 milliseconds; direct-path release 15 to 20 milliseconds; indirect sculpting 25 to 30 milliseconds; dopamine burst 100 to 200 milliseconds; eligibility trace about 1 second; habit consolidation weeks (CITED, Layer 8, The Basal Ganglia Selection Motif).

ENTRY: 2.11.3 THE PAPEZ CIRCUIT

TIME CONSTANTS:
• As the hippocampal-diencephalic memory loop it encodes in under 1 second, replays in ripples of 80 to 250 milliseconds, and consolidates over months to years (CITED, Layer 10, The Episodic Memory System).
• Its head-direction limb resets to a landmark within a few hundred milliseconds and drifts a few degrees per minute in darkness (CITED, Layer 8, The Ring Attractor).

ENTRY: 2.11.4 THE TRIPARTITE SYNAPSE

TIME CONSTANTS:
• Local astrocytic calcium over hundreds of milliseconds; a global calcium surge over seconds; gliotransmitter effect seconds to tens of seconds; the plasticity consequence minutes to hours (CITED, Layer 8, The Tripartite Synaptic Island).

ENTRY: 2.11.5 THE GLIA AS COGNITIVE PARTICIPANTS (ASTROCYTES, MICROGLIA, OLIGODENDROCYTES)

TIME CONSTANTS:
• Astrocytes: potassium uptake tens of milliseconds, redistribution hundreds of milliseconds to seconds, calcium waves travelling 10 to 20 micrometres per second (CITED, Layer 8, The Astrocyte Syncytium).
• Microglia: contact with a single synapse minutes, full parenchymal survey hours, complement-tagged pruning episodes days to weeks, and human cortical pruning across years (CITED, Layer 8, The Microglial Pruning Loop).
• Oligodendrocytes: the corticospinal myelination they build matures over the first two years and beyond, and activity-driven myelin change tracks days to weeks of practice (CITED, Layer 10, The Corticospinal System, myelination maturation over the first two years and beyond; map reorganisation over days to weeks).
