LAYER 10, THE NEURAL AND NEUROENDOCRINE SYSTEMS LAYER: THE TYPES

A Complete, Comprehensive, and Thorough List and Outline of the Systems of the Human Nervous and Endocrine System

Companion outline to the Layer 6 through Layer 9 catalogues in the Certified_Neuroscientist corpus of D. R. Dison, Founder, Artificial Intelligence University (AIU).

Date of compilation: August 9, 2026.


HOW TO READ THIS OUTLINE

Layer 10 is the layer of the distributed system: a chain of Layer 9 regions plus the pathways connecting them, considered as a functional whole.

The framework's dictum: the visual system is not a place - it is an ordered sequence of places.

This outline catalogues forty-four systems in seven parts: the sensory systems, the motor and action systems, the memory and cognitive systems, the large-scale networks, the neuromodulatory broadcast systems, the hormonal axes, and the cross-system dynamics.

A housekeeping note the corpus owes itself: the Layer 9 manuscript's sections 2.10 (the ten large-scale networks) and 2.11 (the loops and pathways) were explicitly labelled Layer 10 constructs lodging in a Layer 9 volume; this outline gives them their proper layer address (Parts Four and Seven), cross-referencing rather than duplicating their treatments.

Every entry carries the corpus's two tags: (COG) for systems participating in cognition, (NON-COG) for systems serving substrate, regulatory, or effector roles.


PART ONE. THE SENSORY SYSTEMS

Ordered sequences of places from transducer to interpretation.

1. The Visual System, Dorsal and Ventral Streams (COG).

Retina to lateral geniculate to primary visual cortex, then the where-and-how dorsal stream to parietal cortex and the what ventral stream to inferotemporal cortex - the most completely mapped sequence in neuroscience, and the corpus's model of hierarchical interpretation.

2. The Auditory System (COG).

Cochlea through the brainstem timing stations to medial geniculate to auditory cortex and the language interfaces - the fastest sensory chain, precise to microseconds at its base and semantic at its top.

3. The Somatosensory System (COG).

The dorsal-column and spinothalamic chains to thalamus and somatosensory cortex - touch, proprioception, temperature, and pain in parallel labelled lines, with pain's affective branch given its own address.

4. The Olfactory System (NON-COG).

Epithelium to bulb to piriform and onward to orbitofrontal and amygdala - the framework's thalamus-skipping exception, tagged for its early-relay role with its limbic connections noted.

5. The Gustatory System (NON-COG).

Taste bud to solitary nucleus to ventroposteromedial thalamus to insular gustatory cortex - the front end of the chemical senses' safety machinery.

6. The Vestibular System (NON-COG).

Labyrinth to vestibular nuclei to oculomotor, spinal, thalamic, and cerebellar targets - the balance and gaze-stabilisation utility, running mostly beneath awareness.

7. The Interoceptive System (COG).

Visceral afferents through vagal and spinal lines to the solitary nucleus, parabrachial nucleus, and insula - the body-state sense that the corpus's Layer 9 volume ties to feeling and self; tagged (COG) for its role in emotion and decision.


PART TWO. THE MOTOR AND ACTION SYSTEMS

8. The Corticospinal (Pyramidal) System (NON-COG).

Motor cortex to spinal motoneurons, the direct command line for skilled voluntary movement - execution machinery par excellence.

9. The Cortico-Basal-Ganglia-Thalamo-Cortical Loop System (COG).

The parallel selection loops - motor, oculomotor, associative, limbic - already treated at Layer 9 section 2.11.1 and re-addressed here as a system: the machine of choosing, tagged (COG) for its associative and limbic loops.

10. The Cerebellar System (NON-COG).

The mossy-and-climbing-fibre calibration engine wrapped around every motor and several cognitive streams - the corpus's supervised learner, tagged for its principal calibration role with its cognitive loops noted.

11. The Oculomotor System (NON-COG).

The saccade and pursuit machinery from frontal eye fields and superior colliculus to the brainstem burst generators - the body's fastest motor system.

12. The Autonomic Motor System (NON-COG).

Sympathetic and parasympathetic outflows with their central autonomic network - the visceral effector system, the framework's rest-and-digest versus fight-or-flight machinery.


PART THREE. THE MEMORY AND COGNITIVE SYSTEMS

13. The Episodic Memory System (COG).

Hippocampal formation, medial temporal cortices, fornix, mammillary bodies, anterior thalamus, retrosplenial and prefrontal partners - the Papez legacy system, the machine of remembered experience.

14. The Semantic Memory System (COG).

The anterior temporal hub with its distributed modality spokes - the machine of meaning, dissociable from episode by lesion and by dementia.

15. The Procedural Memory System (COG).

Basal ganglia and cerebellar loops learning skills and habits without episodic access - knowing-how, catalogued apart from knowing-that.

16. The Working Memory System (COG).

Prefrontal-parietal persistent-activity circuitry with its thalamic support - the blackboard of present thought, the attractor motifs of the Layer 8 catalogue installed as a system.

17. The Reward and Motivation System (COG).

Ventral tegmental dopamine, accumbens, ventral pallidum, orbitofrontal valuation - the teaching-signal economy that prices every option the selection loops consider.

18. The Language System (COG).

The perisylvian network - Broca's and Wernicke's territories, the arcuate and ventral tracts - already networked at Layer 9 section 2.10.6; re-addressed as the species-defining system.

19. The Executive Control System (COG).

Dorsolateral prefrontal, anterior cingulate, and frontoparietal circuitry implementing goals, rule switching, and inhibition - the government of the cognitive economy.

20. The Social Cognition System (COG).

Temporoparietal junction, superior temporal sulcus, medial prefrontal, amygdala - the theory-of-mind and face-and-intention machinery, the biological seat of the framework's Layer 13 interface.

PART FIVE. THE NEUROMODULATORY BROADCAST SYSTEMS

Each treated end-to-end as one organ: source nuclei, projection map, receptor territories, computational role - the mode-setting machinery every modes volume points to.

31. The Dopamine System (COG).

Nigral and tegmental sources, striatal and cortical territories - the reward-prediction-error teacher and vigour dial.

32. The Noradrenaline System (COG).

Locus coeruleus to nearly everywhere - the gain, arousal, and network-reset broadcast.

33. The Serotonin System (COG).

Raphe to forebrain - the patience, mood-colouring, and plasticity-gating broadcast, the most receptor-diverse system in the brain.

34. The Acetylcholine System (COG).

Basal forebrain and brainstem sources - the attention, encoding, and cortical-state broadcast.

35. The Histamine System (NON-COG).

Tuberomammillary wake broadcast - the arousal substrate.

36. The Orexin System (NON-COG).

Lateral hypothalamic state-stabiliser - the finger on the sleep-wake switch, catalogued as a system here and as machinery in the Layer 11 volume.


PART SIX. THE HORMONAL AXES

The endocrine half of Layer 10: each axis one signalling system, hypothalamus to gland to body and back.

37. The Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) Axis (NON-COG).

The stress axis - corticotropin-releasing hormone to adrenocorticotropic hormone to cortisol, with its feedback thermostat and its ultradian pulse.

38. The Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Gonadal (HPG) Axis (NON-COG).

The reproduction axis - the KNDy pulse generator's downstream empire, running puberty, cycling, and gametogenesis.

39. The Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Thyroid (HPT) Axis (NON-COG).

The metabolic set-point axis - the slowest of the great dials.

40. The Growth Hormone and Somatostatin Axis (NON-COG).

The growth and repair axis, pulsing by night in step with slow-wave sleep.

41. The Melatonin Axis (NON-COG).

Suprachiasmatic clock to pineal to every tissue bearing melatonin receptors - the hormone of darkness as a system.

42. The Glucose Homeostasis Axis (NON-COG).

Islet, liver, muscle, and hypothalamic sensing in one loop - the fuel economy underneath all computation.

43. The Gut-Brain Axis (NON-COG).

Enteric nervous system, vagal lines, gut hormones, and microbiome signals as one bidirectional system - the framework's most recently mapped great axis.


PART SEVEN. THE CROSS-SYSTEM DYNAMICS

44. The Global Workspace Dynamics (The Ignition and Broadcast System) (COG).

The capacity-limited, all-or-none ignition by which one coalition's content is broadcast to the whole cortex - the leading mechanistic account of access consciousness, catalogued as a dynamic system whose parts are the networks of Part Four; with it, the metastable switching among networks (salience-triggered hand-offs, default-to-task transitions) that constitutes the stream of thought.


THE COGNITIVE SCOREBOARD

Of the forty-four systems, twenty-seven carry the (COG) tag: every memory and cognitive system, eight of the ten networks' cognitive core, the four great neuromodulatory teachers, the interoceptive sense, the visual, auditory, and somatosensory interpreters, and the workspace dynamics that crown the layer.

The (NON-COG) seventeen are the executors, stabilisers, and economies - motor outflows, early relays, wake substrates, and every hormonal axis.

The layer's lesson: at Layer 10 cognition becomes COOPERATIVE - no region computes attention, memory, or language alone, and the systems of this outline are the smallest wholes of which those words are true.
