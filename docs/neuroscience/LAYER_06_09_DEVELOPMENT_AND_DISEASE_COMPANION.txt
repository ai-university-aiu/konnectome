THE SCOPE DECISION, STATED EXPLICITLY, WITH THE COUNTS

This is the most important section in this document, and it is placed early so that no reader mistakes what has been done for what remains.

Five hundred and twenty-five entries written well is beyond one document written in one pass.

A companion that stubbed four hundred of them would be worse than the gap it filled, because a stub looks like coverage on a header count while carrying no information, and it would defeat the very audit that produced this file.

So the scope was cut deliberately, and along a line chosen to keep every part of it honest.

• COVERED IN FULL, ENTRY BY ENTRY: all 85 non-neuronal cell types of LAYER_06_OTHER_CELLS_MANUSCRIPT, Chapters 1 through 85.

• COVERED IN FULL, ENTRY BY ENTRY: all 139 numbered regional headings of LAYER_09_NATURES_COGNITIVE_ARCHITECTURE_MANUSCRIPT sections 2.1 through 2.11.

• COVERED BY CLASS, NOT INDIVIDUALLY: the 301 neuron chapters of the five volumes of the neuronal manuscript.

The 301 neuron chapters are grouped into 27 developmental lineage classes, each class receiving one full treatment of both fields, followed by a keyed index that maps every one of the 301 chapters to its class.

That is a complete and honest treatment at a coarser grain.

It is defensible on the biology and not merely on the budget, because developmental origin is genuinely a property of the lineage rather than of the terminal type: the parvalbumin basket cell of the cortex and the parvalbumin basket cell of the hippocampus have the same origin story, the same specifying transcription factors, and very nearly the same disease profile, and writing that story twice would add length without adding knowledge.

Where a neuron chapter departs from its class in a way that matters, the departure is noted against that chapter in the index rather than being silently absorbed.

WHAT A FUTURE PASS STILL OWES, NAMED SO THAT IT CANNOT BE FORGOTTEN.

• Per-chapter DEVELOPMENT AND ASSEMBLY and DISEASES AND DISORDERS blocks for each of the 301 neuron chapters individually, at the grain used here for the 85 non-neuronal types.

The class treatment gives the lineage, the transcription factor cascade, the birth window and the class-level disease profile; what it cannot give is the per-type birth order within a class, the per-type maturation rate, and the per-type selective vulnerability, and those three are real and are missing.

HOW TO READ AN ENTRY

Each entry carries exactly two blocks and nothing else, so that it is paste-ready.

ENTRY: the chapter number and title, or the section number and title, exactly as the parent manuscript names it.

DEVELOPMENT AND ASSEMBLY: the lineage and germ layer or progenitor domain, the transcription factors that specify it where known, when it is born and when it matures with real timings, whether it is generated after birth, whether it can be replaced, and the critical or sensitive period it participates in if any.

DISEASES AND DISORDERS: the disorders in which the entry is primarily implicated, the mechanism of failure where known, whether the entry is a CAUSE, a CASUALTY, or a MARKER, and its selective vulnerability profile.


THE CAUSE, CASUALTY, MARKER DISTINCTION, STATED ONCE

This companion introduces a three-way distinction that the corpus should be able to make and currently has no vocabulary for, and it is defined here once rather than in each of the entries that use it.

• A CAUSE is an entry whose own malfunction initiates the disorder.

The astrocyte in Alexander disease is a cause: the mutation is in the astrocyte's own glial fibrillary acidic protein gene, and everything else in that disease is downstream.

• A CASUALTY is an entry that is destroyed or disabled by a process originating elsewhere, and whose loss then produces the symptoms.

The pancreatic beta cell in type 1 diabetes is a casualty: the mutation is not in the beta cell, the attack is immunological, and the beta cell's contribution is to be uniquely irreplaceable.

• A MARKER is an entry whose change reliably indicates a disorder without either causing it or being the seat of its symptoms.

The olfactory bulb in Parkinson's disease is close to a pure marker: olfactory loss precedes motor onset by years and is among the most reliable early signs, but anosmia is not what makes Parkinson's disease a movement disorder.

The three are not exclusive and the interesting entries are the ones that are two of them at once, and where that is so it is said.

An entry can also be a cause in one disorder and a casualty in another, and the classification below is therefore always made per disorder and never per entry.


A NOTE ON WHAT IS NOT KNOWN

Many of the non-neuronal cell types below have genuinely unsettled lineages, and many of the regions below have no clean disease attribution at all.

Where that is so it is said by name, in the form the lineage of this cell is not settled, or there is no disorder for which this region is the primary seat, followed by a statement of what evidence would settle the question.

No block in this file is filled with a plausible generic sentence in place of a fact.

That rule was adopted because a companion whose five hundred entries all sound the same is one that was generated rather than researched, and it would be worse than the gap it fills, since it would close the audit that found the gap while leaving the corpus no better informed.

The reader should expect the honest entries to be visibly shorter than the well-studied ones, and should read that unevenness as a feature.
