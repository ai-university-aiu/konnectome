HOW TO READ THIS OUTLINE

This document has four levels.

Level 1 is the PART, of which there are fifteen.

Level 2 is the CHAPTER, of which there are sixty-eight, numbered continuously from 1 to 68 across the whole work.

Level 3 is the SECTION, numbered as chapter-point-section, for example 12.3.

Level 4 is the SUB-SECTION, numbered as chapter-point-section-point-subsection, for example 12.3.4.

The outline is organised on a single principle: the mind is not installed but grown, and the growing is lawful.

Development, for the purposes of this outline, means the ordered sequence of changes by which a fertilised cell becomes a behaving, knowing, feeling person, and by which that person continues to change across the whole of a life.

Developmental psychology, for the purposes of this outline, means the study of how perception, cognition, emotion, language, and social capacities emerge and change with age.

The neuroscience of developmental psychology, therefore, names the research programme that seeks mechanistic explanations of those emergences in terms of the developing brain: its construction, its wiring, its plasticity, its timetables, and its failures.

The field calls itself developmental cognitive neuroscience, and the two names are used interchangeably here.

Everything before Part Seven is the machinery of development: what builds the brain, how it is studied, what makes it plastic, and what shapes it from outside. Everything from Part Seven onward is the catalogue of what develops: the faculties, the social and emotional mind, adolescence, the ways development goes differently, the theories that bind it together, and the uses the knowledge is put to.

A NOTE ON TAGS

Each chapter carries one of three tags.

• (CORE) marks chapters that state a developmental capacity, timetable, or mechanism that a reader could not skip without leaving a hole in the account.

• (APPARATUS) marks chapters that supply method, framework, or substrate, and that exist to make the CORE chapters checkable.

• (CONTEXT) marks chapters that situate the science historically, clinically, ethically, or in its applications.


A NOTE ON TIMINGS

This outline names real timings wherever the literature supports them, because a developmental science without a calendar is not doing its job: neurulation at gestational weeks three to four, thalamocortical innervation near weeks twenty-four to twenty-eight, synaptic density peaking in visual cortex at four to twelve months and in prefrontal cortex in the second and third years, perceptual narrowing between six and twelve months, explicit false belief near age four, and myelination running into the third decade.

Two corrections this corpus has already recorded are honoured throughout: the claim that the brain matures at twenty-five is an artefact of cohort age limits, not a finding; and a stated timing is either supported by a source or refused, never invented.
