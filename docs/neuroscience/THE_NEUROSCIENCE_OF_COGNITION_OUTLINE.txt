THE NEUROSCIENCE OF COGNITION

A Complete, Comprehensive, and Thorough Outline

Compiled for the Certified_Neuroscientist corpus of D. R. Dison, Founder, Artificial Intelligence University (AIU).

Date of compilation: August 10, 2026.


HOW TO READ THIS OUTLINE

This document has four levels.

Level 1 is the PART, of which there are fifteen.

Level 2 is the CHAPTER, of which there are eighty, numbered continuously from 1 to 80 across the whole work.

Level 3 is the SECTION, numbered as chapter-point-section, for example 12.3.

Level 4 is the SUB-SECTION, numbered as chapter-point-section-point-subsection, for example 12.3.4.

The outline is organised on a single principle: cognition is what a nervous system does when it builds and uses internal models of a world it cannot touch directly.

Everything before Part V is the apparatus by which that claim is made testable, and everything after Part IV is the catalogue of the capacities themselves.

Cognition, for the purposes of this outline, means the set of processes by which a nervous system acquires, represents, transforms, stores, retrieves, and acts upon information.

Neuroscience, for the purposes of this outline, means the empirical and theoretical study of nervous systems at every scale from the ion channel to the population of behaving organisms.

The compound term, the neuroscience of cognition, therefore names the research programme that seeks mechanistic explanations of mental capacities in terms of neural structure and neural dynamics.

The field is also called cognitive neuroscience, and the two names are used interchangeably here.

The structure was checked against the tables of contents of the field's canonical teaching and reference works, retrieved from publisher sources.

A NOTE ON TAGS

Each chapter carries one of three tags.

• (CORE) marks chapters that state a cognitive capacity or the mechanism of one, and that a reader could not skip without leaving a hole in the account.

• (APPARATUS) marks chapters that supply method, mathematics, or substrate, and that exist to make the CORE chapters checkable.

• (CONTEXT) marks chapters that situate the science historically, clinically, ethically, or comparatively.
