LAYERS 4 AND 5, THE MACROMOLECULAR AND ORGANELLAR LAYERS: THE SLOW VARIABLES

A Targeted List and Outline of the Molecular Memory and Persistence Machinery of the Human Nervous and Endocrine System

Companion outline to the Layer 6 through Layer 11 catalogues in the Certified_Neuroscientist corpus of D. R. Dison, Founder, Artificial Intelligence University (AIU).

Date of compilation: August 9, 2026.


HOW TO READ THIS OUTLINE

This is deliberately NOT a full catalogue of Layers 4 and 5 - those layers as wholes are substrate, and the framework abstracts them away.

It is a targeted dossier on their cognitive elite: the SLOW VARIABLES - the molecular and organellar machinery by which memory PERSISTS.

The problem this machinery solves is stated in one sentence: every protein at a synapse turns over in hours to weeks, while memories last for decades - so the weight cannot simply be written in protein; it must be written in self-renewing arrangements OF protein.

The corpus's upper volumes say the weight is the memory; this dossier catalogues what the weight is made of, and the allocation rules that decide which experiences get to write one.

Eighteen entries in four parts: the bistable switches, the tagging and capture machinery, the structural consolidation machinery, and the nuclear and genomic machinery.

Tags as always: (COG) for machinery that participates in cognition - here, nearly everything, since persistence of memory IS cognition - and (NON-COG) for supporting machinery.


PART ONE. THE BISTABLE SWITCHES

Molecular flip-flops that hold one bit longer than their own molecules survive.

1. The CaMKII Switch (COG).

Calcium-calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II: a twelve-subunit ring that, once autophosphorylated by a plasticity event, re-phosphorylates its own exchanged subunits faster than turnover erases the mark - a one-bit store, stable for years, and the most studied candidate for the physical engram bit.

2. The PKM-Zeta Loop (COG).

Protein kinase M zeta: a self-sustaining kinase whose local synthesis maintains itself and holds potentiated AMPA receptor numbers high - the maintenance enzyme whose inhibition can erase established memories in animal models, with the honest replication caveats recorded.

3. The CPEB Prion-Like Switch (COG).

Cytoplasmic polyadenylation element binding protein 3: a translation regulator that converts to a self-templating aggregated state at stimulated synapses - a benign prion as a memory device, persistence by conformational inheritance.

4. The CREB Gate (COG).

Cyclic adenosine monophosphate response element binding protein: the transcription-factor switch that decides whether a synaptic event graduates to gene expression - the gatekeeper between short-term change and long-term memory, and the allocation bias that decides which neurons join an engram.


PART TWO. THE TAGGING AND CAPTURE MACHINERY

The allocation rules: which synapses receive the plasticity products an experience triggers.

5. The Synaptic Tag (COG).

The transient, local mark set at a stimulated synapse - a captured state of kinases and cytoskeleton lasting an hour or two - that entitles that synapse, and only that synapse, to capture plasticity proteins made elsewhere; the addressing system of consolidation.

6. The Plasticity Products (The Capture Cargo) (COG).

The activity-triggered synthesis wave - activity-regulated cytoskeleton-associated protein (Arc), brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), and their companions - shipped cell-wide and captured only at tagged synapses; Arc itself, with its retrovirus-like capsid behaviour, catalogued as the strangest courier in the cell.

7. The Local Translation Stations (COG).

Dendritic ribosomes, transported messenger ribonucleic acid granules, and the machinery of on-site protein synthesis beneath individual spines - the reason a synapse can be rebuilt without asking the distant nucleus for every part.

8. The Slot and Trafficking System (COG).

The AMPA receptor slot scaffold - postsynaptic density protein 95 and its partners - plus the exocytic and endocytic traffic that sets receptor number: the weight's actual physical operand, the number the switches of Part One hold steady.


PART THREE. THE STRUCTURAL CONSOLIDATION MACHINERY

Persistence by architecture: making the change a matter of shape.

9. The Spine Actin Skeleton (COG).

The actin cytoskeleton whose regulated polymerisation enlarges a potentiated spine - structural long-term potentiation - converting a chemical event into geometry that outlives it.

10. The Trans-Synaptic Adhesion Complex (COG).

Neurexin-neuroligin and their kin: the bolts that hold pre- and postsynaptic specialisations in register and signal across the cleft - the junction's structural memory, and a hotspot of autism genetics.

11. The Perineuronal Net (COG).

The chondroitin-sulphate lattice that condenses around interneurons at the close of critical periods, locking mature wiring in place - the brake on plasticity whose enzymatic removal reopens juvenile learning; memory protected by fencing.

12. The Mitochondrial Provisioning System (NON-COG).

The positioning of mitochondria at active synapses and the calcium and adenosine triphosphate (ATP) services they provide - the energetic precondition of every entry above, catalogued as the dossier's supporting act.


PART FOUR. THE NUCLEAR AND GENOMIC MACHINERY

The slowest stores: configuration written into chromatin.

13. The Synapse-to-Nucleus Signalling Relay (COG).

The importin-carried and calcium-wave messages by which a synapse petitions the genome - the request channel of consolidation.

14. The Immediate-Early Gene Response (COG).

The fast transcription wave - c-Fos, Arc, and companions - by which the nucleus answers within minutes; the activity marker by which modern neuroscience reads engrams, and the first stage of the genomic write.

15. The Histone Code (COG).

Acetylation and methylation marks on the spools that package deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) - reversible, experience-driven settings of gene readability that gate consolidation; the mechanism by which histone deacetylase inhibitors enhance memory in animal models.

16. The DNA Methylation Store (COG).

The most durable mark: methyl groups written onto DNA itself by experience - the framework's steroid channel terminus, the maternal-care programming mechanism, and the leading candidate for truly lifelong cellular configuration.

17. The Transposon and Somatic Mosaicism Question (NON-COG).

The genuinely contested frontier - retrotransposition and somatic genomic diversity in neurons - catalogued honestly as a question rather than a mechanism, with the evidence graded.

18. The Homeostatic Set-Point Machinery (COG).

The synaptic scaling apparatus that multiplicatively renormalises all of a neuron's weights to hold its average activity at target - the slow governor that protects relative memory while preventing runaway; the cellular partner of the sleep-dependent renormalisation catalogued in the Layer 11 volume.


THE COGNITIVE SCOREBOARD

Sixteen of the eighteen entries carry the (COG) tag - the highest proportion in the corpus, and rightly so: this dossier was assembled precisely by selecting, from two substrate layers, the machinery that cognition cannot be built without.

The two (NON-COG) entries are the power supply (12) and the open question (17).

The dossier's lesson completes the corpus's memory story: the weight lives at Layer 7; the weight's PERSISTENCE lives here, in switches, tags, structure, and chromatin - and for konnectome the translation is direct: checkpointing, write-allocation policy, storage tiering from fast-volatile to slow-durable, and a renormalisation daemon are not optional conveniences but the engineering names of entries 1 through 18.
